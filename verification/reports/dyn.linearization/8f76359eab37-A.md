## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Where hyperbolicity is used" (line 139): "Hyperbolicity enters the proof above as the strict inequality $Au\cdot u \le -\mu r^{2}$ with $\mu > 0$ in Step 1". What Step 1 uses is the strictly stronger sink hypothesis, that *every* eigenvalue has negative real part; at a hyperbolic saddle no linear change of coordinates makes $Au\cdot u$ negative, so hyperbolicity alone does not yield that inequality. The sentence is true of the theorem proved (whose hypothesis does imply hyperbolicity) but misdescribes which hypothesis the step consumes. Fix: "The sink hypothesis enters as ...", keeping the following sentence about the spectral gap.
2. [minor] idea (line 79): "it equals $-\lambda x^{2} - \mu y^{2}$ for a diagonal $A$, a negative multiple of $|u|^{2}$". That quantity is not a multiple of $|u|^{2}$ unless $\lambda=\mu$; it is bounded above by $-\mu|u|^{2}$, which is what Step 1 proves and what the argument needs.
3. [minor] proof, Step 3 (line 129): "the solution never reaches the boundary of $\overline{B_{\delta}}$" — the hypothesis of the step is $|u(0)| \le \delta$, so a solution may start on that boundary. What is proved is that $r$ is non-increasing and hence the solution never *leaves* $\overline{B_{\delta}}$, which is all Step 4 uses.
4. [minor] statement, Hartman-Grobman (line 73): the hypotheses carried over from :cite[Hirsch, Smale and Devaney]{work=hirsch-smale-devaney-2013 loc="p. 168"} are $C^{1}$ and hyperbolicity, which is faithful to the Linearization Theorem as the book states it; but the unit asserts that the conjugacy "preserves the parametrisation of time" while the companion paragraph reports Strogatz's weaker topological-equivalence form, where it does not. Both readings are correctly attributed, so this is only a presentational risk: a reader may take the stronger clause to be common to both sources. A half-sentence marking the first as the conjugacy form and the second as the equivalence form would remove it.
