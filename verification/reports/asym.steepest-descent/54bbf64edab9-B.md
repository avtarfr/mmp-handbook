## Findings
1. [minor] application (line 284): "$\theta_1=3\pi/4$ producing the phase $-\pi/4$ that appears in every Bessel asymptotic formula" over-attributes. In Hassani's Example 12.5.2 the final $-\pi/4$ is $3\pi/4-\tfrac\pi2-\tfrac\pi2$: the $e^{i3\pi/4}$ from the saddle direction, $e^{-i\pi/2}$ from the prefactor $1/(i\pi)$ and a further $e^{-i\pi/2}$ from the "$+1$" in $i^{-\nu-1}$. Fix: say that $\theta_1=3\pi/4$ is the saddle's contribution to the $-\pi/4$, which it combines with the $1/i$ and $i^{-\nu-1}$ factors to produce, or drop "producing".
2. [source] definition (line 51): the unit reports RHB's and Hassani's argument that $\varphi$ has no interior extremum because at a maximum "$\varphi_{xx}$ and $\varphi_{yy}$ would have the same sign, contradicting $\varphi_{xx}+\varphi_{yy}=0$". At a maximum the second derivatives need only be non-positive, so the books' argument as written does not dispose of the degenerate case; the conclusion is of course correct (maximum principle). The unit reports the sources faithfully, so this is not a defect of the unit. Recorded: `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add asym.steepest-descent rhb-2006 "§25.8.1, p. 906" ...`.

- The leading-order formula was re-derived from scratch: with $z-z_0=\rho e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{i(2\theta+\alpha)}=-1$, $\lambda(f-f_0)=-\tfrac12\lambda A\rho^2=-\lambda\tau^2$ gives $\tau=\sqrt{A/2}\,\rho$ and $dz=e^{i\theta}\sqrt{2/A}\,d\tau$, so the answer is $g(z_0)e^{\lambda f(z_0)}e^{i\theta}\sqrt{2/A}\sqrt{\pi/\lambda}=g(z_0)e^{\lambda f(z_0)}e^{i\theta}\sqrt{2\pi/(\lambda A)}$. Matches the statement.
- The two admissible directions $\theta=(\pi-\alpha)/2$ and $(\pi-\alpha)/2+\pi$ are the same as Arfken's $-\tfrac12\arg w''+(\tfrac\pi2$ or $\tfrac{3\pi}{2})$ and as Hassani's $\theta_1=(n\pi-\theta_2)/2$ with $n=1,3$; Hassani's $\theta_2=\arg(\tfrac12f''(z_0))=\arg f''(z_0)=\alpha$, so there is no hidden factor-of-two clash between the three conventions the note block reconciles. The note's claim that RHB's $\pm$ is exactly the choice between the two $\theta$'s is right, since $-e^{i(\pi-\alpha)/2}=e^{i[(\pi-\alpha)/2+\pi]}$.
- Remark claim that a zero of $g$ at $z_0$ demotes the answer to $O(\lambda^{-3/2})$: with $G(\tau)=g(z(\tau))z'(\tau)$ and $G(0)=0$, the $\tau^1$ term is odd and the surviving $\tau^2$ term gives $\tfrac12G''(0)\cdot\tfrac12\sqrt\pi\lambda^{-3/2}$. Correct.
- Remark claim about $z^\nu$ with $\nu\sim\lambda$ moving the saddle, and about the $O(\lambda^{-1})$ (not $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$) relative correction: both consistent with Hassani (12.33), whose series runs in $\alpha^{-k-1/2}$.

The example itself exercises the "off-chain saddle" trap.

- **Topology claim verified numerically, not just asserted.** I integrated the steepest-descent flow $\dot z=-\overline{f'(z)}/\lvert f'(z)\rvert$ out of each saddle in each admissible direction until $\lvert z\rvert=6$, checking $\operatorname{Im}f$ stayed at its saddle value. From $z_2$: $\theta=\pi/6$ ends at $\arg z\approx0.500\pi$ (valley $V_1$) and $\theta=7\pi/6$ ends at $\arg z\approx0.999\pi$ ($V_2$). From $z_1$: $\theta=5\pi/6$ ends at $\arg z\approx0.500\pi$ ($V_1$) and $\theta=-\pi/6$ ends at $\arg z\approx0.001\pi$ ($V_0$). So the chain really is $V_2\to z_2\to V_1\to z_1\to V_0$, and the imaginary-axis path through $z_3$ really joins $V_3$ to $V_1$ (on $z=i\tau$, $f=-\tau-\tfrac14\tau^4$ is real with its maximum at $\tau=-1$). The example's landscape paragraph is correct in every detail.
