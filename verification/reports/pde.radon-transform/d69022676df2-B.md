## Findings
1. [source] evans-2010, §4.3.2a, Theorem 5(i), Eq. (32), p. 188. Theorem 5(i) states the inversion formula with the weight $r^{n-1}$ and no restriction on the dimension, but it is false for even $n$: the folding step in the proof on p. 189 replaces $(r,\omega)$ by $(-r,-\omega)$, which multiplies $r^{n-1}$ by $(-1)^{n-1}$, so for even $n$ the two halves cancel and the right-hand side is $0$, not $u(x)$. The correct weight for all $n$ is $\lvert r\rvert^{n-1}$, which is what this unit's part 5 carries. Nothing downstream in Evans is affected, because he applies (32) only at odd $n$ (his Theorem 5(ii) and his (35)). This is a defect in the source, not in our text, and the unit handles it exactly as the project requires: it proves the $\lvert\sigma\rvert^{n-1}$ version, flags the difference in its `note` block, and warns readers transporting Evans's (32) to even $n$. The entry is already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (row `pde.radon-transform | evans-2010 | Sec. 4.3.2a, Theorem 5(i), Eq. (32), p. 188`), so I have not added a duplicate. Not blocking.
2. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet: "Compact support enters only once, in Step 2, where the tangential integrals ... are made to vanish." It enters at least twice more in the unit's own proof: Step 1 justifies smoothness in $s$ "because $f$ is smooth and compactly supported", and Step 3 says in as many words that "the double integral converges absolutely because $f$ is continuous with compact support, so Fubini's theorem licenses the rearrangement". Fix: say that compact support is what makes the *derivative rule* work (Step 2) and note the two places where it is doing routine convergence work instead.
3. [minor] same remark, second bullet: "Merely integrable $f$ has $\tilde f$ bounded but with no decay at all". The Riemann-Lebesgue lemma does give $\tilde f(k)\to0$ for $f\in L^1$, and the handbook states it in `fourier.fourier-transform` ("Decay. $\hat f(k)\to0$ as $\lvert k\rvert\to\infty$"), so as written this contradicts a prerequisite unit. The intended point — no *rate* of decay, hence no guarantee that $\lvert\sigma\rvert^{n-1}\tilde f$ is integrable — is correct. Fix: "with no guaranteed rate of decay".
4. [minor] definition block: $y$ is used for two different things one line apart — a point of $\Pi(s,\omega)\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ in "$\Pi(s,\omega)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^n : y\cdot\omega=s\}$", and the coordinate vector in "$y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$" immediately after. Step 1 of the proof then writes "every point of $\Pi(s,\omega)$ has $\lvert y\rvert\ge\lvert y\cdot\omega\rvert=\lvert s\rvert$" in the first sense while $y$ has been the $\mathbb{R}^{n-1}$ coordinate everywhere since. The argument is right; only the letter is overloaded. Fix: name points of the hyperplane $x$.
5. [minor] remark, first bullet: "a plane wave $f(x)=F(x\cdot\omega_0)$ is constant along the hyperplanes $\Pi(s,\omega_0)$, so $\mathcal{R}f(s,\omega_0)=\pm\infty$". True only where $F(s)\ne0$; at a zero of $F$ the integral is $0$. Fix: add "wherever $F(s)\ne0$".
6. [minor] definition block attributes the duality $(\mathcal{R}f,F)=(f,\mathcal{R}^{*}F)$ "for the natural inner products on $\mathbb{R}\times S^{n-1}$ and on $\mathbb{R}^n$" to stein-shakarchi-2003 p. 206. Their Eq. (14) is stated only for $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\mathbb{R}\times S^2$, and they leave its verification to the reader. The generalisation to $n$ is routine and the `note` block does say they work in $\mathbb{R}^3$, so this is not a citation error, but the attribution is broader than the cited page. Fix: say "in $\mathbb{R}^3$" at the citation, or cite it as the $n=3$ case.
