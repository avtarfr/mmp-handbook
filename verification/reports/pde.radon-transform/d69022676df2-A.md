## Findings
1. [source] statement, part 5, and note, third bullet: Evans's Theorem 5(i), Eq. (32), p. 188, states the inversion formula with $r^{n-1}$ and with no restriction on the dimension, and it is false for even $n$: his proof on p. 189 folds the $r<0$ half onto the $r>0$ half by replacing $(r,\omega)$ with $(-r,-\omega)$, under which $r^{n-1}$ picks up $(-1)^{n-1}$, so for even $n$ the two halves cancel and the right-hand side is identically $0$. Nothing in Evans's own text is affected, because he applies (32) only at odd $n$ (Theorem 5(ii) and (35)). Our unit states and proves the $\lvert\sigma\rvert^{n-1}$ form, valid for all $n\ge2$, and sets out the difference in its note block, which is the right handling. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add pde.radon-transform evans-2010 "Sec. 4.3.2a, Theorem 5(i), Eq. (32), p. 188"`; not a unit defect.
2. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 173): "Compact support enters only once, in Step 2" understates it. Compact support is also what makes $\mathcal{R}f(s,\omega)$ finite at all (the bullet concedes this two sentences later), what makes the double integral of Step 3 absolutely convergent so that Fubini applies, and — together with smoothness — what puts $\tilde f$ in the Schwartz class in Step 5. Fix: "enters most sharply in Step 2", or list the three uses.
