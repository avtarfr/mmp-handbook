[minor] :::statement[The elementary solutions], part 1 (line 62) and :::proof Step 1 (line 106): the statement says "for a constant vector $\mathbf{k}$" and gives the condition in the invariant form $\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{k}=k^2$, but the proof immediately replaces it by $\nabla^2w=-\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert^2w$ and concludes "if and only if $\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert=k$", which is the same thing only for *real* $\mathbf{k}$. The complex case is not idle here: the evanescent cylindrical solutions of part 4 ($\alpha^2>k^2$) and the evanescent field quoted in the definition block are exactly complex-$\mathbf{k}$ plane waves. Fix: keep $\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{k}$ throughout the proof — $\partial^2w/\partial x_j^2=-k_j^2w$ sums to $-(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{k})w$ with no modulus anywhere — or say "real constant vector" in the statement.
2. [minor] :::statement[The elementary solutions], part 5 (line 96), and :::application, fourth paragraph (line 360): the sign convention silently switches. Part 2 carefully records that Arfken's table uses $\nabla^2G+k^2G=\delta$ and therefore carries the opposite sign to our $\Phi=e^{ikr}/4\pi r$; part 5 then says "the role of $e^{ikr}/4\pi r$ is played by $-\tfrac i4H_0^{(1)}(k\rho)$ ... $-\tfrac{i}{2k}e^{ik\lvert x\rvert}$", which are Arfken's signs, so under our convention the 2-D and 1-D entries should read $+\tfrac i4H_0^{(1)}$ and $+\tfrac{i}{2k}e^{ik\lvert x\rvert}$. Conversely the application block says Arfken "tabulates" the Yukawa potential "as $e^{-kr}/4\pi r$", whereas Table 10.1 (p. 463) has $-e^{-k\lvert\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2\rvert}/4\pi\lvert\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2\rvert$ — the sign the method block itself gets right in step 5. Fix: either quote all four objects in Arfken's sign and say so once, or convert all of them to our sign.
