## Findings
1. [blocking] definition, last paragraph of the bandwidth discussion: "the rate $1/h=2a$ samples per unit of $x$ is the **Nyquist rate**: the reciprocal of the bandwidth, and twice the highest frequency present." The rate $2a$ *is* the bandwidth as this unit defines it, not its reciprocal; it is the *spacing* $h=1/(2a)$ that is the reciprocal of the bandwidth. The unit contradicts itself three paragraphs later, where part 2 correctly says "a lattice of spacing $1/2a$, the reciprocal of the bandwidth", and it misreports Folland, whose Exercise 15c (p. 255) attaches the phrase to the sample points $\{k/2a\}$ "whose spacing is the reciprocal of the bandwidth". Fix: "The spacing $h=1/(2a)$ is the Nyquist spacing, the reciprocal of the bandwidth; the rate $1/h=2a$ is the Nyquist rate, equal to the bandwidth and to twice the highest frequency present."
2. [blocking] example, part (b): "Both are band-limited, $g\in\mathcal{H}_7$ and $\tilde g\in\mathcal{H}_2$" is false for the $\mathcal{H}_a$ defined in this unit, which is a subspace of $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$: neither $\cos(2\pi\cdot7x)$ nor $\cos(2\pi\cdot2x)$ is in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$. The unit makes exactly this objection itself in the remark ("$\sin$ is not in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$"), so the example contradicts the unit's own definition and its own remark, and the sentence "the sampling theorem is not contradicted: with $h=\tfrac15$ it applies to $\tilde g$" claims an application of part 2 that part 2 does not license. The arithmetic of the alias is right; it is the membership claim that is wrong. Fix: either say plainly that a pure sinusoid is outside $\mathcal{H}_a$ and that the computation is the formal/physical reading of part 4 (as the remark already does for $\sin$), or band-limit the example by multiplying by an $L^2$ envelope whose spectrum sits inside the relevant band.
3. [blocking] application, "Information theory": "Part 3 says a signal of bandwidth $W$ over a time $T$ is carried by about $2WT$ numbers." With this unit's convention, bandwidth $W=2a$ means a sampling rate of $2a=W$ per unit time, hence about $WT$ numbers over a time $T$. The familiar $2WT$ counts samples when $W$ denotes the *highest frequency* (the one-sided band), which is the convention this unit explicitly declines in the definition. As written it is off by a factor of two against the unit's own definitions. Fix: either "about $WT$ numbers" with $W$ the bandwidth, or restate with $W$ named as the highest frequency and say so.
4. [blocking] prereqs (front matter) / proof, Steps 2 and 7: the proof's central step is an appeal to `:ref[]{to=fourier.fourier-series}` ("expand it in the Fourier series of ... with period $L=2a$", and again for the coefficients of the periodisation $P$), but `fourier.fourier-series` is not in `prereqs: [fourier.poisson-summation, fourier.fourier-inversion, fourier.plancherel, measure.lp-spaces]`. Step 1 likewise uses the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma by name with no listed prerequisite supplying it, and the remark's last bullet invokes `found.differentiation-under-integral` and `complex.analytic-continuation`. Fix: add `fourier.fourier-series` at least, and name whichever unit carries Riemann-Lebesgue for the transform on $\mathbb{R}$.
5. [minor] remark, bullet "Convergence is absolute but slow": "for each fixed $x$ Step 5 gives $\sum_k\lvert\operatorname{sinc}(2ax-k)\rvert^{2}=1$". Step 5 proves only that the $s_k$ are orthonormal, which does not by itself evaluate a sum of squared *values* at a fixed point. The identity is true (I checked it numerically to $4\times10^{-7}$ with $a=1.3$ at a random $x$, summing $\lvert k\rvert\le2\times10^{4}$) and follows from part 3 applied to $y\mapsto\operatorname{sinc}(2a(x-y))$, which lies in $\mathcal{H}_a$ with $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2^2=1/(2a)$ and samples $\operatorname{sinc}(2ax-k)$. Fix: cite part 3, not Step 5.
6. [minor] method, step 7: the trapezoidal identity is asserted for spacing $h\le1/(2a)$. That is correct but not sharp: Poisson summation gives $h\sum_kf(kh)=\sum_m\hat f(m/h)$, so the identity already holds for every $h<1/a$ — which is what Stakgold and Holst state on their p. 147 ($\lambda\le2\pi/\Omega$). Fix: give the condition as $h<1/a$ and note that the Nyquist spacing is a special case, or say explicitly that $h\le1/(2a)$ is being used only because it is the sampling spacing already in play.
7. [minor] definition, final paragraph: the unit says "Stakgold and Holst ... $\Omega$ is called the bandwidth. Their $\Omega$ is our $2\pi a$", immediately after defining *our* bandwidth as $2a$. Both are faithfully reported and the conversion $\Omega=2\pi a$ is given, but the single word "bandwidth" is left naming two different quantities a paragraph apart. Fix: add half a sentence - "so what they call the bandwidth is $\pi$ times ours, the band edge in angular frequency rather than the full width in cycles".
