## Findings
1. [blocking] statement, part 4 (Aliasing), and proof Step 7: the hypotheses are too weak for the conclusion to be meaningful or for the proof to go through. (a) The conclusion is about "the samples $f(k/2a)$", but $f\in L^1\cap L^2$ is an equivalence class and has no pointwise values - precisely the objection the unit itself raises in the first bullet of its remark ("For $f\in L^2$ alone, 'the value of $f$ at $k/2a$' is not even defined"). Assuming $\hat f$ continuous does not help; that is automatic for $f\in L^1$ and says nothing about $f$ pointwise, so the hypothesis as stated is also redundant. (b) Step 7 integrates the periodisation term by term ($\int_{-a}^{a}\sum_m=\sum_m\int$) and then applies Fourier inversion to get $\frac1{2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat f\,\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}k\xi/a}=\frac1{2a}f(-k/2a)$. Mere convergence of $P(\xi)=\sum_m\hat f(\xi+2am)$ justifies neither: the interchange needs uniform (or dominated) convergence, and the inversion integral needs $\hat f\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$. Fix: replace "suppose $\hat f$ is continuous and the periodisation below converges" by "suppose $\hat f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\sum_m\hat f(\cdot+2am)$ converges absolutely and uniformly on $[-a,a]$, and take the continuous representative $f(x)=\int\hat f(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi$" - the one hypothesis also makes the samples well defined and licenses both steps, and it is what the last bullet of the remark ("decay faster than $\lvert\xi\rvert^{-1}$ suffices") already has in mind. (Note the worked example (b) satisfies the stronger hypothesis, $\hat g$ being compactly supported, so no number in the unit changes.)
2. [blocking] application, "Information theory", last two sentences: "The raised-cosine pulse of the example is not an accident either: pulses of that family ... vanish at the other sampling instants, so that successive symbols do not interfere." This contradicts the unit's own example. The pulse with $\hat f(\xi)=\cos(\pi\xi/2)$ on $[-1,1]$ is the *root*-raised-cosine (the raised cosine with roll-off $1$ is $\tfrac12[1+\cos\pi\xi]=\cos^{2}(\pi\xi/2)$), and it does *not* vanish at the sampling instants: the example itself lists $f(k/2)=4(-1)^k/[\pi(1-4k^2)]\neq0$ for every $k$, and uses those non-zero numbers to reconstruct $f$; it does not vanish at the integers either ($f(1)=-4/(15\pi)$). Only the full raised cosine, which is this pulse's spectrum squared, satisfies the zero-ISI condition. Fix: say that the example's spectrum is the root-raised cosine, which is the standard *transmit* shape because the transmit-receive pair then realises the full raised cosine, and that it is the full raised cosine - not the pulse of the example - that vanishes at the other symbol instants.
3. [minor] example, part (b), the list of seven common samples: a rounding slip - the sixth value is printed as $0.1550612$ but the exact value is $9\sqrt3/(32\pi)=0.15506125183\ldots$, i.e. $0.1550613$ to the seven decimals used for every other entry in the list. Fix: print $0.1550613$.
4. [minor] method, step 7, last sentence: "The example below verifies the identity at a spacing finer than either bound." The example uses $h=\tfrac12$, which is finer than the quadrature bound $h<1/a=1$ but is *exactly* the Nyquist spacing $1/(2a)=\tfrac12$, not finer than it. Fix: "at the Nyquist spacing, twice as fine as the quadrature bound requires".
