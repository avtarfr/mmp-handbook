## Findings
1. [blocking] proof, Step 1 (line 110): "Fourier inversion (:ref[]{to=fourier.fourier-inversion}) then gives, for almost every $x$, $f(x)=\int_{-a}^{a}g(\xi)e^{2\pi i x\xi}d\xi$." The hypothesis of the invoked theorem is not met, and in general is false here. Every version in `fourier.fourier-inversion` requires $f\in L^{1}$: the Schwartz version asks $f\in\mathcal S$, the "integrable version" asks $f\in L^1$ *and* $\hat f\in L^1$, and the piecewise-smooth version asks $f\in L_1(-\infty,\infty)$. That unit contains no $L^2$ statement at all (the string $L^2$ does not occur in it). Here we know only $f\in L^2$ and $g=\hat f\in L^1\cap L^2$; a typical member of $\mathcal H_a$ is not integrable — take $\hat f=\chi_{[-1/2,1/2]}$, so $f=\operatorname{sinc}\notin L^1$. Since Step 1 is what produces the continuous representative on which every later pointwise statement (the samples, uniform convergence, part 2 "for every $x$") depends, the gap is load-bearing. Fix: justify the step from `fourier.plancherel` instead, which *is* a listed prerequisite: its part 3 gives the unitary extension $U=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\mathcal F$ of $L^2(\mathbb R)$ onto itself, and its definition block records that on $L^1\cap L^2$ the transform is given by the integral; hence $f=\mathcal F^{-1}_{L^2}g$ agrees a.e. with the absolutely convergent integral $\int_{-a}^{a}g(\xi)e^{2\pi i x\xi}d\xi$ because $g\in L^1\cap L^2$ and the two inverse transforms coincide on that overlap. One sentence suffices; the conclusion itself is correct.

2. [minor] frontmatter, `prereqs` (line 7): two results are used that are in the map but not listed. (a) Step 2 (line 134) needs the *completeness* of $\{(2a)^{-1/2}e^{\pi i k\xi/a}\}$ in $L^2[-a,a]$ — i.e. mean-square convergence of the Fourier series — which is `fourier.parseval-mean-square`; the listed `fourier.fourier-series` gives only orthogonality and uniqueness of a uniformly convergent expansion, not completeness. (b) Step 6 (line 177) uses the expansion $f=\sum_k(f,s_k)s_k$ and Parseval's identity along an orthonormal basis, which is `funcan.orthonormal-bases` (other units, e.g. `spec.hermite-polynomials`, list it although it is unwritten). Fix: add both ids, or derive part 3 directly from Plancherel and Step 2 ($\lVert f\rVert_2^2=\lVert g\rVert_{L^2[-a,a]}^2=2a\sum_k\lvert c_k\rvert^2$).

3. [minor] example (b), last paragraph (line 313): "To record $g$ faithfully one must sample at $15$ per unit or faster". The rate is correct for reconstruction by the formula of part 2, but "must" is too strong for $g$ itself: $g$ is a bandpass signal whose spectrum sits in $[6.5,7.5]\cup[-7.5,-6.5]$, and the example has just shown that at $6$ samples per unit the folded copies do *not* overlap, so those samples already determine $g$ among signals known to live in that band. Fix: "to record $g$ faithfully *by the sinc series of part 2*". Also, the reason offered ("since $\hat g$ vanishes at the band edges $\pm7.5$") is not needed: $\mathcal H_{7.5}$ is defined by an almost-everywhere condition, so $g\in\mathcal H_{7.5}$ was already established at line 279 and part 2 applies with $a=7.5$ regardless of the edge values.

4. [minor] idea (line 94): "the Fourier coefficients of $\hat f$ on $[-a,a]$ are ... precisely the values of $f$ at the lattice points $k/2a$". Step 2 gives $c_k=\frac{1}{2a}f(-k/2a)$: there is a factor $1/(2a)$ and a reflection $k\mapsto-k$. The third paragraph of the same block is precise about the analogous constant, so the looseness here is avoidable. Fix: "are the values $f(-k/2a)$, up to the factor $1/(2a)$".
