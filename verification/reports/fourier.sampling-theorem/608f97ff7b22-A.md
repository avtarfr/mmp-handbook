## Findings
1. [blocking] definition (line 31) and proof, Steps 1 and 4 (lines 110-116, 167): the unit silently works in a convention that is not the handbook's, and misstates a prerequisite while doing so. The unit declares $\hat f(\xi)=\int f(x)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}x$ and then writes "so that the inversion formula of :ref[]{to=fourier.fourier-inversion} carries no constant at all". That is false about the referenced unit: `fourier.fourier-inversion` is stated for $\hat f(k)=\int f(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}kx}\,\mathrm{d}x$ and its inversion formula (statement part 2, and part 1 of "Two weaker versions") carries $\tfrac{1}{2\pi}$. The same gap is then used, not merely mentioned: Step 1 invokes that unit's $L^1$ inversion to get $f(x)=\int_{-a}^{a}g(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi$ with no constant, and Step 4 invokes `fourier.plancherel` as an isometry, $\lVert f-Tg_K\rVert_{L^2(\mathbb{R})}=\lVert g-g_K\rVert_{L^2[-a,a]}$, whereas that unit proves $\lVert f\rVert_2^2=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\lVert\hat f\rVert_2^2$ and states explicitly that "our $\mathcal{F}$ is not itself unitary; $(2\pi)^{-1/2}\mathcal{F}$ is". Everything in this unit is correct *in its own convention*, so this is a bridging defect, not a mathematical one — but as written the unit asserts something untrue about a prerequisite and applies two prerequisites in a form they do not have. Fix: do what the sibling unit `fourier.poisson-summation` already does in its "Convention, local to this unit" paragraph — say that the $2\pi$-in-the-exponent convention is local to this unit and is **not** the handbook default, give the dictionary (the $\hat f(\xi)$ here is the $\hat f(2\pi\xi)$ of `fourier.fourier-transform`), and then say that *read through that dictionary* the inversion formula and Plancherel's identity of the cited units carry no constant. One added sentence in the definition plus a half-sentence at Step 4 suffices; no mathematics changes.

[source] statement / method step 7, against stakgold-holst-2011 §2.4, p. 148: the book's prose says (2.4.9) holds "if $h<\pi/\Omega$", but its own (2.4.10a) is stated at $x_k=k\pi/\Omega$, i.e. at $h=\pi/\Omega$ exactly, and the closing sentence of the subsection says "sampling at the uniform spacing $h=\pi/\Omega$". With their closed band condition $\phi^{\wedge}(\omega)=0$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert\ge\Omega$, the non-strict inequality is what is true. The unit follows (2.4.10a) and is therefore right; recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`, not a fault of ours.

3. [minor] example (b) (line 313): "To record $g$ faithfully one must sample faster than $15$ per unit" contradicts the unit's own part 2 applied with $a=7.5$. The unit has just said $g\in\mathcal{H}_{7.5}$, and $\hat g$ is supported in the closed set $[6.5,7.5]\cup[-7.5,-6.5]$, so the rate $15$ exactly (spacing $1/15$) already reconstructs $g$ exactly. Fix: "at $15$ per unit or faster (and in practice faster, by the margin of step 3)".

4. [minor] method step 6 (line 205) and example (b): "Nyquist frequency $1/(2h)$" is used twice but never defined. The definition block defines only the Nyquist *rate* $1/h=2a$ and the Nyquist *spacing* $h=1/(2a)$, and the two numbers differ by a factor $4$ in general, so a reader coming from the definition block has to guess. Fix: add "the Nyquist frequency $1/(2h)$, half the sampling rate" to the definition block, where the other two names are fixed; the sibling `fourier.dft-fft` defines it that way in its method step 3.
