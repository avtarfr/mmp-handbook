## Findings
1. [minor] method (line 210): step 7 asserts the quadrature identity $\int f=h\sum_k f(kh)$ for *every* band-limited $f$ and every $h<1/a$, justifying it by "Poisson summation (:ref[]{to=fourier.poisson-summation}) gives...". The prerequisite's Poisson statement holds for $f\in\mathcal{S}$, for $f$ and $\hat f$ of moderate decrease, or under Folland's bounds $|f(x)|\le C(1+|x|)^{-1-\varepsilon}$; a band-limited $f$ satisfies none of these automatically, and need not even be in $L^1$ — the unit itself makes this point forcefully in Step 1 with $f=\operatorname{sinc}$. Stakgold and Holst hedge at exactly this point, opening their derivation with "Let $\phi(x)$ be a function for which Poisson's formula (2.3.27) holds" (p. 147); our text drops that clause. The result is true for everything in the intended scope (the example's $f$ decays like $x^{-2}$, and I checked $h\sum_k f(k/2)=1$ there to ten figures), and this is a procedural block, so it is not blocking. Fix: restore the hedge — "for a band-limited $f$ to which Poisson summation applies (decay faster than $|x|^{-1}$ suffices)" — and mirror it in the application's "the trapezoidal rule is *exact* on band-limited functions" (line 326 ff.).
2. [minor] idea (line 100): "Each exponential $\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}k\xi/a}$, cut off to the band, inverts to a shifted sinc" carries the opposite sign from the series actually expanded in Step 2, whose terms are $c_k\mathrm{e}^{+\pi\mathrm{i}k\xi/a}$ (the minus sign belongs to Step 5's $\widehat{s_k}$). Harmless, since the family is the same after $k\mapsto-k$, but it momentarily disagrees with the proof it is describing. Fix: write $\mathrm{e}^{+\pi\mathrm{i}k\xi/a}$, or add "(with the index reflected, as Step 4 does)".
3. [minor] statement part 4 (lines 82–86) and remark (line 200): the display $f(x)=\int\hat f(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi$ is said to exist "because $\hat f\in L^1$", and the remark says this "gives a continuous representative *of $f$*". Absolute convergence alone gives a continuous function; that it represents $f$ is the integrable inversion theorem, part 1 of the "Two weaker versions" statement of :ref[]{to=fourier.fourier-inversion}, whose hypotheses ($f\in L^1$, $\hat f\in L^1$) are both in force here. Since Step 1 goes out of its way to say that inversion is *not* being used, leaving this one legitimate use unnamed reads as an omission. Fix: cite the weaker-version statement at that point.
4. [minor] proof Step 2 (line 138): the completeness of $\{(2a)^{-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}k\xi/a}\}$ in $L^2[-a,a]$ is attributed to :ref[]{to=fourier.parseval-mean-square}, which states mean-square convergence on the circle of circumference $2\pi$ for Riemann-integrable $f$, extending to $L^2(-\pi,\pi)$ only as a recorded (unproved) remark. The rescaling from period $2\pi$ to period $2a$ is routine and :ref[]{to=fourier.fourier-series} sets up general period $L$, but neither the rescaling nor the reliance on the $L^2$ extension is mentioned. Fix: one clause noting both.
