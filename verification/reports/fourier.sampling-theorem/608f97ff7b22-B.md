## Findings

1. [blocking] example, part (b) (line 313): "To record $g$ faithfully one must sample faster than $15$ per unit" is false for this $g$, and it contradicts part 2 of our own statement. The example has already established $\hat q(\xi)=\cos\pi\xi$ on $\lvert\xi\rvert\le\tfrac12$ and $0$ outside, so $\hat g(\xi)=\tfrac12[\hat q(\xi-7)+\hat q(\xi+7)]$ is exactly zero for $\lvert\xi\rvert>7.5$ (and vanishes at the edges $\pm7.5$ as well). Hence $g\in\mathcal{H}_{7.5}$ and part 2 applied with $a=7.5$ gives $g(x)=\sum_k g(k/15)\operatorname{sinc}(15x-k)$ exactly: the rate $15$ per unit, which the same paragraph calls the Nyquist rate of $g$, already suffices. The unit is punctilious about exactly this strict/non-strict distinction elsewhere (the "strictness at the band edge" bullet, and the closed condition $\hat\phi(\omega)=0$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert\ge\Omega$ taken from Stakgold and Holst), so the reader will read "must" as a mathematical necessity claim, and as such it is wrong. Fix: write "at $15$ per unit or faster" (the Nyquist rate suffices here because $\hat g$ vanishes at $\pm7.5$), and keep the practical "in practice one oversamples" advice as the separate remark it already is in the hypotheses remark and in method step 3.
