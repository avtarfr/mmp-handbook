1. [blocking] statement "The separated solutions in spherical polars", part 1 (lines 145-151), and proof Step 5 (line 254): the separation constants are declared to be arbitrary **complex** numbers ("complex numbers, subject to no further restriction"), and the polar factor is then written $EP_\ell^m(\cos\theta)+FQ_\ell^m(\cos\theta)$ for those complex $\ell,m$. Our listed prerequisite `spec.associated-legendre` defines $P_l^m$ and $Q_l^m$ **only** for integers $l\ge0$ with $|m|\le l$ ("For integers $l\ge0$ and $\lvert m\rvert\le l$ ... The second, linearly independent solution of (AL) *for integer $l$ and $m$* is ... $Q_l^m$"). So for non-integer $\ell$ or $m$ the symbols in (4), in (5) (line 171) and in Step 5 are undefined in this handbook. The same defect appears at line 166, where the counterexample $u=r^{1/2}P_{1/2}(\cos\theta)$ uses a Legendre function of degree $\tfrac12$ that nothing in the handbook defines. Fix: either (i) restrict part 1 and Step 5 to integer $m$ (which single-valuedness gives anyway) and non-negative integer $\ell$, stating the general-complex case only as "two linearly independent solutions of (AL)" without naming them $P_\ell^m,Q_\ell^m$; or (ii) add the Ferrers functions of general complex degree and order to `spec.associated-legendre` and list it as supplying them. For the counterexample at line 166, replace $P_{1/2}$ by an explicitly exhibited solution, or say "a solution of (AL) with $\ell=\tfrac12$, $m=0$" and note it is unbounded at $\theta=\pi$.

2. [blocking] statement part 1 (lines 148-151) and proof Step 5 (line 254): the claim that the solutions of the associated Legendre equation are $M=EP_\ell^m+FQ_\ell^m$, and hence that (4) is the general separated solution apart from "two exceptional cases" ($\ell=-\tfrac12$ and $m=0$), is **false** even for integer indices, whenever $0\le\ell<|m|$. By our own `spec.associated-legendre` (definition block), $P_l^m\equiv0$ for $|m|>l$, so $\{P_\ell^m,Q_\ell^m\}$ then spans at most one dimension of the two-dimensional solution space. Explicit counterexample satisfying every hypothesis of the statement: take $R(r)\equiv1$, $\Theta(\theta)=\cot\theta$, $\Phi(\varphi)=\cos\varphi$ on $r\in(0,\infty)$, $\theta\in(0,\pi)$, $\varphi\in\mathbb{R}$. All three factors are $C^\infty$ and none vanishes identically; $u=\cot\theta\,\cos\varphi$ is harmonic (verified symbolically with sympy on the spherical Laplacian of the definition block), $R$ solves the Euler equation with $\lambda=0$ (so $\ell=0$) and $\Theta$ solves (AL) with $\ell=0$, $m=1$. Here $\ell\ne-\tfrac12$ and $m\ne0$, so neither listed exception applies, yet $u$ is not of the form (4): with $(\ell,m)=(0,1)$ one has $P_0^1\equiv0$ and $Q_0^1(\mu)=-(1-\mu^2)^{-1/2}=-\csc\theta$, and $\cot\theta$ is not a multiple of $\csc\theta$. Fix: add the hypothesis $|m|\le\ell$ to the "generic" case of part 1 (it is implied once part 2's two regularity conditions are imposed, so part 2 and (4a) are unaffected), and record $0\le\ell<|m|$ as a third exceptional case in which the second solution is not $P_\ell^m$; correspondingly, in Step 5 do not assert $\{P_\ell^m,Q_\ell^m\}$ is a basis without that restriction.

3. [blocking] statement "The separation lemma" (lines 57-63) versus its uses in proof Steps 1 (line 200) and 3 (line 238): the lemma is stated only for **real-valued** functions, $F:A\to\mathbb{R}$ and $G:B\to\mathbb{R}$, but part 1 of the spherical statement permits the separation constants to be non-real complex numbers, which forces the factors $R,\Theta,\Phi$ — and hence the two groups to which the lemma is applied — to be complex-valued ($\Phi''/\Phi\equiv-m^2$ with $\Phi$ real-valued forces $m^2$ real). As written the unit therefore applies the lemma outside its stated hypotheses. Fix: state the lemma for $F:A\to\mathbb{C}$, $G:B\to\mathbb{C}$ (its two-step proof is unchanged word for word), or else state in the spherical statement that the factors are complex-valued and that the lemma is used in that form.

[blocking] proof Step 6 (line 256): "$P_\ell^m$ is finite there only when $\ell$ is a non-negative integer with $0\le\lvert m\rvert\le\ell$, which is the condition established in `spec.associated-legendre`". That is not what the prerequisite establishes, and as phrased it is circular for non-integer $\ell$ (see finding 1: $P_\ell^m$ is not defined there for such $\ell$, so it cannot be "finite" or not). Fix: in Step 6, invoke part 3 of `spec.associated-legendre` in that form ("any solution of (AL) bounded at $\mu=\pm1$ forces $\ell$ to be a non-negative integer with $|m|\le\ell$, and the bounded solution is then a multiple of $P_\ell^m$"), which also removes the need to argue separately that $F=0$.
