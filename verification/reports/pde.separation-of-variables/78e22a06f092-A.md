3. [blocking] application "Normal modes and resonant cavities" (line 416): "The cylindrical example above is the design calculation for a microwave cavity ... a cylinder of radius $1$ cm and height $2$ cm resonates lowest at $\omega/2\pi = ck/2\pi = 13.7$ GHz." The arithmetic from the example's $k$ is right ($k = 2.8723835\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, $ck/2\pi = 13.71$ GHz), but the physical identification is wrong, and so therefore is the stated lowest cavity resonance. An electromagnetic cavity is not the scalar Dirichlet problem of the example: for perfectly conducting walls the TM$_{mnp}$ resonances are $k^2 = (j_{m,n}/a)^2 + (p\pi/L)^2$ with $p = 0,1,2,\dots$ (the $p=0$ mode that the Dirichlet condition $u=0$ at $z=0,L$ excludes is allowed), and the TE$_{mnp}$ resonances use the zeros $j'_{m,n}$ of $J'_m$. For $a = 1$ cm, $L = 2$ cm the lowest is TM$_{010}$, $k = j_{0,1}/a = 2.4048256\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, i.e. $ck/2\pi = 11.5$ GHz (I computed 11.48 GHz with $c = 2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s), with the lowest TE mode, TE$_{111}$ at $j'_{1,1}=1.8412$, close behind at 11.6 GHz. 13.7 GHz is not the lowest resonance of that cavity. Fix: either drop the electromagnetic claim and present the example as the scalar (acoustic/quantum-box) Dirichlet analogue — "if the field vanished on the whole wall the lowest frequency would be 13.7 GHz" — or state the EM boundary conditions properly and quote TM$_{010}$ at 11.5 GHz, noting that it is independent of $L$.
