## Findings
1. [blocking] proof of "The separated solutions in spherical polars", Step 1 (line 186): "divide by $u$" is not justified, and the hypothesis that was put in the statement to deal with it is never used. The statement (line 143) assumes only that "none of the three factors vanishes identically"; that does **not** make $u = R\Theta\Phi$ nonzero on the region, and in every non-trivial case it is zero somewhere inside it — $\Theta = P_\ell^m(\cos\theta)$ has interior zeros for $\ell\ge1$, $\Phi = \cos m\varphi$ has them for $m\ge1$, and $R = Ar^\ell+Br^{-(\ell+1)}$ has one whenever $AB<0$. As written, Step 1 divides by a quantity that vanishes on a set of codimension one and then applies the separation lemma on the whole product region, where the identity it separates does not hold. The same objection applies to the division in Step 3 (by $\Theta\Phi$, via $\sin^2\theta$) and to the catalogue note, which is derived the same way. Note also that Step 1 is where the lemma's product-domain requirement is invoked by name, and the "Where the hypotheses are used" remark (line 274) advertises exactly that requirement — so the gap sits under the unit's own spotlight. Fix (short, and the sibling unit `pde.schrodinger-equation` already does it in its Step 1, which can be copied): carry out the division on the open set $\{r: R(r)\ne0\}\times\{\theta:\Theta(\theta)\ne0\}\times\{\varphi:\Phi(\varphi)\ne0\}$ — nonempty precisely by the stated hypothesis, and a product of open sets, so the lemma applies — and then add that each separated ODE extends from there to the whole interval because both of its sides are continuous: at a zero of $R$ that is a limit of points where $R\ne0$ the identity passes to the limit, and on the interior of $\{R=0\}$ both sides vanish identically. Do the same for $\Theta$ and $\Phi$. With that in place the statement's nonvanishing hypothesis is used, and Step 1's appeal to the product structure is honest.
2. [minor] $\alpha$ carries three unrelated meanings inside the unit: the time-separation constant of method step 2 (line 82, "both equal a constant $\alpha$"), the axial separation constant of the cylindrical catalogue and the cylinder example ($Z''=\alpha^2Z$, lines 113–127 and 328), and the angular eigenvalue of the Hassani remark ($L^2Y=\alpha Y$, line 282). Each is locally defined and no statement is wrong, but a reader meeting $\alpha$ for the third time has to work out which one it is. Fix: rename the angular eigenvalue in the remark (Hassani's $\alpha$ there is the $\ell(\ell+1)$ of the rest of the unit), and say in method step 2 that $\alpha$ is used again for the axial constant below.
3. [minor] `prereqs` (line 7) lists `spec.bessel-orthogonality` but none of the units that define the functions the unit actually names and uses: proof Step 8 and the catalogue conclude with $J_{\ell+1/2}$ and $Y_{\ell+1/2}$ (`spec.bessel-first-kind`, `spec.bessel-second-kind-hankel`), the catalogue rejects $y_\ell$ at the origin (`spec.spherical-bessel`) and names $I_m, K_m$ for $k^2+\alpha^2<0$ (`spec.modified-bessel`), and the cylinder example uses the zeros $j_{m,n}$ and the singularity of $Y_m$ at $\rho=0$. Fix: add the ones that are load-bearing (at least `spec.bessel-first-kind` and `spec.bessel-second-kind-hankel`).
4. [minor] statement, Helmholtz display (5) at line 171: $F$ is simultaneously the name of the solution and the coefficient of $Q_\ell^m$. The unit flags this in the parenthesis at line 174 and the clash is inherited from rhb-2006 Eq. (21.57), but since our solution symbol is ours to choose, renaming it (to $u$, as in (4)) would remove the need for the parenthesis.
5. [minor] catalogue note, Cartesian paragraph (line 103): "a pair of exponentials $e^{\pm\lvert l\rvert x}$ (equivalently $\sinh,\cosh$) if $l^2<0$". When $l^2<0$ the constant $l$ is imaginary, so $\lvert l\rvert$ is not the right modulus to write — the exponents are $\pm\sqrt{-l^2}\,x$. Fix: write $\pm\sqrt{-l^2}$, or say "with $l = i\beta$, the pair $e^{\pm\beta x}$".
