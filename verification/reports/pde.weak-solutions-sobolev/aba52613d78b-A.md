## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", third bullet "*$C^2$ regularity in Part 2 above*" (line 217): the counterexample offered is not a counterexample. The bullet asserts "Drop it and the conclusion is false as stated" and then points at the slit-disc function $u=r^{1/2}\sin(\theta/2)$ of the example. But (i) on the slit disc $B\setminus S$ the tip of the slit is the origin, which lies in $S$ and so is *not* in that domain; $u$ is $C^\infty$ on all of $B\setminus S$, so there it satisfies both the hypothesis and the conclusion of Part 2 and witnesses nothing; and (ii) on the whole disc $u$ is not a weak solution of (P) at all — it is not in $H^1_0(B)$ (it equals $\sin(\theta/2)\not\equiv0$ on $|x|=1$) and, as step 6 of the example itself computes, $-\Delta u$ there is a single layer on the slit, not an $L^2$ function. So neither reading supplies a weak solution of (P) that fails to be classical. Fix: either drop the claim that the conclusion fails and keep only the true observation that two classical derivatives are what lets the integration by parts be run backwards, or give a genuine witness — with merely continuous $f$ a weak solution need not be $C^2$ (the Newtonian potential of a continuous, non-Hölder $f$ is the standard example), which is exactly why Part 2 must assume $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ rather than deduce it.
2. [minor] statement "Weak and classical solutions agree where both make sense" (lines 178–181): several hypotheses are never used. The proof works entirely on an interior set $A$ with $\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset A\Subset\Omega$, so the "$C^1$ boundary" of $\Omega$, the memberships $u\in C(\overline\Omega)$ and the pointwise condition "$u=0$ on $\partial\Omega$" play no role in Part 1 (the text's own caveat says $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ is what carries the boundary condition), and $f\in C(\Omega)$ is needed only in Part 2. Fix: either drop the unused hypotheses or say in the statement that they are carried only to match Stakgold and Holst's framing.
3. [minor] remark, fourth bullet "*Boundedness of $\Omega$*" (line 218): the stated reason, "to keep $L^2(\Omega)\subset L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ convenient", is not a reason — that inclusion holds for every open $\Omega$, bounded or not, by Cauchy–Schwarz on each $A\Subset\Omega$. Fix: say instead that boundedness is not used in this unit at all, and is carried only because the Poisson statement is set up for the problem solved in pde.lax-milgram.
4. [minor] remark, fifth bullet (line 219): Evans's Example 4 gives $u\in W^{1,p}$ unbounded on every open subset only for $0<\alpha<(n-p)/p$, so for $H^1$ ($p=2$) it needs $n\ge3$; as written the bullet reads as if the construction worked in every dimension (in $n=2$ there is no admissible $\alpha$, though a logarithmic variant still works). Fix: add "in dimension $n\ge3$", or cite the parameter range.

[minor] prereqs (line 7) and proof of the weak/classical statement (line 191) and example step 3 (line 262): Green's first identity and the divergence theorem are relied on and `:ref`'d to vcalc.green-identities, which is not in `prereqs`.
