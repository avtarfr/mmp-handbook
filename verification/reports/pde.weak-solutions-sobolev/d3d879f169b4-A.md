## Findings
1. [minor] proof of "The weak derivative is well defined", Step 1 (line 65): the convergence $w*\eta_\varepsilon \to w$ in $L^1(A)$ is asserted ("mollification converges in $L^1$") without proof and without a source. No listed prerequisite supplies it: `measure.lp-spaces` gives density of simple and of $C_c$ functions, not mollifier convergence, and "mollif" occurs nowhere else in the handbook except `pde.poisson-integral-formula`. It is not standard undergraduate calculus. Fix: cite it (e.g. Evans §C.5, which the unit already draws on elsewhere) or prove it in two lines from density of $C_c$ in $L^1(A')$ plus $\|w*\eta_\varepsilon\|_{L^1}\le\|w\|_{L^1}$.
2. [minor] proof, Step 3 (line 75): "Choose an open $A$ with smooth boundary such that $\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subset A$ and $\overline A \subset \Omega$ compact" — the existence of such an $A$ is used but not justified, and smoothness of $\partial A$ is in fact not needed, since every boundary term is killed by the support of $\varphi$. Fix: drop "with smooth boundary" and say the set may be taken to be a finite union of balls covering $\operatorname{supp}\varphi$ inside $\Omega$.
3. [minor] statement "The weak derivative is well defined", line 48: "Stakgold and Holst ... set the first as their Exercise 2.6.1". Exercise 2.6.1 is on printed p. 184, outside the page range `182-183` given for that source entry (the body of §2.6 on p. 183 only remarks that $D^\alpha u$ is unique up to null sets). Fix: extend the locator to p. 184 for that clause.
4. [minor] example, step 5 (lines 293-299): the computation shows that the *classical* $\partial_1\partial_1 u$, defined off the null set $S\cup\{0\}$, is not in $L^2(B)$, and the unit then concludes "$u$ has ... no second [weak derivatives]". The intermediate step — that a weak second derivative on $B$ would restrict to the classical one on the open set $B\setminus S$, whose complement in $B$ is null — is left implicit. Fix: one clause stating it.
5. [minor] example (lines 262, 293): the argument uses that $z\mapsto z^{1/2}$ is holomorphic off the cut and the Cauchy-Riemann consequence $\partial_1\operatorname{Im}f = \operatorname{Im}f'$. `complex.cauchy-riemann` and `complex.branch-points-cuts` exist in the map but are neither listed in `prereqs` nor referred to with `:ref`.
6. [minor] remark, line 229: the bullet is labelled "*Weak derivatives are not local limits.*", but its content is that a Sobolev function can be unbounded on every open set (Evans Example 4). The label does not describe the point. Fix: relabel, e.g. "Sobolev membership is not pointwise regularity".

## Checked
Re-derived every proof step. First proof: the mollifier vanishing lemma (including the choice $\varepsilon<\operatorname{dist}(\overline A,\partial\Omega)$ and the exhaustion $A_k$), the subtraction giving uniqueness, and the $|\alpha|$-fold integration by parts giving consistency. Completeness proof: the slot-by-slot Cauchy argument and the passage to the limit in (W), including the reason $\varphi,\partial_i\varphi\in L^2$. Weak/classical equivalence: Green's first identity on $A$ in both directions, and the bump-function argument (the stated bump is smooth, positive on $B(x_0,r)$ and compactly supported, and $\int q\varphi\ge\tfrac12 q(x_0)\int\varphi>0$ follows). Remark: verified the Heaviside obstruction ($\int H\varphi'=-\varphi(0)$ on $(-1,1)$, and the shrinking-bump dominated-convergence argument), and verified the $C^2$ counterexample in full — for $u=\tfrac12 x(1-|x|)$ I confirmed $u'=\tfrac12(1-2|x|)$, $u''=-\operatorname{sgn}$ off $0$, continuity of $u'$ at $0$ so the two boundary terms $\pm\tfrac12\varphi(0)$ cancel, $\int_{-1}^1 u'\varphi'=\int_{-1}^1(\operatorname{sgn})\varphi$, the soft-threshold truncation putting $u$ in $H^1_0$, and the claim that in one dimension a continuous $f$ forces $u\in C^2$. Also checked that $a$ is not coercive on $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ while (P$'$) with $f=0$ still has only $u=0$ there.
Example: re-derived $\nabla u$ in the polar frame and $|\nabla u|^2=1/(4r)$; the divergence-theorem argument on $\Omega_\varepsilon$ with the $O(\varepsilon^{3/2})$ inner-circle term; the two integrals $\int_B u^2=\pi/3$ and $\int_B|\nabla u|^2=\pi/2$, giving $\|u\|^2_{H^1}=5\pi/6=2.6179939$ and $\|u\|_{H^1}=1.6180216$ (mpmath), which indeed matches the golden ratio $1.6180340$ to five significant figures and no more; $f''(z)=-\tfrac14 z^{-3/2}$ giving $\partial_{11}u=\tfrac14 r^{-3/2}\sin\tfrac{3\theta}{2}$ and the divergent $\tfrac{\pi}{16}\int_0^1 r^{-2}dr$; and the slit-face normal derivative $-\tfrac12 r^{-1/2}$ on *both* faces, which is what makes the two contributions add to $-\int_0^1 r^{-1/2}\varphi(r,0)\,dr$ rather than cancel.
