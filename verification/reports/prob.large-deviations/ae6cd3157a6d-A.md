## Findings
1. [source] statement "Elementary properties", item 2 (lines 66-71): the unit asserts that $\Lambda$ is convex "at every point where it is finite" and justifies it by the formula for $\Lambda''(t)$ plus Cauchy-Schwarz. That argument only covers the interior of $D=\{t:M(t)<\infty\}$; at a finite endpoint of $D$ at which $M$ is still finite, $M$ need not be twice differentiable. The conclusion is true on all of $D$ (Holder), and the unit reproduces Grimmett and Stirzaker p. 226, eq. (5.11.2) and its surrounding sentence faithfully, so this is not a fault of our text. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`. No action needed; the unit only ever uses convexity on the interior (properties 3 and 5, Steps 1 and 3).
2. [minor] prereqs (line 7): the monotone convergence theorem is invoked by name twice in Step 4 (line 208, $\mathbb{E}(X^c)\to 0$; line 217, $\Lambda^{c}(\zeta)\to\Lambda(\zeta)$), and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for expectations is used in property 2 (line 71), but neither `measure.monotone-convergence-fatou` nor `prob.expectation-variance` is a direct prerequisite. Both are reachable through the transitive closure of the four listed prereqs, and published units differ in practice (prob.martingales names `measure.monotone-convergence-fatou`, spec.hypergeometric does not), so this is presentation rather than a gap. Fix: add `measure.monotone-convergence-fatou` to `prereqs`.
3. [minor] proof, Step 4 (line 211): "Step 3 applies to the truncated variables" is correct but leans on two things the three bullets above it do not quite supply. (a) Step 0 normalised $\mu=0$, whereas here only $\mathbb{E}(X^{c})\le 0<a$ holds; Step 3 in fact only needs $a>\mathbb{E}(X^{c})$ and $\tau^{c}>0$, both of which hold. (b) Step 3 cites Step 1 for $\Lambda''(\tau)\in(0,\infty)$, and Step 1's non-degeneracy argument has to be re-run for $X^{c}$. It does go through verbatim, since $c>a$ gives $\mathbb{P}(X^{c}>a)=\mathbb{P}(X>a)>0$ while $\mathbb{E}(X^{c})\le 0<a$, so $X^{c}$ is not a.s. constant. Fix: add one clause recording $\mathbb{P}(X^{c}>a)=\mathbb{P}(X>a)>0$, hence $\operatorname{var}(X^{c})>0$, and noting that Step 3 uses only $a>\mathbb{E}(X^{c})$, not centring.
4. [minor] proof, Step 4, third bullet (line 209): the bullet shows $at-\Lambda^{c}(t)\to-\infty$ and concludes the maximiser is "finite ... $\tau=\tau^{c}>0$". Finiteness follows from that growth plus continuity, but strict positivity does not; it comes from the derivative of $at-\Lambda^{c}(t)$ at $t=0$ being $a-\mathbb{E}(X^{c})>0$. Fix: say so in half a sentence.
5. [minor] application, "Statistical mechanics" (line 309): "$\Lambda(t)$ is, up to sign conventions, the free energy per subsystem at inverse temperature $-t$" is off by more than a sign. With $\beta=-t$, $\Lambda(-\beta)=\log Z$, so $\Lambda$ is $-\beta$ times the free energy per subsystem, not the free energy. Fix: write "$\Lambda(-\beta)=\log Z$, that is, $-\beta$ times the free energy per subsystem at inverse temperature $\beta$".
6. [minor] example, "Against the second-moment bound" (line 297): Chebyshev's inequality bounds the two-sided event $\{\lvert S_{200}-200\rvert\ge 100\}$; the one-sided bound quoted is valid but is not the sharpest Chebyshev-type bound available (Cantelli would give $200/(200+100^2)\approx 0.0196$). The comparison is unaffected. Fix: say "Chebyshev's inequality, applied to the two-sided event, gives only".
