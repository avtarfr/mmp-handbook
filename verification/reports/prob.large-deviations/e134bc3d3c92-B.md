## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Elementary properties of $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda^{*}$", property (5) "Only positive $t$ matters" (line ~101): the identity $\Lambda^{*}(a) = \sup_{t>0}\{at-\Lambda(t)\}$ is asserted under the sole hypotheses $\mu = 0$, $a > 0$, and is false without the finiteness of $M$ near the origin. Counterexample: let $Y$ be Pareto with density $3y^{-4}$ on $[1,\infty)$ (mean $3/2$) and $X = Y - 3/2$, so $\mu = 0$. Then $M(t) = \infty$ for every $t > 0$, while $M$ is finite on $(-\infty,0]$. By Jensen $\Lambda(t) \ge \mu t = 0$ for $t < 0$ (checked numerically: $\Lambda(-2) = 0.41065$, $\Lambda(-1) = 0.14593$, $\Lambda(-0.5) = 0.048273$, $\Lambda(-0.1) = 0.0029007$), so with $a = 1$ every $t<0$ gives $at - \Lambda(t) \le t < 0$ and the supremum over all of $\mathbb{R}$ is attained at $t=0$: $\Lambda^{*}(1) = 0$. The right-hand side, however, is a supremum of $-\infty$ over $t>0$, i.e. $-\infty \ne 0$. The block's own justification silently uses the missing hypothesis, since it invokes $\Lambda'(0) = \mu$, which is property (1) "whenever $M'(0)$ exists" and here it does not. The source states this as eq. (5.11.6) inside the proof of Theorem (5.11.4), under the standing hypothesis that $M$ is finite near the origin (Grimmett and Stirzaker, p. 227, "we have used here the assumption that $M(t) < \infty$ near the origin"); lifting it into a standalone statement block drops that hypothesis, so this is also a weakening relative to the cited source. Note properties (1) and (3) of the same block do carry their provisos, so the omission is isolated. Fix: state property (5) as "If $M$ is finite in a neighbourhood of the origin, $\mu = 0$ and $a > 0$, then ...", and keep the existing justification, which is then correct.
