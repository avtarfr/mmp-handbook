## Findings
1. [minor] example (the line after the Lambda^*(1.5) display): "Equivalently $\Lambda^{*}(a) = a - 1 - \log a$ for $a > 1$" restricts the formula more than necessary. The stationary point $\tau = 1 - 1/a$ lies in the domain $(-\infty,1)$ for every $a > 0$, so the formula holds for all $a>0$; the remark on the hypothesis $a>\mu$ later evaluates exactly this expression at $a = \tfrac12$. Nothing is wrong, but a reader may read the two passages as inconsistent. Fix: write "for $a > 0$" (the case $a>1$ being the one relevant here).
2. [minor] proof, Step 1 (the paragraph beginning "Note also that $\sigma^{2} > 0$"): the passage from property (2), which gives $\Lambda'' \ge 0$, to the strict inequality $\Lambda'' > 0$ uses the equality case of Cauchy-Schwarz (equality iff $Xe^{tX/2}$ and $e^{tX/2}$ are proportional, i.e. $X$ a.s. constant). The unit supplies the needed fact (that $X$ is not a.s. constant) but leaves the equality-case step implicit. Grimmett and Stirzaker make the same step, citing their Theorem (4.5.12), so this is faithful; it is only a gap in exposition. Fix: add "the equality case of Cauchy-Schwarz" to the sentence.
3. [minor] proof, Step 3 (the display $\int_{na}^{nb} d\widetilde{F}_n(u)$ identified with $\mathbb{P}(na < \widetilde{S}_n < nb)$): the Stieltjes integral over $[na,nb]$ is $\mathbb{P}(na < \widetilde{S}_n \le nb)$, so the identification is off by the atom at $nb$. This is harmless (the stated quantity is the smaller one, and the bound is in the right direction; the limit $\tfrac12$ is unaffected) and the source writes it the same way. Fix: use $\le nb$, or say "at most".
