## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Three practical forms of the surface element", part 3 (line 90): the sphere is parametrized "on $(0,\pi)\times(0,2\pi)$", an open rectangle, whereas the definition block (line 29) requires the parameter domain $D$ to be a connected set that is the closure of a bounded open set. The sentence that follows ("Deleting the two poles and one meridian...") presupposes that the patch started on the closed rectangle. Fix: give the patch on $[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi]$ and then delete the poles and the meridian, or say explicitly that the open rectangle is the set on which the patch is regular and injective and that the closure is the domain.
2. [minor] statement "The surface element does not depend on the parametrization" (line 67): that $h = \mathbf{r}^{-1}\circ\bar{\mathbf{r}}$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism is asserted as part of the set-up. For two regular patches with the same image this is a theorem (do Carmo's change-of-parameters proposition in §2-3), not a definition. Fix: either state it as a hypothesis in so many words ("and suppose the change of parameters $h$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism") or cite the proposition that supplies it.
3. [source] remark, bullet on the modulus in the projection formula (line 156): Riley, Hobson and Bence's Eq. (11.10) omits the modulus in the denominator and so returns a negative $dS$ wherever $\partial f/\partial z < 0$. The unit reports the book's form faithfully, flags the problem, and gives the corrected version, which is right. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add vcalc.surface-integrals rhb-2006 "§11.5.1, Eq. (11.10), p. 391"`. No action needed in the unit.

## Checked
- Counterexample search: tried to break each of the three practical forms and the invariance statement. Graph form: $\mathbf{r}_x\times\mathbf{r}_y = (-h_x,-h_y,1)$ verified by hand, third component always $+1$, so "with the upward orientation" cannot fail. Projection form: tested on the sphere, where it is exactly the case the unit warns about — a vertical line meets it twice and the uncut projection cancels the two halves; the warning is correct and necessary. Sphere form: $\mathbf{r}_\theta\times\mathbf{r}_\phi$ computed independently as $a^2\sin\theta(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta) = a\sin\theta\,\mathbf{r}$, so $dS = a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$ and $\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}$, both as stated; at $\theta = 0,\pi$ the cross product vanishes, which is the regularity failure the remark names. Invariance: the sign bookkeeping of Step 4 ($\bar{\hat{\mathbf{n}}} = \operatorname{sgn}(\det Dh)\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, constant sign on a connected domain) survives the degenerate cases I tried (orientation-reversing $h$, a cone's apex, the doubly covering spherical patch on $(0,\pi)\times(0,4\pi)$, which does give twice the right answer as the remark says). Non-orientable case: areas and scalar integrals are indeed defined on the Möbius strip because Steps 1–3 never mention the normal. The Lagrange identity $|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|^2 + (\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b})^2 = |\mathbf{a}|^2|\mathbf{b}|^2$, hence $|\mathbf{r}_u\times\mathbf{r}_v| = \sqrt{EG-F^2}$, holds as stated. I could not break anything.
