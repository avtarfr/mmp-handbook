## Findings
1. [blocking] statement[Three practical forms of the surface element], form 2 (line 84): "If the piece of $S$ projects one-to-one onto a region $R$ of the $xy$-plane, then on it $f_z \ne 0$ and $dS = dA/\lvert\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}\rvert = \lvert\nabla f\rvert\,dA/\lvert f_z\rvert$." The implication asserted is false: a one-to-one vertical projection does not force $f_z \ne 0$. Counterexample: $f(x,y,z) = z^3 - y$, so $\nabla f = (0,-1,3z^2) \ne \mathbf{0}$ everywhere, and $S = \{f = 0\} \cap \{\lvert x\rvert < 1, \lvert z\rvert < 1\}$ is the sheet $y = z^3$. Its projection onto the $xy$-plane is injective — for each $(x,y)$ with $\lvert y\rvert < 1$ there is exactly one $z = y^{1/3}$ on $S$ — yet $f_z = 3z^2$ vanishes on the whole line $z = 0$ of $S$, where the surface is vertical ($\hat{\mathbf{n}} = (0,-1,0)$, so $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}} = 0$) and the displayed formula has a zero denominator. So $f_z \ne 0$ is a genuine extra hypothesis, not a consequence; and it is exactly the hypothesis Riley, Hobson and Bence's derivation needs, since their $dA = \lvert\cos\alpha\rvert\,dS$ (p. 391) is useless where $\cos\alpha = 0$. Fix: make it a hypothesis — "Suppose the piece of $S$ projects one-to-one onto a region $R$ of the $xy$-plane *and* satisfies $f_z \ne 0$ on it (equivalently, is nowhere vertical: $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}} \ne 0$). Then ..." — and, if the sharper remark is wanted, note that the two conditions are independent: $f_z \ne 0$ on a connected piece does give a one-to-one projection locally, but not conversely.

The two sentences are in tension; the first should admit several curves from the start, since that is what the Stokes unit needs and what Arfken's Fig. 3.13 (right) is about. 4. [minor] statement[The surface element does not depend on the parametrization] (line 67): "let $h = \mathbf{r}^{-1}\circ\bar{\mathbf{r}}$ be the change of parameters, a $C^1$ diffeomorphism from the interior of $\bar D$ onto the interior of $D$". That $h$ *is* a diffeomorphism is a theorem for regular patches with the same image (do Carmo §2-5), not something that follows from the words "change of parameters"; here it is silently assumed. The proof then re-derives $\det Dh \ne 0$ from the cross products at the end of Step 2, which is circular-looking if the hypothesis has already granted it. Fix: say explicitly that $h$ being a $C^1$ diffeomorphism is assumed (citing do Carmo for the fact that it is automatic), and in Step 2 drop "consistent with $h$ being a diffeomorphism" or recast it as the independent check it is.
