1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used" (line 258): the claim that the sign of the
   background/observation error correlation decides the *direction* of the error in the reported
   analysis covariance is false outside the scalar case. The text asserts flatly that with
   $C = \mathbb{E}[e_b\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}]$ positive definite "the true error *exceeds* the
   reported $A$ and the system is overconfident", and that "for negative correlation the reported
   $A$ ... is merely pessimistic". Counterexample with $n=p=2$ and $H=I$ (so not even a sign
   artefact of $H$), all matrices symmetric positive definite and the joint error covariance
   $\begin{psmallmatrix}B&C\\C^{\mathsf T}&R\end{psmallmatrix}$ positive definite (eigenvalues
   $0.0323, 0.7303, 8.4078, 9.7410$):
   $B=\begin{psmallmatrix}2.321839&3.521742\\3.521742&6.361903\end{psmallmatrix}$,
   $R=\begin{psmallmatrix}0.580056&-0.918092\\-0.918092&9.647616\end{psmallmatrix}$,
   $C=\begin{psmallmatrix}0.407712&0.033097\\0.033097&0.054346\end{psmallmatrix}$ (eigenvalues
   $0.0513, 0.4108$, so $C \succ 0$). Using the uncorrelated gain $K_\ast = BH^{\mathsf T}S^{-1}$,
   the reported $A = (I-K_\ast H)B$ has diagonal $(0.312767,\,1.338030)$ while the true
   $A_{\text{true}} = (I-K_\ast H)B(I-K_\ast H)^{\mathsf T} + K_\ast RK_\ast^{\mathsf T}
   + (I-K_\ast H)CK_\ast^{\mathsf T} + K_\ast C^{\mathsf T}(I-K_\ast H)^{\mathsf T}$
   (the unit's own cross-term formula, line 252) has diagonal $(0.478046,\,0.571719)$. Component 2
   of the true error is *below* the reported value, and $\operatorname{tr}A_{\text{true}}
   - \operatorname{tr}A = -0.601$: with $C \succ 0$ the scheme is pessimistic, not overconfident,
   and the error is not even of one sign across components. Replacing $C$ by $-C$ (the joint
   covariance has the same eigenvalues, by congruence with $\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)$, so it is
   equally admissible) reverses the diagonal differences to $(-0.165279,\,+0.766311)$, so the
   "negative correlation is merely pessimistic" half fails in the same example. A one-dimensional
   counterexample also exists as soon as $H<0$: $H=-1$, $B=2$, $R=0.5$, $C=0.4$ gives $K_\ast=-0.8$,
   reported $A=0.4$, true $A=0.272$. Fix: either restrict the claim to the scalar case
   (where $0<K_\ast H<1$ makes the cross term $2(1-K_\ast H)K_\ast C$ carry the sign of $C$, and the
   unit's own $H=1,B=2,R=0.5,C=0.6$ example does behave as described: reported $0.4$, true $0.592$),
   or state the correct general fact, which is only that
   $A_{\text{true}} - A = (I-K_\ast H)CK_\ast^{\mathsf T} + K_\ast C^{\mathsf T}(I-K_\ast H)^{\mathsf T}$
   is in general indefinite, so the reported covariance can err in either direction, component by
   component, whatever the sign of $C$.
