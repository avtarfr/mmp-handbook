## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Algebra and lattice operations" (line 116), supporting statement "Closure under the usual operations" (a) and (c) (lines 72, 78): the complex cases are reduced to the real ones "by splitting into real and imaginary parts", but only the *forward* direction of that reduction is justified. The text argues that $\operatorname{Re}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}f$ are measurable because $z\mapsto\operatorname{Re}z$, $z\mapsto\operatorname{Im}z$ are continuous. What the conclusion needs is the converse — that $u,v:X\to\mathbb{R}$ measurable makes $u+iv:X\to\mathbb{C}$ measurable — and that is nowhere stated or proved in the unit. It is not a formality: Folland isolates it as Cor. 2.5, p. 45 and proves it from $\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}}$ (Prop. 1.5), i.e. by exactly the product-$\sigma$-algebra route this proof block announces it is avoiding; he also invokes Cor. 2.5 again to get Cor. 2.9, the complex pointwise-limit statement that the unit's (c) asserts in its last sentence. As it stands, "so are $f+g$ and $fg$" for $\mathbb{C}$-valued $f,g$, and the complex half of (c), are unproved. Fix: add the two-line argument that the generating criterion already supplies — every open $U\subseteq\mathbb{C}$ is a countable union of open rectangles $I\times J$ with rational corners, and $f^{-1}(I\times J)=(\operatorname{Re}f)^{-1}(I)\cap(\operatorname{Im}f)^{-1}(J)\in\mathcal{M}$ — and record "$f$ is measurable iff $\operatorname{Re}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}f$ are" as a fourth consequence of the generating criterion, so that both directions are available where they are used.

3. [minor] definition block (line 31): "Because preimage respects unions and complements, a composition of measurable mappings is measurable." The reason is that preimage composes, $(g\circ f)^{-1}(E)=f^{-1}(g^{-1}(E))$; the union/complement identities are what make $\mathcal{G}$ a $\sigma$-algebra later, not what gives this. Fix: replace the causal clause.

The countable version is true and follows at once, but it is ours, not Folland's; and only the two-piece case ($D$ and $X\setminus D$) is actually used here.
