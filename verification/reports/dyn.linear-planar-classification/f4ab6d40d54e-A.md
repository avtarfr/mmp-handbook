## Findings
1. [blocking] statement, case 2 (line 80): "every orbit off the fast eigenline arrives tangent to the slow eigendirection, while its direction tends to the fast eigendirection as $t \to -\infty$". The second clause is false for the orbits that lie on the *slow* eigenline. Those orbits are off the fast eigenline, yet $x(t) = c_{s}e^{\lambda_{s}t}v_{s}$ has direction $\pm v_{s}/|v_{s}|$ for every $t$, so its direction does not tend to the fast eigendirection as $t \to -\infty$; it never leaves the slow one. The source states this clause for the typical trajectory only (Strogatz p. 133, "In backwards time ... the trajectories become parallel to the fast eigendirection", after "Trajectories *typically* approach the origin tangent to the slow eigendirection"), so the unit has strengthened a generic statement into a universal one. Fix: state the backward-time clause for orbits off *both* eigenlines (equivalently $c_{s}c_{f} \neq 0$), keeping the tangency clause for orbits off the fast eigenline.
2. [blocking] proof, Step 3 (line 125): "Dividing instead by $c_{f}e^{\lambda_{f}t}$ and letting $t \to -\infty$ gives convergence to $v_{f}$". This divides by $c_{f}$, which is zero precisely for the slow-eigenline orbits that the statement's case 2 includes; the hypothesis $c_{f} \neq 0$ is never imposed, and nothing earlier in Step 3 excludes it (the step opens with "suppose $c_{s} \neq 0$" only). So the proof does not prove case 2 as stated, and the one case it fails to cover is exactly the one where the claim is false. Fix: add $c_{f} \neq 0$ to this half of the argument and match the statement to it.
3. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 165): ":cite[Hirsch, Smale and Devaney]{work=hirsch-smale-devaney-2013 loc=\"p. 63\"} point out that a single point of the $(T,D)$ plane carries a two-parameter family of matrices, which may differ in exactly these two respects". Two over-readings of the source. HSD p. 63 say "infinitely many different matrices" (they do not count the parameters), and they list the rotation sense and the number of independent eigenvectors with "such as", i.e. as examples and not as an exhaustive list. The exhaustiveness is moreover only true up to a linear change of coordinates: two matrices with $T = -3$, $D = 2$ can have quite different pairs of eigendirections and so visibly different portraits in the plane. Fix: say "infinitely many matrices" (the two-parameter count is then ours, which is fine), replace "in exactly these two respects" by "among other things in these two respects", or add "up to a linear change of coordinates".
4. [minor] statement, case 3 (line 89): "Every orbit winds about the origin with constant angular velocity $\omega$", with $\omega > 0$, whereas Step 4 of the proof establishes that in the $(p,q)$ basis the rotation is through the angle $-\omega t$, so "the polar angle decreases at the constant rate $\omega$". The statement should say angular *speed*, or fix a sign convention; as written the statement and the proof assert opposite senses of rotation, and the method block (step 7) then has to recover the sense from the field.
5. [minor] frontmatter, hirsch-smale-devaney-2013 source entry (lines 15-16): the locator claims "§8.4, the definitions of stable, asymptotically stable and unstable equilibrium, p. 174", and `pages: "61-63, 174"`. On p. 174 one finds the definition of a stable equilibrium and the first sentence of the definition of asymptotic stability; that definition is completed, and *unstable* is defined, on p. 175. Fix: cite pp. 174-175.
6. [minor] proof, Step 5 (lines 152-155): "$\frac{(c_{1}+c_{2}t)v + c_{2}w}{|c_{1}+c_{2}t|} \to \pm v$ as $t \to \pm\infty$" pairs the two signs with the two time directions, but the limit is $\operatorname{sgn}(c_{2})\,v$ as $t\to+\infty$ and $-\operatorname{sgn}(c_{2})\,v$ as $t\to-\infty$, so the correspondence is with the sign of $c_{2}$, not with the direction of time. The conclusion drawn (tangency to the eigenline at both ends) is unaffected.
