[source] definition (line 44): the unit defines the rank as "the dimension of a maximal commuting subalgebra", reporting Arfken, Weber and Harris p. 848 ("the maximum number of independent generators that all mutually commute") and noting that Hassani's footnote 4, p. 969 instead says "dimension of the Cartan subalgebra" while his so(3) argument on p. 970 uses the maximal-commuting form. The two are not equivalent in general: sl(4,C) has rank 3 but contains the 4-dimensional abelian subalgebra spanned by E_13, E_14, E_23, E_24, so counting Casimirs by maximal abelian dimension would give 4 there. The unit reports both books faithfully and names both descriptions, so this is not a failure. Recorded with tools/discrepancy.mjs against arfken-2013. Fix (optional): add half a sentence saying the two agree for the semisimple algebras treated here but not in general.

3. [minor] application (line 183): "the two Casimirs are the only labels it carries" is false for massless irreducible unitary representations of the Poincare group, where $P^2 = W^2 = 0$ and inequivalent reps are separated by helicity, which is not a Casimir eigenvalue (the photon's $h = +1$ and $h = -1$ reps have identical Casimir eigenvalues). The claim is correct for $m > 0$. It sits in an `original` block, so we own it. Fix: restrict to the massive case, e.g. "for $m > 0$ the two Casimirs are a complete set of labels; the massless case is labelled by helicity instead".
4. [minor] example (b) (line 175): "Two labels, as Arfken, Weber and Harris say, is why the weight diagrams of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ are two-dimensional" — the nearest antecedent is the two *Casimirs* just discussed, but a weight diagram is plotted with the eigenvalues of the two commuting generators $T_3, T_8$, which is what p. 849 is actually about ("the rank of a group indicates the number of indices needed to label the basis"). Fix: say "two commuting generators, and so two labels on the states".
5.
