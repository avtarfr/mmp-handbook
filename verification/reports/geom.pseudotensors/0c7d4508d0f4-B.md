## Findings

1. [blocking] application "Crystal and continuum physics" (line 150, `provenance=original`):
   "a pseudotensor of odd rank is not killed by the same argument, which leaves room for
   effects such as optical rotation." The general clause is right, but optical rotation is
   the wrong example and contradicts the rule stated in the same sentence. Natural optical
   activity is carried by the gyration tensor $g_{ij}$, a second-rank *axial* tensor (or
   equivalently by the third-rank *polar* tensor $\gamma_{ijl}$ in the spatial-dispersion
   form $\varepsilon_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ij}^0+i\gamma_{ijl}k_l$). Apply the unit's own
   definition with $L=-I$: an axial tensor of rank $n$ picks up
   $\det L\,(-1)^n=(-1)^{n+1}$, so axial tensors of *even* rank vanish in a centrosymmetric
   group, exactly as polar tensors of odd rank do; the polar rank-3 $\gamma$ vanishes too.
   Centrosymmetric crystals are therefore not optically active (quartz, class 32, is; calcite
   is not), which is the standard statement of Neumann's principle for gyrotropy. Fix: keep
   the general clause and change the example to an odd-rank axial property that a
   centrosymmetric crystal really can have — a rank-1 axial quantity such as a spontaneous
   magnetisation is the simplest — or move optical rotation to the list of effects that
   *require* the absence of a centre, alongside piezoelectricity.
2. [minor] remark "Orientation and the square root" (line 110): "*$g \neq 0$* is needed
   before $\sqrt g$ can be used". For a real positive square root the condition is $g>0$,
   which is what the Euclidean charts of
   :ref[]{to=geom.tensor-operators-general-coordinates} supply and what that unit's own
   remark states; $g\neq 0$ alone is not enough. Fix: write $g>0$ (and note $|g|$ for the
   indefinite case, as the sibling unit does).
3. [minor] application "Rotation and the hat map" (line 147): "its dual pseudovector
   $\omega_i = \tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}\Omega_{kj}$". The displayed formula is correct — with
   $\Omega=\dot RR^{\mathsf T}$ and $\Omega v=\omega\times v$ one has
   $\Omega_{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}\omega_k$, so $\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}\Omega_{kj}=+\omega_i$ —
   but by statement 3 of this same unit the *dual* of $\Omega$ is
   $\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}\Omega_{jk}=-\omega_i$. Calling $\omega$ "the dual" while silently
   transposing the indices invites exactly the sign error that statement 3 exists to
   prevent. Fix: say that $\omega$ is the dual of $-\Omega$, or that the hat map is
   statement 3 read backwards up to the sign fixed by $\Omega v=\omega\times v$, and keep
   the formula as written.
