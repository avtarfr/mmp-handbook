## Findings
1. [minor] proof, "Part 1, independence of the base point": the reduction is stated only for base points that overlap. "the part of $[b,b+L]$ outside $[a,a+L]$ is a translate by $\pm L$ of the part of $[a,a+L]$ outside $[b,b+L]$" is true when $\lvert b-a\rvert<L$, but false as written when $b$ is a further base point (for $b=a+3L$ the two sets are the whole intervals and the translate is by $3L$). The conclusion is unaffected, since the $L$-periodic integrand lets one replace $b$ by $b-kL\in[a,a+L)$ first. Fix: open the paragraph with "we may assume $a\le b<a+L$, since shifting $b$ by a multiple of $L$ changes neither interval's integral of an $L$-periodic function", or say "a translate by an integer multiple of $L$".
2. [minor] remark, "A hypothesis we did not impose": "Arfken, Weber and Harris give the same name to the last three" is slightly generous to the source. On p. 935 Arfken names exactly two conditions as the Dirichlet conditions (finitely many finite discontinuities, finitely many extrema on $[0,2\pi]$); the existence of the coefficient integrals is required in a separate sentence just above, not inside the named list. Fix: "name the middle two of these, requiring the existence of the coefficient integrals separately". The substantive point made here — that Arfken omits periodicity and calls these sufficient but not necessary — is correct, and the footnote on p. 935 confirms the latter.
3. [minor] definition, "Even and odd extensions": "These are the only even and odd extensions there are, so the construction involves no choice" sits awkwardly one sentence after conceding that the even extension's value at the origin is unfixed. Fix: "up to the value at the origin, these are the only ones".
