[blocking] definition "Fourier coefficients and Fourier series" (line 79): "If $f$ is real-valued then $\hat f(-n)=\overline{\hat f(n)}$, **and conversely**" is false for the class of functions the definition works with (integrable $f$). Counterexample: $f=\mathrm{i}\,\chi_{\{a\}}$ on $I=[a,a+L]$ is integrable, has $\hat f(n)=0$ for every $n$, hence satisfies $\hat f(-n)=\overline{\hat f(n)}$, and is not real-valued. The unit contradicts itself here: the remark (line 193) says "Changing $f$ at finitely many points leaves every $\hat f(n)$ unchanged, so no theorem can conclude $f=g$ pointwise from $\hat f=\hat g$", which is exactly the obstruction to this converse. Fix: state the converse in the only form available, e.g. "conversely, if $\hat f(-n)=\overline{\hat f(n)}$ for all $n$ then $f$ is real at every point where $f$ is continuous (by Stein and Shakarchi's Theorem 2.1, quoted in the remark below), and in particular a continuous $f$ is real-valued"; or drop "and conversely" and keep only the equivalence with "$a_n,b_n$ real", which is an identity and is correct as written.

2. [blocking] example, Step 2 (line 239) and Step 4 (line 267): the identification of the sum of the series with $\varphi$ does not follow from what is cited. The chain offered is "M-test $\Rightarrow$ uniform convergence, and part 2 above then identifies the sum". Part 2 gives only that the sum $g$ of the uniformly convergent series satisfies $\hat g(n)=B_n=\widehat{\varphi_{\mathrm{odd}}}(n)$ for every $n$; concluding $g=\varphi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ needs a uniqueness statement, which part 2 explicitly is not — the unit's own remark (line 191) says so: "part 2 is a statement about a series that is *given* as convergent; it does not say that the Fourier series of a function converges to it". The same gap carries the $x=0$ evaluation of the cosine series in Step 4, where the two displays are "forced" to agree. The final numbers ($\pi^3/32$, $\pi^2/6$) are independently correct — I checked both to 16 figures — so only the justification is missing. Fix: after the M-test step, apply Stein and Shakarchi's Corollary 2.2 (already quoted in the remark at line 193) to $g-\varphi_{\mathrm{odd}}$: both are continuous on the circle ($\varphi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ because $\varphi(0)=\varphi(L)=0$) and all their Fourier coefficients agree, hence they are equal; likewise with $\varphi_{\mathrm{even}}$ in Step 4. 4. [blocking] proof, "Part 1, the real relations" (line 163): the count is wrong, and the stated arithmetic gives the wrong answer. Expanding $\cos\cos$ into four exponential terms with coefficient $\tfrac14$, the terms of frequency $r-p$ *and* $-(r-p)$ both survive when $r=p>0$ — two terms, each contributing $L$, giving $2\cdot\tfrac14 L=L/2$. The text says "exactly one term survives with coefficient $\tfrac14$ ... giving $L/2$", which would give $L/4$; the same undercount is made for $\sin\sin$. Fix: "two of the four terms survive, those of frequency $\pm(r-p)$, each with coefficient $\tfrac14$ (for the sines, $-\tfrac14\cdot(-1)=\tfrac14$), so the integral is $2\cdot\tfrac14 L=L/2$". While there, the justification given for the sine-cosine case ("the coefficient pattern $\tfrac1{4\mathrm{i}}(+,-,+,-)$ is antisymmetric under $r\mapsto-r$ while the surviving exponential integrals are symmetric") is opaque and does not name which pairs cancel; the direct version is one line: the surviving frequencies are $\pm(r-p)$ with coefficients $+\tfrac1{4\mathrm{i}}$ and $-\tfrac1{4\mathrm{i}}$, so they cancel, and for $r=p=0$ all four coefficients sum to zero.
