## Findings
1. [minor] proof (line 163): in "Part 1, the real relations" the bookkeeping is miscounted — "for $r=p>0$ exactly one term survives with coefficient $\tfrac14$ ... giving $L/2$". One term of coefficient $\tfrac14$ over an interval of length $L$ gives $L/4$. In fact *two* of the four exponential terms survive at $r=p$, the pair at frequencies $\pm(r-p)=0$, each contributing $\tfrac14 L$, which is how $L/2$ arises. The final values in the display of part 1 are right (I verified all nine real relations numerically at a non-symmetric base point); only the sentence miscounts. Fix: "two terms survive, each with coefficient $\tfrac14$ (for the sines, $-\tfrac14\cdot(-1)=\tfrac14$ each), giving $L/2$".
2. [minor] proof (line 161): "Note that the base point $a$ dropped out of the calculation; this is the only place periodicity is used in part 1" sits awkwardly beside the third paragraph of the same proof, which uses the periodicity of $f$ explicitly ("since the integrand $fe_{-n}$ has period $L$") to get base-point independence. Only the periodicity of the *exponentials* is in play at line 161. Fix: say "only the periodicity of the exponentials is used here", and let the later paragraph own the periodicity of $f$.
3. [minor] statement, part 4 (line 145): "Both series therefore have period $2l$, and represent $\varphi$ on $(0,l)$ while representing its reflection on $(-l,0)$." Nothing in this unit proves representation — the definition block states explicitly that $\sim$ "carries no claim about convergence, and none is made here", and part 2 only covers series *given* as uniformly convergent. Fix: "...and are the Fourier series of $\varphi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ and $\varphi_{\mathrm{even}}$, which agree with $\varphi$ on $(0,l)$ and with its reflection on $(-l,0)$; whether they converge there is the business of :ref fourier.dirichlet-convergence."
