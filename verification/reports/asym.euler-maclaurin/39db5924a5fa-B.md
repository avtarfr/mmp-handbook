2. [minor] remark, oscillating summand (line 170): the quoted sizes drop constants in a way that makes them not upper bounds for small $\omega$. The correction term is bounded by $\frac{|B_{2p}|}{(2p)!}\,2\omega^{2p-1} = 4\zeta(2p)\,\omega^{-1}(\omega/2\pi)^{2p}$, not $(2/\omega)(\omega/2\pi)^{2p}$; and the remainder bound is $\frac{|B_{2q}|}{(2q)!}(b-a)\omega^{2q} = 2\zeta(2q)(b-a)(\omega/2\pi)^{2q}$, not $(b-a)\,\omega\,(\omega/2\pi)^{2q}$ — the latter understates the bound whenever $\omega < 2\zeta(2q) \approx 2$. Checked at $\omega=1$, $b-a=1$, $q=1$: true bound $2\zeta(2)/(2\pi)^2 = 0.0833$, the unit's expression $0.0253$. The conclusion drawn (geometric in $(\omega/2\pi)^{2q}$, threshold at $\omega=2\pi$) is unaffected and correct. Fix: replace the prefactors by $2\zeta(2p)$ / $2\zeta(2q)(b-a)$, or soften "at worst" to "up to a constant".
3. [minor] note "Why $|\widetilde B_{2q}| \le |B_{2q}|$" (lines 144-161), labelled `original`: its central display, $\widetilde B_k(x) = -k!\sum_{j\neq0}e^{2\pi ijx}/(2\pi ij)^k$, is literally the last result of Stein & Shakarchi Problem 5(e), p. 99 — a page this unit already cites in its frontmatter, though its `role` line does not mention the Fourier series.
