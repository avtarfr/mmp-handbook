## Findings
1. [minor] statement "The five tests", part 5 (line 90): the Leibniz hypothesis is given non-strictly, $u_{n+1} \le u_n$ for $n \ge N$, whereas Riley, Hobson and Bence print the strict condition ($u_n < u_{n-1}$ for all $n > N$, p. 130). The non-strict form is the correct and more general one, Arfken's "monotonically decreasing (for sufficiently large $n$)" (p. 11) covers it, and the unit's own proof uses only $\ge$, so nothing here is wrong; but the remark already records two other places where the unit departs from a printed form, and this one is not among them. Fix: add a bullet to "Where the hypotheses are used, and sharper forms" noting that the condition is taken non-strictly. (The non-strict form also matters for the error bound: $\lvert S - s_m\rvert \le u_{m+1}$ can be an equality, e.g. $u = (1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12, \tfrac14, \tfrac14, \dots)$, which is why the unit's "$\le$" is right where Arfken's "smaller than" would not be.)
2. [minor] remark, bullet "Sharper divergence halves" (line 203): the justification given ("because the condition $u_{n+1}/u_n \le r < 1$ for all large $n$ is exactly $\limsup_n u_{n+1}/u_n < 1$") supports only the $\limsup$ half. The $\liminf$ half is true as asserted ($\liminf_n u_{n+1}/u_n > 1$ forces $u_{n+1}/u_n \ge r > 1$ eventually, hence $u_n \to \infty$), but it is not an equivalence: $\liminf = 1$ does not give $u_{n+1}/u_n \ge 1$ eventually. Fix: justify the two halves in separate clauses, the divergent one as an implication rather than an identification.
3. [minor] method "Deciding whether a series converges", step 2 (line 189): the step uses "absolute convergence settles the question", i.e. the main theorem of found.absolute-convergence, which cannot be added to `prereqs` because that unit lists this one as its prerequisite. The inline `:ref` is the right handling and no change is required; recorded only so the prerequisite audit is on the record. Everything else the unit uses (monotone convergence, the Cauchy criterion for sequences) comes from found.limits-of-sequences, which is listed.
