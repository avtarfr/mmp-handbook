## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Quantum mechanics: probability that escapes" (line 248): weak convergence to zero does **not** license the pointwise-in-region claim as stated. The text says that for normalised $\psi_n\rightharpoonup0$, "physically the wave packet has spread out or run off to infinity, so that every fixed observation region eventually sees nothing", and then reads the gap $\lim\lVert\psi_n\rVert^2-\lVert0\rVert^2=1$ as "precisely the probability that escaped". The claim "every fixed observation region eventually sees nothing" is $\int_B|\psi_n|^2\to0$ for every fixed bounded $B$, i.e. $\lVert\chi_B\psi_n\rVert\to0$. That is *strong* local convergence, and weak convergence does not give it: it would make multiplication by $\chi_B$ a compact operator on $L^2$, which it is not. **Counterexample, and it is inside this very unit.** Part D of the example block (line 215) takes $f_n(t)=\sqrt2\sin(n\pi t)$ on $(0,1)$, notes $\lVert f_n\rVert=1$ and proves $f_n\rightharpoonup0$. Here the observation region $B=(0,1)$ contains *all* of the probability, $\int_0^1|f_n|^2=1$ for every $n$, and nothing escapes anywhere — the whole sequence is supported in one bounded interval for all time. The unit also proves in that same block that $f_n^2\rightharpoonup1$, i.e. the probability density converges weakly to the uniform density on $(0,1)$, not to $0$. The same point on the line: $\psi_n(x)=e^{inx}\varphi(x)$ with $\varphi$ a fixed normalised function supported in $[0,1]$ satisfies $\psi_n\rightharpoonup0$ by Riemann-Lebesgue while $\int_0^1|\psi_n|^2=1$ for every $n$. So weak nullity has a third mechanism besides spreading and translation to infinity — oscillation, momentum running off to infinity — and under it no probability escapes in position space at all; the identification of the norm gap with "the probability that escaped" is wrong in that case. The application block is `original`, so this is our own material, not a source being reported. Fix: state the implication in the direction that is true and say what the gap actually is. Weak nullity means every *fixed* reading $\langle g,\psi_n\rangle$ dies, which is the statement that no fixed finite-resolution instrument sees anything in the limit, not that a region is emptied; the three mechanisms (spreading, translation, oscillation) should be named, with Part D cited as the oscillation case that keeps all its probability in a bounded interval; and the norm gap should be described as the probability that fails to be captured by the weak limit, not as probability that left every region. If a statement about regions is wanted, it is available for the first two mechanisms only, and needs a hypothesis (tightness failing, or a compact embedding) that weak convergence alone does not supply.
