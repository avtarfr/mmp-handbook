## Findings
1. [blocking] application (line 234, "Homogenization, turbulence and effective media"): the homogenization sentence is wrong in two ways, and the direction of the inequality is reversed. The text says "A composite material whose conductivity oscillates on a scale $1/n$ has conductivity field converging weakly to its average, while the energy density, being quadratic, converges weakly to something strictly larger than the energy of the average field. The effective conductivity of the composite is therefore *not* the average of the constituents, and the discrepancy is exactly the semicontinuity gap of part 5." (a) It is the *conductivity* that is said to oscillate, but the energy density $a|E|^2$ is **linear** in $a$ and quadratic only in the field; weak limits do pass through linear expressions, so "being quadratic" names the wrong lever. (b) The conclusion is false in the canonical case. Take the 1D laminate $a_n$ alternating between $1$ and $9$ on intervals of length $1/(2n)$ in $(0,1)$, with $-(a_nu_n')'=0$, $u_n(0)=0$, $u_n(1)=1$. Then $\bar a = 5$, the flux $j_n = a_nu_n'$ is constant and $j_n \to a^{*} = 1/\overline{(1/a)} = 1.8$ (harmonic mean), the weak limit has $u' = 1$, and the energy densities satisfy $\int a_n|u_n'|^2 = j_n^2\,\overline{(1/a)} \to a^{*} = 1.8$, whereas "the energy of the average field" $\bar a|u'|^2 = 5$. So the weak limit of the energy density is $1.8$, strictly **smaller**, not larger; equivalently $a^{*}=1.8 \le \bar a = 5$ (Reuss below, Voigt above), which is the opposite of the ordering the text asserts. The block is `provenance=original`, so this is our own claim, not a source's. Fix: either state the correct mechanism — a *fixed* conductivity with an oscillating field $E_n \rightharpoonup \bar E$ gives $\lim \int a|E_n|^2 \ge \int a|\bar E|^2$, which is the part 5 gap — or, if the conductivity is the oscillating object, say instead that $a^{*}\neq\bar a$ because the weak limit of the product $a_nE_n$ is not the product of the weak limits, and drop both "being quadratic" and "strictly larger". (The Reynolds-stress sentence that follows, $\overline{u^2}-\bar u^2 \ge 0$, is correct and unaffected.)
2. [minor] example, Part D (line 220): "the same Riemann–Lebesgue argument gives $\cos(2n\pi\,\cdot\,)\rightharpoonup 0$". No Riemann–Lebesgue argument has been given — the argument used earlier (part 8 of the proof) is Bessel's inequality, and Riemann–Lebesgue is neither a listed prerequisite nor :ref'd. The claim itself is true and follows from part 8 applied to the orthonormal system $(\sqrt2\cos(2n\pi t))_{n\ge1}$ in $L^2(0,1)$. Fix: say "the same Bessel argument, applied to the orthonormal system $\sqrt2\cos(2n\pi t)$", or add a :ref to the Fourier unit that carries Riemann–Lebesgue.
