## Findings
1. [blocking] remark (line 169): "**Weak Cauchy does not imply weakly convergent** unless the space is reflexive; Kreyszig ... sets this as a problem." Two things are wrong. (i) As written the sentence makes reflexivity *necessary* for weak sequential completeness, and that is false: $\ell^1$ and $L^1(\mu)$ are weakly sequentially complete and are not reflexive. The unit itself raises $\ell^1$ two blocks earlier (statement, closing paragraph, Schur's theorem), so the claim sits badly beside our own text. (ii) It misreports the cited problem. Kreyszig's Problem 10 (p. 263) reads "If $X$ is reflexive, show that $X$ is weakly complete" — a one-directional sufficiency statement only; he nowhere asserts the converse. The following sentence, "this is the same gap as in part 9, seen from the other side", looks like the source of the slip: Eberlein-Shmulian really is an "if and only if" (Stakgold and Holst, Thm 4.8.9, p. 297), and the equivalence has been carried across to weak completeness, where it does not hold. Fix: state the sufficiency only, e.g. "A weak Cauchy sequence need not converge weakly; reflexivity is enough to guarantee that it does, which Kreyszig sets as Problem 10, p. 263. Reflexivity is not necessary — by Schur's theorem $\ell^1$ is weakly sequentially complete without being reflexive." If the necessity direction is wanted at all, it must be dropped, not merely softened.

2. [minor] proof "Parts 1 to 5" (line 115): the general normed-space half of part 5 invokes Kreyszig's Theorem 4.3-3, whose hypothesis is $x_0 \neq 0$ (p. 223: "let $x_0 \neq 0$ be any element of $X$"). The exclusion of $x=0$ is made only inside the preceding Hilbert-space sentence ("if $x = 0$ the claim is trivial"), so in the general argument the norming functional is produced without the hypothesis that licenses it. Fix: move "if $x = 0$ both sides are handled at once, since $\liminf\lVert x_n\rVert \ge 0$" out of the Hilbert paragraph so that it covers both arguments, or add "assume $x \neq 0$" before the appeal to Thm 4.3-3.

3. [minor] remark (line 168): the two Folland locators are off. Exercise 51 (a subspace is norm closed iff weakly closed) is on printed p. 171, not p. 170 — p. 170 carries Exercises 43-49 only — and the frontmatter `pages: "168-170"` for folland-1999 therefore does not cover it. Separately, Exercise 49 is cited for "the unit *sphere* is not [weakly closed]", but 49a states only that every nonempty weakly open set in an infinite-dimensional Banach space is norm unbounded; it does not mention the sphere or its weak closure. The sphere claim is correctly sourced in the same sentence to Reed and Simon (p. 113, Problem 40). Fix: cite Exercise 51 as p. 171 and extend the page range, and either drop the Folland citation for the sphere or label it as the ingredient (49a) rather than the statement.

4. [minor] proof "The criterion ..." (line 150): "every linear functional is continuous" on a finite-dimensional normed space is asserted with a `:ref` to funcan.normed-banach-spaces, but that unit states the equivalence of norms and the linear-combinations lemma, not the continuity of functionals. The bridge is one line ($|f_i(x)| = |a_i| \le \sum_j |a_j| \le c^{-1}\lVert x\rVert$ by the linear-combinations lemma) and, given the no-"clearly" rule, should be written rather than referred to. Fix: add that one line, or phrase the claim as a consequence of the lemma the prerequisite does prove.

5. [minor] definition (line 33): the completeness of $X'$ for a general normed $X$ is attached to a `:ref` to funcan.riesz-representation, which is a Hilbert-space unit and does not prove it (it in turn refers the reader on to funcan.normed-banach-spaces). The proof of part 4 cites the right source for this (Stakgold and Holst, Thm 4.8.4, p. 294). Fix: point the definition at that fact directly, or drop the parenthetical ref and let the proof carry it.

6. [minor] idea (line 83): "Lower semicontinuity is a single application of Cauchy-Schwarz (or, in a Banach space, of the norming functional)". The norming functional needs no completeness, and the remark two blocks later makes a point of saying that completeness of $X$ is nowhere used; "in a Banach space" works against that. Fix: "or, in a general normed space, of the norming functional".

7. [minor] method (line 175): step 3 justifies $\sum_k |c_k|^2 \le \liminf_n \lVert x_n\rVert^2$ by Fatou's lemma for the counting measure. Fatou is not among the prerequisites and the measure chapter is not referenced. Fix: either `:ref` the measure unit, or replace the appeal by the elementary truncation argument (for each $K$, $\sum_{k\le K}|c_k|^2 = \lim_n \sum_{k\le K} |(x_n,e_k)|^2 \le \liminf_n \lVert x_n\rVert^2$ by Bessel, then let $K \to \infty$), which uses only the listed funcan.orthonormal-bases.
