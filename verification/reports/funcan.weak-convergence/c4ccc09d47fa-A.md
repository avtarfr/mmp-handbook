## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Quantum mechanics: probability that escapes" (line 248): weak convergence to zero does **not** license the pointwise-in-region claim. The text says that $\psi_n\rightharpoonup0$ with $\|\psi_n\|=1$ means "the wave packet has spread out or run off to infinity, so that every fixed observation region eventually sees nothing", and then reads the gap $\lim\|\psi_n\|^2-\|0\|^2=1$ as "precisely the probability that escaped". What a fixed observation region $B$ sees is $\int_B|\psi_n|^2$, which is *quadratic* in $\psi_n$; weak convergence controls only the linear readings $\langle \psi_n,f\rangle$, and part 5 of this very unit, together with Part D of the example ("the weak limit of the square is $1$, not $0$"), is the warning that the two are different. Counterexample, inside this unit's own framework: on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ take $\varphi$ continuous, supported in $[0,1]$, $\|\varphi\|=1$, and set $\psi_n(x)=e^{inx}\varphi(x)$. For any $f\in L^2$, $\varphi\bar f\in L^1$ by Cauchy-Schwarz, so $\langle\psi_n,f\rangle=\int e^{inx}\varphi\bar f\to0$ by Riemann-Lebesgue: $\psi_n\rightharpoonup0$ with $\|\psi_n\|=1$. Yet $\int_0^1|\psi_n|^2=1$ for every $n$ — the region $[0,1]$ sees *everything*, always, and no probability has escaped anywhere in position space. What has run off to infinity is the momentum, not the packet. So the stated implication is false, and the reading of the norm gap as "the probability that escaped" is false with it: the gap is $1$ in this example while the escaped position-space probability is $0$. Fix: either (a) weaken the claim to what is true — $\psi_n\rightharpoonup0$ says every fixed *linear* reading, $\langle\psi_n,f\rangle$ for fixed $f$ (an overlap with a fixed state, a fixed interference amplitude), tends to zero, while the total probability stays $1$; or (b) keep the escape picture but name the stronger hypothesis that delivers it, namely $\psi_n\to0$ in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ (equivalently $\int_B|\psi_n|^2\to0$ for every bounded $B$), which is what "every fixed observation region eventually sees nothing" means and which is strictly stronger than weak convergence. Option (b) is also the one scattering theory actually uses, so the following sentence about bound states survives it; under (a) the sentence "the semicontinuity gap ... is precisely the probability that escaped" must go, since in general the gap measures loss of *overlap*, not loss of position-space probability.
