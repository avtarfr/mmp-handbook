## Findings
1. [minor] statement "General order" (line 86) defines $W_k$ as "what $W$ becomes when its $k$-th column $(\varphi_k,\varphi_k',\dots,\varphi_k^{(n-1)})$ is overwritten by $(0,0,\dots,0,1)$". Coddington p. 123 defines it as "the determinant obtained from $W(\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n)$ by replacing the $k$-th column $(\varphi_k,\varphi_k',\dots,\varphi_k^{(n-1)})$ by $(0,0,\dots,0,1)$". The sentence is reworded and the automated overlap check did not flag it, but the parenthesised column lists are identical and the structure is one-to-one. Fix (cheap): define $W_k$ by what it is -- the cofactor-style determinant in which column $k$ is the last standard basis vector -- rather than by the replacement recipe.
2. [minor] proof, Step 6 (line 144): "$y_p'(x_0) = u_1(x_0)y_1'(x_0) + u_2(x_0)y_2'(x_0) = 0$ by Step 1". What makes this zero is $u_1(x_0) = u_2(x_0) = 0$ (both integrals start at $x_0$), not Step 1; Step 1 is what licenses writing $y_p'$ in that form at all. Fix: "$\dots$ by Step 1, and both $u_k(x_0) = 0$".
3. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 150): "the integral formula still makes sense and produces a solution with $n-1$ continuous derivatives" for piecewise-continuous $b$. True, but "a solution" then means a solution in the piecewise/Carathéodory sense (the $n$-th derivative jumps where $b$ does), which the unit's own definition of a solution (every $n$-th derivative existing at every point) does not admit. Fix: say explicitly that this is a solution off the jump points.
