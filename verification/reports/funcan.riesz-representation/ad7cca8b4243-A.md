1. [blocking] method, item 5 (line 123): the reciprocal-basis formula $h_j^{*} = \sum_k (G^{-1})_{jk} h_k$ with $G_{ik} = (h_k, h_i)$ is wrong over $\mathbb{C}$. The unit's own definition (line 37) puts $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ and makes the inner product conjugate-linear in the second slot. Writing $h_j^{*} = \sum_k c_{jk} h_k$, the condition $(h_i, h_j^{*}) = \delta_{ij}$ gives $c = M^{-1}$ with $M_{ik} = (h_i, h_k)$. The stated $G$ is $M^{T} = \overline{M}$, so the formula yields $\overline{M^{-1}}$. I checked this numerically on three random vectors in $\mathbb{C}^4$: with the stated $G$, $(h_i, h_j^{*})$ is far from $\delta_{ij}$ (diagonal entries $\approx 1.23 + 0.07i$); with $G_{ik} = (h_i, h_k)$ it is exactly $\delta_{ij}$. This is our own material. S&H (4.7.4)-(4.7.5) give only the existence argument, not the Gram formula, so the source does not cover it. The real example in Part B is unaffected. Fix: define $G_{ik} = (h_i, h_k)$, which equals $(h_k, h_i)$ in the real case, or restrict the formula explicitly to real $H$.
