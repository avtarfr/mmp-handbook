The comparison is also loose: $\sum(-1)^n$ shows that the *converse* of Abel's theorem fails (a radial limit exists while the series diverges), not that end-point convergence alone fails to identify the sum. 4. [source] hassani-2013, Thm 10.6.2, p. 320: asserts uniform convergence "within its circle of convergence", i.e. on the whole open disc, which the geometric series contradicts (tail at $z=\rho$ is $\rho^N/(1-\rho)$, unbounded as $\rho \to 1^-$). This is a two-source conflict with Ahlfors Thm 2(i), p. 38; the unit states the version it follows, cites it, and notes the difference in a remark, which is exactly what is required, so it passes. Already on file in `verification/discrepancies.md`; no duplicate entry added.
5. [source] hassani-2013, Thm 10.6.3, p. 321: states termwise integration along $\gamma$ with no condition on the path. The unit's proof step 6 supplies piecewise smoothness and says the source does not name such a condition — faithful reporting of an imprecision in the source, not a defect of our text.

## Checked
- Counterexample search: probed the statements at the edges. $R=0$ (coefficients $n!$) and $R=\infty$ (coefficients $1/n!$) — both handled by the conventions in the definition and by the "nothing to prove" branch in the proof. Boundary behaviour: $\sum z^n$ (diverges at every point of $|z|=1$), $\sum z^n/n$ (converges except at $z=1$), $\sum z^n/n^2$ (converges absolutely everywhere on the circle) — all three as the remark claims, so part 3 cannot be strengthened. Uniformity: confirmed that it fails on the full open disc and holds on each $|z|\le\rho$.
