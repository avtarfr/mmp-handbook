## Findings

1. [blocking] frontmatter `prereqs` (line 7): the unit's proofs use two named results of `found.absolute-convergence`, which is not listed as a prerequisite and which the handbook does not treat as free background (its "Bound on the sum" block is marked `provenance=original`, with the explicit note that it is "stated here as the handbook's own").
   - Abel proof, line 207: "$\sum_n s_n z^n$ converges absolutely and $s_N z^N \to 0$. Letting $N \to \infty$ ..." — the passage to the limit needs *absolute convergence implies convergence*, which is the first statement of `found.absolute-convergence`. Nothing in the four listed prereqs supplies it: the M-test as stated in `found.uniform-convergence` is about a family $u_n : E \to \mathbb{C}$, and `found.series-convergence-tests` gives only the Cauchy criterion, vanishing terms, and the five tests.
   - Proof 2, step 3, lines 128-138: the passage from the identity to $\lvert f(w)-f(w_0)-(w-w_0)g(w_0)\rvert \le \tfrac12 K\lvert w-w_0\rvert^2$ is the triangle inequality for an *infinite* series, $\lvert\sum_n c_n\rvert \le \sum_n \lvert c_n\rvert$, i.e. the "Bound on the sum" statement of `found.absolute-convergence`. The same inequality is used again in the Abel proof at line 222.
   Fix: add `found.absolute-convergence` to `prereqs` and cite it at those three places (a `:ref` at line 207 and at line 135 would be enough). This creates no cycle: `found.absolute-convergence` has prereqs `[found.limits-of-sequences, found.series-convergence-tests]`, neither of which reaches this unit, and `complex.elementary-functions` already lists both that unit and this one side by side.

2. [minor] proof 1, Part 1 (line 66): "the limit superior is the least number that the sequence eventually stays below" is not true as a characterisation — there need be no least such number ($x_n \equiv 1$ has $\limsup = 1$ but is never $< 1$). The deduction made from it is nevertheless correct. Fix: "$\limsup$ is the *infimum* of the numbers the sequence eventually stays below, so the sequence eventually stays below any number strictly greater than it", which is immediate from the definition $\inf_k \sup_{n \ge k}$ given in `found.limits-of-sequences`.

3. [minor] proof 1, Part 2 (line 76): "the limit superior is a limit point of the sequence from above" fails when $L = +\infty$, which is exactly the case $R = 0$ that this part must also cover (e.g. $a_n = n^n$). Again the conclusion drawn — $\lvert a_n\rvert^{1/n} > 1/\rho$ for infinitely many $n$ whenever $1/\rho < L$ — is correct for all $L \in [0,+\infty]$. Fix: derive it from $\sup_{n \ge k}\lvert a_n\rvert^{1/n} \ge L > 1/\rho$ for every $k$.

4. [minor] proof 2, step 3 (line 135): "the inner sum is bounded by $\rho^{\,n-2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} j = \tfrac12 n(n-1)\rho^{\,n-2}$" drops the factor $\lvert w - w_0\rvert$ carried by the preceding bound on $\lvert w^j - w_0^{\,j}\rvert$; the displayed conclusion at line 138 restores it and is right. Fix: write the bound as $\tfrac12 n(n-1)\rho^{\,n-2}\lvert w-w_0\rvert$.

5. [minor] Abel statement (lines 175-181) and the equivalence paragraph of its proof (line 227): both tacitly assume $\lvert z\rvert < 1$, and it is needed. The step $1 - \lvert z\rvert \ge \tfrac12(1 - \lvert z\rvert^2)$ is false for $\lvert z\rvert > 1$, and a Stolz sector of opening close to $180^{\circ}$ does contain points with $\lvert z\rvert > 1$ (e.g. $1 + r e^{i\,91^{\circ}}$ for small $r$), which matters when $R > 1$ and $f$ is in fact defined there. Fix: restrict the approach to $\lvert z\rvert < 1$ in the statement and say so at the start of that paragraph.

6. [minor] proof 1, Part 3 (line 84): "the first three series of the remark below" does not point unambiguously at the intended triple, since the remark's first series occur in its first and second bullets; the three meant are those of the third bullet. Also "almost everywhere" is used for "at all but one point", which is not its measure-theoretic sense. Fix: cite the third bullet of the remark explicitly and say "at all but one point".

7. [minor] idea before proof 2 (line 110): "the constant being the value of the twice-derived series at a radius $\rho < R$". The constant $K$ of the proof is the value at $\rho$ of the twice-derived series *of the absolute values*, $\sum n(n-1)\lvert a_n\rvert\rho^{\,n-2}$, not of the twice-derived series itself. Fix: insert "with $\lvert a_n\rvert$ in place of $a_n$".

8. [minor] application, first paragraph (line 302): "for Legendre's equation the recursion gives $R = 1$" holds for the generic, non-terminating solution; in the terminating case — which is the case the very next clause invokes — the series is a polynomial and the radius is infinite. Fix: say "for non-integer degree the recursion gives $R = 1$", or "as long as the series does not terminate".
