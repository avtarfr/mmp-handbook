## Checked
- Counterexample search: probed the one-sidedness, stability and normalisation hypotheses.
  (i) Advanced Green's function: I checked directly that $G_{\mathrm{adv}}=-H(\tau-t)u_\tau(t)$
  really does satisfy $LG=\delta$ — continuity at $\tau$ holds because $u_\tau(\tau)=0$, and the jump
  is $0-(-u_\tau'(\tau))=1/a_2(\tau)$, the right sign — and that
  $G-G_{\mathrm{adv}}=u_\tau(t)[H(t-\tau)+H(\tau-t)]=u_\tau(t)$ solves the homogeneous equation. So
  both the sign and the "they differ by a homogeneous solution" claim survive.
  (ii) Marginal stability: for the undamped oscillator $P(\zeta)=m\zeta^2+k$ gives
  $P(-i\omega)=k-m\omega^2$, whose zeros are $\omega=\pm\sqrt{k/m}$, i.e. exactly on the real axis —
  so the remark's counterexample to Step 4's hypothesis is correct, and the statement block is
  careful to put the poles only in the *closed* lower half-plane while claiming analyticity only in
  the open upper half-plane. The two are consistent; I looked for a contradiction here and found
  none.
  (iii) Asymmetry: if $t>\tau$ then $G(t,\tau)=u_\tau(t)$ while $G(\tau,t)=0$, so "one of them is
  zero whenever $t\neq\tau$" is right, and no choice of $L$ repairs it.
  (iv) Pole map: the claim "the singularities of $\hat G$ are $\omega=i\zeta_j$ with $\zeta_j$ the
  roots of $P$, so $\operatorname{Im}\omega=\operatorname{Re}\zeta_j$" is an identity
  ($P(-i\omega)=0 \iff -i\omega=\zeta_j \iff \omega=i\zeta_j$), and I confirmed it against the
  worked example: $\zeta^2+2\zeta+5$ has roots $-1\pm 2i$, and $i(-1\pm2i)=\mp2-i$, which is the
  $\pm 2-i$ printed in item 6. No counterexample to any statement as written.
- Calculations (sympy + scipy, `.venv/bin/python`; script kept locally as
  `chk_causal.py`):
  - Transform convention: $\int g'(t)e^{i\omega t}dt=-i\omega\hat g$ by parts, so
    $\widehat{g^{(k)}}=(-i\omega)^k\hat g$ and $P(d/dt)\mapsto P(-i\omega)$. For $m=1$, $c=2$, $k=5$,
    $P(-i\omega)=5-\omega^2-2i\omega$, which is the printed $1/(k-m\omega^2-ic\omega)$.
  - Impulse response normalisation: $\frac{d}{ds}\bigl[\frac{1}{m\omega_d}e^{-\gamma s}\sin\omega_d
    s\bigr]_{s=0}=\frac{1}{m}$, and the value at $s=0$ is $0$ — the two conditions the unit requires.
    With $m=1$, $c=2$, $k=5$: $\gamma=1$, $\omega_d=\sqrt{5-1}=2$, so $G=\tfrac12 e^{-s}\sin 2s$.
  - Antiderivative: $\frac{d}{ds}\bigl[-\tfrac15 e^{-s}(\sin 2s+2\cos 2s)\bigr]-e^{-s}\sin 2s$
    simplifies to $0$ — the printed primitive is right.
  - Both pulse-response branches: sympy's $\tfrac12\int_0^t e^{-s}\sin 2s\,ds$ minus the printed
    $\tfrac15-e^{-t}(\sin 2t+2\cos 2t)/10$ is identically $0$, and
    $\tfrac12\int_{t-1}^{t}$ minus the printed $t\ge1$ expression is identically $0$. The two
    branches agree at $t=1$ (both give 0.19716719021092), so $u$ is continuous there.
  - The two checkable numbers: $u(1)=0.19716719021092$ and $u(3)=-0.036104101808807$. The unit's
    $0.19716719$ and $-0.03610410$ are both correct. I also integrated
    $\ddot u+2\dot u+5u=f$ with the same rectangular pulse numerically (`solve_ivp`, rtol $10^{-12}$):
    $u(1)=0.197167190210919$, $u(3)=-0.03610410180840868$ — agreement to the eight decimals the unit
    claims, and $u(3)<0$ confirms the "ringing back through zero" remark.
  - Small-$t$ expansion: sympy gives $t^2/2-t^3/3-t^4/24+O(t^5)$, so the printed
    $\tfrac12t^2-\tfrac13t^3+O(t^4)$ is right, including that damping first enters at third order,
    and $\ddot u(0)=1=f/m$.
  - Resonance: minimising $(k-mX)^2+c^2X$ over $X=\omega^2$ gives $X=k/m-c^2/(2m^2)=k/m-2\gamma^2=3$;
    I also verified the general identity $(k-mX)^2+c^2X=c^2\omega_d^2$ at that $X$, so
    $|\hat G|_{\max}=1/(c\omega_d)$ is not a numerical coincidence. Numerical scan of
    $|5-\omega^2-2i\omega|^{-2}$ in steps of $10^{-3}$ peaks at $\omega=1.732$ with value
    $0.0624999999$, matching the printed peak position $\sqrt3=1.7320508$ and value $0.0625$;
    `numpy.roots` gives the poles as $2-i$ and $-2-i$, as printed. $\hat G(0)=1/k=0.2$ and
    $\int_0^\infty \tfrac12 e^{-t}\sin 2t\,dt=0.2000000000$, so the last claim of item 7 holds.
  - Duhamel/step-response claim of statement (iv): integrating
    $u=\int_a^t(-\partial F/\partial\tau)f\,d\tau$ by parts gives
    $-F(t,t)f(t)+F(t,a)f(a)+\int_a^t Ff'\,d\tau$, and $F(t,t)=0$, which is exactly the printed form.
  - Laplace cross-check in the conventions remark: $\bar G(s)=1/P(s)$ with zero data, and
    $s=-i\omega$ takes it to $1/P(-i\omega)$ — the substitution the remark states.
