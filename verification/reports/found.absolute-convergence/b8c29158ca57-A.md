## Findings
1. [blocking] frontmatter `summary` (line 6): "a convergent series that is not absolutely convergent can be rearranged to any sum whatever" is stated with no restriction on the terms, so it claims more than the unit proves and more than is true. The `statement` block (line 65) restricts Riemann's theorem to series of **real** terms, and the unit's own remark (line 92) gives a complex counterexample: for $a_n = i(-1)^n/(n+1)$ every term is purely imaginary, every convergent rearrangement has a purely imaginary sum, and no real $L \neq 0$ is attainable. `docs/contributing/phase-concepts.md` makes a summary that overstates the statement a failing defect. Fix: "a convergent series of **real** terms that is not absolutely convergent can be rearranged to any sum whatever".
2. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", third bullet (line 92): "Part 1 is stated for real terms only, **as in both sources**" is not what the sources say. Arfken p. 15 states Riemann's theorem for "a conditionally convergent series" with no restriction on the terms, and Riley, Hobson and Bence p. 124 state it immediately after saying "In general an infinite series $\sum u_n$ can have complex terms", again with no restriction. The restriction to $\mathbb{R}$ is the handbook's own correction, not the books'. Fix: say that both sources state the theorem without restricting the terms, that as printed the claim is false for complex series, and that this unit therefore states the real case and defers to Levy-Steinitz for the rest - i.e. flag it as a departure from the sources rather than agreement with them.
3. [source] arfken-2013 §1.1, p. 15 and rhb-2006 §4.3.1, p. 124: both state Riemann's rearrangement theorem for any conditionally convergent series, which is false as printed for complex terms (counterexample as in finding 2). Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`; this is not held against the unit.

## Checked

"Absolute convergence implies convergence" and $|\sum a_n| \le \sum|a_n|$ are correct and supported (Ahlfors's contraction argument, p. 35; Hassani p. 319). The rearrangement statement matches Ahlfors Exercise 3 (p. 37) and Arfken's first bullet on p. 14, and the added claim that the rearrangement is again absolutely convergent is true. Riemann parts 1-3 are each correct for real terms, including part 3 (no limit in $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$). The Cauchy product statement matches RHB §4.4(iv) p. 131 and Arfken p. 14, and the claim that any other summation order of the $a_k b_l$ gives $AB$ follows from $\sum_{k,l}|a_k||b_l| = (\sum|a_k|)(\sum|b_l|) < \infty$. The Mertens bullet correctly reports Ahlfors Exercise 6 (p. 37), including that only one factor need converge absolutely and that the product need not then be absolutely convergent. I verified the conditionally-convergent-square counterexample: for $a_n = (-1)^n/\sqrt{n+1}$ the Cauchy product is $c_n = (-1)^n\sum_{k=0}^{n}[(k+1)(n-k+1)]^{-1/2}$, and the sum is a Riemann sum for $\int_0^1 dt/\sqrt{t(1-t)} = \pi$; numerically it gives 2.293, 2.852, 3.049, 3.112, 3.132, 3.139 at $n = 10^1 \dots 10^6$, so $|c_n| \to \pi$ and the terms do not vanish. The Levy-Steinitz description (translate of a subspace of $\mathbb{C}$: point, line, or all of $\mathbb{C}$) is correct. The terminology bullet is accurate for both sources. `prereqs: [found.series-convergence-tests]` is used and sufficient.
