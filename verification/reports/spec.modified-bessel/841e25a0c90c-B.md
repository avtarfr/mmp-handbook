## Findings

1. [blocking] idea (line 121): the Liouville substitution is inverted. The text says "a solution
   divided by $\sqrt{x}$ obeys $v'' + (1 - \tfrac{4\nu^{2}-1}{4x^{2}})v = 0$". It is a solution
   *multiplied* by $\sqrt{x}$ that obeys the normal form. Checked symbolically: putting
   $y = v/\sqrt{x}$ into $y'' + y'/x + (1-\nu^2/x^2)y = 0$ gives exactly
   $v'' + (1 - \tfrac{4\nu^{2}-1}{4x^{2}})v = 0$, i.e. $v = \sqrt{x}\,y$; putting
   $y = \sqrt{x}\,v$ instead leaves a residual $2v'/x$ term, so $v = y/\sqrt{x}$ does **not**
   satisfy the displayed equation. This also contradicts our own remark at line 263, which states
   the substitution correctly as "Setting $y = v/\sqrt{x}$ removes the first-derivative term", and
   contradicts the cited source (Hassani p. 432, Example 14.4.13, "substituting $v/\sqrt{x}$ for
   $y$"). Internal inconsistency in an `original` block.
   Fix: "a solution multiplied by $\sqrt{x}$ obeys ..." (or "writing $y = v/\sqrt{x}$, the function
   $v$ obeys ...").

2. [minor] remark (line 265): for $\nu < \tfrac12$ the argument establishes at most one zero on
   $x \ge \tfrac12\sqrt{1-4\nu^{2}}$ and, by the Euler comparison, at most one zero on
   $(0,\tfrac12\sqrt{1-4\nu^{2}})$ — which together permit two zeros in $(0,\infty)$, not one. So
   "the conclusion still holds unchanged" overstates what is proved: item 6's conclusion (at most
   one zero in $(0,\infty)$) is not obtained for $\nu < \tfrac12$ by this reasoning.
   Fix: say that each of the two pieces carries at most one zero, and either close the gap (a zero
   of $v$ in the inner segment forces $v$ monotone of one sign thereafter) or state the weaker
   conclusion explicitly. The scope of item 6 itself ($\nu \ge \tfrac12$) is correct and is not
   affected.

3. [minor] method (line 284): "non-zero for $x_1 \neq x_2$ because $I_\nu$ is increasing and
   $K_\nu$ decreasing" needs $\nu \ge 0$. For $\nu < 0$, e.g. $I_{-1/2}(x) = \sqrt{2/\pi x}\cosh x$
   is not monotone on $(0,\infty)$. The determinant is still non-zero (it is a Wronskian-type
   quantity), but the stated reason fails.
   Fix: add "for $\nu \ge 0$", which is the only case the method's separation constants produce.

4. [minor] proof "The Wronskian is exactly $-1/x$" (line 245): "subtracting the two relations of
   each family in item 2 gives $K_\nu' = -K_{\nu+1} + \tfrac{\nu}{x}K_\nu$". The two identities are
   obtained by *adding* the pair after eliminating $K_{\nu-1}$ (resp. $I_{\nu-1}$), not by
   subtracting. The resulting formulas are correct (verified numerically, see below).
   Fix: "combining the two relations of each family".

5. [minor] frontmatter (line 11 `loc`, line 13 `role`): the arfken-2013 role claims "the generating
   function and the integral representations" among what the source supplies, but no block in the
   unit presents a generating function or an integral representation (Exercises 14.5.1, 14.5.6,
   14.5.10 and Eqs. (14.112)-(14.113) are cited but never used). The citation is accurate about the
   source; it is just broader than the unit.
   Fix: trim the unused items from `loc`/`role`.
