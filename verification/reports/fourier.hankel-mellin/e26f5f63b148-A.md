[blocking] proof "The Mellin theorems", Step 2 (line 219): the holomorphy of $F$ on the strip is justified by appealing to found.differentiation-under-integral, whose hypotheses are not met and which the handbook elsewhere says explicitly does not cover this case. That unit's statement "Fixed limits of integration" is for a **bounded** interval $[a,b]$, a **real-valued** integrand $f : U\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$, and continuity of $f$ and of $\partial f/\partial x_i$ **on all of** $U \times [a,b]$; it has no dominating-function hypothesis at all, so producing a dominating function ("That dominating function is independent of $z$, so :ref[]{to=found.differentiation-under-integral} allows differentiation under the integral sign") does not satisfy it. Here the interval is $(0,\infty)$, the integrand is complex-valued, and it is unbounded at $t = 0$ whenever $a > 0$ (the hypothesis only gives $\lvert f(t)\rvert \le Ct^{-a}$). This is exactly the situation spec.gamma-function flags in its own closing remark: "the Leibniz rule for differentiating under the integral sign (found.differentiation-under-integral) does *not* apply to Euler's integral, because it is stated for a bounded interval and a real parameter while here the interval is unbounded and the integrand unbounded at $t=0$; the analyticity is obtained instead by truncating the integral and applying Morera's theorem". Euler's integral is the Mellin transform of $e^{-t}$, as this very unit says at line 71, so the two units now give incompatible accounts of the same step. Separately, even granted differentiation under the integral in the two real directions, the inference to holomorphy needs the Cauchy-Riemann step spelled out (the $\xi$-derivative is $i$ times the $\sigma$-derivative, and both are continuous), which the text does not supply. The conclusion is true; the argument as written is not. Fix: either truncate to $\int_{1/n}^{n}$ and pass to the limit with Morera, as spec.gamma-function does, adding the needed prerequisite, or state and justify a dominated-convergence version of differentiation under the integral on an unbounded interval with a complex parameter and make the Cauchy-Riemann step explicit.
