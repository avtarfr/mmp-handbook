## Findings
1. [minor] remark (line 204): "The proof uses the full strength twice — in step 2 with $E$ arbitrary, and in step 5 with the test set $E \cap B_n$." The non-arbitrary test set $E \cap B_n$ is used in step 4 (testing $A_n$ on $E \cap B_n$); step 5 tests $B_n$ on $E$. Fix: change "step 5" to "step 4".
2. [minor] definition (line 35): $\mu_0$ is defined only on finite disjoint unions of *bounded* h-intervals, $\mu_0(\bigsqcup_{j\le n}(a_j,b_j]) = \sum (b_j-a_j)$, although $\mathcal{A}$ also contains the unbounded h-intervals $(a,\infty)$ admitted two sentences earlier. Folland (p. 33) is equally elliptic, so this is faithful, but the unit uses $\mathcal{A}$-wide statements later. Fix: add "with $\mu_0(I) = \infty$ for any unbounded h-interval $I$".
3. [minor] proof "Lebesgue measure: the five properties", inner regularity (line 166): "$m(E)$ is the limit of the measures of these finite unions" is not literally what the construction gives — the finite unions $H_n = \bigcup_{|j| \le n} K_j$ satisfy $m(H_n) \ge m(E \cap (-n, n+1]) - 3\varepsilon$, and it is $m(E \cap (-n,n+1])$ that rises to $m(E)$ by continuity from below. Fix: state the $3\varepsilon$ explicitly, as the bounded case does, and conclude $\sup_K m(K) \ge m(E) - 3\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon$.

## Checked
I re-derived every step of all four proofs. For Prop. 1.10 I checked $\mu^*(\varnothing)=0$, monotonicity and the $\varepsilon 2^{-j}$ double-series argument. For Caratheodory I checked each of the seven steps, in particular that the four-term identity in step 2 really yields $A \cup B \in \mathcal{M}$, that step 4's induction needs $B_n \cap A_n^c = B_{n-1}$ (disjointness) and $\mu^*(\varnothing)=0$, and that the chain of three inequalities in step 5 closes, giving both $B \in \mathcal{M}$ and countable additivity at $E = B$. For the five properties I verified: $m(\{x\}) = \lim 1/n = 0$ via continuity from above; the $2\varepsilon$ bound in outer regularity; the bounded inner-regularity chain $m(K) = m(\overline E) - m(\overline E \cap U) \ge \dots > m(E) - \varepsilon$ (the subtraction is legitimate because $\overline E$ is bounded); the infinite-measure case of the structure theorem, where $\sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} 2^{-|j|} = 3$ gives the stated $3/k$ and the order of the union-then-intersection is genuinely needed; the passage from the $G_\delta$ to the $F_\sigma$ form by complementation; and the dilation argument, including that it is $|r|^{-1} m_r$, not $m_r$, that extends $\mu_0$, and the $r = 0$ case under $0 \cdot \infty = 0$. For the Vitali argument I checked the equivalence relation, that every $x \in [0,1)$ lies in exactly one $N_r$ (both existence and the uniqueness step $r - s \in \{0,\pm1\} \cap (-1,1)$), and that only translations and the normalisation of $[0,1)$ are used, so the stated version is strictly weaker in hypotheses than Folland's congruence version, as the unit says. I compared everything against Folland pp. 19-20 (the Vitali construction and the $n \ge 3$ finite-additivity remark), pp. 28-32 (Prop. 1.10, Thm 1.11, Prop. 1.13, Thm 1.14 with its four clauses) and pp. 33-39 (h-intervals, Prop. 1.15 with the compactness step, Thm 1.16, Lemma 1.17, Thm 1.18, Thm 1.19 with Exercise 25 left to the reader, Thm 1.21, and the p. 38 nowhere-dense remark); against Reed-Simon pp. 14-15 (the open-cover definition of Lebesgue measure, Thm I.3, Banach-Tarski) and pp. 20-21 (the three Lebesgue-Stieltjes examples and $m(\text{Cantor set}) = 0$); and against Evans pp. 684-685 (Thm 1(i)-(iv)). No locator is wrong and no statement is weaker or stronger than its source. The fat-Cantor example I checked in closed form: the claim $\ell_n = (2^n+1)2^{-(2n+1)}$ satisfies $\ell_0 = 1$ and the recursion $\ell_n = (\ell_{n-1} - 4^{-n})/2$, gives $\ell_1 = 3/8$, $\ell_2 = 5/32$, $\ell_3 = 9/128$, satisfies $\ell_n > 4^{-(n+1)}$ so the deletions fit, and yields $m(K_n) = 2^n \ell_n = 1/2 + 2^{-(n+1)}$ with $m(K_0),\dots,m(K_3) = 1, 3/4, 5/8, 9/16$; the cross-check $\sum_{n\ge1} 2^{n-1}4^{-n} = \sum_{n \ge 1} 2^{-(n+1)} = 1/2$ agrees, so $m(K) = 1/2$ both ways.
