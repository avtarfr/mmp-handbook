## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Lebesgue measure: the five properties", part (d) (line 166): "The case $m(E)=\infty$ follows by doing this on each $E\cap(j,j+1]$ and intersecting" names the wrong operation and hides the one real difficulty. Running the finite-measure argument on $E_j=E\cap(j,j+1]$ gives $E_j=V_j\setminus N_j$ with $V_j$ a $G_\delta$; since $E=\bigcup_j E_j$, the sets $V_j$ must be *unioned*, not intersected ($\bigcap_j V_j$ is not even a superset of $E$ — it is contained in $\overline{(0,1]}$-sized overlaps and is empty for most $E$). And the union cannot simply be taken either: a countable union of $G_\delta$ sets need not be $G_\delta$. Fix: do the $\varepsilon$-step before passing to the limit. For each $k\in\mathbb{N}$ and each $j\in\mathbb{Z}$ use outer regularity to pick an open $U_{j,k}\supseteq E_j$ with $m(U_{j,k}\setminus E_j)<2^{-|j|}/k$; then $W_k=\bigcup_j U_{j,k}$ is open, contains $E$, and $m(W_k\setminus E)\le\sum_j m(U_{j,k}\setminus E_j)\le 3/k$, so $V=\bigcap_k W_k$ is a $G_\delta$ with $E\subseteq V$ and $m(V\setminus E)=0$. Folland leaves this case as Exercise 25, p. 38, so the argument is ours to supply.
