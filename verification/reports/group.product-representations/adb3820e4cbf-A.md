## Findings
1. [minor] statement, (P5) (lines 74-77): the hypotheses do not say that $f_1 f_2 \cdots f_k$ is integrable on $\Omega$, which Steps 3 and 4 of the proof need (the substitution and the interchange of the finite average with the integral). For a non-compact $\Omega$ such as $\mathbb{R}^3$ this is a real assumption, not a convention. Fix: add "with $f_1\cdots f_k$ integrable" to the hypothesis, or say that $J$ is assumed to exist.
2. [minor] definition (lines 29 and 40): $m$ and $n$ change meaning inside one block. In the matrix paragraph $A$ is $m\times n$ and $B$ is $m'\times n'$; in the representation paragraph $m$ and $n$ are the degrees of $T$ and of $S$, so $n$ is now the second factor's dimension rather than the first factor's column count. Nothing downstream is wrong, but the reader has to re-read. Fix: use $p\times q$ and $p'\times q'$ in the matrix paragraph, or $m,n$ and $m',n'$ consistently.
3. [minor] example, part (d) (line 251): "Step 5 of the method asks for the conjugate of $\chi^{\Gamma_f}$" does not match step 5, which is about reducing a two-factor overlap to (P4) and says nothing about conjugating a character. More to the point, steps 1-3 of the method never state that in $J = \int \overline{\Psi}\,\xi\,\phi\,d\tau$ the factor $\overline{\Psi}$ carries the *conjugate* representation of $\Psi$; that is invisible for $D_6$, where every character is real, but it is the standard trap for a group with complex characters (e.g. $C_3$). Fix: add the conjugation to method step 1 or 2, and point the parenthetical at the right step.
4. [minor] method, step 3 (line 181): "if the product row is itself a row of the table other than the trivial one, then $m_1 = 0$ by orthogonality" is true but narrower than the shortcut actually used in the example, where $\chi_{\mathrm{anti}} = (1,1,1,1,-1,-1)$ is recognised as the $A_2$ row directly. Fix: optional, but the useful version is "if the product row is a sum of non-trivial rows", or simply refer to the orthogonality of the trivial row against every other row.
