[source] definition (line 27): "Two such forms $\mu$ and $\mu'$ satisfy $\mu' = h\mu$ for a nowhere vanishing function $h$, which must keep one sign; ... splits the nowhere vanishing $n$-forms into two classes". This is a faithful report of :cite hassani-2013, the paragraph after Def. 28.6.1, p. 898 ("Clearly, $h$ has to be either positive or negative everywhere ... fall into two classes"). Both claims need $M$ connected: on $M = S^1 \sqcup S^1$, $\mu = d\theta$ on both components and $\mu' = d\theta$ on the first, $-d\theta$ on the second gives a nowhere vanishing $h$ of neither one sign, and the classes number $2^2 = 4$. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add geom.stokes-forms hassani-2013 "Sec. 28.6, the paragraph after Def. 28.6.1, p. 898"`. Not a defect of the unit.
2. [source] statement/remark: Arfken's hypothesis that the region be simply connected ((4.106) and its first bullet, p. 245) is weaker than Lee's and Hassani's. The unit handles this correctly under source-authority.md: the remark bullet "The sources differ on simple connectivity, and we follow Lee" states which version is followed, cites all three, and explains that no step of the proof uses the hypothesis. Already logged in `verification/discrepancies.md` against this unit, so no new entry made.
3. [minor] idea (line 76): "the surviving term in $d\omega$ carries a factor $(-1)^{n-1}$ ... together with a further minus sign from the fundamental theorem on $[0,\infty)$. Those two factors are the same sign". Read literally the two factors $(-1)^{n-1}$ and $-1$ are the same sign only for even $n$. What is true, and what the proof shows, is that their **product** $(-1)^n$ is the same sign as the discrepancy between the induced and the standard orientation of $\partial\mathbb{H}^n$, which is also $(-1)^n$. Fix: "Those two factors multiply to $(-1)^n$, which is exactly the orientation sign, and that is why the theorem has no sign in it."
4. [minor] frontmatter, lee-2013 locator: "Prop. 15.21 (a nowhere tangent vector field orients a hypersurface), p. 385". The proposition is numbered and begins on p. 384; p. 385 carries the rest of its statement and its proof. The cited page does contain the material, so nothing is misattributed. Fix: "pp. 384-385".
5. [minor] example (a): "Note that $d\omega$ has no $dy\wedge dz$ or $dz\wedge dx$ part, so the answer does not depend on the height of the cap". The conclusion is right, but the reason given only covers surfaces that are graphs over the same disc; the general reason, which is the one step 5 of the method gives, is that $d\omega$ is closed, so two surfaces with the same oriented rim bound a region and differ by $\int_V d(d\omega) = 0$. Fix: give the closedness reason, keeping the coefficient observation as the quick check.
