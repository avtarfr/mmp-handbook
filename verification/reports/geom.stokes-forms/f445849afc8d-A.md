## Findings
1. [source] arfken-2013, §4.7, first bullet of the hypotheses of Eq. (4.106), p. 245: the theorem is stated only for a simply connected region $R$ ("one with no holes"). The hypothesis is superfluous — Lee Thm 16.11 and Hassani Thm 28.6.3 ask only for an oriented manifold with boundary and a compactly supported form, and nothing in the proof uses it. The unit reports Arfken's wording faithfully, says it follows Lee and Hassani, and flags the difference in its hypotheses remark, so this is a source matter and not a defect. Already recorded in verification/discrepancies.md (entry for geom.stokes-forms / arfken-2013).
2. [source] hassani-2013, §28.6, the paragraph after Def. 28.6.1, p. 898: "h has to be either positive or negative everywhere" and the resulting count of exactly two classes both need $M$ connected; a manifold with $k$ components carries $2^k$ orientations. The unit's definition block states the connectedness hypothesis, gives the $2^c$ count, and attributes the unqualified version to Hassani, so our text is correct. Already recorded in verification/discrepancies.md (entry for geom.stokes-forms / hassani-2013).
3. [minor] proof "The general Stokes theorem", step 1: the displayed chain passes from $\sum_i\psi_i\,d\omega$ to $d\omega$, but the justification given ("because $\sum_i\psi_i$ is constant where $\omega \ne 0$") only disposes of the $d(\sum_i\psi_i)\wedge\omega$ term. The other equality holds because $d$ is local, so $\operatorname{supp} d\omega \subseteq \operatorname{supp}\omega$ and $\sum_i\psi_i\equiv 1$ there. Fix: add that clause; the step is correct as it stands, only under-justified.
4. [minor] example, part (a), closing paragraph: "$S$ together with $S'$ reversed is a closed surface in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$, every closed surface in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ bounds a region". The Jordan–Brouwer argument needs $S\cup(-S')$ to be an embedded surface, which two caps sharing only a rim need not be (they may meet elsewhere). The conclusion is immediate from the theorem itself, $\int_{S'}d\omega = \oint_{\partial S'}\omega = 24\pi$, with no topology at all. Fix: either add "disjoint apart from the rim" or replace the detour by the one-line Stokes argument.
5. [minor] definition block: the standing hypothesis is "$n \ge 1$", but the last paragraph then defines the integral over an oriented $0$-manifold, which case 1 of the second statement uses. Fix: allow $n \ge 0$ in that sentence, or note that it is a separate convention.
6. [minor] definition block and step 1 of the proof call a chart "positively oriented" or "negatively oriented" without requiring its domain to be connected; on a disconnected domain the comparison function may change sign and neither label applies. The cover can always be refined to connected domains. (Lee leaves the same gap on p. 413.) Fix: say "connected chart domains".

## Checked
Re-derived the whole proof. Step 1: confirmed $\omega=\sum_i\psi_i\omega$ and the vanishing of $d(\sum_i\psi_i)\wedge\omega$. Step 2: checked the reduction with $\int_M d\omega=\int_{\varphi(U)}d[(\varphi^{-1})^*\omega]$ by naturality, and checked independently that a negatively oriented boundary chart really does flip both sides, since $\mu\mapsto$ a negative multiple of $dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n$ forces $\iota_N\mu$ to pull back to a negative multiple of the model's induced form. Step 3: re-derived $d\widehat\omega=\sum_i(-1)^{i-1}\partial_i\omega_i\,dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^n$ (the $i-1$ transpositions), the vanishing of the $i<n$ terms, and the $i=n$ term $(-1)^{n-1}\cdot(-1)=(-1)^n$. Step 4: re-derived the boundary sign independently — $(-\partial_n,\partial_1,\dots,\partial_{n-1})$ differs from $(\partial_1,\dots,\partial_n)$ by $(-1)\cdot(-1)^{n-1}=(-1)^n$, so $(x^1,\dots,x^{n-1})$ is positively oriented for $\partial\mathbb{H}^n$ exactly when $n$ is even — and confirmed it matches both step 3 and Lee's Example 15.26. The two $(-1)^n$ factors do cancel, as the idea block claims.

Checked the four classical cases, including every orientation claim, by evaluating the relevant form on an explicit frame: case 2, $(dx\wedge dy)(\nu,t)=\det[\nu\ t]>0$ gives $t$ at $+90^\circ$ from the outward $\nu$ (tested at $(1,0)$ on the unit disc, giving the anticlockwise traversal); case 3, $\beta(\hat{\mathbf n})(\nu,t)=\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot(\nu\times t)$, using the identity $\beta(\mathbf A)(u,v)=\mathbf A\cdot(u\times v)$ that the dictionary (ii) establishes, so "outward first" is the right-hand rule (tested at $(1,0,0)$ on the unit disc with $\hat{\mathbf n}=\hat z$); case 4, $\det[\hat{\mathbf n}\ e_1\ e_2]>0$ gives the outward normal. Case 1's induced orientation ($+1$ at $b$, $-1$ at $a$) also agrees with $\iota_N\mu$ for $\mu=dx$.

Verified the example symbolically with sympy. (a) $d\omega=3(x^2+y^2)\,dx\wedge dy$; $G_x\times G_y=(2x,2y,1)$, so the graph parametrisation is orientation-preserving; $3\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^2 r^3\,dr\,d\theta=24\pi$ and $16\int_0^{2\pi}(\sin^4 t+\cos^4 t)\,dt=24\pi$, matching the boxed value $24\pi\approx 75.3982$. I also checked the rim orientation independently at $(2,0,0)$: with $\nu=(1,0,-4)$ outward within $S$, $\nu\times\hat y=(4,0,1)\parallel\hat{\mathbf n}$, so the anticlockwise parametrisation used is the right one. (b) Confirmed each of the four terms contributes $+\tfrac14\,dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3\wedge dx^4$ (the written $(-1)^{i-1}$ cancelling the transposition sign), so $d\omega=dx^{1234}$; confirmed $\omega=\tfrac14\,\iota_N(dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^4)$ from $\iota_{\partial_i}(dx^{1234})=(-1)^{i-1}dx^{\widehat i}$; and $\int_{-1}^{1}\tfrac{4\pi}{3}(1-t^2)^{3/2}dt=\pi^2/2$, which agrees with $\tfrac14|S^3|=\tfrac14\cdot 2\pi^2$ and with $\pi^{2}/\Gamma(3)\cdot$ the standard ball volume. $\pi^2/2\approx 4.93480$ as printed.

Checked the two counterexamples in the remark and the method: $M=(0,1)$ with $f(x)=x$ gives $\int_M df=1$ against an empty boundary, so compact support is genuinely needed; and in $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$ the hemispheres carrying the *same* oriented equator have normals that are outward on the upper cap and inward on the lower, so $\beta(\mathbf r/|\mathbf r|^3)$ integrates to $2\pi$ and $-2\pi$ — I re-derived the lower cap's induced rim direction from "outward first" to confirm the sign, and the difference $4\pi$ is the solid angle, as it must be.
