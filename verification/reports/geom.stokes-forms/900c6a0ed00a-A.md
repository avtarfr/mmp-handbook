## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "The four classical cases", Case 3 (line 155): "the right-hand side of the theorem is the flux of the curl and ... the left-hand side is the circulation" has the two sides interchanged. The proof block opens by fixing the referent — "Each case is the theorem applied to a particular $\omega$" — so "the theorem" is $\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega$. With $M=S$ and $\omega=\mathbf{A}^{\flat}$, the **left** side is $\int_S d\omega=\int_S\beta(\nabla\times\mathbf{A})$, the flux of the curl, and the **right** side is $\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf{A}^{\flat}$, the circulation. Case 4, two lines below, uses the same phrase with the opposite (correct) convention — "by (iii) the left-hand side is $\iiint_V\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}\,dV$" and "by (ii) the right-hand side is ... the flux" — so the unit contradicts itself as well as the theorem. Fix: in Case 3 swap "right-hand" and "left-hand".
2. [blocking] definition, "Orientation" (line 27): "Two such forms $\mu$ and $\mu'$ satisfy $\mu'=h\mu$ for a nowhere vanishing function $h$, **which must keep one sign**; calling them equivalent when $h>0$ splits the nowhere vanishing $n$-forms into **two classes**." Both italicised claims need $M$ connected. On a manifold with $c$ components $h$ may have a different sign on each, and there are $2^{c}$ classes, not two. This is not an idle case here: the remark on simple connectivity (line 167) relies on an annulus, a solid torus and a spherical shell being admissible, "their boundaries having two components", and $\partial M$ is precisely where this definition is then applied. Fix: add "if $M$ is connected" to the sign claim and the two-classes claim, or define an orientation as an equivalence class of nowhere vanishing $n$-forms under $\mu'\sim h\mu$, $h>0$, which is correct without connectedness.
3. [minor] idea (line 92): "Those two factors are the same sign, which is why the theorem has no sign in it." The two factors are $(-1)^{n-1}$ and $-1$, which are not the same sign; what is true is that their *product* $(-1)^{n}$ is the same as the sign by which the induced orientation of $\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}$ differs from the standard one, which is what makes the two sides agree. As written the sentence has no stated comparand. Fix: "Their product is $(-1)^{n}$, which is exactly the sign relating the induced orientation to the standard one — which is why ...".
4. [minor] method, item 5 (line 176): "Two surfaces with the same oriented boundary give the same integral of any closed form, because their difference bounds." The conclusion needs the difference to bound, which is a hypothesis, not a reason: in $\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$ the upper and lower hemispheres have the same oriented rim, yet the closed solid-angle $2$-form integrates to $+2\pi$ over one and $-2\pi$ over the other, their difference being the non-bounding cycle $S^{2}$. Fix: state it as "when their difference bounds (always the case in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, and more generally when $H_{k}$ of the ambient manifold vanishes)".
5. [minor] proof, case (ii) (line 139): "A $2$-form on a two-dimensional parameter domain is determined by its value on $(\mathbf{r}_u,\mathbf{r}_v)$" — $\mathbf{r}_u$ and $\mathbf{r}_v$ are vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$, not tangent to the parameter domain $D$. The object determined by $\beta(\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{r}_u,\mathbf{r}_v)$ is the pullback $\mathbf{r}^{*}\beta(\mathbf{A})$, evaluated on $(\partial_u,\partial_v)$. Fix: "the pullback $\mathbf{r}^{*}\beta(\mathbf{A})$ is determined by its value on $(\partial_u,\partial_v)$, which is $\beta(\mathbf{A})(\mathbf{r}_u,\mathbf{r}_v)$".
6. [minor] remark, "Compact support is not decoration" (line 161): "to kill the $n-1$ terms transverse to no boundary" is hard to parse; those are the $n-1$ directions *parallel* to $\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}$, along which the variable runs over all of $\mathbb{R}$. Fix: "the $n-1$ directions along the boundary".
7. [source] statement / remark: Arfken requires the region to be simply connected (§4.7, first bullet of the hypotheses of (4.106), p. 245), which Lee (Thm 16.11, p. 411) and Hassani (Thm 28.6.3, p. 899) do not.
