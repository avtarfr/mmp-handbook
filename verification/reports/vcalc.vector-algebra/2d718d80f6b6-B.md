## Findings

[minor] definition "Scalar and vector products" (line 37): "the dot product and the identities below that involve no cross product hold in $\mathbb{R}^n$ for every $n$" is vacuous as written — every identity stated below (the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ identity, BAC–CAB, Lagrange) involves a cross product or $\varepsilon_{ijk}$. Fix: say what is meant, e.g. that the dot product, its bilinearity and $|\mathbf{a}|^2 = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}$ hold in every $\mathbb{R}^n$, while everything below is three-dimensional.
2. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 166): "The scalar and dot-product identities, by contrast, need no restriction on $n$." Read as intended ("scalar product = dot product") this is right, but "scalar ... identities" can be read as the *scalar triple product*, which is 3-D only, so the sentence can be read as a false claim. Fix: write "the dot-product identities".
3. [minor] proof, step 6 (line 110): the displayed chain re-uses $i,j,k$ both as the fixed free indices of the previous steps and as the renamed dummies, and the renaming sentence ("Renaming the dummies to $i,j$ and the free indices to $k,m$") has to be read twice. The algebra is correct; a line naming the intermediate free pair $(j,m)$ before the rename would make it readable.
