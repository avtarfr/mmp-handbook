## Findings
1. [minor] method, step 5 (line 219), and the use made of it in example 1, item 3 (line 249): the interchange being justified there is a $\partial/\partial b$ or $\partial/\partial\alpha$ taken under an integral over the *whole* of $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{R}^n$, which is outside the theorem of found.differentiation-under-integral (that theorem needs a compact $t$-interval; the unit's own remark says so). The sentence names the right hypothesis - a dominating integrable bound valid on a neighbourhood of the parameter value - but the result that turns that hypothesis into the interchange is measure.dominated-convergence, which is not in `prereqs` (it is reachable only through measure.fubini-tonelli). The mathematics is right: $|\partial_\beta^2 e^{-\alpha x^2 + \beta x}| = x^2 e^{-\alpha x^2 + \beta x} \le x^2 e^{-\alpha x^2 + \beta_1 x}$ for $\beta \le \beta_1$, which is integrable. Fix: add measure.dominated-convergence to `prereqs` and point the parenthetical at it alongside found.differentiation-under-integral.
2. [minor] proof[Several dimensions], Step 7 (line 201): "Steps 1 and 3 used only that $A$ is real symmetric" is not accurate as written. Step 1 also concluded $\lambda_i > 0$, $\det A > 0$ and the invertibility of $A$, and Step 3's first substitution is the translation $y = x - A^{-1}b$, which needs $A$ invertible - exactly what may fail here ($\lambda_i = 0$ is one of the cases Step 7 treats). What does carry over to a merely symmetric $A$ is the orthogonal diagonalisation of Step 1 and the rotation $x = Qy$ of Step 3, which is all Step 7 and the statement's display actually use. Fix: "The orthogonal diagonalisation of Step 1 and the rotation of Step 3 used only that $A$ is real symmetric, so for any such $A$ ...".
