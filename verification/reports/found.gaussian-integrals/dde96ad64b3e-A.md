## Findings
1. [blocking] definition[Notation] / statement[The one-dimensional Gaussian integral and its moments], parts 1 and 2, and remark (double-factorial bullet): the gamma function is used as a load-bearing symbol but is nowhere defined, and nothing in `prereqs` supplies it. Part 1 asserts "In gamma-function form the same statement is $\Gamma(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}$", part 2's first equality *is* $\Gamma(m+\tfrac12)/\alpha^{m+1/2}$, and the remark asserts the identity $\Gamma(m+\tfrac12) = (2m-1)!!\sqrt{\pi}/2^{m}$ — three used results about an undefined object. `prereqs` lists only found.change-of-variables; spec.gamma-function is neither listed nor cross-referenced, and $\Gamma$ is not standard undergraduate calculus in the sense the review criteria use. The Notation block is where this belongs and already takes the trouble to define $(2m-1)!!$ and to fix $(-1)!! = 1$, which makes the omission conspicuous. Fix: add one line to the Notation block, $\Gamma(z) = \int_0^\infty t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt$ for $z > 0$ (or Arfken's equivalent Eq. (13.6), $\Gamma(z) = 2\int_0^\infty e^{-t^2}t^{2z-1}dt$, which is on a page this unit already cites), and cite it; or list spec.gamma-function as a prerequisite.

2. [minor] statement[The multidimensional Gaussian integral], closing paragraph: the divergence claim is made about "the integral", i.e. the one in the main display with the linear term $b^{\mathsf T}x$, but the factorisation shown is $\int e^{-\lambda_i y_i^2/2}\,dy_i$, with no linear term — that is the $b = 0$ case. The conclusion is right for every real $b$ (diagonalising turns the factors into $\int e^{-\lambda_i z_i^2/2 + \tilde b_i z_i}\,dz_i$, still $+\infty$ when $\lambda_i \le 0$, and all factors are positive so the product is $+\infty$), but as written the stated reason does not cover the stated claim. Fix: either write the factors with the transformed linear term, or say that it suffices to treat $b = 0$ because $e^{b^{\mathsf T}x}$ is bounded below by a positive constant on any ball and the integrand is positive.

3. [minor] remark, first bullet: "each factor is $\int e^{-y_i^2/(2\lambda_i)}\,dy_i$, which is infinite if $\lambda_i \le 0$". At $\lambda_i = 0$ the factor is not infinite — it is undefined, because $V$ is then singular and the $V^{-1}$ in the exponent does not exist, so the integral never gets written down. The bullet's conclusion ("that step needs $\lambda_i > 0$ for every $i$") is correct. Fix: "which is infinite if $\lambda_i < 0$, while $\lambda_i = 0$ makes $V$ singular so that $V^{-1}$ does not exist at all".

4. [minor] statement[The one-dimensional Gaussian integral and its moments], part 2 and its `src`: the cited Arfken Exercise 13.1.10(b) (printed p. 608) gives $\int_0^\infty x^{2s}e^{-ax^2}dx = \Gamma(s+\tfrac12)/(2a^{s+1/2}) = \bigl((2s-1)!!/(2^{s+1}a^{s})\bigr)\sqrt{\pi/a}$ — over the half-line, exactly half the unit's value. Part 3's half-line formula is quoted from Arfken (a) directly, so the unit silently uses one range for part 2 and the source's range for part 3. Both of the unit's formulas are correct (I checked the doubling), but the reader comparing with p. 608 sees a factor-of-2 discrepancy with no signal. Fix: note in the `src` or in a parenthesis that part 2 is twice Arfken's half-line value, by symmetry of the integrand.
