## Findings
1. [blocking] example "An operator norm that is a supremum and not a maximum" (line 226): the unit says our treatment is Stakgold and Holst's Example 4 "with the interval placed at the other endpoint". It is not: Stakgold and Holst (§5.1, Example 4, p. 303) take $u_\varepsilon$ equal to 1 on $1-\varepsilon < x < 1$, which is the *same* interval our $u_\varepsilon = \varepsilon^{-1/2}\chi_{(1-\varepsilon,1)}$ uses. The only differences from the source are the normalisation of $u_\varepsilon$ and the explicit arithmetic. The statement therefore misreports the cited page. Fix: delete "with the interval placed at the other endpoint and", leaving "…is Stakgold and Holst's example, with the arithmetic carried out explicitly" (or say instead that we normalise $u_\varepsilon$ and evaluate the two bounds numerically).
2. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", last bullet (line 146): the bullet is headed "Submultiplicativity is an inequality, not an identity" and the stated inequality is $\|ST\| \le \|S\|\|T\|$, but the witness given is $TS = 0$. For the exhibited $S = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $T = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ one has $ST = S$, so $\|ST\| = 1 = \|S\|\,\|T\|$ — equality, not strict inequality. Nothing asserted is false (the products are computed correctly), but a reader who forms $ST$ with these matrices gets the opposite of the point being made. Fix: swap the names, or write "while $\|TS\| = 0 < \|T\|\,\|S\|$".

This is a pre-existing ordering warning in another part and cannot be fixed from this unit (the run forbids editing `map.yaml`); recorded only so it is not mistaken for a new defect.

Part 2's "continuity at one point" direction genuinely needs linearity ($\sqrt{|t|}$ and $t^2$ both break it, as the remark says). Part 4's failure in infinite dimensions is witnessed by $d/dt$ on $\{t^n\}$ in $C[0,1]$ ($\|x_n\|=1$, $\|x_n'\|=n$) and by multiplication by $1/x$ on its dense domain in $L^2(0,1)$ — for the latter, the unit's family $u_\varepsilon = (1-\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\chi_{(\varepsilon,1)}$ does work: $\|u_\varepsilon/x\|^2 = (1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\int_\varepsilon^1 x^{-2}dx = 1/\varepsilon \to \infty$. The non-closed-range witness $(Tu)_j = u_j/j$ on $\ell^2$ checks out: the range is dense (it contains every finitely supported sequence) and omits $(1/j)_j \in \ell^2$. Part 7's uniqueness really does need density. No counterexample found to any stated part.
