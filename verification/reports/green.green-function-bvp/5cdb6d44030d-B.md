## Findings
1. [minor] application (line 457): "piecewise cubic, with three derivatives continuous and a jump in
   the third" is at best ambiguous and at worst wrong. By the $n$th-order rule quoted in this unit's
   own first statement block (order $n-2$ and below continuous, order $n-1$ jumps), a fourth-order
   beam Green's function has $G$, $G'$ and $G''$ continuous — *two* derivatives — and a jump in
   $G'''$. Read literally, "three derivatives continuous" contradicts "a jump in the third" in the
   same clause. Fix: "with $G$, $G'$ and $G''$ continuous and a jump in the third derivative".
2. [minor] idea (line 173), labelled `original`: the bookkeeping "four free constants / four
   conditions" is also exactly how Hassani organises the proof of his Theorem 20.3.5 (p. 621: "To
   determine $G(x,y)$ we must determine four unknowns. We also have four relations"). The label is
   defensible, since the count is read straight off this unit's own construction proof and the
   wording owes nothing to Hassani, but a `src=` pointing at p. 621 would be more honest. No
   rewording is needed.
3. [minor] definition (line 61): "Conditions 1, 3 and 4 are Stakgold and Holst's (3.2.7)" — their
   (3.2.7) also contains the two boundary conditions, i.e. condition 2 as well. Fix: "Conditions 1–4
   are Stakgold and Holst's (3.2.7)", or "Conditions 1, 3 and 4 are the local part of (3.2.7)".

4. [minor] application (line 461): ":ref[the next unit]{to=green.eigenfunction-expansion-green}".

## Checked
- Counterexample search: tested the statements against the degenerate and edge cases they invite.
  (i) The jump statement's hypothesis is genuinely needed: $G \equiv 0$ satisfies conditions 1–3 and
  the one-sided-limit hypothesis, so the delta equation cannot be dropped — the unit says this
  itself. (ii) $a_2$ vanishing: $L=((1-x^2)u')'$ on $[-1,1]$ with "finite at $\pm 1$" does give
  $u_1=u_2=1$ (the second solution is $\operatorname{artanh} x$, unbounded at both ends), so the
  construction really fails there, as claimed. (iii) The homogeneous-problem hypothesis: for
  $u''+\pi^2u=f$ on $(0,1)$, $u(0)=u(1)=0$, the solvability condition is
  $\int_0^1 f\sin\pi x\,dx=0$; $f\equiv 1$ gives $2/\pi \neq 0$ (no solution) and $f=x-\tfrac12$
  gives $0$ by antisymmetry about $x=\tfrac12$ (infinitely many) — both as stated. (iv) Symmetry
  without self-adjointness: the unit's own second example is a counterexample to symmetry, and I
  confirmed independently that the weaker relation survives there. (v) Periodic conditions: I
  verified by hand that $[a_2(vu'-uv')]_a^b=0$ under $u(a)=u(b)$, $a_2(a)u'(a)=a_2(b)u'(b)$ for both
  $u$ and $v$, so the periodic claim in the reciprocity block is correct. (vi) Unmixedness: Step 5 of
  the representation proof does fail for mixed $B_1$, and the unit says so. No counterexample found
  to any statement as written.
