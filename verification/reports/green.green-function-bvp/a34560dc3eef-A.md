## Findings

1. [blocking] statement "Continuity and the jump condition" (line 73): the hypothesis as spelled out does not contain the delta equation, so the statement is false as written. "Let $G(\cdot,\xi)$ satisfy $LG=\delta(\cdot-\xi)$ **in the sense that** it is twice continuously differentiable on each of $[a,\xi)$ and $(\xi,b]$, solves $LG=0$ there, and has one-sided limits …" defines the hypothesis to be exactly piecewise regularity plus $LG=0$ off $\xi$; $G\equiv 0$ satisfies all of it and has neither the asserted continuity (vacuously true) nor the asserted jump $1/a_2(\xi)\neq 0$. The proof does use the delta: Step 1 divides $LG=\delta$ by $a_2$ and Step 3 integrates a unit mass. Fix: make the delta equation a hypothesis in its own right, e.g. "Let $G(\cdot,\xi)$ satisfy $LG=\delta(\cdot-\xi)$ as a distribution on $(a,b)$, and be twice continuously differentiable on each of $[a,\xi)$ and $(\xi,b]$ with one-sided limits of $G$ and $\partial G/\partial x$ at $\xi$" — "in the sense that" must become "and".

2. [blocking] method "Building and using a Green's function", step 8 (line 361): the two boundary data are attached to the wrong solutions. The unit gives $\dfrac{\gamma_1}{B_2u_1}u_1(x) + \dfrac{\gamma_2}{B_1u_2}u_2(x)$ and attributes it to Stakgold and Holst's (3.2.16); the book's (3.2.16) (p. 196, checked on the page image, where the OCR loses the $\gamma$ subscripts) reads $\dfrac{\gamma_2}{B_2u_1}u_1(x) + \dfrac{\gamma_1}{B_1u_2}u_2(x)$. The book is right: with $B_1u_1=0$ and $B_2u_2=0$, writing $u=Au_1+Bu_2$ gives $B_1u = B\,B_1u_2 = \gamma_1$ and $B_2u = A\,B_2u_1 = \gamma_2$, so $A=\gamma_2/B_2u_1$ and $B=\gamma_1/B_1u_2$. As printed the recipe produces a function with $B_1u=\gamma_2$, $B_2u=\gamma_1$. Fix: swap $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ in that display.

3. [blocking] remark "Conventions that differ between books" (line 342): "in general Hassani's $G$ is our $G$ divided by nothing at all" is wrong, and contradicts the sentence immediately before it. With the same operator $L=a_2\partial_x^2+a_1\partial_x+a_0$, our $G$ solves $LG=\delta(x-\xi)$ and Hassani's $G_H$ solves $LG_H=\delta(x-y)/w(x)=\delta(x-y)/w(y)$, so $G_H(x,y)=G(x,y)/w(y)$ — exactly the factor that turns our jump $1/a_2$ into his $1/(p_2(y)w(y))$ quoted two lines earlier, and exactly the factor the compensating $w$ in his representation $u(x)=\int w(y)G_H(x,y)f(y)\,dy$ undoes. The remark is `original`, so this is our own claim. Fix: say that Hassani's $G$ is our $G$ divided by $w$ in the source variable, which is why the two give the same $u$; the rest of the paragraph (including "mixing the two … would be wrong by a factor $w$") is then correct.

4. [minor] definition (line 67): "The two forms are equivalent: the next statement derives 3 and 4 from the delta equation." Only one direction is derived anywhere in the unit; the converse (that 1, 3, 4 imply $LG=\delta$ distributionally) is never shown. Fix: either add the one-line converse, or write "the next statement derives 3 and 4 from the delta equation; the converse is a short computation with the jump".

5. [minor] proof "Continuity and the jump condition", Step 2 (line 187): the parenthetical justification is weaker than the claim it supports. After "no other term contains a $\delta'$", the equation reads $J\delta' + c\,\delta + h = a_2(\xi)^{-1}\delta$ with $h$ locally bounded, and testing against a *fixed* $\varphi$ with $\varphi(\xi)=0,\varphi'(\xi)=1$ leaves the unknown $\int h\varphi$; what is needed is that $\delta'$ is independent of $\delta$ **and of bounded functions** (take $\varphi_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon\psi((x-\xi)/\varepsilon)$ with $\psi(0)=0,\psi'(0)=1$, so $\int h\varphi_\varepsilon\to0$). The conclusion and the sentence "no other term contains a $\delta'$" are correct. Fix: state the independence as being from $\delta$ and from bounded functions, or use the scaled test function.
