[blocking] :::proof (the source function), Step 4 (line 199): the proof concludes that (4) is a solution of (1) from "it solves (1) strictly inside the cone" (Step 3) plus "outside the cone $S\equiv0$, which solves (1) trivially". That does not follow. $S$ is *discontinuous* across $\lvert x\rvert = ct$ — the proof says so in the same sentence, the jump being $1/2c$ — and a jump across a surface in general leaves $\delta$ and $\delta'$ terms in $\Box S$, so a function that solves the equation separately on two open sets need not solve it on their union. The statement being proved is that $S$ is the source function of the Cauchy problem on the whole line, so the equation has to hold across the cone, not only on either side of it. The terms do cancel, but for two specific reasons that the proof never gives. Fix: write $S = H(\phi)g$ with $\phi = t - \lvert x\rvert$ and $g = \frac{1}{2c}J_0(\mu\sqrt{t^2-x^2/c^2})$, which is smooth because $J_0$ is even and entire, so $g$ is an entire function of $t^2-x^2/c^2$; then (with $c=1$, away from $x=0$) $\Box S = \delta'(\phi)(\phi_t^2-\phi_x^2)g + \delta(\phi)(\phi_{tt}-\phi_{xx})g + 2\delta(\phi)(\phi_tg_t - \phi_xg_x) + H(\phi)\Box g$. The $\delta'$ term vanishes because $\phi_t^2-\phi_x^2 = 0$, i.e. because the cone is characteristic — which is the precise content of "the mass term moves no signal faster"; and the $\delta$ term vanishes because, writing $g = G(t^2-x^2)$, $\phi_tg_t - \phi_xg_x = g_t + \operatorname{sgn}(x)g_x = 2(t-\lvert x\rvert)G'(t^2-x^2) = 2\phi G'$, which $\delta(\phi)$ annihilates. What is left is $H(\phi)\Box g$, and Step 3 has already shown $\Box g = -\mu^2g$.
