6. [source] strauss-2008, p. 374, the displayed square-root identity after Eq. (3). Strauss
   displays it as the *square* $(\mathcal D + im)^2 = c^{-2}\partial_t^2 - \Delta + m^2$. Read as
   a square it is false: with $(\gamma^0)^2 = I$, $(\gamma^j)^2 = -I$ and his anticommutation (6)
   one gets $\mathcal D^2 = c^{-2}\partial_t^2-\Delta$, so $(\mathcal D+im)^2 = \mathcal D^2 +

   for the product $(\mathcal D+im)(\mathcal D-im) = \mathcal D^2+m^2$, which is what the unit
   displays while recording what Strauss prints. Already in `verification/discrepancies.md`
   (`pde.klein-gordon-equation | strauss-2008`); not re-added.

## Checked

- **Counterexample search.**
  - Dispersion relation (3): the substitution is an identity, so the only way to break it is a
    degenerate $\mathbf k$ or $A$. $A\neq0$ is hypothesised; $\mathbf k = \mathbf 0$ breaks only
    part 1's velocity formulas (finding 1), not (3) itself. Part 3's gap claim is right: $\omega^2
    \ge \mu^2$ with equality only at $\mathbf k = \mathbf 0$.
  - Source function (4) outside the cone, on the cone and at $t\downarrow0$: the case split is
    Strauss's, $S$ is left undefined on $\lvert x\rvert = ct$ (measure zero) and for $t<0$, and
    the proof is run on $\{t>0\}$ only, so nothing is claimed that the formula does not deliver.
  - Step 4's distributional argument: tested the two cancellations it rests on. $\phi_t^2-\phi_x^2
    = 1-1 = 0$ (the cone is characteristic) kills $\delta'$, and $\phi_tg_t-\phi_xg_x =
    2(t-\lvert x\rvert)G'(t^2-x^2) = 2\phi G'$, so $\delta(\phi)\phi = 0$ kills the $\delta$ term.
    Both are correct as written. The claim that $x = 0$ needs no separate treatment holds because
    $\phi>0$ there for $t>0$.
  - $\mu$ imaginary (the telegraph remark): checked that $\omega^2 = c^2k^2-\tfrac14$ has real
    roots for $ck>\tfrac12$ and purely imaginary roots for $ck<\tfrac12$, never genuinely complex;
    that $\lambda = \sqrt{\tfrac14-c^2k^2}\le\tfrac12$ with equality only at $k=0$, so
    $e^{-t/2}e^{\lambda t}$ never grows; and that the $k=0$ telegraph equation $u_{tt}+u_t=0$ has
    solutions $a+be^{-t}$, so the constant survives. The remark's distinction between the
    tachyonic instability of (1) at imaginary $\mu$ and the damping of the telegraph equation is
    sound.
  - Conserved density (8): $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ look as if they depend on $M$ and $\hbar$
    separately, which (1) does not contain; they do not, since $i\hbar/2Mc^2 = i/2\mu$ and
    $\hbar/M = c^2/\mu$. Well defined.
  - Superposition of the two $\omega$ branches: both roots give solutions and $\rho$ carries the
    sign of $\omega$, so $\rho$ genuinely takes both signs. No sign convention rescues it.
