2. [minor] note[The same theorem in the other sources' hypotheses] (line 216): "A fourth route to
   the same conclusion is to observe that the Picard map …". The three preceding bullets are three
   alternative sets of *hypotheses* for the same theorem, not three routes to it; counting the
   contraction-mapping argument as the fourth route therefore does not parse against what precedes
   it. (The contraction argument itself is correct: $\lvert T\varphi - T\psi\rvert(x) \le
   K\lvert x-x_0\rvert \sup\lvert\varphi-\psi\rvert \le K\alpha\sup\lvert\varphi-\psi\rvert$.) Fix:
   "A different route" or "A second proof".

3. [minor] remark (line 199): "**The bound $M$ and the number $b/M$** are used only in step 1".
   $M$ is invoked as a hypothesis only there, but it propagates into the constants $M_p$ of step 3,
   the tail estimate of step 6 and the published error bound of part 3, so "used only in step 1"
   reads more strongly than intended. Fix: "are invoked only in step 1, to keep the graphs inside
   $R$ (they then ride along in the constants of steps 3 and 6)".

4. [minor] example[Without the Lipschitz condition] (line 236): "For any $\tau \ge 0$ define
   $y_\tau$". Both cited sources restrict the parameter to be positive — Coddington takes "$k$ any
   positive number" (p. 190) and Hirsch, Smale and Devaney "for any $\tau > 0$" (p. 143). The $\tau =
   0$ member is a genuine extra solution (zero for $x \le 0$, $x^3$ for $x > 0$, distinct from $x^3$

   `adapted` block. Fix: say "$\tau > 0$, as both sources do, and $\tau = 0$ as well", or restrict to
   $\tau > 0$.

5. [minor] statement (line 80): "$\lvert f(x,y)\rvert \le M$ for all $(x,y) \in R$" is stated without

6. [source] coddington-1989 §5.6, Theorem 7, p. 210: the restatement of the bound hypothesis omits

**Counterexample search.** I attacked the statement as written.

- Part 1's interval: $\alpha = \min\{a, b/M\}$ is forced, not merely convenient — the
  remark's $y'=1+y^2$ computation shows $\alpha \le \max_{b>0} b/(1+b^2) = \tfrac12$ (confirmed
  symbolically: `sp.maximum` returns $1/2$), against a true half-interval $\pi/2$, so no choice of
  rectangle recovers the true interval. Degenerate case $M=0$: finding 5.
- Part 2's uniqueness: tried to break it by taking $I'$ larger than $I$ and by taking solutions whose
  graphs leave $R$. The hypothesis "with graphs contained in $R$" is exactly what blocks the second
  attempt and is Coddington's Theorem 10 hypothesis; the unit carries it, and Corollary 1 on p. 223
  does not drop it. The reflection trick in step 5 is correct: $\tilde\varphi(x) = \varphi(2x_0-x)$
  satisfies $\tilde\varphi' = -f(2x_0-x,\tilde\varphi)$, the reflected right-hand side has the same
  Lipschitz constant, and $\tilde\varphi(x_0) = y_0$, so the $x \ge x_0$ argument transfers.
- Dropping the Lipschitz condition: the unit's own $y'=3y^{2/3}$ example is the counterexample and it
  is correct (below). Dropping continuity: Hirsch–Smale–Devaney's step vector field, correctly
  reported.
- Part 3's error bound: tested numerically against the unit's own example at the worst point
  $x = \alpha$ (table below). Every error is strictly below its bound; the bound is never violated
  and never attained.
- Step 2's induction: checked the algebra independently —
  $K\int_{x_0}^{x} MK^{p-1}\lvert t-x_0\rvert^{p}/p!\,dt = MK^{p}\lvert x-x_0\rvert^{p+1}/(p+1)!$,
  and the base case $p=1$ really is step 1 with $k=0$. Step 3's $M_p = (M/K)(K\alpha)^p/p!$ is the
  same constant as step 2's bound at $\lvert x-x_0\rvert = \alpha$, and
  $\sum_{p\ge1}M_p = (M/K)(e^{K\alpha}-1)$. Step 6's $(k+1+q)! \ge (k+1)!\,q!$ is correct and gives
  the stated $e^{K\alpha}$ factor.
