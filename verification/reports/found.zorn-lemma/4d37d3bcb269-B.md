## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used" (line 140): "Kreyszig, Folland and Dummit and Foote all state nonemptiness separately" is not accurate for Folland. Kreyszig's 4.1-6 (p. 211) reads "Let $M \neq \emptyset$ be a partially ordered set" and Dummit and Foote's Zorn's Lemma (p. 909) reads "If $A$ is a nonempty partially ordered set"; Folland's 0.2 (p. 5) has no nonemptiness clause at all, and gets it only from his definition of a partial ordering, which is declared on a nonempty set (p. 4). The mathematical point is unaffected -- all three do require nonemptiness -- but the sentence describes where they put it, and for Folland it is a standing convention rather than a separate hypothesis. Fix: say that Kreyszig and Dummit and Foote state it in the lemma while Folland builds it into the definition of a partial ordering.
2. [minor] definition "Partial orders, chains, bounds and maximal elements" (line 32, bullets at lines 51-54): the block's Kreyszig locator is "§4.1, Def. 4.1-1, p. 210", which covers the three order axioms, chain, upper bound and maximal element, but the four examples that follow are Kreyszig's Examples 4.1-2 to 4.1-5 (4.1-2 on p. 210; 4.1-3, 4.1-4, 4.1-5 on p. 211) -- the same four orders, reordered, with 4.1-3's "the only maximal element is $X$" carried over. The frontmatter source entry does list them; the block-level `src` does not. Fix: extend the block locator to "§4.1, Def. 4.1-1 and Examples 4.1-2 to 4.1-5, pp. 210-211".
3. [minor] note "A stronger-looking phrasing" (line 150): "a chain $C$ extends to a maximal chain $C'$" is a step beyond the Hausdorff maximal principle as item 4 states it (line 88), which only asserts that *some* maximal chain exists, not that a given chain sits inside one. It is true, but by a further application of the principle to the chains of $M$ containing $C$ (a maximal element there is maximal among all chains, since any chain properly containing it also contains $C$). Fix: add that half-line, or state item 4 in the "every chain extends to a maximal chain" form.
4. [minor] method (line 159): "it succeeds precisely when $P$ can be tested on finitely many elements at a time" overstates one direction. Finite character is sufficient, and the sentence that follows proves exactly that; it is not necessary. Take $M$ = the infinite subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ under inclusion: the union of any nonempty chain is infinite, so step 4 succeeds, yet "is infinite" is not testable on finitely many elements. Fix: "it succeeds whenever".
5. [minor] example (line 169): the recalled definition, "a subset $J \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ closed under subtraction and under multiplication by arbitrary integers", is satisfied vacuously by $\emptyset$. Harmless in the example itself, where $I_0 \subseteq J$ forces $J$ nonempty, but the recalled definition should say nonempty (or "a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z},+)$ closed under ...").
6. [minor] proof (line 100 against line 88): the preamble fixes "$X$ is a nonempty set", while Step 1 proves the Hausdorff maximal principle, which item 4 asserts for *any* poset. Step 1's argument never uses nonemptiness ($\mathcal{L} \ni \emptyset$ is nonempty whatever $X$ is), so the restriction is an unnecessary weakening of what is proved. Fix: scope the nonemptiness assumption to Steps 2 and 3.
