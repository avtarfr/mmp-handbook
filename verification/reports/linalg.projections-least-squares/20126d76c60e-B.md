## Findings
1. [source] remark, second bullet (line 175): the unit reports Stakgold & Holst's Remark 2 (§4.5, p. 267) faithfully, but that remark's example does not work. With $M=\{f\in C[0,1]:f(0)=0\}$ and $u\equiv1$ in the sup norm, every $f\in M$ has $|1-f(0)|=1$, so $\lVert u-f\rVert_\infty\ge1$ for all $f\in M$, while $f=0$ lies in $M$ and attains $\lVert u-f\rVert_\infty=1$. The distance is $1$ and it *is* attained (by $f=0$ and by every $f\in M$ with $0\le f\le2$). What the example exhibits is failure of *uniqueness* of the nearest point in a Banach space, not failure of existence. Per `docs/contributing/source-authority.md` this does not fail the unit; recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add linalg.projections-least-squares stakgold-holst-2011 "§4.5, Remarks on the Proof, Remark 2, p. 267" ...`. If the bullet is ever reworded, "has no closest element" should become "has no *unique* closest element", and the heading "makes the nearest point exist and be unique" is the honest reading of what the parallelogram law buys.
2. [minor] statement part 5 (line 55): the converse is stated with the hypothesis $\dim_{\mathbb F}V<\infty$, but the proof of the converse (lines 92-98) never uses it: $x=Px+(x-Px)$, the computation $(x-Px,Pv)=0$, and $N\cap N^{\perp}=\{0\}$ hold in any inner product space. The style guide asks that a hypothesis be named where it is used. Fix: drop it, or say what it is guarding against.
3. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 174): the bullet is headed "Finite dimension of $M$ is doing real work" and then explains that work as closedness ("which is what makes the minimum in part 2 attained"). In *our* proof finite dimension is used to produce an orthonormal basis of $M$ (line 63), and part 2 then follows from the orthogonal splitting, with no limit and no closedness argument anywhere; closedness is what Stakgold & Holst's minimising-sequence route needs. Fix: say that finite dimension buys the basis, and that it also implies closedness, which is the form the hypothesis takes in the books.
4. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 176): "Kreyszig's Theorem 3.3-1 assumes only that $M$ is convex and complete" — Kreyszig (p. 144) also assumes $M\neq\emptyset$, which is a real hypothesis here since the infimum over an empty set does not exist. Fix: add "non-empty".
