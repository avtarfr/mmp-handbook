## Findings
1. [source] remark[Where the hypotheses are used, and conventions], second bullet (line 175): the Banach-space counterexample — "in $C[0,1]$ with the supremum norm, let $M$ be the continuous functions vanishing at $0$; the function $u\equiv 1$ has no closest element in $M$" — reports Stakgold and Holst's Remark 2 (p. 267) faithfully, word for word in substance. But the infimum *is* attained: every $f\in M$ has $\sup_x\lvert 1-f(x)\rvert\ge\lvert 1-f(0)\rvert = 1$, and $f=0\in M$ gives exactly $1$, so $d(u,M)=1$ and $f=0$ is a closest element. What the example really exhibits is failure of *uniqueness* ($f(x)=x$ attains $1$ as well), which is the other half of what the parallelogram law buys. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add` against `stakgold-holst-2011 "§4.5, Remarks on the Proof, Remark 2, p. 267"`. Per `docs/contributing/source-authority.md` this does not fail the unit; the unit's own framing of the bullet ("makes the nearest point exist **and be unique**") is wide enough to cover the correct reading.
2. [minor] statement[Projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace] part 5 (line 55) and proof (lines 92–98): the converse's hypothesis $\dim_{\mathbb F}V<\infty$ is never used. The argument given — $x = Px+(x-Px)$, $(x-Px,Pv) = (P(x-Px),v) = 0$ by self-adjointness and $P^2=P$, uniqueness from $N\cap N^{\perp}=\{0\}$ — is purely algebraic and proves the converse in any inner product space. The restriction is presumably inherited from Kreyszig Thm 9.5-1 (p. 480), which assumes $P$ *bounded* on a Hilbert space and which finite-dimensionality makes automatic. This is not an error, but as it stands the unit states less than it proves and gives no reason for the hypothesis. Fix: either drop it, or keep it and add a sentence (here or in the remark) saying that it stands in for Kreyszig's boundedness assumption.
3. [minor] remark, fourth bullet (line 177): the conditioning claim ("forming $A^{*}A$ squares the condition number of $A$, so part 4 ... loses about twice as many digits as part 5") is correct and standard, but is outside the block's `src=` pages — Stakgold and Holst §4.5 pp. 266–268 and Kreyszig §3.3 pp. 144–145 contain no floating-point analysis. The same applies to the fifth bullet. Fix: cite a numerical-linear-algebra source for these two bullets, or mark them original.
4. [minor] application, third paragraph (line 255): "the variational bound it [Rayleigh-Ritz] gives is the same inequality $\lVert Px\rVert\le\lVert x\rVert$ seen from the other side" misidentifies the bound. The Rayleigh-Ritz bound is $\min_{u\in M}R(u)\ge\min_{u\in V}R(u)$ for the Rayleigh quotient $R$ — minimising over a subspace can only raise the minimum — which is a different fact from the norm contraction of part 3. Fix: say that restricting the minimisation of the Rayleigh quotient to the trial subspace can only raise it, which is why the Ritz value is an upper bound for the ground-state energy; the projection content is that the Ritz vector is the best approximation in $M$.
