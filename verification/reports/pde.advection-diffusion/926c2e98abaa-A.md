## Findings
1. [minor] proof, Step 3 (line 104): Tonelli's theorem is named and used ("$\int S_D(\cdot,t)=1$ gives conservation of mass by Tonelli's theorem"), but `measure.fubini-tonelli` is neither in `prereqs` nor cross-referenced, whereas the project's practice elsewhere is to do both (fourier.convolution-theorem, found.gaussian-integrals, spec.hermite-polynomials all list it and `:ref` it at the point of use). Not blocking: the result is in the dependency closure already, via pde.heat-kernel -> fourier.convolution-theorem -> measure.fubini-tonelli, and the step itself is correct and correctly conditioned ("the integrand being non-negative"). Fix: add `:ref[]{to=measure.fubini-tonelli}` at the invocation, and optionally the direct prereq.
2. [minor] proof, Step 3 (line 104): "Convolution of two densities with finite second moments adds their means and adds their variances, and a translation adds to the mean only" is asserted bare, and it is the entire content of part 1 of the statement — the one step that is not re-derived in the unit. It is a standard fact (and pde.heat-kernel line 212 uses the Gaussian special case the same way), so not blocking, but part 1 is `original` material and the reader is given nothing to check it against. Fix: either a one-line derivation (both moment identities follow from the same Tonelli interchange already invoked two sentences earlier: $\int x\,(p*q)(x)dx=\int x p+\int x q$ and likewise for the second central moment) or a reference to the probability unit that will carry it (`prob.joint-distributions` / `prob.expectation-variance`).
3. [minor] definition "The Peclet number" (line 50): "diffusion spreads it across that length in the time $L^2/D$" is stated as an exact time, but the unit's own variance law $\mathrm{var}=2Dt$ (statement part 1, line 80; example item 3, line 195) gives $L^2/(2D)$ for the time to spread a distance $L$. The factor 2 is dropped silently here and then kept scrupulously everywhere else, so the "ratio of these times" reading of $\mathrm{Pe}=VL/D$ is off by exactly that factor. The conventional definition $\mathrm{Pe}=VL/D$ is of course right; it is the derivation that over-claims. Fix: say the diffusive time is $L^2/D$ *up to a constant of order one*, or define $\mathrm{Pe}$ by the scaling argument that follows (which is exact) and present the time-ratio only as the interpretation.
4. [minor] statement "Solution in a moving frame", part 1 (line 80): $\bar x(0)$ is used without definition. The statement is asserted only for $t>0$ and $u(\cdot,0)$ is never introduced, so $\bar x(0)$ has to be read as the mean of the probability density $g/M$ — which is what Step 3 in fact uses. Fix: define $\bar x(0)=M^{-1}\int y\,g(y)\,dy$ and $\mathrm{var}(0)$ correspondingly where they first appear.
5. [minor] statement part 2 (line 85): "The cloud therefore stays sharp relative to the distance travelled *exactly when* $\mathrm{Pe}$ is large and the initial width is small compared with $L$" states an equivalence between a quantitative ratio and two informal conditions, neither of which is given a threshold. Fix: "stays sharp ... when and only when", or better, state it as the quantitative reading of $\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(0)+2DL/V}\,/\,L$.
