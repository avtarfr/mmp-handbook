## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Solution in a moving frame", part 1 (line 80): the parenthetical "Both moment hypotheses are needed" is false. For a non-negative $g$ of finite mass, Cauchy-Schwarz against the finite measure $g\,dy$ gives $\int|y|g \le (\int y^2 g)^{1/2}(\int g)^{1/2}$, so $\int y^2 g < \infty$ already forces $\int |y| g < \infty$: the first-moment hypothesis is redundant, and no $g$ witnesses its necessity. The example $g(y)=(1+y^2)^{-1}$ is correct but only shows that *some* moment hypothesis is needed (it has neither moment). Fix: drop "$\int |y|\,g(y)\,dy < \infty$" from the hypothesis list (or keep it and say it is implied by the next one), and reword the parenthesis to "A moment hypothesis is needed: a bounded continuous $g \ge 0$ of finite mass, such as $g(y)=(1+y^2)^{-1}$, may have neither a mean nor a variance."
2. [minor] example "A release into a river", item 5 (line 192) and application, third paragraph (line 242): $\sigma$ is used for the standard deviation of the cloud ($4\sigma/V$ with $\sigma = 141.4\,$m) and for the Wiener process's instantaneous standard deviation, while the same letter is the travelling-wave velocity throughout lines 127-234. Each use is locally unambiguous and item 5's $\sigma$ is never defined in that block. Fix: write $4\sqrt{2Dt}/V$ in item 5, or name the width $s$ there, and keep $\sigma$ for the front velocity (the Grimmett-Stirzaker $\sigma$ is the book's own and can stay if flagged as such).
3. [minor] example "A release into a river", item 2 (line 189): "The peak passes the intake at $t = L/V = 2000\,\mathrm{s}$" is right for the travelling maximum of the profile in $x$, but a reader will take it as the time of maximum concentration *at the intake*, which is not the same: maximising $u(L,t) = M(4\pi Dt)^{-1/2}e^{-(L-Vt)^2/4Dt}$ in $t$ gives $t = 1980.1\,\mathrm{s}$, about 1% early (the $t^{-1/2}$ prefactor pulls it back). Also $2000\,\mathrm{s}$ is $33.3$ min, not $33$ min. Fix: say "the centre of the cloud reaches the intake at $t = L/V = 2000\,\mathrm{s} \approx 33\,\mathrm{min}$; the measured maximum is a little earlier, at $1980\,\mathrm{s}$, because the amplitude is still falling as $t^{-1/2}$".
