## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Solution in a moving frame", part 1 (line 80): the parenthetical "(Both moment hypotheses are needed: ... $g(y)=(1+y^2)^{-1}$ ...)" is false, and the hypothesis $\int|y|\,g(y)\,dy<\infty$ is redundant. With $g\ge0$, $\int g = M<\infty$ and $\int y^2 g<\infty$, Cauchy-Schwarz applied to $|y|\sqrt g\cdot\sqrt g$ gives $\bigl(\int|y|g\bigr)^2 \le \bigl(\int g\bigr)\bigl(\int y^2 g\bigr) < \infty$, so finiteness of the first absolute moment follows from the other two hypotheses and cannot be "needed". The exhibited $g(y)=(1+y^2)^{-1}$ has *both* moments infinite, so it does not separate the two hypotheses either; it only shows that some moment hypothesis is required. Fix: delete "$\int|y|\,g(y)\,dy<\infty$" from part 1 (and adjust Step 3's "its first two moments were assumed finite" to "its second moment was assumed finite, whence its first is too, by Cauchy-Schwarz"), and either drop the parenthetical or replace it with a genuine separating example for the second-moment hypothesis, e.g. the bounded continuous $g(y)=(1+|y|^3)^{-1}\ge0$, which has $\int g<\infty$ and $\int|y|g<\infty$ but $\int y^2 g=\infty$ (the integrand is $\sim|y|^{-1}$), so the variance of part 1 does not exist.

2. [minor] statement, part 2 (line 85) and example "A release into a river" (line 184): part 1's hypotheses force $\mathrm{var}(0)>0$ strictly - a continuous bounded $g\ge0$ with $0<M<\infty$ cannot have zero variance - so the clause "For a release that starts concentrated, $\mathrm{var}(0)=0$" describes a case the statement's own hypotheses exclude. The example then says in one paragraph both "A point release is not one of the continuous bounded data the statement above admits, so we do not use its formula" and "parts 1 and 2 of the statement apply to it with $\mathrm{var}(0)=0$". The mathematics is right (the paragraph independently identifies $M\,S_D(x-Vt,t)$ as a Gaussian of mean $Vt$ and variance $2Dt$), but the attribution is not. Fix: in part 2 write the limiting case as a remark about the $\mathrm{var}(0)\to0$ limit rather than as an instance of the statement, and in the example say that the conclusions of parts 1-2 are re-derived directly for the kernel rather than that those parts "apply".

3. [minor] proof, Step 3 (line 104): "$\int S_D(\cdot,t)=1$ gives conservation of mass by Fubini's theorem, the integrand being non-negative" - the theorem being used for a non-negative integrand without a prior integrability hypothesis is Tonelli's, not Fubini's. Fix: name Tonelli (or "Fubini-Tonelli").

4. [minor] proof of "The velocity is fixed by the areas" (line 158): "Both sides are integrable on $\mathbb{R}$: the right-hand side because $v'\in L^2$, so the left-hand side has a limit at each end" conflates $\tfrac{d}{ds}E$ with $E$ - it is $E(v(s),v'(s))$, not the left-hand side $\tfrac{d}{ds}E$, that has limits at $\pm\infty$, and those limits come from $v\to0,1$, $v'\to0$ and continuity of $E$, not from integrability. Fix: argue directly that $\int_{-R}^{R}\tfrac{d}{ds}E\,ds = E(v(R),v'(R)) - E(v(-R),v'(-R)) \to E(1,0)-E(0,0)$ as $R\to\infty$, and that the left side tends to $-\sigma\int(v')^2$, finite because $v'\in L^2$.

5. The note's "This is one of the handbook's few statements given without a complete proof" is defensible (that unit's own note flags three claims it repeats on Evans' authority), but a reader is not told where the fuller treatment is. Fix: add a `:ref` to `pde.travelling-waves` at the bistable statement and compress the route summary, or drop the prerequisite if the duplication is intended.

6. [minor] statement, bistable travelling fronts (lines 127-132): "A travelling wave is a solution of the form $u(x,t)=v(x-\sigma t)$. Substituting, $v$ must satisfy [ODE], [limits at $\pm\infty$]" reads as if the limit conditions also follow from substituting; they are imposed, as Evans' (16) makes clear. Fix: separate "substituting gives the ODE" from "we look for a profile subject to the conditions".
