## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Solution in a moving frame", part 1 (line 80): the parenthetical "**Both** moment hypotheses are needed" is false. For $g \ge 0$ with $\int g = M < \infty$, a finite second moment forces a finite first moment, since $|y| \le \tfrac12(1+y^2)$ gives $\int|y|g \le \tfrac12\bigl(M + \int y^2g\bigr) < \infty$ (equivalently by Cauchy–Schwarz, $\int|y|g \le (\int y^2g)^{1/2}M^{1/2}$). So $\int|y|g\,dy<\infty$ is a redundant hypothesis, and the statement asserts that it cannot be dropped. The counterexample $g(y)=(1+y^2)^{-1}$ supports only the weaker (true) claim that finite mass alone gives no moments; it does not support the claim made. Fix: assume only $\int y^2 g(y)\,dy<\infty$, and reword the parenthetical to "A finite second moment is needed and does not follow from finite mass: the bounded continuous $g(y)=(1+y^2)^{-1} \ge 0$ has $\int g = \pi$ but no mean and no variance; the first moment is then automatically finite, since $|y| \le \tfrac12(1+y^2)$." Step 3 of the proof should likewise say that the mean exists because the second moment does.
