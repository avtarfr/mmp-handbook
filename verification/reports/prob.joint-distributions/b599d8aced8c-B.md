## Findings
1. [minor] statement, part 1 (line 73 ff.) against remark (line 149): the statement's hypotheses on T are injectivity on an open set containing the support and continuity of the partial derivatives of T^{-1}; it does not require J != 0. The remark then says "A non-vanishing Jacobian, and continuous partial derivatives ... are used in exactly one place, the second display of the proof of part 1", naming a hypothesis the statement never made. (The formula does in fact survive J vanishing on a null set — x = y^3 in one dimension is the standard instance, and the unit's own polar example has |J| = s -> 0 at the origin — so the statement is not wrong, but it and the remark do not line up.) Fix: either add "with J(y) != 0 on R, except possibly on a Lebesgue-null set" to part 1, or reword the remark to say that the change-of-variables theorem invoked in the proof carries that hypothesis and that the null set where it fails costs nothing.
2. [minor] remark (line 157), src on line 146: "the footnote and Exercise (4.5.1) on marginals, p. 111". The footnote is indeed on p. 111 ("It does not follow that the marginal density functions are themselves continuous functions. See Exercise (4.5.1)"), and it supports the claim; but Exercise (4.5.1) itself, and the attribution "Clarke's example" that our sentence uses, are on p. 115. Fix: cite "the footnote on p. 111 and Exercise (4.5.1), p. 115". The frontmatter page range 110-115 already covers both.
3. [minor] remark (line 147): "applying it to Y = X^2 gives half the right answer". Exactly half only when the density of X is symmetric about 0, which is the case in the source's Example (4.7.1) (X standard normal) but not in general: for X with density on (-1, 2), say, the discarded branch carries a different share. Fix: "loses one of the two branches — for a standard normal, exactly half".
