[minor] proof (line 100, Step 3): the step invokes "part 2 of :ref[Green's identities]" for a ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$, but that unit states its three identities for a bounded domain $V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^3$; only a remark there records that Evans gives them in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Fix: add a half-sentence pointing at that remark, e.g. "in the $\mathbb{R}^n$ form noted there (Evans, Appendix §C.2)", so the $n$-dimensional use is visibly licensed.
2. [minor] frontmatter (line 7): `vcalc.divergence-theorem` is listed as a prerequisite but is not used anywhere in the unit — Step 3 goes through Green's identities and Step 6 through polar coordinates. Fix: drop it, or use it explicitly (it is already a prerequisite of `vcalc.green-identities`).
3. [minor] remark (line 138): "taking $u_1 = 1$ shows that the second integral vanishes on its own". On the punctured disc named two sentences earlier, Ahlfors's (58) gives only that $\int_{|z|=r} r\,u_r\,d\theta$ is *constant* in $r$; it vanishes because $u$ is harmonic on the *whole* disc, so a single circle is homologous to zero there (Ahlfors p. 165, "constant in the case of an annulus and zero if $u$ is harmonic in the whole disk"). Fix: say "taking $u_1 = 1$, and using that $u$ is harmonic on the full disc so that a single circle is homologous to zero, shows the second integral vanishes".
4. [minor] application (line 359): "Five-point finite-difference and piecewise-linear finite-element discretizations of $-\nabla^2$ satisfy a discrete maximum principle" is stated without its mesh hypothesis. The five-point stencil does on a uniform grid, but $P_1$ finite elements satisfy a discrete maximum principle only on a non-obtuse (Delaunay-type) triangulation, where the stiffness matrix is an M-matrix; on an obtuse mesh it can fail. Fix: add "on a uniform grid" and "on a non-obtuse triangulation" respectively.
