## Findings
1. [blocking] statement, part 3 (line 51), together with remark, third bullet (line 89): the hypothesis "$S_1\cap S_2 \ne \emptyset$ is connected" is never used, and the remark's explanation of it is wrong. Part 3 assumes $f_1 = f_2$ on *all* of $S_1\cap S_2$; the piecewise function is then well defined and analytic whether or not the overlap is connected, and the proof (line 75) confirms this -- it uses only "$f_1$ and $f_2$ agree on $S_1\cap S_2$ by hypothesis" and locality of analyticity. The remark then says "If $S_1\cap S_2$ has two components, agreement on one does not force agreement on the other, and the piecewise formula need not be well defined", which describes a hypothesis the statement does not make: with agreement assumed on the whole overlap, two components cause no trouble at all. The $\log$ illustration is a genuine phenomenon but illustrates a different statement. Fix: make the hypothesis load-bearing by weakening the agreement assumption -- "$S_1 \cap S_2$ is non-empty and connected and $f_1 = f_2$ on some non-empty open subset of $S_1\cap S_2$" -- after which part 1 upgrades that to agreement on the whole overlap, the remark's bullet becomes correct, and the two $\log$ branches become a real counterexample. Otherwise delete "is connected" from part 3 and rewrite the bullet.
2. [blocking] proof, part 5 (lines 81-83): the bisection has no well-defined invariant, and the step that closes the contradiction is missing. (a) The invariant is stated as "continuation around its perimeter fails to return the initial germ", but the perimeter of a subrectangle $R^{(n)}$ is a loop based at *its own* corner, not at the corner of $R$ where the initial germ lives, so "the initial germ" is not defined for it and the inherited property cannot be checked. (b) At the limit the proof concludes "continuation around $\partial R^{(n)}$ is governed by the single element $(f_\infty,\Omega_\infty)$ throughout" from $H(\partial R^{(n)}) \subseteq \Omega_\infty$ alone. That does not follow: the germ transported from the corner of $R$ to the corner of $R^{(n)}$ need not be the germ of $f_\infty$ there, and if it is not, $(f_\infty,\Omega_\infty)$ does not govern the circuit -- and whether transport is path-independent is precisely what is being proved, so it cannot be assumed here. Ahlfors supplies both missing pieces on p. 296: his loops are $\pi^{(n)} = \sigma_n\Gamma_n\sigma_n^{-1}$, based at the corner $0$ of $R$, with $\sigma_n$ a polygon from $0$ to the lower-left corner of $R^{(n)}$ and **$\sigma_n$ a subarc of $\sigma_{n+1}$**, the limit arc $\sigma_\infty$ ends at $P_\infty$ and carries the germ of $(f_\infty,\Omega_\infty)$, and he states explicitly that for large $n$ "the germ obtained at the terminal point of $\sigma_n$ will belong to the function element $(f_\infty,\Omega_\infty)$". Fix: carry the connecting arcs $\sigma_n$ in the invariant, note that they are nested so that $\sigma_n \to \sigma_\infty$, and add the sentence that for large $n$ the germ at the end of $\sigma_n$ is the germ of $f_\infty$, which is what makes the circuit around $\Gamma_n$ trivial.
3. [minor] example "Two series, one function" (line 125): two numbers in our own text are off. Fix: "$+0.3$" and "about fifteen terms".
