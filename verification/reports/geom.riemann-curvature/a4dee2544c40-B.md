## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Conventions, and the general count" (line 165), with the same claim made in
   the definition block (line 44): the unit attributes to Stakgold and Holst an index-ordering
   convention they do not use, and the reason given contradicts the conclusion drawn from it.

   The unit says: "In their expression the *first* lower differentiation index $b$ belongs to the
   outer derivative, whereas in ours it is the last. The two differ by one transposition of the
   last two indices, so their $R^{a}{}_{dbc}$ is the negative of the tensor $R^{a}{}_{dbc}$ defined
   here." Line 44 likewise says they "order the two differentiation indices the other way round".

   Stakgold and Holst (p. 728) use the same semicolon convention we do: $V^{a}{}_{;bc}$ means
   $(V^{a}{}_{;b})_{;c}$, so the *last* index $c$ is the outer derivative, exactly as in our
   $v_{i;jk}=(v_{i;j})_{;k}$. I verified this from their own displayed formula rather than from the
   notation: expanding $(V^{a}{}_{;b})_{;c}-(V^{a}{}_{;c})_{;b}$ from scratch gives
   $[\Gamma^{a}_{bd,c}-\Gamma^{a}_{cd,b}+\Gamma^{a}_{ec}\Gamma^{e}_{bd}-\Gamma^{a}_{eb}\Gamma^{e}_{cd}]V^{d}$,
   which is character for character the expression the unit quotes from p. 728. So their index
   placement is ours; nothing is transposed.

   The sign difference is real but has a different cause: they commute a **contravariant** field
   and we commute a **covariant** one, and the two commutators carry opposite signs
   ($[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = +R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^{\sigma}$ against
   $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\omega_\sigma = -R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\omega_\rho$). The unit's own
   text notices the vector/covector difference ("write the commutator for a **vector** field") but
   then ascribes the sign to index ordering instead.

   This matters because the stated reason, taken at face value, yields the opposite of the stated
   conclusion: if $b$ really were the outer derivative in their notation, then
   $V^{a}{}_{;bc}-V^{a}{}_{;cb}=[\nabla_b,\nabla_c]V^{a}=R^{a}{}_{dbc}V^{d}$ in *our* sign
   convention, and their tensor would equal ours, not its negative. The conclusion the unit draws
   ("their $R^{a}{}_{dbc}$ is the negative of ours") is nonetheless correct — I checked it both
   from the commutator and from the explicit formula.

   Fix: replace the explanatory sentence with the true reason. Something like: "Both conventions
   put the outer derivative on the last index; the sign differs because they commute a
   contravariant field and we a covariant one, and the two commutators have opposite signs. Hence
   their $R^{a}{}_{dbc}$ equals ours with the last two indices transposed, that is, its negative."
   Line 44 needs the same correction.
