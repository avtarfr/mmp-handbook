## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Lower semicontinuity in the calculus of variations" (line 232): the sentence "The infimum is not attained" contradicts the two clauses immediately before it, and is false for the variational problem as the unit sets it up. The admissible class is stated as "the $u$ that are piecewise smooth with $u(0)=u(1)=0$", and $u_k$ — continuous, piecewise linear, $u_k(0)=u_k(1)=0$ — is a member of that class with $E(u_k)=0$ exactly (the text says so itself: "Then $E(u_k)=0$ for every $k$, so $\inf E=0$"). Since $E\ge0$ on the class and the value $0$ is achieved by every $u_k$, the infimum is attained, by infinitely many admissible functions. The block is `provenance=original`, so this is our own material. Fix: delete the clause, or make the energy one whose infimum really is unattained by adding a term that penalises the oscillation, e.g. $E(u)=\int_0^1\bigl[(u'^2-1)^2+u^2\bigr]dx$ (then $E(u_k)=\int_0^1 u_k^2\le 1/(2k)^2\to0$, so $\inf E=0$, while $E(u)=0$ would force $u\equiv0$ and $u'^2\equiv1$ simultaneously, which is impossible). Either repair leaves the paragraph's actual point — that the limit of a minimising sequence need not be a minimiser, because the *gradients* do not converge — intact; that point does not need non-attainment.
2. [minor] application (line 232): with the infimum attained, "the direct method" framing of the paragraph is slightly overstated: what the example exhibits is failure of lower semicontinuity along the sequence, not failure of existence. Worth one clause once finding 1 is fixed.
