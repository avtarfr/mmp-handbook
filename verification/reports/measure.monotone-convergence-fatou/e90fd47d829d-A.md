## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Lower semicontinuity in the calculus of variations" (line 232): "The infimum is not attained" is false for the functional as defined. The admissible class is "the $u$ that are piecewise smooth with $u(0) = u(1) = 0$" and $E(u) = \int_0^1 (u'(x)^2-1)^2\,dx$. Every $u_k$ in the text is admissible (piecewise linear, hence piecewise smooth, with $u_k(0) = u_k(1) = 0$) and the text itself establishes $E(u_k) = 0$ for every $k$ and $\inf E = 0$. So the infimum **is** attained — already by $u_1$, the single tent with slope $+1$ on $(0,\tfrac12)$ and $-1$ on $(\tfrac12,1)$, and by every $u_k$. The sentence also sits oddly beside the correct sentence three lines later, which says only that Fatou "does not by itself turn the limit of a minimising sequence into a minimiser" — that is about the limit $u \equiv 0$, not about attainment of the infimum. Fix: either delete the clause "The infimum is not attained, and" (nothing else in the paragraph depends on it; the point actually being made is the failure of lower semicontinuity along $u_k \to 0$ uniformly, which the rest of the paragraph proves), or move to the Bolza functional $E(u) = \int_0^1 [(u'^2-1)^2 + u^2]\,dx$, for which the same sawtooth gives $E(u_k) = \int_0^1 u_k^2 = 1/(12k^2) \to 0$ (checked numerically: $0.0092593$ at $k = 3$) while no admissible $u$ attains $0$, since that would force $u \equiv 0$ and $\lvert u'\rvert \equiv 1$ simultaneously — there the non-attainment claim is true.

5. [source] statement of Fatou's lemma / remark bullet 3, against `stakgold-holst-2011` §0.7, Thm 0.7.3, p. 47: that theorem is stated for an arbitrary sequence of measurable functions on a measurable $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, with no non-negativity and no integrable lower bound, and is false as printed ($f_j = -j\mathbf{1}_{(0,1/j)}$ gives $0 \le -1$). Our unit states the non-negative Folland–Evans version, says explicitly that Stakgold and Holst's hypotheses differ, and gives exactly this counterexample, so it reports the source faithfully.
