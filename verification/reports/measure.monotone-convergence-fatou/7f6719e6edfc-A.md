## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Lower semicontinuity in the calculus of variations" (line 224): "Fatou's lemma gives $\int_\Omega \liminf_k F_k \le \liminf_k \int_\Omega F_k$ for non-negative integrands, which says exactly that the energy of the limit cannot exceed the limiting energy - so the limit is a minimiser." The second clause does not follow and is false as stated. Fatou bounds $\int \liminf_k F(x,u_k,\nabla u_k)$, not $E(u) = \int F(x,u,\nabla u)$; identifying the two needs $\liminf_k F(x,u_k,\nabla u_k) \ge F(x,u,\nabla u)$ a.e., i.e. essentially a.e. convergence of the gradients, which is exactly what the direct method does not supply (a minimising sequence bounded in $W^{1,p}$ gives only weak convergence of $\nabla u_k$). Counterexample: $\Omega = (0,1)$, $F(p) = (p^2-1)^2$ with $u_k$ the sawtooth of slope $\pm 1$ and period $1/k$, $u_k \to 0$ uniformly. Then $F_k \equiv 0$, so Fatou reads $0 \le 0$ and is satisfied, while $E(u_k) = 0$ for every $k$ and $E(0) = 1 > 0$: the energy of the limit strictly exceeds the limiting energy, and the limit is not a minimiser. Lower semicontinuity in the direct method is precisely the part Fatou does not give (it needs convexity of $F$ in $\nabla u$). Fix: state the hypothesis under which the gloss is true - $F_k \to F(x,u,\nabla u)$ a.e., e.g. when $F$ depends on $(x,u)$ only and $u_k \to u$ a.e. - and present Fatou as the step that converts a.e. convergence of the integrands into lower semicontinuity, rather than as delivering lower semicontinuity of $E$ by itself.
2. [minor] proof "Monotone convergence", step 6 (line 105): from "$f - f\mathbf{1}_E$ and $f_n - f_n\mathbf{1}_E$ ... have zero integral" the step concludes $\int f = \int f\mathbf{1}_E$, which needs additivity of the integral on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$. That is step 1 of the next proof in this unit, and it is proved from the everywhere version of monotone convergence just established, so nothing is circular - but the use is silent here, whereas the parallel step 4 of the Fatou proof (line 143) does cite it. Fix: either cite "additivity on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ (step 1 of the next proof, which uses only the version just proved)", or argue directly from the supremum definition: for simple $\varphi \le f$, $\int \varphi = \int \varphi \mathbf{1}_E \le \int f \mathbf{1}_E$ by finite additivity of the stage-1 integral over the disjoint pair $E, Z$.
