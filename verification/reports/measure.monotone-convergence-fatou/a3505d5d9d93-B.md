## Findings
1. [minor] application (line 230): "which by the a.e. corollary above needs $\nabla u_k \to \nabla u$ a.e." states a sufficient condition as a necessary one. A.e. convergence of the integrands $F_k \to F(x,u,\nabla u)$ is what is actually required; gradient convergence is one way to get it, and is not necessary (if $F$ does not depend on $p$, or is constant in $p$ along the oscillation, the integrands can converge with the gradients diverging). Fix: write "for which it is enough that $\nabla u_k \to \nabla u$ a.e." or "which the a.e. corollary supplies once $\nabla u_k \to \nabla u$ a.e.".
2. [minor] remark (line 151) and application (line 246): both attribute to Stakgold and Holst, §0.7, p. 47, the observation that Fatou's lemma is "the engine behind lower semicontinuity" of energy functionals. The page says only that the relationship between the Lebesgue integral and the lim-inf "is fundamentally important to the study of variational problems in partial differential equations"; it does not mention lower semicontinuity. The substance of the attribution is right and the frontmatter `role` states it accurately ("Fatou's lemma matters for variational problems"), but the body sharpens the source's wording. Fix: say they single out variational problems in PDE, and keep "lower semicontinuity" as our own specification.
