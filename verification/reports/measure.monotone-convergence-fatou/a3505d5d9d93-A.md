## Findings
1. [source] statement[Fatou's lemma] / remark bullet 3 — :cite[Stakgold and Holst]{work=stakgold-holst-2011 loc="§0.7, Thm 0.7.3, p. 47"} state Fatou's lemma for an arbitrary sequence of measurable functions on a measurable $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}$, with no non-negativity and no integrable lower bound, although their Thm 0.7.2 on the facing page does carry $0 \le f_i \le f_{i+1}$. As printed it is false, and the unit's own $-n\mathbf{1}_{(0,1/n)}$ refutes it. The unit follows Folland Thm 2.18 (p. 52) and Evans Thm 3 (p. 686), says explicitly that Stakgold and Holst's hypotheses differ, and supplies the counterexample, so the source is reported faithfully and this is not blocking. Already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (two entries for this unit against stakgold-holst-2011, §0.7, Thm 0.7.3, p. 47); no new entry added.
2. [minor] application, Bolza paragraph (last sentence): "The derivatives $u_k'$ oscillate between $+1$ and $-1$ and converge at no point." $u_k'$ is undefined at the corners $j/(2k)$, and some points are corners for every $k$ (e.g. $x = 1/2 = k/(2k)$), so at those points the sequence does not exist rather than failing to converge. The substance is correct: at every non-corner $x \in (0,1)$ the sign is $+1$ iff $\lfloor 2kx \rfloor$ is even, and since $\lfloor 2kx\rfloor \to \infty$ with increments taking both the values $\lfloor 2x\rfloor$ and $\lceil 2x\rceil$, one of which is odd, the parity changes infinitely often. Fix: "converge at no point off the corners", matching the earlier clause "$u_k'(x) = \pm 1$ off the finite set of corners".
3. [minor] application, paragraph beginning "The direct method minimises": "the integrands converge, which by the a.e. corollary above *needs* $\nabla u_k \to \nabla u$ a.e." A.e. convergence of the gradients (with $F$ continuous) is sufficient for $\liminf_k F_k = F(x,u,\nabla u)$ a.e., not necessary. Fix: "for which it is enough that $\nabla u_k \to \nabla u$ a.e."
4. [minor] remark bullet 5 and application, final sentence of the Bolza discussion: the unit attributes to :cite[Stakgold and Holst]{work=stakgold-holst-2011 loc="§0.7, p. 47"} the specific observation that Fatou's lemma is "the engine behind lower semicontinuity of energy functionals". Their p. 47 says only that the lemma "is fundamentally important to the study of variational problems in partial differential equations"; the lower-semicontinuity reading is ours, and it is a fair gloss, but the attribution is more specific than the page. Fix: attribute the variational-problems claim to them and present the lower-semicontinuity mechanism as the unit's own.
5. [minor] proof[Monotone convergence], step 2: $\varphi = \sum_{j=1}^{N} a_j \mathbf{1}_{E_j}$ is introduced only as "simple"; step 4's identity $\int_{A_n}\varphi = \sum_j a_j \mu(E_j \cap A_n)$ uses that this is the *standard* representation (distinct non-zero $a_j$, disjoint $E_j$), as defined in :ref[]{to=measure.lebesgue-integral}. Re-derived the Bolza example in full. The sawtooth with slope $+1$ on odd $j$ from $u_k(0) = 0$ reaches $1/(2k)$ at odd endpoints $j/(2k)$ and $0$ at even ones (net rise after $j$ intervals is $\lceil j/2\rceil - \lfloor j/2 \rfloor$ steps of $1/(2k)$), so $u_k(1) = 0$ and $\lVert u_k\rVert_\infty = 1/(2k)$. With the $u^2$ term present the first term vanishes a.e. and $E(u_k) = 2k\int_0^{1/(2k)} t^2 dt = 2k/(24k^3) = 1/(12k^2)$, confirmed numerically for $k = 1,2,3,5,10$. Hence $\inf E = 0$, and it is not attained: $E(u) = 0$ forces $u = 0$ a.e. and $u'^2 = 1$ a.e., but a continuous $u$ vanishing a.e. vanishes identically, giving $u' = 0$ and $E(u) = 1$. Also confirmed $E(0) = 1$, $F_k = u_k^2 \le 1/(4k^2)$, $\liminf_k F_k = 0$ a.e., and that Fatou therefore reads $0 \le 0$ rather than $1 \le 0$.
