## Findings
1. [source] remark, bullet "Non-negativity is used in Fatou's lemma" (line 149) and frontmatter source stakgold-holst-2011: Stakgold and Holst's Thm 0.7.3, p. 47, states Fatou's lemma for an arbitrary sequence of measurable functions on a measurable $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ with no lower bound at all, and is false as printed — $f_j = -j\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,1/j)}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ gives $\int \liminf f_j = 0 \le -1 = \liminf \int f_j$, which is false. The unit reports the source accurately, follows Folland and Evans instead, says so explicitly, and gives this counterexample itself, so the unit passes. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add measure.monotone-convergence-fatou stakgold-holst-2011 "§0.7, Theorem 0.7.3, p. 47" …`.
2. [minor] statement "Monotone convergence theorem" (line 53), almost-everywhere clause: the clause assumes only that the $f_n$ lie in $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ and that $f_n(x) \uparrow f(x)$ for a.e. $x$, but writes $\int f$, which presupposes that $f$ is measurable. On an incomplete measure space it need not be: take $X = \mathbb{R}$ with the Borel $\sigma$-algebra and Lebesgue measure, $f_n \equiv 0$, and $f = \mathbf{1}_{V}$ for a non-Borel $V$ inside the Cantor set; then $f_n \uparrow f$ off a null set and $\int f$ is undefined. Folland's Cor. 2.17 (p. 51), the cited source, writes "$f \in L^{+}$" into the hypotheses for exactly this reason, and step 6 of the proof uses the measurability of $f$ when it writes $\int f = \int f\mathbf{1}_E$. The conclusion is never false, only occasionally meaningless, so this is minor rather than blocking. Fix: "if the $f_n$ and $f$ are in $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ and $f_n(x)\uparrow f(x)$ for a.e. $x$". The same wording gap appears in the Fatou statement's corollary (line 75), where Folland's Cor. 2.19 again requires $f \in L^{+}$.
3. [minor] example, preamble (line 170): "The integrand is unbounded at both ends" is wrong, and the rest of the same sentence says so — $\ln(1/x)/(1-x) \to 1$ as $x \uparrow 1$ (I checked: at $x = 1 - 10^{-6}$ the integrand is $1.0000005$), so it is bounded near the right endpoint and unbounded only at $0$. Nothing in the computation depends on it. Fix: "The integrand is awkward at both ends — unbounded like $\ln(1/x)$ as $x \downarrow 0$, and a $0/0$ limit as $x \uparrow 1$".
