## Checked

- **Counterexample search.**
  - Condition 2 of the definition at $n=1$: for $G=-\tfrac i{2k}e^{ik\lvert x\rvert}$ the outward combination
    $\partial_rG-ikG$ vanishes identically; for the incoming function it is $-e^{-ik\lvert x\rvert}$, modulus $1$.
    The unit's claim that the condition still bites at $n=1$ holds.
  - Condition 2 at $n=2$, where the unit does not check it: $o(r^{-1/2})$ is exactly the right threshold.
    $H_0^{(1)}$ gives $\partial_\varrho G-ikG \sim -G/(2\varrho) = O(\varrho^{-3/2})$, which passes, and $H_0^{(2)}$
    gives $-2ikG = O(\varrho^{-1/2})$, which fails. So step 2 of the two-dimensional proof is sound.
  - Uniqueness of the table entries: in one dimension $\tfrac12\lvert x\rvert + ax$ also satisfies $G''=\delta$, so
    "arbitrary additive constant" alone would not pin the first column down; the note block's standing assumption of
    rotation invariance (evenness at $n=1$) removes the linear term, so the statement is consistent with the unit's
    own conventions. Not a finding.
  - Normalisation of all three Helmholtz entries by flux through a small sphere/circle: $4\pi r^2\partial_rG\to1$
    (3-D), $2\pi\varrho\,\partial_\varrho G\to1$ using $H_1^{(1)}(z)\approx-2i/\pi z$ (2-D), and the derivative jump
    $G'(0^+)-G'(0^-)=1$ (1-D). All give $+1$, consistent with $\nabla^2G=\delta$ and with
    green.laplace-green-functions, which fixes the same sign convention.
  - Degenerate cases: $k>0$ is required in the definition, so the $k\to0$ limit appears only in the statement's
    prose, where it is correctly hedged to the one- and two-dimensional columns ($-\tfrac i{2k}e^{ik|x|} =
    -\tfrac i{2k}+\tfrac12|x|+O(k)$; $-\tfrac i4H_0^{(1)} = \tfrac1{2\pi}\ln\varrho + \text{const} + o(1)$; the
    three-dimensional limit is exact).
  - Method step 5 in two dimensions: see finding 1 — the only place where a step as written does not do what it
    says, and the unit's own proofs do not use it.
