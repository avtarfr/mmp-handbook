1. [blocking] :::idea (line ~86, second paragraph): "Taking the principal value catches half of each
   and gives the standing wave; Hassani notes that this is what a naive principal value produces,
   and that the result then fails to satisfy the inhomogeneous equation at all, because the
   principal-value contour does not correspond to any physical situation."
   Counterexample: for $\nabla^2+k^2$ the principal value gives
   $G_{\mathrm{std}}(r) = -\cos(kr)/(4\pi r)$ (I evaluated
   $\mathrm{P}\!\int_0^\infty q\sin qr/(k^2-q^2)\,dq = -\tfrac\pi2\cos kr$), and this **does**
   satisfy $(\nabla^2+k^2)G=\delta$: symbolically $\nabla^2 G + k^2G = 0$ for $r>0$, and the Laurent
   expansion at the origin is $-1/(4\pi r)+O(r)$, which is exactly the required singularity. It is
   also the half-sum of two Green's functions, so it is automatically one. In one dimension the
   principal value likewise gives $\sin(k|x|)/(2k)$, whose derivative jump at $0$ I computed to be
   exactly $1$, again a Green's function.
   What Hassani says on p. 685 is about the **wave** operator: his Eq. (22.47), obtained by taking
   the principal value of the $\omega$ integral, "satisfies only the homogeneous wave equation with
   no delta function on the RHS". That happens there because the $t>0$ and $t<0$ contours close in
   opposite half-planes, so the symmetric prescription produces the *difference* of the retarded and
   advanced functions. No such sign flip occurs in the spatial $q$ integral of the Helmholtz
   problem, where the contour closes in the upper half-plane for every $r>0$. Transplanting the
   warning to Helmholtz makes a false statement and misreports the source.
   Fix: say that the principal value gives the standing-wave Green's function, which is a perfectly
   good Green's function but describes a cavity rather than radiation; and keep Hassani's warning
   where it belongs, as a remark that the analogous prescription for the *wave* operator does not
   produce a Green's function at all.
