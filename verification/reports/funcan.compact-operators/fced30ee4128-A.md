## Findings
1. [blocking] example, step 4 (line 189): the finite-rank truncation is written as $V_{N}=\sum_{n\le N}s_{n}(\cdot,\psi_{n})\varphi_{n}$, but $\varphi_{n}$ and $\psi_{n}$ are never defined. Nothing in the unit produces a singular system for $V$: step 2 only diagonalises $V^{*}V$ and names the numbers $s_{n}$. Worse, $\varphi_{n}$ already has a different meaning inside the unit — in the proof of part 5 (line 107) $\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots\}$ is the orthonormal basis of $H$ used to build the projections $P_{n}$ — so the same symbol carries two meanings. Fix: define $\psi_{n}$ as the normalised eigenvectors of $V^{*}V$ for $\mu_{n}$ (explicitly, $\psi_{n}(t)=\sqrt{2}\cos((n-\tfrac12)\pi t)$) and $\varphi_{n}=V\psi_{n}/s_{n}$, and use a symbol other than $\varphi$.
2. [blocking] example, step 4 (line 191): the error identity $\lVert V-V_{N}\rVert=s_{N+1}$ is asserted with only a `:ref` to linalg.svd. That unit is the singular value decomposition of matrices (map scope: "existence of the SVD, relation to eigenvalues of A*A, best low-rank approximation (Eckart-Young), pseudoinverse"), it is not among this unit's prerequisites, and it is not in their transitive closure (linalg.svd sits behind linalg.hermitian-spectral-theorem and linalg.projections-least-squares, neither of which is reachable from this unit's `prereqs`). No Schmidt expansion of a compact operator is stated anywhere in the unit, so the step that the truncation error equals the first discarded singular value is unjustified at the generality used. Fix: either derive the expansion $V=\sum_{n}s_{n}(\cdot,\psi_{n})\varphi_{n}$ inline from the eigenexpansion of $V^{*}V$ (the pieces are orthogonal, so $\lVert V-V_{N}\rVert=\sup_{n>N}s_{n}=s_{N+1}$ is then two lines), or add the prerequisite that supplies it.
3. [source] proof "Parts 9 and 10", part 10 (line 130): "choose $n$ with $\sum_{i,j>n}\lvert k_{ij}\rvert^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}$". Read in the usual way ($i>n$ *and* $j>n$) this does not control $\iint\lvert K-s_{n}\rvert^{2}$, which equals the sum of $\lvert k_{ij}\rvert^{2}$ over all pairs with $\max(i,j)>n$. The unit is faithful here: Stakgold and Holst write exactly this index range (p. 357, the displayed identity before the choice of $n$). Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs` against stakgold-holst-2011 §6.1, p. 357; not counted against the unit. A one-word change ("over all $(i,j)$ outside the $n\times n$ block") would make our version correct without departing from the source's argument.
4. [minor] proof "Parts 5–8", part 6 (line 99): "using self-duality of the inner product twice" does not name what is being used. The two displayed equalities are the definition of the norm from the inner product and the defining property of the adjoint, the latter used once. Fix: "by the defining property of the adjoint".
5. [minor] proof "Parts 5–8", part 7 (line 95): the proof is written for general normed $X$ and $Y$ and finishes with "weak limits are unique". In a general normed $Y$ that needs Hahn-Banach to separate points, and funcan.hahn-banach is not among the prerequisites (it is a sibling unit in this chapter, not upstream of this one).
