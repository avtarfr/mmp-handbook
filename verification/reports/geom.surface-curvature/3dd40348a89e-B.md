## Findings
1. [blocking] method, step 6: "If $F = f = 0$ ... the work collapses: $k_1 = e/E$, $k_2 = g/G$". This is false under the unit's own labelling convention, and the unit's own worked example is the counterexample. The definition block fixes "$k_1$ for the largest and $k_2$ for the smallest value that $\mathrm{II}_p$ takes on the unit circle", and statement 3 and 4 repeat $k_1 \ge k_2$. In an orthogonal line-of-curvature chart the two principal curvatures are $e/E$ and $g/G$, but which is the larger depends on the surface: for the paraboloid of the example, $e/E = (1+r^2)^{-3/2} < g/G = (1+r^2)^{-1/2}$ for every $r>0$, and the example says so explicitly — "The second is the larger, so $k_1 = (1+r^2)^{-1/2}$ and $k_2 = (1+r^2)^{-3/2}$", i.e. $k_1 = g/G$, the opposite of step 6. A reader who applies step 6 to the unit's own example gets the two labels interchanged. Fix: write step 6 as "the principal curvatures are $e/E$ and $g/G$ (the larger of the two is $k_1$)", leaving $K = eg/(EG)$ and $H = \tfrac12(e/E+g/G)$ as they stand, since those are symmetric in the two and are correct.
2. [minor] definition, "Sign convention" bullet: "Reversing the orientation sends $N\mapsto -N$, hence $e,f,g \mapsto -e,-f,-g$, $k_1,k_2 \mapsto -k_1,-k_2$". Same root cause as finding 1. As an unordered pair the principal curvatures do change sign, but with the convention $k_1 \ge k_2$ fixed three bullets earlier the reversal sends $k_1 \mapsto -k_2$ and $k_2 \mapsto -k_1$ (e.g. $(k_1,k_2)=(3,1)$ becomes $(-1,-3)$). $H \mapsto -H$ and $K$ unchanged are both right either way, and nothing downstream uses the bullet, so this is cosmetic — but it is worth fixing in the same pass. Fix: "$\{k_1,k_2\} \mapsto \{-k_1,-k_2\}$ as a pair (the labels swap)".
3. [minor] idea block: "the matrix of $dN$ in the basis $\{\mathbf{x}_u,\mathbf{x}_v\}$ is $-\mathrm{II}\cdot \mathrm{I}^{-1}$". With $dN(\mathbf x_u)=N_u=a_{11}\mathbf x_u+a_{21}\mathbf x_v$ as the proof sets it up, the matrix of $dN$ (images as columns) is $A=(a_{ij})$ with $A^{\mathsf T} = -\mathrm{II}\,\mathrm{I}^{-1}$, so $A = -\mathrm{I}^{-1}\mathrm{II}$; the displayed product is the transpose of the matrix of $dN$ (this is exactly do Carmo's Eq. (3) arrangement, p. 157). Nothing is affected, because the idea block only goes on to take the determinant and the trace, which agree for a matrix and its transpose. Fix: write $-\mathrm{I}^{-1}\mathrm{II}$, or add "up to transpose, which does not affect $\det$ or $\operatorname{tr}$".
4. [minor] definition, "Classification of points": the four cases are given in terms of $\det(dN_p)$ and $dN_p$, which is faithful to do Carmo Def. 7, p. 148; but the unit has just defined $K = \det(dN_p)$ in the preceding bullet, so writing the classification in terms of $K$ (and $dN_p \ne 0$) would be easier to use and would match how the example applies it ("$K>0$ ... every point is elliptic"). Not wrong. No change required.
