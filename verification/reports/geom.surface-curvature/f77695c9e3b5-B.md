## Findings
1. [blocking] definition, "Sign convention" bullet: "do Carmo defines $\mathrm{II}$ with the minus sign shown, so that on the unit sphere with the *outward* normal every normal curvature equals $+1$." This is false, and it misreports the source. do Carmo, §3-2, Example 2, p. 140, fixes $N=(-x,-y,-z)$ and says in so many words that "$N$ points toward the center of the sphere", obtaining $dN_p(v)=-v$; he notes in the same paragraph that with the *outward* field $\bar N=(x,y,z)$ one gets $d\bar N_p(v)=+v$. It is with his inward $N$ that Example 7, p. 145, concludes $\mathrm{II}_p(v)=1$. With the outward normal $\mathrm{II}_p(v)=-\langle v,v\rangle=-1$. Checked symbolically on the sphere of radius $a$ in the chart $\mathbf x(\vartheta,\varphi)=a(\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi,\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi,\cos\vartheta)$, whose $\mathbf x_\vartheta\times\mathbf x_\varphi$ is outward: $E=a^2$, $F=0$, $G=a^2\sin^2\vartheta$, $e=-a$, $f=0$, $g=-a\sin^2\vartheta$, hence $K=1/a^2$ and $H=-1/a$. The bullet is also internally inconsistent with the remark's own "$\mathrm{II}_p(v)>0$ means the surface bends towards $N$" (a great circle bends away from the outward normal), and with the unit's paraboloid example, where the positive curvatures come from an $N$ that the unit itself describes as pointing "into the concave side". Fix: replace "outward" by "inward (pointing toward the centre)", as do Carmo does.
2. [blocking] method, step 7 ("Sanity checks"): "a sphere of radius $a$ with outward normal must give $K = 1/a^2$ and $H = 1/a$." $K=1/a^2$ is right for either orientation, but with the outward normal $H=-1/a$ (computation in finding 1). As written the check would cause a correct computation to be rejected, or an orientation to be silently flipped. Fix: "with the inward normal ... $H = 1/a$", or keep "outward" and write $H=-1/a$.
3. [blocking] remark, bullet "Why $-\langle dN_p(v),v\rangle$ and not $+$": "The sign is chosen so that a sphere with its outward normal has $k_1 = k_2 = 1/a > 0$ and $\mathrm{II}$ positive." Same error as finding 1: with the outward normal $k_1=k_2=-1/a$ and $\mathrm{II}=-1$ on unit vectors. The rest of the bullet ("$\mathrm{II}_p(v)>0$ means the surface bends towards $N$") is correct and is what shows the first half to be wrong. Fix: "a sphere with the normal pointing to its centre".
4. [blocking] application, "Young-Laplace": "$\Delta p = 2\sigma H$ ... with $H$ computed for the normal pointing from the high-pressure to the low-pressure side." In this unit's (do Carmo's) convention that gives, for a bubble of radius $R$ with the higher pressure inside, the outward normal and hence $H=-1/R$, so $\Delta p=-2\sigma/R$ — contradicting the correct "$k_1=k_2=1/R$ and $\Delta p = 2\sigma/R$" stated in the next sentence. The orientation rule is backwards: $H$ must be computed with the normal pointing *into* the high-pressure side, i.e. towards the centre of curvature, which is also what makes $k_1=k_2=+1/R$. Fix: "with $N$ pointing from the low-pressure side into the high-pressure side". (Evans's "$n$ times the mean curvature" sentence quoted alongside is reported correctly; p. 431 says exactly that.)
5. [minor] definition block, `src`: the block also defines the asymptotic direction ("A direction with $k_n = 0$ is **asymptotic**"), which is do Carmo's Definition 9, p. 150. The block's `src` lists only "Defs. 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8, pp. 141–149" and the frontmatter `loc` likewise stops at Prop. 4; p. 150 is inside the declared `pages` range but is not in either locator. Fix: add "Definition 9, p. 150 (asymptotic direction)".
