## Findings
1. [minor] example "A basin of attraction, with a number", Step 5 (line 178): "$\dot L < 0$ on the punctured disc of radius $\rho$ exactly when $\rho \le \min\{-a(\theta)/b(\theta) : b(\theta) > 0\}$". This is correct for the *open* punctured disc $0 < r < \rho$, which is the reading the rest of the example uses; for the closed one it fails at the minimising angle, where $a(\theta)+b(\theta)\rho_{\max} = 0$ and hence $\dot L = 0$. Worth a word, since $\rho_{\max}$ is then quoted as a radius.
2. [minor] statement "LaSalle's invariance principle" (line 54): the hypothesis list is faithful to the source (Hirsch-Smale-Devaney p. 199 ask only that $P$ be closed, a neighbourhood of $x_*$, and positively invariant), but every use of it in this unit takes $P$ compact — $\{r \le \rho\}$ in Example 1 and $\{L \le c\}$ in Example 2, where the unit explicitly checks boundedness. Saying in the method or the remark that the applications supply compactness would make clear that the unbounded case is never relied on.
3. [minor] proof, Step 5 (line 89): "fix $s > 0$ small enough that $z(s)$ is defined". The step also needs $z([0,s])$ to stay inside $\mathcal{O}\setminus\{x_*\}$ for (c) to give $L(z(s)) < L(z_0)$; that follows from $z_0 \in \overline{B_\delta} \subset \mathcal{O}$ together with the uniqueness argument already made in Step 4, but it is left implicit.
