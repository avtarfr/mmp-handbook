## Findings
1. [blocking] proof, "The indentation", last sentence (line 120): "For a pole of order $k\ge2$ the same computation gives $\delta^{1-k}$ times a non-zero constant, which diverges." The constant is zero for every **odd** $k \ge 3$. Computing $\int_{C_\delta^{\uparrow}}(z-x_0)^{-k}dz$ exactly, with $z = x_0+\delta e^{i\theta}$ and $\theta$ from $\pi$ to $0$, gives $i\delta^{1-k}\int_\pi^0 e^{i(1-k)\theta}d\theta$, and sympy evaluates this to $-i\pi$ for $k=1$, $-2/\delta$ for $k=2$, $\mathbf{0}$ for $k=3$, $-2/(3\delta^3)$ for $k=4$, $\mathbf{0}$ for $k=5$ and $-2/(5\delta^5)$ for $k=6$: non-zero exactly when $k$ is even. This is not a side issue, because the unit's own recipe (step 1, line 185) says the opposite -- "A higher-order pole whose odd-order singular terms alone survive can sometimes be rescued; Arfken sets this as Exercise 11.7.11, p. 522" -- and that exercise is real: printed p. 522 reads "A function $f(z)$ is analytic along the real axis except for a third-order pole at $z = x_0$ ... Show that the Cauchy principal value technique is applicable." So the proof contradicts both the recipe and the cited source, and a reader who believes the proof will conclude Exercise 11.7.11 is impossible. Fix: "For a pole of even order $k$ the same computation gives a non-zero multiple of $\delta^{1-k}$, which diverges; for odd $k \ge 3$ the arc contribution of that term vanishes identically, which is the loophole mentioned in step 1 of the recipe."
2. [minor] frontmatter `prereqs` (line 7): the indentation proof uses the $ML$ estimate and cites :ref[]{to=complex.contour-integrals}, which is not listed (`prereqs` has complex.infinite-integrals, complex.residue-theorem). Fix: add complex.contour-integrals.
3. [minor] definition "Cauchy principal value" (line 39): "with the obvious extension to several interior singularities and to **infinite limits**" leaves unsaid how the limits at $\pm\infty$ are taken, and the proof of statement 2 delivers the symmetric limit $\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^{R}$ (it closes the contour with a semicircle of radius $R$). For the unit's second example the integrand decays only like $1/x$, so the distinction is live -- there it happens not to bite, because $\int^{\pm\infty}\sin(3x)/(x-2)\,dx$ converges by Dirichlet's test, but the statement does not say so. Fix: say explicitly that at $\pm\infty$ the limit is the symmetric one unless the integral converges, as the companion unit complex.infinite-integrals does for its own case.
