[minor] `application`, second bullet (line ~300): "The retarded Green's function of the wave equation, the Feynman propagator and the advanced propagator differ only in how the contour passes the poles at $\omega=\pm k$, that is, in the sign of $i\varepsilon$ in $1/(\omega^2-k^2\pm i\varepsilon)$." The first clause is right; the gloss after "that is" cannot be, since one expression with two signs cannot encode three propagators. $1/(\omega^2-k^2+i\varepsilon)$ is the Feynman propagator (poles at $+k$ pushed down, $-k$ pushed up) and the minus sign gives the anti-time-ordered one; the retarded propagator is $1/[(\omega+i\varepsilon)^2-k^2]$, with *both* poles below the axis, and the advanced has both above. Fix: "...that is, in which side of each pole the contour runs: $1/[(\omega+i\varepsilon)^2-k^2]$ puts both poles below the axis (retarded), $1/[(\omega-i\varepsilon)^2-k^2]$ both above (advanced), and $1/(\omega^2-k^2+i\varepsilon)$ one on each side (Feynman)."
3. [minor] `application`, third bullet (line ~305): "$-i\pi\delta(E_i-E_f)$, whose modulus squared gives the transition rate". The golden-rule rate is *linear* in the delta term and quadratic in the matrix element: $\Gamma = -2\operatorname{Im}\Sigma = 2\pi\sum_f\lvert V_{if}\rvert^2\delta(E_i-E_f)$. The squaring belongs to $V_{if}$, not to the delta term. Fix: "and $-i\pi\delta(E_i-E_f)$, which, weighted by the squared matrix element and doubled, is the transition rate."
