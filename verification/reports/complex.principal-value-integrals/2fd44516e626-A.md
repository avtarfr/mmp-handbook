The constant is zero whenever $k$ is odd, so the claim is false, and it contradicts the unit's own method step 1 (line 185), which says a higher-order pole "whose odd-order singular terms alone survive can sometimes be rescued". Doing the computation for a term $a_{-m}(z-x_0)^{-m}$ on $C_\delta^{\uparrow}$ gives
   $$ i\,a_{-m}\,\delta^{1-m}\int_{\pi}^{0} e^{i(1-m)\theta}\,d\theta \;=\; a_{-m}\,\delta^{1-m}\,\frac{1-(-1)^{1-m}}{1-m}, $$
   whose numerator is $2$ for even $m$ and $0$ for odd $m$. Concretely $\int_{C_\delta^{\uparrow}} (z-x_0)^{-3}dz = i\delta^{-2}\int_\pi^0 e^{-2i\theta}d\theta = i\delta^{-2}(1-e^{-2\pi i})/(-2i) = 0$ exactly, for every $\delta$. This is precisely Arfken's Exercise 11.7.11 (p. 522), where $f = a_{-3}(z-x_0)^{-3} + a_{-1}(z-x_0)^{-1} + g$ has a convergent principal value and the small semicircle contributes $\pm i\pi a_{-1}$ -- the $a_{-3}$ term contributing nothing.
