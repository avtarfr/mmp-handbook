[blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used, and how sharp the conclusions are", paragraph "Finiteness of $N$ in gambler's ruin" (line 235): "The mean duration behaves differently: $D_k \to \infty$ for $p \le \tfrac12$" is false for $p < \tfrac12$, and it contradicts the unit's own formula in the statement block. With $\rho = q/p > 1$, the term $N(1-\rho^{k})/(1-\rho^{N})$ in $D_k = \frac{1}{q-p}\bigl(k - N\frac{1-\rho^{k}}{1-\rho^{N}}\bigr)$ has a numerator growing linearly in $N$ and a denominator growing geometrically, so it tends to $0$ and $D_k \to k/(q-p) < \infty$: for a downward-drifting walk ruin is certain *and* fast. Only $p = \tfrac12$ gives $D_k = k(N-k) \to \infty$, and only there is the moral that follows ("'ruin is certain' and 'ruin happens quickly' are different statements, exactly the null recurrence of part (b)") correct — at $p < \tfrac12$ the walk is transient, not null recurrent, so the null-recurrence analogy does not apply to it either. This is our own `original` block getting our own material wrong. Fix: "The mean duration behaves differently at the two ends: for $p < \tfrac12$ it converges, $D_k \to k/(q-p)$, so ruin is both certain and quick; only at $p = \tfrac12$ does $D_k = k(N-k) \to \infty$, so that ruin is certain while the expected time to it is infinite — the null recurrence of part (b) seen from another side."
