## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, "Finiteness of $N$ in gambler's ruin" (line 235): "The mean duration behaves differently: $D_k \to \infty$ for $p \le \tfrac12$." This is false for $p < \tfrac12$. With $\rho = q/p > 1$, the term $N(1-\rho^{\,k})/(1-\rho^{\,N})$ vanishes as $N \to \infty$, so $D_k \to k/(q-p)$, a finite number: the expected time to ruin on the half-line with downward drift is $k/(q-p)$ (Wald). I checked it numerically at $p=\tfrac25$, $k=5$: $D_5 = 19.18,\ 24.80,\ 25.000$ for $N = 10, 20, 50$, converging to $k/(q-p) = 25$. The claim holds for $p = \tfrac12$ (where $D_k = k(N-k) \to \infty$) and, in fact, also for $p > \tfrac12$ (where $D_k \sim N(1-\rho^k)/(p-q) \to \infty$ because absorption at $0$ may never happen) — so as written it is wrong in exactly the one case it is wrong in, and it then carries the next sentence, which draws the "ruin is certain but not quick" moral and ties it to null recurrence. That moral is only valid at $p = \tfrac12$. Fix: "$D_k \to \infty$ for $p = \tfrac12$ (and also for $p > \tfrac12$, where absorption at $0$ is not certain), whereas for $p < \tfrac12$ the mean time to ruin stays finite, $D_k \to k/(q-p)$. So at $p=\tfrac12$ 'ruin is certain' and 'ruin happens quickly' come apart, which is the null recurrence of part (b) seen from another side."
2. [blocking] statement, gambler's ruin (line 65) and proof (line 142): "The finiteness of $D_k$ shows that absorption occurs with probability one" / "Finiteness of $D_k$ shows in passing that absorption occurs with probability one". The argument is circular: $D_k$ was *defined* as the mean duration and the difference equation $D_k = 1 + pD_{k+1} + qD_{k-1}$ was solved in the reals, which presupposes $D_k < \infty$; nothing before this point rules out $D_k = \infty$ (in $[0,\infty]$ the recursion is satisfied by $D \equiv \infty$ as well, so the displayed solution is not forced). The finiteness that is then "shown" is the finiteness that was assumed. The source does not make this claim — G&S p. 80 puts $\mathbb{P}(T<\infty)=1$ and $\mathbb{E}(T^k)<\infty$ in Exercise (3.9.1), i.e. it is proved separately — so this is our own addition, and it is also what licenses the statement's last clause that $1-p_k$ is the probability of reaching $N$. Fix: prove the a priori bound first, in one sentence: from any state the walk is absorbed within $N$ steps with probability at least $\min(p,q)^N$, so $\mathbb{P}(T > mN) \le (1-\min(p,q)^N)^m$; hence $T$ has a geometric tail, $\mathbb{P}(T<\infty)=1$ and $D_k = \mathbb{E}_k(T) < \infty$. Then the difference equation may be solved, and the closing sentence becomes "absorption is certain by the tail bound above, so $1-p_k$ is the probability of reaching $N$."
