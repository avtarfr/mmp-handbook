[blocking] application (line 351): the sentence "Recurrence in the ideal chain is the statement that the chain revisits itself often, which is why the excluded-volume interaction that forbids this changes the exponent in $d \le 4$ and leaves it alone above" is wrong twice over, and the first error contradicts the unit's own headline theorem.
   (a) *Recurrence is the wrong property, and this unit has just proved so.* The unit defines recurrent as "returns to its starting point with probability one" (definition, line 45) and then proves in part (d) that the three-dimensional walk is **transient**. But $d=3$ is exactly the case the polymer sentence is about, and there the exponent does change ($\nu\approx0.588$ against the ideal $1/2$). So the stated reason - recurrence - is absent precisely where the stated effect is largest. What actually matters is the number of *self-contacts*, which is a different count and diverges even when the walk is transient: the expected number of self-intersecting pairs of a $d=3$ ideal chain of $n$ steps is $\sum_{m}(n+1-2m)\,p_0(2m)$, and since $\sum_m p_0(2m)=G-1$ converges this grows **linearly** in $n$ with slope $G-1=0.5164$. Computed from the exact multinomial sums (asymptotic tail beyond $m=60$): $5.80$ at $n=20$, $39.94$ at $n=100$, $476.3$ at $n=1000$, $1984.1$ at $n=4000$, i.e. $0.290$, $0.399$, $0.476$, $0.496$ contacts per step, climbing to $0.5164$. So the ideal chain in space self-intersects without limit while returning to its *starting point* only with probability $0.3405$: the two statements are independent, and only the second is recurrence. (The relevant perturbative count is the non-analytic part, $n^{(4-d)/2}$, which is what makes $d=4$ the critical dimension - nothing to do with the $d=2$ threshold for recurrence.)
   (b) *The inequality is wrong at the marginal dimension.* The upper critical dimension for a self-avoiding walk is $d_c=4$, and at $d=4$ the exponent is the ideal $\nu=1/2$ (logarithmic corrections only); the exponent is changed for $d<4$, not $d\le4$. As written the sentence asserts a changed exponent at $d=4$, where it is unchanged.
   Fix: drop "recurrence" from this sentence. The correct threshold statement is the Flory one: for a chain of $n$ steps the dimensionless excluded-volume parameter is $z\sim v\,n^{2}/R^{\,d}$ with $R=\sqrt n$, that is $z\sim v\,n^{(4-d)/2}$ ($n^2$ monomer pairs interacting with strength $v$ inside the volume $R^d=n^{d/2}$ the ideal chain occupies), so the interaction is relevant for $d<4$, marginal at $d=4$ (logarithms, $\nu=\tfrac12$) and irrelevant above. If a link to the trichotomy is wanted, make it the honest one: the same family of sums $\sum n^{-d/2}$ governs both questions, but the thresholds differ, $d=2$ for return to the origin and $d=4$ for excluded volume.
