1. [minor] statement, part (c) (line ~69): "The set of them attracts a two-sided neighbourhood of $\Gamma$ if $\lambda_u < \lambda_s$ and repels one if $\lambda_u > \lambda_s$" over-reaches. Every neighbourhood of $\Gamma$ contains points of the entry section on the far side of the local stable manifold ($u < 0$ in the proof's own coordinates); those leave the square through $u = -\delta$ and never return, so they are not attracted. What Step 4 actually proves - and what is true - is attraction (resp. repulsion) of a two-sided neighbourhood of the closed orbits themselves, i.e. of the interval $(0,u_1]$ on the section, from inside and from outside. The proof block states this correctly; only the statement's wording is loose. Fix: write "attracts a two-sided neighbourhood of the closed orbits" (or "of the annulus they lie in") instead of "of $\Gamma$". Not treated as blocking: the result proved is correct, the main assertions (existence, one-sidedness in $\mu$, the $[u_0,2u_0]$ localisation, the period law) are untouched, and the proof says the right thing.

The induced equation is $\dot\rho = 2\rho\,(\mu + \rho - \rho^2)$, not $\dot\rho = \mu + \rho - \rho^2$; the extra factor $2\rho$ is positive on $\rho > 0$, so zeros and their stability are unaffected, but "precisely" is too strong. Fix: "is the saddle-node normal form up to the positive factor $2\rho$, which changes neither the zeros nor their stability on $\rho>0$".
