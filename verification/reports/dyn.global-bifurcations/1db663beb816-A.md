## Findings

1. [blocking] statement "Three prototypes", part (c), and proof "(c) The logarithm at a homoclinic bifurcation", Step 4: the proof does not prove the part of (c) about existence of a *limit* cycle or about stability. The statement asserts "there is a limit cycle near $\Gamma$" - a limit cycle is an isolated closed orbit - and "Every closed orbit near $\Gamma$ is attracting if $\lambda_u<\lambda_s$ and repelling if $\lambda_u>\lambda_s$ - a stable limit cycle in the first case and an unstable one in the second". What Step 4 establishes is: the return map $R$ is continuous, increasing, maps $(0,u_1]$ into itself, satisfies $u_0 \le R(u) \le u_0 + u/2$ there, and therefore has at least one fixed point, every fixed point lying in $[u_0,2u_0]$, with the iterates of every point of $(0,u_1]$ converging to *some* fixed point. That is strictly weaker. An increasing continuous self-map of an interval may have a repelling fixed point $b$ (with $R(u)<u$ just below $b$ and $R(u)>u$ just above), and the two bounds do not exclude this inside $[u_0,2u_0]$: they force $R(u)\ge u_0$ and $R(u)\le u_0+u/2$, both of which are compatible with such a configuration for $u<2u_0$. So the proof gives neither isolation nor the stability of each closed orbit, and the unit's own parenthetical concedes the point - "That there is exactly one is true but needs more than the bounds used here: with a $C^1$ linearization at the saddle one computes $R'(u)=O(u^{\nu-1})\to 0$, so that $R$ is a contraction." A statement block may not assert what the proof block declines to prove. (The period conclusion is unaffected: Step 5 only needs $u_\ast \in [u_0,2u_0]$, which Step 4 does give.) Fix: either weaken (c) to match the proof - "there is a closed orbit near $\Gamma$; every closed orbit near $\Gamma$ enters the section at a distance comparable to $u_0(\mu)$, and the set of them attracts a two-sided neighbourhood when $\lambda_u<\lambda_s$" - or add the $C^1$ linearisation at the saddle (Hartman's theorem for planar $C^2$ fields) as a cited ingredient and derive uniqueness, isolation and stability from $R'(u)\to0$, which is the route the parenthetical already names.

2. [blocking] application "Hysteresis in a driven pendulum, and two kinds of neuron", third sentence of the first paragraph: "At strong damping the cycle dies when a fixed point appears on it, an infinite-period bifurcation; at weak damping it dies earlier, by running into the saddle, a homoclinic bifurcation." The direction is reversed. In Strogatz's §8.5 (pp. 270-272, and the stability diagram Fig. 8.5.10 on p. 272) the homoclinic destruction of the whirling cycle happens at $I_c < 1$, on a curve tangent to $I = 4\alpha/\pi$ as $\alpha \to 0$; at strong damping the cycle is destroyed instead at $I = 1$ by the infinite-period bifurcation. So as $I$ is lowered the weakly damped cycle survives *past* $I=1$ and dies **later**, at a lower drive, not earlier. The unit's own preceding sentence says exactly this - "lowering it again does not switch it back until a different, lower threshold" - so the paragraph contradicts itself as well as its source, and as written it would leave no bistable range at all at weak damping, which is the phenomenon the paragraph is about. Fix: "at weak damping the cycle survives below $I=1$ and dies later, at a lower drive $I_c<1$ (tangent to $4\alpha/\pi$ as $\alpha\to0$), by running into the saddle: a homoclinic bifurcation".

3. [minor] application, same paragraph: "The two thresholds are exactly the bifurcations of this unit." At weak damping the upper threshold - the switch-on as $I$ is raised past $1$ - is the saddle-node bifurcation of *fixed points* that destroys the rest state, which Strogatz lists on p. 272 as a third kind alongside the two bifurcations of cycles and which is not treated in this unit. Only the lower threshold is a bifurcation of cycles. Fix: "the lower threshold is one of the bifurcations of this unit; the upper one is the saddle-node of fixed points at $I=1$ that destroys the rest state".
