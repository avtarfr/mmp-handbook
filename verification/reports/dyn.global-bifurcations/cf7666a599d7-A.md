1. [blocking] statement, part (c) (line 64): "The set of them attracts a two-sided neighbourhood of $\Gamma$ if $\lambda_u < \lambda_s$ and repels one if $\lambda_u > \lambda_s$" is false as stated. The homoclinic loop $\Gamma$ has an inside and an outside, and only the inside is attracted. In the proof's own coordinates the entry edge $s = \delta$ meets the interior of the loop in $u > 0$ and its exterior in $u < 0$; Step 4 establishes that $R$ maps $(0,u_1]$ into itself with monotone convergence, which is a one-sided statement. A point with $u < 0$ is on the far side of the stable curve, crosses $N$ and leaves through the edge $u = -\delta$ along the other branch of the unstable manifold, and the proof says nothing about its return - generically it does not return near $\Gamma$ at all. The only exterior points that are captured are those whose transition image $u_0(\mu) + a\,s_{\rm out}$ is still positive, a layer of thickness $O(u_0) = O(|\mu-\mu_c|)$ that shrinks to nothing as $\mu \to \mu_c$, so there is no two-sided neighbourhood of $\Gamma$ of any fixed size. Checked numerically in the unit's own family $\dot x = y$, $\dot y = x - x^2 + \epsilon y(\alpha-x)$ at $\epsilon = 0.1$, $\alpha = \alpha_c + 10^{-3}$, run backwards in time so that the case is $\lambda_u < \lambda_s$ and the cycle attracts (DOP853, rtol $10^{-11}$). The captured outside layer is about $3\times10^{-4}$ wide, comparable to $|\mu - \mu_c| = 10^{-3}$ and not to the $O(1)$ size of $\Gamma$. Fix: say that the set of closed orbits attracts (resp. repels) a one-sided neighbourhood of $\Gamma$, namely a fixed-size neighbourhood on the side into which the unstable manifold returns - equivalently, that each closed orbit attracts a two-sided neighbourhood of *itself*, of width $O(|\mu-\mu_c|)$ on the outer side.
