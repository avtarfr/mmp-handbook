   Step 4 (line 214) is explicit in the other direction: the bounds give that the closed orbits
   "attract **as a set** the trajectories around them", and "that is the attraction asserted in
   (c), and it is exactly as much as these bounds give. They do not give isolation. An increasing

   whole interval of fixed points there. Uniqueness is nevertheless true, and with it isolation and

   The two cannot both stand. If $R$ has a repelling fixed point $u_r \in (u_0, 2u_0]$ - a
   configuration Step 4 names as not excluded - the closed orbit through $u_r$ repels on both
   sides and attracts no neighbourhood of itself at all; if the fixed-point set contains an
   interval, an orbit interior to it attracts nothing either. The attraction of an individual
   orbit is exactly the "stability of the single cycle" that Step 4 lists among the things the
   avoided ingredient would buy. The same sentence is also unqualified as to case: when
   $\lambda_u > \lambda_s$ the orbits repel, so "does attract" is wrong there on its face.
   Fix: say what the proof gives, e.g. "the attracted set contains a two-sided neighbourhood of
   each of these closed orbits, extending only $O(\lvert\mu-\mu_c\rvert)$ on the outer side and a
   fixed distance on the inner side" (which is what Step 4 and Step 2 do establish, the outer
   boundary being the stable manifold); or keep the present wording but attribute it to the
   uniqueness argument the unit declines to run, and drop it from the scope of "everything
   asserted above ... is proved without it".
