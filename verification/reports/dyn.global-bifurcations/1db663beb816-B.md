[blocking] definition "Local and global bifurcations of cycles", the infinite-period bullet (line 26): "at $\mu_c$ the orbit is no longer periodic, having become a fixed point together with **a pair of trajectories** connecting it to itself". There is one such trajectory, not two. At a saddle-node on an invariant cycle the invariant closed curve minus the single fixed point is one orbit, whose $\alpha$- and $\omega$-limit are both that point. Prototype (b) of this unit is the concrete witness: at $\mu = 1$ the flow on the unit circle is $\dot\theta = 1 - \sin\theta \ge 0$, vanishing only at $\theta = \pi/2$, so $\theta$ increases strictly monotonically on the rest of the circle and every non-fixed point of the circle lies on the single orbit running from $\pi/2$ round to $\pi/2 + 2\pi$. The claim also contradicts the unit's own definition of a homoclinic orbit two bullets later ("a trajectory that approaches the same fixed point as $t \to +\infty$ and as $t \to -\infty$"): by that definition there is exactly one here. The wrong count is ours, not Strogatz's - he says only that "a fixed point appears on the circle" (p. 262) and does not count connections - so this is our own material and it has to be right. As written it invites the reader to picture a figure-eight of two loops, which is not the infinite-period picture but closer to the post-bifurcation one, where the two fixed points are joined by two heteroclinic arcs. Fix: "a fixed point together with a single trajectory that leaves it and returns to it", or "a fixed point and one orbit homoclinic to it"; the "pair" belongs to the next sentence, after the fixed point splits in two.
