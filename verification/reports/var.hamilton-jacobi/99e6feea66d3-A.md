## Checked
Part (a), re-derived line by line: $\Psi(t,a)=(t,\mathsf{Q}(t,a))$ has Jacobian determinant $\det\mathsf{Q}_a \neq 0$, so with injectivity the inverse function theorem gives open $D$ and $C^1$ $\Psi^{-1}$; $\partial_t\sigma = L$ by the fundamental theorem of calculus; the $a_j$-differentiation under the integral sign is over a *fixed* interval with a $C^1$ integrand ($L \in C^2$, $\mathsf{Q} \in C^2$), so it is legitimate, and $\partial_{a_j}\partial_s\mathsf{Q}=\partial_s\partial_{a_j}\mathsf{Q}$ holds for $\mathsf{Q} \in C^2$; with $L_q = \partial_sP$ along each extremal the integrand is exactly $\partial_s(P\cdot\partial_{a_j}\mathsf{Q})$ ($P \in C^1$ because $L \in C^2$, $\mathsf{Q}\in C^2$), and the $t_0$ boundary term vanishes because $\mathsf{Q}(t_0,a)\equiv q_0$; invertibility of $\mathsf{Q}_a$ then forces $\nabla S = P$ and $\partial_tS = L - P\cdot\partial_t\mathsf{Q} = -H(t,q,P)$ by the definition of $H$ in var.canonical-equations together with $\partial_t\mathsf{Q} = \hat v(t,q,P)$ from (R). Part (b): differentiating the equation in $q_i$ and the momentum along the curve and subtracting gives $\dot p_i = -H_{q_i}$; $C^2$ of $S$ is what licenses both the differentiation and the exchange of mixed partials, and $\frac{d}{dt}S = \partial_tS + \nabla S\cdot\dot q = p\cdot\dot q - H$. Part (c): steps 7–9 are the two differentiations (in $\alpha_j$ of the equation, in $t$ of $\partial_{\alpha_j}S=\beta_j$) whose difference is $\sum_k S_{q_k\alpha_j}(\dot q_k - H_{p_k})=0$, cancelled by the nondegeneracy; step 10's implicit-function argument is correct ($\partial_\alpha S$ is $C^1$ since $S \in C^2$, and the $q$-Jacobian is the transpose of the mixed Hessian), and the appeal to ODE uniqueness is justified because $H \in C^2$ makes $(H_p,-H_q)$ locally Lipschitz. Every listed prerequisite is used (canonical equations and (R); inverse/implicit function theorem; differentiation under the integral sign; ODE uniqueness; characteristics in the second remark), and nothing outside them is invoked.
