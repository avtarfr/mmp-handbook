## Findings
1. [minor] summary (line 6) and the closing sentence of the statement (line 61): both say more than part (c) delivers. The summary says a complete integral "produces all solutions of the canonical equations"; the closing sentence says the family "contains every motion whose momentum at some time $t_\ast$ equals $\nabla S(t_\ast,q(t_\ast),\alpha)$ for some admissible $\alpha$". What part 2 and step 10 actually prove is local in two ways: the agreement holds only *near* $t_\ast$ (it comes from ODE uniqueness at $t_\ast$, and nothing shows the family member extends as far as the given motion), and the hypothesis is a real restriction, since $\alpha \mapsto \nabla S(t_\ast,q_\ast,\alpha)$ need only be a local diffeomorphism onto an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, not onto all of it. Gelfand and Fomin are equally loose ("constitute a general solution", p. 93), so this is not infidelity, but the unit's own statement is the sharper one and the summary should not outrun it. Fix: "produces, near each such time, every solution whose momentum it matches" in the summary, and "contains, near such a $t_\ast$, every motion ..." in the closing sentence.

[minor] statement (a) (line 47): in the display $\nabla S(t,q) = L_v\bigl(t,q,\partial_t\mathsf{Q}(t,a)\bigr)$ the label $a$ is unbound; it is $a = a(t,q)$, the inverse map of the definition block, as the parenthetical gloss says in words but the formula does not. Fix: write $a(t,q)$ in the display.
3. [minor] proof, step 1 (line 71): "The integrand is $C^1$ in $a$ ... so we may differentiate under the integral sign" names the wrong hypothesis. What the rule needs (and what holds here) is that the $a_j$-derivative of the integrand is continuous in $(s,a)$ jointly, which follows from $L \in C^2$ and $\mathsf{Q}\in C^2$ on the compact $s$-interval $[t_0,t]$. Fix: state the joint continuity.
