## Findings

1. [minor] statement, part 1 (line 57): "Equivalently, without assuming unitarity, ...". The second display is the primary identity (it is what the proof derives, and it holds for any matrices of the irreducible representations); the first follows from it only once the matrices are taken unitary, which the definition block has already stipulated. "Equivalently" is therefore slightly loose - the two are equivalent for unitary matrices, and the second is the more general statement. Fix: "The underlying identity, which needs no unitarity, is ...".

2. [minor] proof, part 4 (line 133): "the first step using linearity in the first slot and that the $m_\lambda$ are real integers". Linearity in the first slot alone gives $(\sum_\lambda m_\lambda \chi^{(\lambda)}, \chi^{(\mu)}) = \sum_\lambda m_\lambda (\chi^{(\lambda)},\chi^{(\mu)})$ for any complex $m_\lambda$; realness is not used there (it is used, harmlessly, in the $(\chi,\chi)$ line that follows and in part 5's $\overline{\chi^{(\lambda)}(e)} = n_\lambda$). Fix: drop the clause, or move it to the second display.

3. [minor] statement, part 6 and proof (lines 78, 194): half of part 6 ($r \le \rho$, and with it the second orthogonality relation) is taken from Hassani rather than proved. This is stated openly in the statement block and the locators are exact (Eqs. (24.21)-(24.27), pp. 741-742; Thm 24.5.2, p. 743 - I checked both, and Hassani's Eq. (24.27) is the column relation in the form given here, with $|G|/c_j$ in place of $|G|/c_i$, the same thing under $\delta_{ij}$). Worth noting only because the unit's `kind` is `theorem` and this is the one claim in it that the unit does not establish.
