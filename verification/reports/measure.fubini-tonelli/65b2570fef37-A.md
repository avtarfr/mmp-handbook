## Findings
1. [minor] definition (line 41): "Intersections of rectangles are rectangles, so the finite disjoint unions of rectangles form an algebra $\mathcal{A}$" — closure under intersection alone does not make the finite disjoint unions an algebra; one also needs that the complement of a rectangle is a finite disjoint union of rectangles (the "elementary family" condition, Folland Prop. 1.7, which Folland invokes at exactly this point on p. 64). The conclusion is right, the one-clause justification is not sufficient. Fix: add "and the complement of a rectangle is a finite disjoint union of rectangles", citing Folland Prop. 1.7.
2. [minor] definition (line 48) and remark (line 141): the outer-measure formula $(\mu\times\nu)(E) = \inf \sum_j \mu(A_j)\nu(B_j)$ is introduced as something Reed and Simon record "for $\sigma$-finite factors", but it is then the load-bearing step of the diagonal counterexample, where $\nu$ is counting measure on $[0,1]$ and is *not* $\sigma$-finite. The step is in fact legitimate — that infimum is by definition the Carathéodory outer measure generated by the premeasure $\pi$ on $\mathcal{A}$, so it needs no $\sigma$-finiteness — but as written the reader is given a $\sigma$-finite-only tool and then sees it used outside that range. Fix: in the definition block say that the infimum formula is simply the Carathéodory outer measure of $\pi$ and holds for any factors, and attribute to Reed and Simon only the uniqueness clause, which does need $\sigma$-finiteness.
3. [minor] proof "Sections: the monotone class argument", step 6 (line 117): "Let $j \to \infty$. Both integrands increase pointwise ..., so monotone convergence gives the identity in the limit." Monotone convergence handles the two right-hand sides; the left-hand side $(\mu\times\nu)(E\cap(X_j\times Y_j)) \to (\mu\times\nu)(E)$ is continuity of a measure from below, which is not named. Fix: name it, as step 3 does.
