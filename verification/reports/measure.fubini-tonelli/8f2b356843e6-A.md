## Findings
1. [minor] definition (line 54): the covering-formula bullet introduces "the outer measure of an arbitrary $E \subseteq X\times Y$" and then writes the display as $(\mu\times\nu)(E) = \inf\sum_j \mu(A_j)\nu(B_j)$. At that point $\mu\times\nu$ has been defined only on $\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}$, so for a general $E$ the left side is the induced outer measure, not $\mu\times\nu$. The mathematics is right (Folland p. 64 defines $\mu\times\nu$ as the restriction of that outer measure, so the two agree on $\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}$, which is all the diagonal counterexample needs, since $D$ is Borel). Fix: write $(\mu\times\nu)^{*}(E)$ in the display and add a half-sentence that it agrees with $\mu\times\nu$ on $\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}$.
2. [minor] remark (line 144): the diagonal counterexample uses $D \in \mathcal{B}_{[0,1]}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{[0,1]}$ without saying why — the theorem being contradicted applies only to product-measurable sets, so this is the hypothesis that must be seen to hold. Fix: one clause, that $\mathcal{B}_{[0,1]}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{[0,1]} = \mathcal{B}_{[0,1]^2}$ because $[0,1]$ is a separable metric space (Folland Prop. 1.5 / Cor. 1.6, p. 23, already in the citable range) and $D$ is closed.
3. [minor] proof "Sections: the monotone class argument", step 6 (line 116): "Step 5 applies to each restricted space" silently identifies $\mu\times\nu$ restricted to subsets of $X_j\times Y_j$ with the product of the restricted measures $\mu|_{X_j}$ and $\nu|_{Y_j}$. True, and Folland p. 67 elides it in the same way, but the handbook's own rule is to justify at the step. Fix: add "because the premeasure on rectangles inside $X_j\times Y_j$ is the same for both constructions".
4. [minor] example "Evaluating a double integral by Tonelli", step 2 (line 187): the interchange here is of three factors (counting measure on $\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ and two copies of Lebesgue measure), while the statement block gives Tonelli for two factors only. The extension to finitely many factors is in the cited range (Folland p. 65) and is listed in the front-matter `loc`, but is not invoked in the text. Fix: either cite the $n$-factor remark at this step, or present it as two successive two-factor applications.
5. [minor] example (line 202): "Nothing here is absolutely convergent by inspection" reads oddly for a non-negative integrand, where absolute convergence is just convergence — and the integral does converge, to $\pi^2/6$. The intended point is that finiteness is not visible in advance. Fix: "Nothing here is visibly finite in advance".
6. [minor] idea (line 100) against remark (line 144): the idea names the decreasing-intersection step as the place where $\sigma$-finiteness is consumed, while the remark correctly names two places (that step and the exhaustion in step 6). Not a contradiction, but the idea is the weaker description of the proof that follows. Fix: add "and again when the space is exhausted".
