## Findings

1. [minor] proof "The Poincare lemma", Step 0 (line 110): the step rests on "pullback along a diffeomorphism commutes with $d$", attributed by `:ref` to `geom.pullbacks-pushforwards`, which is not in `prereqs` (line 7). It cannot simply be added: in `content/map.yaml` `geom.pullbacks-pushforwards` (line 799) comes *after* this unit (line 777), so listing it would trip the map-order warning in `tools/check/graph.mjs`. Fix: rest the step instead on the naturality of $d$ with respect to pullback, which belongs to `geom.exterior-derivative` (already a prerequisite) — or, simpler, do the translation in coordinates, where $\tilde\omega_I(x) = \omega_I(x+c)$ and $\partial_j$ commutes with the shift, so no pullback machinery is needed at all.

2. [minor] proof, Step 1 (lines 126–132) and Step 3 (line 161): the monomial formula for $K$ is derived for an *increasing* multi-index and then stated "for arbitrary (not necessarily increasing) indices" without justification, and Step 3 applies it to the tuple $(j, i_1, \dots, i_p)$, which may also *repeat* an index when $j \in I$. Both cases are fine — the first display of Step 1 defines $K$ invariantly, so the coordinate formula follows by multilinearity for any tuple, and when $j = i_a$ the only two surviving terms are $b = 0$ and $b = a$, whose contributions are $x^{i_a}dx^I$ and $(-1)^a(-1)^{a-1}x^{i_a}dx^I$ and cancel, consistently with $dx^j \wedge dx^I = 0$ — but as written the reader has to supply this. Fix: one sentence after the monomial display saying both.

3. [minor] method "Finding a primitive", "Non-uniqueness" (line 216): "Any two primitives differ by a closed $(p-1)$-form" is right, but the next clause silently upgrades closed to exact ("for $p \ge 2$ it is the ... freedom $\eta \mapsto \eta + d\alpha$"). That form holds only because $U$ is star-shaped and $p - 1 \ge 1$, so that the lemma itself applies one degree down. Fix: say so, since on a general domain the difference is closed but need not be $d\alpha$ — which is exactly the point examples (a) and (b) make.

4. [minor] statement "The Poincare lemma", final paragraph (line 90): "the sphere $S^2$ is simply connected but carries a closed $2$-form that is not exact, as the second example below shows in the equivalent setting of $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$". The example proves nothing about $S^2$; transferring it needs homotopy invariance, which the unit only mentions later in the remark. The detour is also unnecessary: $V = \mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}$ is itself a region meeting Hassani's stated hypothesis (every closed curve in $V$ shrinks to a point in $V$) on which the conclusion fails, which is precisely the counterexample wanted. Fix: name $V$ rather than $S^2$ here, or defer the $S^2$ remark.

5. [minor] example "A closed 2-form on $\mathbb{R}^3$...", Step 4 (line 270): "These three numbers are exactly $(x^2, -2xy, xy)$" — they are functions of $(x,y,z)$, not numbers.
