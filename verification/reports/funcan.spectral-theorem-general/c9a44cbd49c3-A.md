[minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", Separability bullet (line 278): "What survives without separability is the multiplication form over a measure space that is merely semifinite" is true but weaker than what the argument actually delivers, and "semifinite" is a term the unit never defines and no listed prerequisite supplies. The cyclic decomposition of Step 6 needs separability only for the *countability* of the family; run by Zorn over an arbitrary index set it still writes $\mathcal{H}$ as $\bigoplus_{n\in I}L^2(\mathbb{R},d\mu_n)$ with every $\mu_n$ finite, so the measure space is a disjoint union of finite measure spaces (strictly localizable), which is strictly stronger than semifinite. Semifinite is the correct *conclusion* - if $\mu(E)=\infty$ then some $\mu_n(E\cap M_n)$ is positive and finite - so the sentence is not wrong, only imprecise, and it errs on the safe side rather than overclaiming. Fix: say what survives is the multiplication form over a disjoint union of finite measure spaces - semifinite, and in general not even $\sigma$-finite, as the same counterexample shows (a $\sigma$-finite $M=\bigcup_k M_k$ cannot carry uncountably many disjoint positive-measure sets, since each would meet some $M_k$ in positive measure). The failure of $\sigma$-finiteness is the sharper and more informative statement, and it is the one the counterexample already proves.

Hypotheses remark. Checked each bullet against the proof. The $\ell^{2}([0,1])$ counterexample in the Separability bullet is genuine: $Ae_t=te_t$ is bounded and self-adjoint, each $t\in[0,1]$ is an eigenvalue with eigenspace $\mathbb{C}e_t$; the $\lambda$-eigenspace of $T_f$ really is $\{\varphi : \varphi=0$ a.e. off $f^{-1}(\lambda)\}\cong L^{2}(f^{-1}(\lambda),\mu)$ (such a $\varphi$ automatically lies in $\mathcal{D}(T_f)$), one-dimensionality forces $f^{-1}(t)$ to be an atom modulo null sets, and uncountably many disjoint positive-measure sets do force $\mu(M)=\infty$ - for some $k$, uncountably many have measure $\ge1/k$.
