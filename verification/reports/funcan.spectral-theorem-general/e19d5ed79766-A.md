3. [blocking] statement, part D (line 89) and Step 14 (line 233): part D claims $(A\psi,\varphi) = \int\lambda\,d(P_\lambda\psi,\varphi)$ "for $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(A)$ and $\varphi \in \mathcal{H}$", and the whole justification offered is "polarisation gives the version with two vectors". Polarisation applied to $(A\cdot,\cdot)$ needs $\psi + i^k\varphi \in \mathcal{D}(A)$ for $k=0,1,2,3$, i.e. $\varphi \in \mathcal{D}(A)$, which is exactly the hypothesis Reed and Simon state (p. 263: "for $\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)$"). The stated claim is true but is a strengthening of the source, so it is ours to prove: one needs $\lvert(P_\Omega\psi,\varphi)\rvert \le \mu_\psi(\Omega)^{1/2}\mu_\varphi(\Omega)^{1/2}$, whence $\int\lvert\lambda\rvert\,d\lvert(P_\lambda\psi,\varphi)\rvert \le \lVert A\psi\rVert\,\lVert\varphi\rVert$, giving absolute convergence for every $\varphi\in\mathcal{H}$ and continuity in $\varphi$, so that the identity extends from the dense set $\mathcal{D}(A)$. The same point is used again, unremarked, in the injectivity argument of Step 14 ("for every $\varphi\in\mathcal{H}$"). Fix: add that estimate, or restrict $\varphi$ to $\mathcal{D}(A)$ as the source does.
