## Findings
1. [blocking] :::remark[Where the hypotheses are used], bullet "Boundedness of $h$ in part C" (line 247): the claim "$\mathcal{D}_h = \mathcal{H}$ if and only if $h$ is bounded on the spectrum" is false in the "only if" direction. $\mathcal{D}_h$ depends on $h$ only through its class modulo sets null for every spectral measure. Counterexample: $A = $ multiplication by $x$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R},dx)$, so $\sigma(A)=\mathbb{R}$ and every $\mu_\psi = \lvert\psi\rvert^2dx$ is absolutely continuous; let $h = 1$ off $\mathbb{Q}$ and $h(p/q) = q$. Then $h$ is Borel and unbounded on $\sigma(A)$, yet $\int\lvert h\rvert^2 d\mu_\psi = \lVert\psi\rVert^2 < \infty$ for every $\psi$, so $\mathcal{D}_h = \mathcal{H}$. This is an `original` block, so it is our own material. Fix: say "essentially bounded on the spectrum", i.e. bounded off a set that is null for every spectral measure $\mu_\psi$ - the same essential-vs-literal distinction the unit is careful about in part B and in the essential-range definition.

2. [blocking] :::proof[Part A for bounded operators], Step 3, the closing parenthesis (line 139): "The real form of the theorem, Folland's Thm 4.45, p. 139, would not do: he shows on p. 140 that it fails for complex algebras not closed under conjugation." Both halves are wrong here. (a) The real form *would* do in this situation: $\sigma(A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ is a compact Hausdorff space, Thm 4.45 applied to the real polynomials gives density in $C(\sigma(A),\mathbb{R})$, and splitting a complex $h$ into real and imaginary parts gives density of the complex polynomials in $C(\sigma(A))$. (b) Folland's counterexample concerns a complex algebra that is *not* conjugation-closed (polynomials in $z$ on the unit circle in $\mathbb{C}$), whereas the sentence two lines earlier in the same step establishes that the algebra here *is* conjugation-closed, so the counterexample does not bear on it. Fix: drop the claim that the real form would not do, or replace it with the accurate remark that the complex form is used because it applies directly to the complex algebra $\mathcal{A}$, the real form needing the extra real/imaginary-part step.
