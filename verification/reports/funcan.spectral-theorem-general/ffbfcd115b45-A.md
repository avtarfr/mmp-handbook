2. [minor] idea (line 123): "Polynomials are dense in $C(\sigma(A))$ by Weierstrass". The algebra at issue is the *complex* polynomials, and Step 3 correctly invokes the complex Stone-Weierstrass theorem (Folland Thm 4.51); the classical Weierstrass theorem gives only the real case. Fix: say "by Stone-Weierstrass" in the idea, as Step 3 does.
3. [minor] proof "Part A for bounded operators", Step 2 (line 131): the reason given for $(\bar P P)(A)$ being self-adjoint is that "$\bar P P$ takes real values on $\sigma(A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}$". At this point only the polynomial calculus exists, and $\sigma(A)$ may be finite, so real-valuedness *on $\sigma(A)$* does not yet determine the operator. The operative fact is that $\bar P P$ has real coefficients (its $k$th coefficient $\sum_{i+j=k}\bar a_i a_j$ is its own conjugate), whence $(\bar PP)(A)^{*} = (\overline{\bar PP})(A) = (\bar PP)(A)$. The conclusion is right. Fix: justify by the real coefficients of $\bar P P$.
4. [minor] proof "Part A in general", Step 12 (line 216): "$(f-\lambda)^{-1}$ is bounded and multiplication by it inverts $T_f-\lambda$" skips the check that $T_{(f-\lambda)^{-1}}$ lands in $\mathcal{D}(T_f)$. It is one line - $|f(f-\lambda)^{-1}\varphi| \le (1+|\lambda|/\varepsilon)|\varphi|$ - but it is the only place the two-sidedness of the inverse is at issue. Fix: add the estimate.
5. [minor] statement, part E (line 106): "$N = 1$ is possible exactly when $A$ has a cyclic vector". The "if" direction is Steps 5-7. The "only if" direction is left implicit in the preceding sentence ("the measures $\mu_n$ are the spectral measures of a family of cyclic vectors"), which does deliver it for the representation the construction produces, but not for an arbitrary representation with $N=1$. It is true in general - in $L^2(\mathbb{R},d\mu_1)$ with $A$ multiplication by $\lambda$ the constant $1$ is cyclic, in the polynomial sense when $\mu_1$ is compactly supported and in the $C_\infty$ sense otherwise. Fix: a half-sentence noting that the constant function is cyclic in the $N=1$ representation.
6. [minor] application (line 338): the sentence cites ":cite[Reed and Simon]{loc=§VII.2, Example 4, p. 229}" for two observations. The Fourier-transform one is Example 4 on p. 229; the "rigorous form of the physicists' Dirac notation" one is the paragraph straddling pp. 227-228. Also, Example 4 says the Fourier transform is *one example of* a spectral representation, whereas the unit writes "*is* the spectral representation of $-i\,d/dx$" - harmless in context ("put the point bluntly"), but spectral representations are not unique. Fix: split the locator, or cite "pp. 227-229".
