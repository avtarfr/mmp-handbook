1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", bullet **Separability** (line 278): "Dropping it costs the countability of the direct sum in part E, not the theorem." It also costs the **finiteness of $\mu$**, which part A states in bold and which the very next bullet of the same remark calls "part of the statement as Reed and Simon give it". Counterexample: let $\mathcal{H} = \ell^2([0,1])$ with orthonormal basis $\{e_t\}_{t\in[0,1]}$ and let $A$ be the bounded self-adjoint operator $Ae_t = t\,e_t$. Suppose $U:\mathcal{H}\to L^2(M,\mu)$ were unitary with $UAU^{-1}=T_f$, $f$ real and $\mu$ finite. Each $t\in[0,1]$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ with a one-dimensional eigenspace; the $\lambda$-eigenspace of $T_f$ is $L^2\bigl(f^{-1}(\lambda),\mu\bigr)$, so each $E_t := f^{-1}(t)$ has $\mu(E_t)>0$ and, being one-dimensional, is an atom. The $E_t$ are pairwise disjoint and uncountably many, so for some $n$ uncountably many satisfy $\mu(E_t)>1/n$, giving $\mu(M)=\infty$ — a contradiction. So no finite measure space represents this $A$. The failure is already visible inside the unit: Step 7 obtains $\mu(M)\le\tfrac13$ precisely by summing $\lVert\varphi_n\rVert^2=4^{-n}$ over a **countable** family, and an uncountable family of positive masses is not summable; the bullet therefore also contradicts Step 7 and the adjacent "Finiteness of $\mu$" bullet. Fix: "Dropping it costs the countability of the direct sum in part E, and with it the finiteness of $\mu$ in part A — an uncountable disjoint union of copies of $\mathbb{R}$ carries no finite measure with every piece of positive mass. What survives is the multiplication-operator form over a general (non-finite) measure space, together with parts B, C and D."
