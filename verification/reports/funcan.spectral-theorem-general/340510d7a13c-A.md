## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Part A in general…", Step 13, last sentence (the uniqueness argument for part C): "Uniqueness: properties 1 and 2 fix $h(A)$ for polynomials and hence, by density and property 2, for continuous $h$; property 4 then fixes it on the smallest class of functions closed under bounded pointwise limits containing $C(\mathbb{R})$, which is all bounded Borel functions." Four things are wrong. (i) A non-constant polynomial is not a bounded Borel function on $\mathbb{R}$, so the map whose uniqueness is at issue is not even defined on polynomials. (ii) Properties 1 and 2 mention only the algebra structure and the norm; they contain no reference to $A$ whatsoever, so they cannot fix $h(A)$ for any non-constant $h$ — the map $h \mapsto h(B)$ for any other self-adjoint $B$ satisfies them equally. The only property linking the calculus to $A$ is property 3 (and, derivatively, property 4). (iii) Polynomials are not dense in the bounded continuous functions on $\mathbb{R}$ in sup norm, so "by density" does not get from polynomials to continuous $h$ (this works for bounded $A$ on the compact set $\sigma(A)$, which is not the situation of part C). (iv) $C(\mathbb{R})$ contains unbounded functions, so it is not a subclass of the domain of the calculus either. Fix: run the standard argument. Let $r(x) = (x+i)^{-1}$, a bounded continuous function. If $\phi$ satisfies 1–4, then for $h_n(x) = x\chi_{[-n,n]}(x)$ one has $r\cdot(h_n+i) \to 1$ pointwise, boundedly, so by 1 and 4, $\phi(r)\phi(h_n+i) \to I$ strongly; and by 3, $\phi(h_n+i)\psi \to (A+i)\psi$ for $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(A)$, so $\phi(r)(A+i)\psi = \psi$, i.e. $\phi(r) = (A+i)^{-1}$ is forced. By the $*$-homomorphism property $\phi$ is then forced on the $*$-algebra generated by $r$ and $1$, which by Stone-Weierstrass on the one-point compactification is dense in $C(\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\})$, hence (property 2) on all of it; property 4 and a monotone-class argument then force it on all bounded Borel $h$.
2. [blocking] note "The spectral-family form, and the two sources", first bullet (line "Both numbers are the same, since $h(A)$ is normal and $\mu_\psi$ is a positive measure"): the stated reason is false. Within a single convention, $(\psi, h(A)\psi)$ and $(h(A)\psi,\psi)$ are complex conjugates of each other, and for a complex-valued bounded Borel $h$ they are genuinely different numbers — $\int h\,d\mu_\psi$ need not be real, and normality of $h(A)$ has no bearing on it. What is true, and is the point the bullet is trying to make, is that Reed and Simon's $(\psi,h(A)\psi)$ *in their convention* is the same number as our $(h(A)\psi,\psi)$ *in ours*, because the two conventions are related by swapping the slots and $\mu_\psi$ is defined identically in both (it is a squared norm). Fix: replace the reason with that one, and drop "since $h(A)$ is normal".
3. [blocking] frontmatter `prereqs`: three results that the proofs use are missing. Step 6 uses Zorn's lemma to get the maximal family of orthogonal cyclic subspaces (`found.zorn-lemma`, which exists and which the sibling unit `funcan.orthonormal-bases` does list for the same use, and which is not even `:ref`-ed here); Step 8 uses the monotone convergence theorem (`measure.monotone-convergence-fatou`, `:ref`-ed in the text but absent from `prereqs`); Step 9 uses the closed graph theorem (`funcan.uniform-boundedness-open-mapping`, likewise `:ref`-ed but absent). Fix: add all three to `prereqs`.
4. [minor] statement, part A, last sentence: "The measure space and $f$ may in addition be chosen so that $f \in L^p(M,d\mu)$ for every $p$ with $1 \le p < \infty$." This clause is not part of Reed & Simon's Theorem VIII.4 (p. 260–261); in their Proposition 2 (p. 260) $f \in L^p$ is a *hypothesis*, not a conclusion. The claim is true and the unit does prove it (the reweighting at the end of Step 11), but it is our addition to a block whose `src` points only at the theorem. Fix: mark it as ours, e.g. "one can arrange in addition …", or cite it to the step that proves it.
5. [minor] proof, Step 9, the displayed identity: the middle expression should be $\bigl((A-i)(A-i)^{-1}\psi,\varphi\bigr)$, not $\bigl((A-i)^{-1}(A-i)\psi,\varphi\bigr)$. Both happen to equal $(\psi,\varphi)$ for $\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)$, so no conclusion is affected, but the factors as printed are in the wrong order for the manipulation being performed (moving $A+i$ across the inner product turns it into $A-i$ acting on the first entry).
6. [minor] proof, Step 1: the factorisation argument for "$\subseteq$" assumes $P$ non-constant. The constant case is trivial but should be excluded explicitly.
7. [minor] proof, Steps 3, 4 and 10: Stone-Weierstrass, Riesz-Markov and the spectral theorem for bounded *normal* operators are each used as named external results with no `:ref` and no unit behind them. Step 10 in particular ("one runs Steps 1–7 with the two-variable polynomial algebra") hides the commuting spectral mapping theorem that makes the isometry of Step 2 work for $P(B,C)$. Reed & Simon are equally brief here (p. 261, "the easy extension … is outlined in Problems 3, 4, and 5 of Chapter VII"), so this is acceptable as a cited reduction, but the unit should say that it is importing the bounded-normal case rather than implying Steps 1–7 transfer verbatim.
