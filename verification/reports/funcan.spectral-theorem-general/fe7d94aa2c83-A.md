## Findings
1. [source] statement, part A (line 72) and proof Step 8 (line 166): the converse clause carries Reed and Simon's hypothesis that $\mu$ be finite (Prop. 1, p. 259), but Step 8's argument - truncation for density, then the monotone-convergence bound on $\mathcal{D}(T_f^{*})$ - never uses it and works on an arbitrary measure space. Finiteness is genuinely needed only for the second half of their Prop. 1, $\sigma(T_f)$ = essential range, which the unit splits off into Step 12. Faithful reporting of the source, so not a failure; recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs` against reed-simon-1980 §VIII.3, Prop. 1, p. 259. Our text is not inconsistent: the "Finiteness of $\mu$" remark already puts the use in Steps 11 and 12 only.
2. [minor] idea (line 122): "Polynomials are dense in $C(\sigma(A))$ by Weierstrass". The algebra at issue is the *complex* polynomials, and Step 3 correctly invokes the complex Stone-Weierstrass theorem (Folland Thm 4.51); the classical Weierstrass theorem gives only the real case. Fix: say "by Stone-Weierstrass" in the idea, as Step 3 does.
3. [minor] proof "Part A for bounded operators", Step 2 (line 136): the reason given for $(\bar P P)(A)$ being self-adjoint is that "$\bar P P$ takes real values on $\sigma(A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}$". At this point only the polynomial calculus exists and $\sigma(A)$ may be finite, so real-valuedness *on $\sigma(A)$* does not yet pin the operator down. The operative fact is that $\bar P P$ has real coefficients - its $k$th coefficient $\sum_{i+j=k}\bar a_i a_j$ is its own conjugate - whence $(\bar PP)(A)^{*} = (\overline{\bar PP})(A) = (\bar PP)(A)$. The conclusion is right. Fix: justify by the real coefficients of $\bar P P$.
4. [minor] proof "Part A in general", Step 12 (line 207): "$(f-\lambda)^{-1}$ is bounded and multiplication by it inverts $T_f-\lambda$" skips the check that $T_{(f-\lambda)^{-1}}$ lands in $\mathcal{D}(T_f)$, which is what makes the inverse two-sided. It is one line: $\lvert f(f-\lambda)^{-1}\varphi\rvert \le (1+\lvert\lambda\rvert/\varepsilon)\lvert\varphi\rvert$. Fix: add the estimate.
5. [minor] statement, part E (line 94): two small gaps in an otherwise careful paragraph. (a) "$N = 1$ is possible exactly when $A$ has a cyclic vector": the "if" direction is Steps 5-7 and the "only if" is immediate for the representation the construction produces, but not as stated for an arbitrary representation with $N=1$. It is true in general - in $L^2(\mathbb{R},d\mu_1)$ with $A$ multiplication by $\lambda$ the constant function $1$ is cyclic, in the polynomial sense when $\mu_1$ is compactly supported and in the $C_\infty$ sense otherwise. (b) The new aside showing $\bigcap_n \mathcal{D}(A^{n})$ is dense is correct - $\mu_{\psi_k}$ is carried by $[-k,k]$ with mass $\le\lVert\psi\rVert^2$, so $\int\lambda^{2n}d\mu_{\psi_k}\le k^{2n}\lVert\psi\rVert^2$, and $\psi_k\to\psi$ by p.v.m. axiom 3 - but it needs $\mathcal{D}(A^{n}) = \{\psi:\int\lambda^{2n}d\mu_\psi<\infty\}$ for every $n$, whereas part D states that description only for $n=1$. Fix: a half-sentence for each.
6. [minor] application (line 342): the single locator ":cite[Reed and Simon]{loc=§VII.2, Example 4, p. 229}" is attached to two observations. The Fourier-transform one is Example 4 on p. 229; the "rigorous form of the physicists' Dirac notation" one is the paragraph straddling pp. 227-228. Also Example 4 says the Fourier transform is *one example of* a spectral representation, where the unit writes "*is* the spectral representation of $-i\,d/dx$" - harmless under "put the point bluntly", but spectral representations are not unique. Fix: widen the locator to pp. 227-229, or split it.
