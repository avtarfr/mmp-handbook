## Findings
1. [blocking] proof, Step 2 (line 152) and Step 3 (line 168): "The function $f$ is twice continuously differentiable, so Itô's formula in the general form applies" is false under the hypotheses of the statement it is proving. The statement (line 74) assumes only that $\alpha$ is continuous, so $\Phi_t = \exp(-\int_0^t\alpha)$ is $C^1$ but need not be $C^2$, and $f(t,x) = \Phi_t x$ then has $f_{tt} = \Phi''_t x$ undefined. The Itô formula being invoked is the general form of :ref[prob.ito-calculus], which requires (quoting the source, Theorem (13.9.2), p. 610) "$f$ twice continuously differentiable on $[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}$", i.e. genuinely $C^2$ — not $C^{1,2}$. The same objection applies verbatim to $g(t,y) = \Phi_t^{-1}y$ in Step 3, whose $g_{tt} = \alpha'\Phi_t^{-1}y + \alpha^2\Phi_t^{-1}y$ likewise needs $\alpha'$. Nothing else in the unit repairs this: the handbook nowhere states a $C^{1,2}$ version of Itô's formula. The conclusion (5)-(7) is correct; only the justification of the central step is not. Fix: either add "$\alpha$ continuously differentiable" to the hypotheses of the linear statement (the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck application has $\alpha \equiv -\beta$ constant, so nothing in the unit is lost), or add to :ref[prob.ito-calculus] the standard $C^{1,2}$ form of (5) and cite that form here instead.
