## Findings
1. [source] remark "Ito versus Stratonovich", the table and the note after it (lines ~243-250):
   Grimmett and Stirzaker, p. 604, write that for the midpoint evaluation "the answer is the more
   familiar $W_t^2$". The correct midpoint limit is $\tfrac12W_t^2$; the book has dropped the
   factor $\tfrac12$, as its own Exercises (13.7.3)(c) and (d) on the facing column show, and as the
   two flanking values $\tfrac12(W_t^2\mp t)$ it gives require. The unit prints $\tfrac12W_t^2$,
   proves it in mean square, states that it is departing from the book's prose and why, and cites
   both the prose and the exercise. That is the right handling of an internal conflict in a source,
   and the mathematics the unit prints is correct, so this is not a fault of the unit. Recorded with
   `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.
2. [minor] statement "The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process", item 4 (line ~140): the block opens "Fix
   $\beta>0$ and $\sigma>0$", so "this one is the only one" reads as a claim about the process with
   those particular parameters, which is false - every pair $(\beta,\sigma)$ gives a stationary
   Gaussian Markov process with continuous autocovariance. Grimmett and Stirzaker's Problem
   (13.12.4) has the same looseness ("such a process is the only stationary Gaussian Markov process
   with continuous autocovariance function"), and the intended meaning is clearly "the only ones are
   the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes". Fix: "every stationary Gaussian Markov process with a
   continuous autocovariance function is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, for some $\beta>0$ and
   $\sigma>0$".
3. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", the entry
   "$\int_0^t\Phi_s^2\delta(s)^2\,ds<\infty$" (line 230): this is listed among the hypotheses, but
   it is not one - under the statement's standing assumptions ($\delta$ continuous, $\alpha\in C^1$
   so $\Phi$ continuous) it holds automatically on every bounded interval, as Step 3 itself observes.
   Fix: present it as a condition that the hypotheses deliver, and say what would have to be weakened
   (a merely locally integrable or unbounded $\delta$) for it to bite.
4. [minor] remark "Ito versus Stratonovich", the dictionary (line ~255): $h$ is introduced as a
   function of the state alone, $h(X_s)$ with $h$ continuously differentiable, but it is then
   evaluated as $(h'\sigma)(t_j,X_{t_j})$ and the conclusion is written
   $\tfrac12\int_0^t(h'\sigma)(s,X_s)\,ds$, as though $h'$ took two arguments. Fix: write
   $h'(X_{t_j})\sigma(t_j,X_{t_j})$ and $\tfrac12\int_0^t h'(X_s)\sigma(s,X_s)\,ds$; the same
   correction applies to the Stratonovich conversion $\mu=\tilde\mu+\tfrac12\sigma_x\sigma$, which
   is correct but is reached through the ambiguous notation.
