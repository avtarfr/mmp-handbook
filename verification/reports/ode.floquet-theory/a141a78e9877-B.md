2. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used...", closing sentence of the trap bullet (line 168): "here those of $A(t)$ turn once per period" is loose. The eigenvalues of $A(t)$ are the non-real constants $(-1 \pm i\sqrt7)/4$, so $A(t)$ has no real eigendirection to turn. The exact fact behind the sentence is that $A(t) = R(-t)\,B\,R(-t)^{-1}$ with the constant $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 1 \\ -1 & -1\end{pmatrix}$ and $R(\theta)$ the rotation by $\theta$ (I verified this symbolically: $R(-t)^{\mathsf T}A(t)R(-t)$ simplifies to $B$ exactly), so the frame in which $A$ is constant turns by $-t$ and has made a half-turn, $R(-\pi) = -I$, after the period $T = \pi$. Fix: say that the frame diagonalising $A(t)$ rotates at unit rate and returns to itself (up to sign) after one period, rather than speaking of eigenvectors turning.
3. [minor] automated check: `warn ode.floquet-theory: coddington-1989 pp. 46, 110, 116 never mention floque/theory`. This is a false positive of the keyword heuristic. Coddington never uses the word "Floquet" anywhere (I searched the whole work: no matches), but all three pages treat linear equations with periodic coefficients, which is this unit's topic, and each cited sub-exercise is exactly where the locator says it is. No fix needed; recorded so the warn is not re-raised.
