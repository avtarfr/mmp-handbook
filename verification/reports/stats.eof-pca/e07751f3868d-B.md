## Findings
1. [minor] remark, bullet "Rank is limited by the smaller dimension" (line 139): "centring puts the rows in the hyperplane $\sum_t x_t = 0$" is loose. What centring gives is the single linear relation $\sum_t x_t = 0$ among the $n$ rows; equivalently each *column* of $X$ lies in the hyperplane $\{y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \sum_t y_t = 0\}$. As written it can be read as each row lying in a hyperplane of $\mathbb{R}^p$, which is false in general. The conclusion drawn (rank $\le n-1 = 49$) is correct. Fix: "centring imposes the relation $\sum_t x_t = 0$ on the rows, so they are linearly dependent".
2. [minor] remark, bullet "Scaling is a modelling choice" (line 140): the sentence "For a field in one unit this choice is usually inappropriate, but a weight proportional to the square root of the area each grid point represents is needed, or dense high-latitude grid points are over-counted" is garbled by "but ... or". The content agrees with method step 2 ($\sqrt{w_j}$ on column $j$) and is correct. Fix: "...is usually inappropriate, though a weight proportional to the square root of the area ... is still needed, or else dense high-latitude grid points are over-counted."
3. [minor] definition (line 55): "neither uses normality" is slightly too strong for Theorem (4.9.5)(b) as the source states it. That theorem identifies the *parameter* $V$ of the $N(\mu,V)$ density with the matrix of covariances, and its proof does use the density (G&S defer it to characteristic functions, Example (5.8.6)). What is genuinely distribution-free is the reading $V = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)'(X-\mu)]$ displayed on p. 133, and Exercise (4.9.3). The unit is otherwise careful and explicit that the source states both in the normal section, so nothing is misattributed. Fix: restrict the "no normality needed" remark to the second-moment identity $V_{jk} = \operatorname{cov}(X_j,X_k)$ and to Exercise (4.9.3).
