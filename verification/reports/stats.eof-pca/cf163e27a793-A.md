## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet (line 129): the two numbers in the centring illustration are computed with different divisors, and the percentage drawn from them is wrong under either convention. The bullet frames the comparison as centred versus uncentred $X^{\mathsf T}X/(n-1)$; for the single variable taking the values $10$ and $12$ ($n=2$, $n-1=1$) that gives sample variance $2$ and uncentred second moment $244$, not $122$. The figure $122$ is $\tfrac12\sum x_t^2$, i.e. the divisor $n$, while the variance $2$ uses $n-1$; the unit has just insisted on $n-1$ (definition block, and the "$n-1$ versus $n$" bullet below). The claimed $98\%$ follows only from mixing the two: with a consistent divisor the mean-dominated part is $242/244 = 121/122 = 99.2\%$ either way. Fix: use $244$ (or restate both with divisor $n$, as $1$ and $122$) and change $98\%$ to about $99\%$.
2. [minor] statement, part 3 (line 73): "singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_p \ge 0$" and $z_k = \sigma_k u_k$ silently assume $n \ge p$. In the case the remark stresses ($p = 10^4$, $n = 50$) there are only $n$ singular vectors $u_k$, so $u_k$ is undefined for $k>n$ even though $\lambda_k$ and $e_k=v_k$ still make sense (and $z_k = Xv_k = 0$ there). Fix: say that $\sigma_k := 0$ and $z_k := 0$ for $k > \min(n,p)$, or state part 3 for $k \le \min(n,p)$.
3. [minor] statement, part 1 (line 65): the uniqueness clause "whenever $\lambda_{k-1} > \lambda_k > \lambda_{k+1}$" has no reading at $k=p$; the proof supplies the convention $\lambda_0 = +\infty$ but nothing for $\lambda_{p+1}$. Fix: add "read $\lambda_{p+1} = -\infty$" alongside $\lambda_0 = +\infty$.
4. [minor] application block (line 218): the leading EOF of *hemispheric* sea-level pressure is conventionally the Arctic Oscillation / Northern Annular Mode; the published NAO index is PC1 of the Atlantic sector (or a station-pair difference). As written the sentence attaches the wrong name to the stated domain. Fix: either restrict the domain to the North Atlantic sector or name the hemispheric mode the AO/NAM and mention the NAO as its Atlantic-sector counterpart.
