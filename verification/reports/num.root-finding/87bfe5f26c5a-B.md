## Findings
1. [minor] frontmatter `summary` (line 6): "the error recursions that give them orders 1, 2 and 1.618" assigns order 1 to bisection, but under this unit's own definition (|e_{n+1}|/|e_n|^p -> C in (0,infty)) bisection has no order and no asymptotic error constant: the unit's own example records e_5 = -8.015e-4 and e_6 = 1.4824e-2, a ratio of 18.5. The body is careful about this (the definition block says bisection "is not of that form", and the example says only the bound is monotone); the summary is the one loose place. Fix: say "orders 2 and 1.618, against bisection's guaranteed halving of the bracket", or "a guaranteed bit per step".

2. [minor] `statement` (b) (line 71): "The order is therefore 2 whenever f''(xi) != 0, and better than 2 when f''(xi) = 0." With only f in C^2, f''(xi) = 0 gives e_{n+1}/e_n^2 -> 0, i.e. convergence faster than quadratic, but not an order p > 2 in the sense of the definition block (that needs more smoothness, e.g. f in C^3 and f'''(xi) != 0 for order 3). Fix: "faster than quadratic when f''(xi) = 0, though the definition above then assigns no order without further smoothness".

3. [minor] `statement` (b) and (c) (lines 69, 81) and `proof` (c) step 3 (line 151): the displayed limits e_{n+1}/e_n^2 and e_{n+1}/(e_n e_{n-1}) presuppose e_n != 0, and in (c) "is well defined" fails in the degenerate case where an iterate lands exactly on xi: then x_{n+1} = x_n, f(x_n) - f(x_{n-1}) = 0 at the next step, and the mean value theorem of step 3 has no interval to work on. Fix: add the usual proviso "unless some f(x_n) = 0, in which case xi has been found exactly and the iteration stops" to (c), and "with x_n != xi for all n" to the two limits, as the definition block already requires.

4. [minor] `proof` block src (line 120): attributed to "rhb-2006:§27.1.3-27.1.4 and §27.2, pp. 990-994". Those pages do treat these topics, so the citation is not wrong, but they contain no proof of bisection's convergence, prove the second order of Newton-Raphson by the fixed-point route F'(xi) = f f''/[f']^2 = 0 (p. 993) rather than by the Lagrange remainder used here, and say nothing about the secant method. The route of parts (a), (b) and (c) is ours. Fix: label the proof `original` and keep an inline :cite to §27.1.3-27.1.4 for the constructions, or narrow the src to the constructions it actually adapts. (This is over-attribution, not under-attribution, so not blocking.)

No [source] items: both books are reported faithfully and I found nothing in the cited pages I would dispute.
