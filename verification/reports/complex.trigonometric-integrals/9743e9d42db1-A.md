## Findings
1. [minor] method "Evaluating a trigonometric integral by residues", step 4: the text says that a
   pole on the circle means "the hypothesis that the denominator of $R$ does not vanish on
   $s^2+c^2=1$ has failed". The implication is true, but that is not the unit's hypothesis: the
   definition block assumes only that $\theta\mapsto R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ is finite, and the
   remark's first bullet goes out of its way to say that non-vanishing of $Q$ on the circle is
   "strictly stronger than needed". Fix: name the stated hypothesis instead -- "the hypothesis that
   $R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ is finite at every real $\theta$ has failed (in particular $Q$ vanishes
   there without being cancelled)".
2. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet, and the parallel sentence in the
   definition block: in the example $R(s,c)=c^2/(1-s)$ the expression is literally $0/0$ at
   $(s,c)=(1,0)$, i.e. at $\theta=\pi/2$, so $\theta\mapsto R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ is not
   *defined* there, let alone finite, until the common factor is cancelled. The point being made is
   right and the resulting $F(z)=-(z+i)^2/(2z^2)$ really does have no pole on $|z|=1$ (I reduced it;
   its residue at the double pole $z=0$ is $-i$, giving $2\pi$ as claimed). Fix: state the hypothesis
   on the reduced function -- "$R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, after cancellation of common factors,
   extends to a finite continuous function of $\theta$" -- or say in the bullet that the quotient is
   first reduced on the circle.
3. [minor] application, tight-binding bullet: "the denominator becomes
   $-t\bigl(z^{2}-(E/t)z+1\bigr)$" drops a factor $1/z$; in fact
   $E-t(z+z^{-1}) = -t\bigl(z^{2}-(E/t)z+1\bigr)/z$. Nothing downstream changes (the roots, their
   product $1$, and the on/off-circle dichotomy are all properties of the quadratic), but as written
   the equality is false. Fix: write "the denominator becomes $-t(z^2-(E/t)z+1)/z$" or "clearing
   $z$, the relevant factor is the palindromic quadratic $z^2-(E/t)z+1$".
