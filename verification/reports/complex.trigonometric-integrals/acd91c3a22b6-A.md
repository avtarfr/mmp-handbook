Example 1: $F(z) = (z^2-1)^2/(2iz^2(z^2-4z+1))$ is the correct reduction, the poles are $z=0$ (order 2) and $2\pm\sqrt3$ with product $1$, $\operatorname{Res}_0 F = 4/(2i) = -2i$ via $(N/D)'(0) = 4$, $\operatorname{Res}_{z_-}F = 12/(-4i\sqrt3) = i\sqrt3$ after the exact cancellation of $(7-4\sqrt3)$, and $I = 2\pi(2-\sqrt3)$; quadrature of the original integral gives $1.6835744289538659$ against $2\pi(2-\sqrt3) = 1.6835744289538659$. Example 2: the $1/z$ from $d\theta$ does cancel, $\operatorname{Res}_{z_-}F = -4i\cdot(-(z_-+z_+))/(z_--z_+)^3 = -2i/(3\sqrt3)$, and $J = 4\sqrt3\pi/9$; quadrature gives $2.4183991523122905$ against $4\sqrt3\pi/9 = 2.4183991523122905$, and the printed bracket $2\pi/9 = 0.698 < J < 2\pi = 6.283$ is right. Example 3: $5+3\cos\theta = (3z^2+10z+3)/(2z)$, $3z^2+10z+3 = 3(z+3)(z+1/3)$ with root product $1$, residue $1/8$ at $-1/3$, $K = \pi/4$; quadrature gives $0.7853981633974483 = \pi/4$, and the independent closed form $\pi/\sqrt{a^2-b^2} = \pi/\sqrt{16}$ agrees. I also checked the two general claims in the remark: roots of a palindromic quadratic have product $1$, so exactly one is inside unless both are on the circle (both moduli would have to be $1$), and an integrand odd under $\theta\mapsto-\theta$ integrates to zero over a full period by periodicity.
