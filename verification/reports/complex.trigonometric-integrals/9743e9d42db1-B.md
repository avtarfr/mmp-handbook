## Findings
1. [minor] application, "Lattice and Green's-function sums" (line 250): "the denominator becomes $-t\bigl(z^{2} - (E/t)z + 1\bigr)$" drops a factor $1/z$. From $E - 2t\cos\theta = E - t(z+z^{-1})$ one gets $-t\bigl(z^2 - (E/t)z + 1\bigr)/z$; the omitted $z^{-1}$ is exactly the factor that, together with the $1/(iz)$ from $d\theta$, decides the pole structure at the origin, which is the point step 3 of the method insists on. The physics drawn from the bullet (palindromic quadratic, reciprocal pair, band condition, $i0$ prescription) is unaffected. Fix: write "$E-2t\cos\theta = -t(z^2-(E/t)z+1)/z$, whose numerator is a palindromic quadratic".
2. [minor] definition "The substitution" (line 33) and remark (line 75): the hypothesis "$\theta \mapsto R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ is finite for every real $\theta$" is applied, in the remark's own example $R(s,c) = c^2/(1-s)$, at the point $(s,c) = (1,0)$ where the quotient is literally $0/0$ rather than finite. The example's point is correct and its value ($2\pi$) is right — I confirmed that after cancellation $F(z) = -(z+i)^2/(2z^2)$ has its only pole at the origin — but the wording of the hypothesis does not quite admit the example it is illustrated with. Fix: phrase the hypothesis as "$R$ restricted to the circle $s^2+c^2=1$ agrees with a function finite at every point of it", or as "$\theta\mapsto R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ is bounded and extends continuously to all real $\theta$".
