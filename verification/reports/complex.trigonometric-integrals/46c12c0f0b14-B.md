2. [minor] `summary` (frontmatter, line 6): "is then the sum of the residues inside, times $2\pi$". The residues *of what* is not said, and the nearest antecedent is the contour integral just named, whose integrand the unit calls $F(z) = R(\cdot)/(iz)$ -- for which the factor is $2\pi i$, as method step 5 correctly states. The $2\pi$ is right only for the residues of $R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)/z$, which is Arfken's convention (his Eq. (11.94) is $I=(-i)2\pi i\sum$ residues of $f/z$). Not blocking because the source-sanctioned reading makes it true, but it should not be left ambiguous. Fix: "is then $2\pi$ times the sum of the residues of $R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)/z$ inside the circle".
3. [minor] `application`, third bullet (line ~230): "Whether the relevant pole lies inside or outside the circle decides whether $G$ decays or oscillates, i.e. whether $E$ lies inside the band." The poles of $1/(E-2t\cos\theta)$ in $z$ satisfy $tz^2-Ez+t=0$, a reciprocal pair, so for $\lvert E\rvert>2\lvert t\rvert$ one is inside and one outside, while for $\lvert E\rvert<2\lvert t\rvert$ **both lie on** the unit circle (and with the retarded prescription $E+i0$ they split, again one inside). So in every case one pole is inside, and the stated dichotomy does not separate the two regimes. Fix: "Whether the poles lie *on* the unit circle ($E$ in the band, so $G$ oscillates and the integral needs an $E+i0$ prescription) or off it ($E$ outside the band, so $G$ decays exponentially) is what decides the behaviour."
