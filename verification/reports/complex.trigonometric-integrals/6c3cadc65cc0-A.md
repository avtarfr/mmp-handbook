## Findings

1. [blocking] remark, first bullet (line 75): two false claims, both in a `provenance=original` block, so they are our own.

   (a) "The denominator of $R$ non-vanishing on the circle **is exactly what keeps** the poles of $F$ off $\lvert z\rvert = 1$ and the original integral convergent" asserts an equivalence. Only one direction holds. The hypothesis is sufficient (the definition block proves that) but not necessary.

   (b) "Riley, Hobson and Bence impose it directly in the $z$-plane, requiring the transformed integrand to have finitely many poles inside the circle and none on it (p. 861); **the two formulations agree**." They do not agree: RHB's condition is strictly weaker.

   Counterexample to both, verified: take $R(s,c) = c^2/(1-s)$. As a rational function of two variables it is in lowest terms ($\gcd(c^2, 1-s) = 1$ in $\mathbb{C}[s,c]$), and its denominator *does* vanish at $(s,c) = (1,0)$, a point of the real circle -- so the unit's hypothesis fails. Yet on the circle $c^2 = (1-s)(1+s)$, so $R(\sin\theta,\cos\theta) = 1 + \sin\theta$, finite for every real $\theta$; the integral converges, $\int_0^{2\pi}(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta = 2\pi$; and sympy gives the transformed integrand as $F(z) = -(z+i)^2/(2z^2)$, whose only pole is at the origin, so RHB's condition holds and the method works unchanged (residue $-i$ at $z=0$, $2\pi i \cdot (-i) = 2\pi$, correct). So the unit's hypothesis can fail while $F$ has no pole on the circle and the integral converges, which refutes (a), and RHB's formulation admits an integrand the unit's rejects, which refutes (b).

   Fix: say that the condition is sufficient, not necessary -- "non-vanishing of $Q$ on the circle is enough to keep the poles of $F$ off $\lvert z\rvert = 1$" -- and state the relation to RHB correctly: RHB's condition on $F$ is weaker, because a zero of $Q$ on the circle may be cancelled by a zero of $P$ once $s,c$ are restricted to $s^2+c^2=1$, and then $F$ has no pole there after all. Alternatively adopt the weaker hypothesis throughout (next finding), after which the two formulations really are equivalent.

That is a one-line argument, it is the source's hypothesis, and it makes the remark's claim of agreement with RHB true.
