## Findings
1. [source] method (line 240): Arfken's prose recipe on p. 236 says to count the negative
   metric-tensor diagonal entries among the differentials of the *complementary* form, while
   every worked case on pp. 236-237 counts them among the differentials of the form being
   starred; the two prescriptions differ by the global factor $(-1)^\nu$. I confirmed both
   halves on the pages: the prose does say "the number of differentials in $\omega_0$", and
   the $\star 1$ and $\star(dt\wedge dx_1)$ examples do count inside $\omega$. The unit follows
   the examples, which are the ones consistent with Eq. (4.82), with Hassani Def. 26.6.1 and
   with $\alpha\wedge\star\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\mu$, and it flags the wording
   explicitly. Already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (line 210); no new entry
   added, to avoid a duplicate. Not a failure.
2. [minor] definition "The Hodge star" (line 84): "The same characterisation is used verbatim
   for indefinite metrics by Hassani and by Arfken". Hassani does state the characterisation
   (Theorem 26.6.4, p. 822) for a general inner product space with index $\nu$, but Arfken
   never writes $\alpha\wedge\star\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\mu$ anywhere on pp. 235-240
   -- he defines $\star$ by the complement-plus-two-signs recipe, as the unit's own method
   block says at line 232. The sentence is defensible if "characterisation" is read loosely as
   "construction", but as written it attributes to Arfken a definition he does not use.
   Fix: "...is used for indefinite metrics by Hassani (Theorem 26.6.4), and Arfken tabulates
   the Minkowski case from his own recipe; the only thing that changes is...".
3. [minor] example, Step 4 (line 356): "the $dy\wedge dz$ term contributing nothing because it
   shares $dz$ with the $dt\wedge dz$ term" explains only one of the two products that die.
   $(2\,dy\wedge dz)\wedge(5\,dx\wedge dy)$ also vanishes, because it repeats $dy$. A reader
   redoing the expansion of $F\wedge F$ meets both.
   Fix: "...because it repeats $dz$ against the $dt\wedge dz$ term and $dy$ against the
   $dx\wedge dy$ term".
4. [minor] note "What the star is for" (line 27), labelled `original`: the sentence
   "$\Lambda^p$ and $\Lambda^{n-p}$ ... have the same dimension $\binom np=\binom n{n-p}$, so
   they are isomorphic" is the opening move of Hassani's §26.6, p. 820. The surrounding
   framing (the one-way ladder, no Laplacian, the eight-scalar-equations payoff) is genuinely
   our own and the shared content is a binomial identity, so I do not read this as following a
   book's statement or proof route; but a `src` to hassani-2013 §26.6, p. 820 would cost
   nothing and would remove the question.
