## Findings
1. [minor] statement, part (d) (line 133): the reason given for $\delta\circ\delta=0$ is "$d\circ d=0$ and $\star$ is invertible by (b)". Invertibility of $\star$ is not what makes the two inner stars collapse; what is needed is that $\star\star$ is a *scalar* multiple of the identity, which is exactly what (b) supplies, so that $\star d\star\star d\star = \pm\,\star d\,d\star = 0$. The pointer to (b) is right, the word is wrong. Fix: "as $d\circ d=0$ and $\star\star$ is $\pm\mathrm{id}$ by (b)" (this is also how Hassani justifies it, Definition 28.6.4, p. 900).
2. [minor] remark "Where each hypothesis is used", index bullet (line 225): "It enters only through $\epsilon_I\epsilon_J = (-1)^\nu$ in Step 3 of the first proof" is too strong for the unit as a whole. $\nu$ also appears explicitly in the definition of $\delta$ (the factor $(-1)^{\nu+1}$, line ~100) and in $\star\mu = (-1)^\nu$ and $\langle\mu,\mu\rangle = (-1)^\nu$ in the definition blocks. The statement is true of the proof of (a)-(b), where the unit already says it. Fix: restrict the claim to the proof of the $\star\star$ rule, and note that it reappears as the explicit normalising factor in $\delta$.
3. [minor] prereqs (line 7): two results are *used*, not merely gestured at, in the application but are not listed: the Poincaré lemma (line 480, to pass from $dF=0$ to $F=dA$) and the Dirac delta (line 470, to read $\rho$ as $q\delta^3$ and apply Stokes distributionally). Both are reached only by :ref. geom.poincare-lemma sits one line later than this unit in map.yaml, but sibling units in this part (geom.stokes-forms, geom.exterior-derivative) do list later units such as geom.smooth-manifolds and geom.partition-of-unity as prerequisites, so forward prerequisites are the house style. Fix: add geom.poincare-lemma and vcalc.dirac-delta-basics to prereqs. (The other :refs — vcalc.green-identities, vcalc.vector-identities, var.noether-theorem, green.wave-heat-green-functions — are genuine signposts and correctly left out.)
4. [source] hassani-2013, Theorem 28.6.6, p. 901 (unit line 125): the book's only integrability hypothesis is that $\alpha\wedge\star\beta$ have compact support, and the unit reproduces it. That hypothesis makes the *difference* of the two integrands compactly supported, since it is $d(\alpha\wedge\star\beta)$, but neither $\int_M\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle\mu$ nor $\int_M\langle\alpha,\delta\beta\rangle\mu$ need converge separately; the usual hypothesis is compact support of $\alpha$ or of $\beta$ (Lee's Problem 16-22 instead takes $M$ compact without boundary, which the unit also records). Faithful report of the source, so not blocking; recorded in verification/discrepancies.md.
