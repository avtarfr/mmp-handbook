## Findings

1. [blocking] example "The star of an electromagnetic field, and the two invariants", Step 1 (lines 302–308) and proof "The translation", Step 3 (line 404): the duality statement has the wrong sign. The unit's own (correct) general formula
   $\star F = E_x\,dy\wedge dz - E_y\,dx\wedge dz + E_z\,dx\wedge dy + B_x\,dt\wedge dx + B_y\,dt\wedge dy + B_z\,dt\wedge dz$
   is the Faraday 2-form of the pair $(E', B') = (-B,\, E)$, not of $(B,\,-E)$: substituting $E\mapsto B$, $B\mapsto -E$ into $F = -E_x dt\wedge dx - \dots + B_x dy\wedge dz$ gives exactly $-\star F$. The numerical claim is therefore also false: with $E' = (2,0,5)$ and $B' = (0,0,-3)$ the Faraday form is $-2\,dt\wedge dx - 5\,dt\wedge dz - 3\,dx\wedge dy$, whereas the unit computed (correctly) $\star F = 3\,dx\wedge dy + 5\,dt\wedge dz + 2\,dt\wedge dx$. The same error is repeated in Step 3 of the translation proof ("the same pattern as Step 1 with $E\mapsto B$, $B\mapsto -E$"); the substitution that actually carries $dF$'s coefficients $(\nabla\times E + \partial_t B,\ \nabla\cdot B)$ to $d\star F$'s coefficients $(\partial_t E - \nabla\times B,\ \nabla\cdot E)$ is $E\mapsto -B$, $B\mapsto E$. This is `original` material and it contradicts the unit's own Minkowski table and its own displayed $\star F$. Fix: replace "$E' = B$, $B' = -E$" by "$E' = -B = (-2,0,-5)$, $B' = E = (0,0,3)$" and "$F$ with $E\mapsto B$ and $B\mapsto -E$" by "$F$ with $E\mapsto -B$ and $B\mapsto E$" in both places (note $\star$ is a quarter turn in the direction opposite to the one claimed; $\star\star = -1$ on $2$-forms is consistent with either, so the sign must be read off the table).

2. [minor] remark "Where each hypothesis is used", bullet "The index $\nu$" (line 215): "would flip the sign of $\star\star$ on $p$-forms of every even degree" is true but misleadingly partial. Omitting $(-1)^\nu$ multiplies $\star\star$ by $(-1)^\nu = -1$ in Minkowski space in *every* degree, odd as well as even. Fix: "in every degree".

3. [minor] remark "Conventions, and what changes in SI units", item 1 (line 426): "$\star F$ reversed in sign on 1-forms" — $F$ is a $2$-form, so the clause as written is incoherent. What is true is that passing to $(-,+,+,+)$ with the same $\mu$ gives $\star' = (-1)^p\star$ on $p$-forms, so $\star$ reverses on $1$- and $3$-forms and is unchanged on $2$-forms; in particular $\star F$ itself is *unchanged*. Fix: drop the $F$ and say "$\star$ reversed in sign on $1$-forms (and on $3$-forms), unchanged on $2$-forms, so $\star F$ is unaffected". The sentence would also read better with the three clauses not strung together by "hence ... but ...".

4. [minor] proof "Part (c)", Step 1 (line 182): the sign computation writes $(-1)^{-p^2}$. A negative exponent on $-1$ is harmless but unnecessary; $(-1)^{\nu+1}(-1)^{\nu}(-1)^{n(p+2)}(-1)^{p(n-p)} = -(-1)^{2np+2n}(-1)^{p^2}\cdot(-1)^{-2p^2}$ is easier to present as "$(-1)^{n(p+2)+p(n-p)} = (-1)^{2np+2n-p^2} = (-1)^{p^2} = (-1)^p$". The result $\star\delta\beta = -(-1)^p d\star\beta$ is correct and matches Hassani p. 901.
