## Findings

1. [blocking] example "The star of an electromagnetic field, and the two invariants", Step 1 (lines 302 and 308): the duality is identified with the wrong sign. The computed $\star F$ is right, but the reading off of its fields is not. With the unit's own conventions ($F = -E_x\,dt\wedge dx - \dots + B_z\,dx\wedge dy - B_y\,dx\wedge dz + B_x\,dy\wedge dz$, signature $(+,-,-,-)$, $\mu = dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$), the displayed general formula
$\star F = E_x\,dy\wedge dz - E_y\,dx\wedge dz + E_z\,dx\wedge dy + B_x\,dt\wedge dx + B_y\,dt\wedge dy + B_z\,dt\wedge dz$
is $F(E',B')$ with $E' = -B$ and $B' = +E$, not $E' = B$, $B' = -E$. Matching coefficients: $-E'_x\,dt\wedge dx = B_x\,dt\wedge dx$ gives $E'_x = -B_x$, and $B'_z\,dx\wedge dy = E_z\,dx\wedge dy$ gives $B'_z = E_z$. So the numbers asserted for this field, "$E' = B = (2,0,5)$ and $B' = -E = (0,0,-3)$", are both wrong in sign: $\star F = 3\,dx\wedge dy + 5\,dt\wedge dz + 2\,dt\wedge dx$ corresponds to $E' = (-2,0,-5)$ and $B' = (0,0,3)$. The fields stated would give $-\star F$. Fix: write "$E' = -B = (-2,0,-5)$ and $B' = E = (0,0,3)$", and in the following sentence "which is $F$ with $E \mapsto -B$ and $B \mapsto E$".

2. [blocking] proof "The translation", Step 3 (line 404): the same substitution is stated backwards, and here it is checkable against the two displayed coefficient formulas in the same proof. Applying $E \mapsto B$, $B \mapsto -E$ to Step 1's coefficients $(\nabla\times E + \partial_t B)$ and $\nabla\cdot B$ gives $(\nabla\times B - \partial_t E)$ and $-\nabla\cdot E$, which is the *negative* of Step 3's $(\partial_t E - \nabla\times B)$ and $\nabla\cdot E$. The substitution that works is $E \mapsto -B$, $B \mapsto E$. Fix: "the same pattern as Step 1 with $E \mapsto -B$, $B \mapsto E$", consistently with the corrected example.

3. [minor] definition "The Hodge star" (line 76): "Two immediate consequences, both in Lee's Problem 16-18(d)" is followed by three displayed formulas, and only $\star 1 = \mu$ / $\star f = f\mu$ are in Lee's 16-18(d) (p. 438, which states just $\star f = f\,dV_g$). $\star\mu = (-1)^\nu$ is not there; in Lee's Riemannian setting it is 16-18(e) with $k=0$, and in the indefinite case it is the unit's own one-line derivation given immediately after. Fix: attribute only $\star f = f\mu$ to 16-18(d) and present $\star\mu = (-1)^\nu$ as derived here, and make the count agree with the number of displays.

4. [minor] remark "Where each hypothesis is used", the index bullet (line 215): "would flip the sign of $\star\star$ on $p$-forms of every even degree" is true but misleadingly partial. Dropping $(-1)^\nu$ in Minkowski space multiplies $\star\star$ by $-1$ in *every* degree, odd as well as even ($\star\star = -\mathrm{id}$ for $p$ even and $+\mathrm{id}$ for $p$ odd; both flip). Fix: "in every degree".

5. [minor] remark "Conventions, and what changes in SI units", item 1 (line 426): "$(-1)^\nu$ unchanged in even dimensions but $\star\star$ unchanged on $2$-forms and $\star F$ reversed in sign on $1$-forms" is garbled twice over: "but" joins two statements that both say "unchanged", and $F$ is a 2-form, so "$\star F$ ... on $1$-forms" has no referent. The intended (and correct) claim is that $\star$ itself reverses sign on 1-forms when every $\epsilon_i$ is flipped, while $\star$ on 2-forms, and hence $\star F$, is unchanged. Fix: rewrite as "$(-1)^\nu$ is unchanged in even dimensions, so $\star\star$ is unchanged on $2$-forms; $\star$ itself reverses sign on $1$-forms, while $\star F$ is unchanged."

6. [minor] remark "Where each hypothesis is used", orientability bullet (line 214): "in which $\star$ occurs an even number of times or occurs twice" says the same thing twice. Fix: delete the second clause.
