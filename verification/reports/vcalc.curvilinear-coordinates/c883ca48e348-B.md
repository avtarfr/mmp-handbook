[minor] example (line 192): the two products are introduced as "$h_uF_u = -\tfrac12 v(u^2+v^2)$ and $h_vF_v = \tfrac12 u(u^2+v^2)$" and then substituted straight into the divergence formula, which actually calls for $h_2h_3a_1 = h_vh_zF_u$ and $h_3h_1a_2 = h_zh_uF_v$. The numbers are right only because $h_u=h_v$ and $h_z=1$ here, so the two sets of combinations coincide; as written the labels are the curl's combinations, used silently in the divergence. Fix: note that $h_vh_zF_u = h_uF_u$ and $h_zh_uF_v = h_vF_v$ in this system, or display the divergence's combinations separately.
2. [minor] definition (line 31): "Both of our sources allow the conditions to break down on a few exceptional points and curves". Riley, Hobson and Bence state this explicitly (p. 364, "except perhaps at or on certain isolated points or lines"), but the block's `src` for Arfken is pp. 183–184, which says nothing of the kind; the nearest Arfken support is on p. 187 ("For $\rho = 0$, $\varphi$ is not well defined"), inside the frontmatter page range but outside this block's locator. Fix: extend the block's Arfken locator to p. 187, or attribute the clause to Riley, Hobson and Bence alone.
3. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used" (line 137): "swapping two coordinates, say $u_1 \leftrightarrow u_2$, reverses the sign of the determinant and hence of $\nabla\times\mathbf{a}$". Relabelling coordinates cannot change the physical curl; what reverses is the value the determinant formula returns, which is why the formula fails in a left-handed labelling. Fix: "...reverses the sign of the determinant, so the formula no longer returns $\nabla\times\mathbf{a}$".
4. [minor] example step 4 (line 179): $\lvert dw^2/2 \,/\, dw\rvert$ parses as $\lvert (dw)^2/(2\,dw)\rvert$; write $\lvert d(w^2/2)/dw\rvert = \lvert w\rvert$.
