## Findings

1. [blocking] application (line 199): the separation claim is false as written. Substituting
   $\Phi = U(u)V(v)e^{ikz}$ into $\nabla^2\Phi + k^2\Phi = 0$ makes $\Phi_{zz} = -k^2\Phi$ cancel the
   $+k^2\Phi$ term exactly, leaving only the transverse part
   $\frac{1}{u^2+v^2}\bigl(U''V + UV''\bigr) = 0$, i.e. $U'' = \lambda U$, $V'' = -\lambda V$:
   exponentials and trigonometric functions, *not* Weber's parabolic cylinder equation. The
   $u^2 + v^2$ that is supposed to produce the parabolic-cylinder terms disappears with the $k^2$,
   so nothing in this substitution ever reaches a Fresnel integral. I confirmed the cancellation
   symbolically (sympy): with $e^{ikz}$ the separated equation is
   `U''/U + V''/V = 0`; with a general $e^{ik_zz}$ it is
   `U''/U + V''/V + (k^2 - k_z^2)(u^2+v^2) = 0`.
   Fix: the straight-edge problem is two-dimensional, so drop the $z$-factor and take
   $\Phi = U(u)V(v)$, which gives $U'' + (k^2u^2 - \lambda)U = 0$ and
   $V'' + (k^2v^2 + \lambda)V = 0$ — two parabolic cylinder equations, as claimed. Equivalently,
   keep a propagating factor $e^{ik_zz}$ and say that the transverse wavenumber
   $k_\perp^2 = k^2 - k_z^2$ must be non-zero, replacing $k^2$ by $k_\perp^2$ in the two equations.
   The sentence about $H$ "factoring as a sum" should also be rephrased: $H = u^2+v^2$ *is* a sum,
   and it is that additive form which lets the equation separate after multiplying through by $H$.

2. [minor] remark (line 137) against proof (line 105): the bullet says right-handed labelling "is
   used only for the curl", but proof step 3 opens "use right-handedness and then step 2 twice" and
   relies on $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_1 = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_2\times\hat{\mathbf{e}}_3$. The bullet's
   *conclusion* is right — the divergence formula is handedness-independent, because in a
   left-handed frame the sign appears twice ($a_1\hat{\mathbf{e}}_1 = -(a_1h_2h_3)\nabla u_2\times\nabla u_3$
   and $\mathbf{B} = -\hat{\mathbf{e}}_1/(h_2h_3)$) and cancels — but as phrased it contradicts the
   proof. Fix: say that the divergence proof uses the labelling as a convenience and the sign
   cancels, so only the *curl formula* depends on it. Relatedly, bullet 1 (line 135) credits
   $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_1 = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_2\times\hat{\mathbf{e}}_3$ to orthogonality alone; it needs
   orthonormality **and** right-handedness.

3. [minor] proof, step 3 (line 109): "this is where $\mathbf{r}$ being $C^2$ is used, since it makes
   the $u_j$ twice differentiable" skips a step. The definition assumes only a $C^1$ inverse, so
   $u_j \in C^2$ does not follow from $\mathbf{r}\in C^2$ by itself; it follows from the inverse
   function theorem, whose hypothesis holds here because the columns of $D\mathbf{r}$ are mutually
   orthogonal and non-zero ($h_i > 0$), hence independent, so $\det D\mathbf{r}\neq 0$ and
   $\mathbf{r}$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism. The claim is true; only the justification is missing.
   Fix: name the inverse function theorem and the non-vanishing Jacobian, or assume a $C^2$ inverse
   in the definition.
