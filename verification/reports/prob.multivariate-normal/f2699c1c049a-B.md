2. [minor] remark, bullet "Normality of every linear combination is the right hypothesis" (line 194): the second half does not support the first. The sentence claims that checking $X_1$, $X_2$ and $X_1+X_2$ for normality is not enough, then offers "the example above fails it at $a=(1,1)^{T}$ or $a=(1,-1)^{T}$ depending on the cut-off". For the Grimmett-Stirzaker example the sum $X+Y$ equals $2X$ on $\{|X|<a\}$ and $0$ on $\{|X|\ge a\}$, so it has an atom of mass $\mathbb{P}(|X|\ge a)>0$ at the origin and is non-normal for **every** finite $a>0$; likewise $X-Y$. So the example never is an instance in which $X_1+X_2$ is normal, and "depending on the cut-off" is not how the two directions split. The headline claim is true (counterexamples with all three marginals normal do exist), but this example does not witness it. Fix: either drop the "depending on the cut-off" clause and say plainly that this example already fails at $a=(1,1)^{T}$ and at $a=(1,-1)^{T}$, or replace the first sentence with "checking finitely many directions is never enough".
