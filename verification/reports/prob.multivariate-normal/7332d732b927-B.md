The unit computes $\mathbb{P}(X_1\le3\mid X_2=0,X_3=3)=\Phi(0.744208)=0.771625$ against the unconditional $\Phi(1)=0.841345$, then says "knowing the other two coordinates has made the large value of $X_1$ *more* surprising, because it has raised the expected value of $X_1$ less than it has shrunk its spread". Both halves are wrong. (i) The conditional tail is $\mathbb{P}(X_1>3\mid\cdot)=0.228375$ against the unconditional $0.158655$, so $X_1$ exceeding $3$ is about $1.44$ times *more* likely once the other coordinates are known: the value $3$ has become **less** surprising, not more. Equivalently the standardised distance falls from $z=1$ to $z=0.744208$. (ii) The reason given would, if true, push $z$ up, not down. Fix: say that conditioning has made the value $3$ less surprising, because the mean rose by $0.8$ while the standard deviation only fell from $2$ to $1.6125$, so the mean shift dominates and $z$ drops from $1$ to $0.744$.
