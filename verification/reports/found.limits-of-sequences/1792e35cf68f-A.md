## Findings

[minor] definition "Supremum, monotone sequences, limsup and liminf" (line 48): "They exist for every real sequence, since $k \mapsto \sup_{n \ge k} x_n$ is non-increasing and $k \mapsto \inf_{n \ge k} x_n$ is non-decreasing." The monotonicity of the two auxiliary sequences is not what makes $\limsup$ and $\liminf$ exist; existence is the sentence immediately before, that every subset of $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ has a supremum and an infimum. Fix: give that as the reason, and keep the monotonicity as a separate observation (it is what the later proof of the convergence criterion actually uses).
2. [minor] example "A convergent sequence that no monotonicity argument reaches" (line 155, title): the title overclaims. The odd-index terms of $x_{n+1} = 1/(1+x_n)$ decrease ($1, \tfrac23, \tfrac58, \tfrac{13}{21}, \dots$) and the even-index terms increase ($\tfrac12, \tfrac35, \tfrac{8}{13}, \dots$), so monotone convergence does apply to each subsequence; only the sequence itself is non-monotone. The body's claim (line 162, "they fall and rise alternately, so monotone convergence has nothing to say") is correct as it stands. Fix: retitle, e.g. "A convergent sequence that monotone convergence does not reach directly".
