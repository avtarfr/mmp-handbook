## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Elementary properties" (line 95): the bold label of claim 3 reads "Each non-negative function induces a measure", but the claim and its proof (line 131, "each simple function induces a measure") only cover simple $\varphi$. The general $L^{+}$ statement is true (Folland leaves it as Exercise 14, p. 52) but is neither stated precisely nor proved here, so label and content disagree. Fix: rename the label "Each non-negative simple function induces a measure", or state the claim for $f \in \mathcal{L}^{+}$ and cite Folland Exercise 14.
2. [minor] note "Two words that are not synonyms…" (line 85): "a function with $\int f = +\infty$ is here called measurable and non-negative, never integrable" is false as a blanket statement under the unit's own stage 3. Counterexample built from the unit's definitions: $f = x\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)} - \mathbf{1}_{[-1,0]}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ has $\int f^{+} = \infty$, $\int f^{-} = 1$, so $\int f = +\infty$ while $f$ is not non-negative. Fix: "a function with $\int f = +\infty$ is here said to have an integral but not to be integrable" (dropping "non-negative").
3. [minor] remark, bullet "Enlarging the class of integrands is not the main gain" (line 190): Folland p. 58 lists the two advantages as (i) the convergence theorems and (ii) *a wider class of integrable functions*; completeness of the function spaces appears there as the "crucial effect" of (ii), not as the second item of the list. The unit's substance is faithful to the page, the attribution of the list is not. Fix: "Folland p. 58 names the convergence theorems first, and says that the point of the wider class is the completeness of the resulting function spaces".
4. [minor] proof "Comparison with the Riemann integral", step 4 (line 177): "By construction $g_{P_k} \le f \le G_{P_k}$" fails at the single point $x = a$, where every $G_P$ and $g_P$ built from the half-open intervals $(t_{j-1},t_j]$ of step 1 vanishes while $f(a)$ is arbitrary. Nothing downstream breaks, since $\{a\}$ is null and step 4 only needs $g \le f \le G$ a.e. Fix: add "off the single point $a$" (or "a.e.") to the sandwich.
5. [minor] proof "Comparison with the Riemann integral", step 2 (line 167): "partitions realising both suprema to within $1/k$" — one of the two quantities in the preceding sentence is an infimum ($\inf_P S_P f$). Fix: "realising the infimum and the supremum to within $1/k$".
