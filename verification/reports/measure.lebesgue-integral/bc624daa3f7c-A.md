## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Comparison with the Riemann integral", Step 2 (line 167): the sequence of partitions constructed there is not nested, so the claim "each $P_k$ refines $P_{k-1}$" is false, and with it the claims of Step 3 that "$g_{P_k}$ increases with $k$ and $G_{P_k}$ decreases" and that $G = \lim_k G_{P_k}$, $g = \lim_k g_{P_k}$ exist pointwise. As defined, $P_k$ is the common refinement of the first $k$ chosen partitions **together with the uniform partition into $k$ pieces**; $P_{k-1}$ contains the uniform partition into $k-1$ pieces, whose points are in general not in $P_k$. Concretely on $[a,b]=[0,1]$: $P_2 \supseteq \{0,\tfrac12,1\}$ and $P_3 \supseteq \{0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1\}$, and unless one of the chosen partitions happens to contain $\tfrac12$, $P_3$ does not contain $\tfrac12$, so $P_3$ does not refine $P_2$. Folland (p. 57) states the nesting as a property of the sequence he chooses ("each of which includes the preceding one"), so this is our own construction, not the source's. Fix: build the sequence recursively — let $P_1$ be the common refinement of the first chosen pair with the one-piece partition, and $P_k$ the common refinement of $P_{k-1}$, the $k$th chosen pair, and the uniform partition into $k$ pieces. Nesting is then automatic and the mesh still tends to $0$.
2. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", last bullet (line 190): "$f(x) = x^{-a}$ on $(0,1]$ with $a \ge 1$ has ... no Lebesgue integral either" contradicts the unit's own conventions. $x^{-a}$ is non-negative and measurable, so by stage 2 of the definition block (line 62, "No finiteness is assumed and none is concluded") its Lebesgue integral exists and equals $+\infty$; and the note block (line 85) states explicitly that in this unit "a function with $\int f = +\infty$ is here called measurable and non-negative, never integrable" — i.e. it has an integral but is not integrable. The sentence is a faithful report of Stakgold and Holst p. 44, whose own construction (a greatest lower bound over approximants with a common bound) does make the integral fail to exist, so the source is not at fault; what is wrong is importing their phrasing into our Folland convention without flagging the clash, which is the "two sources conflicting" case of `docs/contributing/source-authority.md`. Recorded as a discrepancy against stakgold-holst-2011. Fix: write "is not Lebesgue integrable either — its integral exists and equals $+\infty$", and add half a clause noting that Stakgold and Holst, whose construction differs, say the integral does not exist.
3. [minor] proof "Comparison with the Riemann integral", Steps 1 and 4 (lines 160–165, 177): $G_P$ and $g_P$ are built from indicators of $(t_{j-1},t_j]$, which cover $(a,b]$ but not the point $a$, so $G_P(a) = g_P(a) = 0$ and the assertion "$g_{P_k} \le f \le G_{P_k}$ for each $k$" (line 177) fails at $x = a$ unless $f(a) = 0$. Harmless, since $\{a\}$ is null and Step 4 only needs $g \le f \le G$ a.e. for the conclusion $G = f$ a.e.; Folland's proof has the same gap. Fix: say "$g_{P_k} \le f \le G_{P_k}$ on $(a,b]$, hence a.e.".
4. [minor] statement "Elementary properties", claim 3 (line 95): the bold label "Each non-negative function induces a measure" promises more than the claim, which is only for $\varphi \ge 0$ simple (Folland Prop. 2.13d), and more than the proof in Step 3 delivers. The general statement for $f \in \mathcal{L}^+$ is true (Folland Exercise 14, p. 52) but needs monotone convergence. Fix: label it "Each simple function induces a measure", or state the general version and cite the extra ingredient.
5. [minor] remark, last bullet (line 190): "Folland lists the two real advantages as the convergence theorems and the completeness of the resulting function spaces" misreports the list. On p. 58 Folland's two advantages are (i) much more powerful convergence theorems and (ii) a wider class of functions can be integrated; completeness appears a few lines later as the "crucial effect" of (ii), not as item two. The bullet's own heading ("Enlarging the class of integrands is not the main gain") is exactly Folland's point that the added generality "is rarely used in computing specific integrals", so only the attribution is loose. Fix: "Folland's two advantages are the convergence theorems and a wider class of integrands, the latter mattering, he says, chiefly because it makes the integral-norm function spaces complete".
