[source] remark (line ~197): the unit reports Stakgold and Holst's verdict (§0.7, Example 3 discussion, p. 44) that for $f(x)=x^{-a}$ on $(0,1]$ with $a \ge 1$ "neither the improper Riemann integral nor the Lebesgue integral exists". Under the stage-2 construction this unit follows (Folland §2.2, p. 50), a non-negative measurable function always has an integral in $[0,\infty]$; what fails for $a \ge 1$ is finiteness, not existence. This is already on record in `verification/discrepancies.md` (measure.lebesgue-integral / stakgold-holst-2011, §0.7, Example 3 discussion, p. 44). The unit states which convention it follows, cites it, and explains the difference in a remark, which is exactly the required handling of two conflicting sources; no action needed on the unit.
2. [minor] proof "Comparison with the Riemann integral", step 1 (line ~160): "stage 1 of the definition gives $\int G_P \, dm = S_P f$". Stage 1 defines the integral only for *non-negative* simple functions, and $f$ is only assumed bounded, so $M_j$ and $m_j$ may be negative and $G_P, g_P$ are then signed simple functions. The identity is still correct, but it rests on stage 3 together with linearity (claim 4), not on stage 1 alone. Fix: say "stage 1 together with linearity (claim 4), the sets having finite measure", or note that one may assume $f \ge 0$ after adding a constant.
3. [minor] note block, "Two forward references" (line ~90): "both are proved from the three stages defined above and from the monotonicity of the integral on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ (claim 1)". The handbook's proof of dominated convergence also uses claim 4 (linearity on $L^1$, to cancel $\int g$) and claim 6 (null sets), and the proof of additivity on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ uses claim 2. The conclusion drawn — that nothing is circular — is still correct, since none of claims 1–7 is proved from monotone or dominated convergence except claim 4, which uses only additivity on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$, and that in turn uses only the monotone convergence theorem. Fix: widen the list to "claims 1, 2 and 6 and the three stages", or simply say that the convergence theorems use only claims proved before claim 4.
