## Findings
1. [minor] definition (line 65): "Because a simple $\varphi \ge 0$ is one of its own competitors, the two definitions agree where both apply." The reason given establishes only $\int_{\text{stage 2}} \varphi \ge \int_{\text{stage 1}} \varphi$. The reverse inequality needs monotonicity of the stage-1 integral on simple functions (Folland Prop. 2.13c, p. 49), which this unit never states separately; it does follow from claim 2, since $\psi \le \varphi$ simple gives $\varphi = \psi + (\varphi - \psi)$ with $\varphi - \psi \ge 0$ simple, so $\int \varphi = \int \psi + \int(\varphi - \psi) \ge \int \psi$. Fix: add that half-sentence, citing claim 2 forward.
2. [minor] proof "Comparison with the Riemann integral", step 1 (line 167): "stage 1 of the definition gives $\int G_P\,dm = S_P f$". $f$ is only assumed bounded, so $M_j$ and $m_j$ may be negative and $G_P, g_P$ are not in $\mathcal{L}^{+}$; stage 1 as written defines $\int \varphi$ only for $\varphi \ge 0$ simple. The identity is still right (apply stage 3 to $G_P^{\pm}$, each a non-negative simple function of finite-measure support, and claim 2), but as written the step invokes a definition outside its stated range. Fix: say that for a signed simple function supported on a set of finite measure, stage 3 together with claim 2 gives $\int \varphi = \sum_j a_j \mu(E_j)$, and use that.
3. [minor] note "Two forward references" (line 89): the stated ground for non-circularity — that monotone and dominated convergence "are proved from the three stages defined above and from the monotonicity of the integral on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ (claim 1 below)" — is accurate for monotone convergence but not for dominated convergence, whose proof in measure.dominated-convergence also uses linearity on $L^{1}$ (claim 4) and the null-set facts (claim 6). The conclusion is unaffected: the chain claim 1 → MCT → additivity on $\mathcal{L}^{+}$ → claim 4 → Fatou → DCT → Riemann comparison is acyclic, and DCT is invoked only after claims 4 and 6 are proved. Fix: name claims 4 and 6 in that sentence, or weaken it to "from the three stages and from the elementary properties proved below, in an order that closes no loop". (The heading "Two forward references" against "Three places below" in the next clause reads oddly on first pass, though two units is the intended count.)
4. [minor] proof "The elementary properties", step 5 (line 143): the complex case of claim 5 is proved only for $\int f \ne 0$; the case $\int f = 0$ is never disposed of (it is immediate, the right-hand side being non-negative). Fix: one clause.
5. [minor] example, steps 2-3 (lines 228-261): step 2 is headed "confirm it from the definition", but the error bound $0 \le \tfrac13 - \int \varphi_n \le 2^{-n}$ that drives step 3, and hence the conclusion "the supremum over all simple minorants is therefore $\tfrac13$", already uses $\int_{[0,1]} x^2\,dm = \tfrac13$ obtained in step 1 from the comparison theorem. What steps 2-3 establish independently is the lower bound $\sup \ge \lim_n \int \varphi_n$; the identification of that limit with $\tfrac13$ is not independent. Fix: call it a consistency check, or evaluate $\lim_n 2^{-3n/2}\sum_{k<2^n} k(\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt{k})$ directly.
6. [source] remark, bullet on $x^{-a}$ (line 198): Stakgold and Holst (§0.7, p. 44) write that for $a \ge 1$ "neither the improper Riemann integral nor the Lebesgue integral exists", which conflicts with the stage-2 convention this unit follows, under which the integral exists in $[0,\infty]$ and equals $+\infty$. The unit states the version it follows, cites it, and explains the difference, which is what the two-sources rule asks; the divergence is already on record in verification/discrepancies.md (measure.lebesgue-integral / stakgold-holst-2011, §0.7 Example 3 discussion, p. 44), so no new entry was added. Not a reason to fail.
