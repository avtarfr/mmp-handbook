## Findings
1. [blocking] definition "Simply connected" (lines 100-103): the two "reformulations, each equivalent" are not equivalent to the definition, because both drop the path-connectedness half of it. Counterexample to each: let $X$ be the disjoint union of two open disks in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (equivalently $\{z : |z| < 1\} \cup \{z : |z - 3| < 1\}$ with its subspace topology). Every loop in $X$ lies in one disk and contracts there, and any two paths of $X$ with the same start and the same end lie in one disk and are path-homotopic there; yet $X$ is not path-connected, so it is not simply connected. The cited source states the hypothesis: Lee's Exercise A.61 (p. 613) reads "Let $X$ be a path-connected topological space. Show that $X$ is simply connected if and only if every pair of paths in $X$ with the same starting and ending points are path-homotopic", and his preceding paragraph gives the loop form only as a consequence ("This means that every loop is path-homotopic to a constant path"). The unit's own conventions remark confirms the omission: it reports do Carmo's definition (p. 388) as "an *arcwise connected* $B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$ ... any two arcs with the same endpoints are homotopic" and then calls that "the second form of our definition", but our second form has no connectivity requirement. Fix: open the list with "For a path-connected $X$, each of the following is equivalent to triviality of $\pi_1$", or carry "path-connected and" into each bullet.
