## Findings
1. [source] remark "Three ways the $t$ interval is written" (line 178): the unit reports Arfken's Example 23.7.3 faithfully, and the report is correct - Arfken does divide by $\sqrt{5}$, the square root of the degrees of freedom, not of the sample size, while his own Eq. (23.118) and the paragraph above it make $n$ the number of data points. Arfken's half-widths 1.720 and 2.188 are therefore too wide by $\sqrt{6/5} = 1.0954$; the correct values on his own data are 1.570 and 1.996. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs` against arfken-2013, "Example 23.7.3 and Eq. (23.118), pp. 1177-1178". Not a defect of the unit: it states the convention it follows (RHB), shows the two agree identically through $s/\sqrt{N-1} = S/\sqrt N$, and gives the corrected 1.570.
2. [source] (same block, line 178): a second slip in the same Arfken example - the data are listed with 5.69 but the displayed mean is computed with 5.60. The listed data do give the printed 5.885 and 1.9035, so only the displayed sum is wrong. Recorded against arfken-2013, "Example 23.7.3, p. 1178". The unit quotes only Arfken's final numbers and is unaffected.
3. [minor] statement, item after 4 (line 91): "since $z_{0.8413} = 1$ ... at level $1 - 2(1 - 0.8413) = 0.683$". Both equalities are rounded: $\Phi(1) = 0.8413447$, so $z_{0.8413} = 0.99986$, and $1 - 2(1 - 0.8413) = 0.6826$, not $0.683$. RHB write the second with "$\approx$" (p. 1238). Fix: use $\approx$ in both places, or quote $0.6827$ as the level.
4. [minor] statement, item 4 (line 89): "an estimate $\hat\sigma_{\hat a}$ may be substituted for $\sigma_{\hat a}$ when the latter is unknown" is asserted without the condition that makes it work. The proof (step 4, line 142) establishes asymptotic coverage only for known $\sigma_{\hat a}$; the substituted version needs $\hat\sigma_{\hat a}/\sigma_{\hat a} \to 1$ in probability (Slutsky), which is also what RHB mean by "provided $\hat\sigma_{\hat a}$ is a fairly good estimate" (p. 1239). Fix: add the consistency condition, or mark the substitution as an approximation as RHB do.
5. [minor] remark "What the confidence level says" (line 164): the sentence "if a large number of samples of the same size were drawn and the interval computed each time, the true value would lie inside on 95% of those occasions" keeps the clause skeleton of RHB p. 1236-1237 ("If a large number of samples, each of size $N$, were analysed then the interval $[a_-,a_+]$ would contain the true value $a$ on a fraction $1-\alpha-\beta$ of the occasions"). The block is correctly labelled `adapted`, attributes the point in text, and is otherwise entirely our own, so this is not a close paraphrase of the block; but this one sentence could be turned further from the source.
