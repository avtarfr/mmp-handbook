## Findings

1. [minor] remark[Where the hypotheses are used], first bullet (line 166): "Removing a point from $\mathbb{R}^2$ leaves $\partial/\partial x$ a Killing field whose flow is defined only for a finite time at each point" is false at most points. On $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}$ the flow is $\theta_t(x,y) = (x+t,y)$, which is defined for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ at every point with $y \neq 0$; only the points of the punctured $x$-axis have a finite escape time, and even there only in one time direction. The point the bullet is making — that a Killing field need not be complete, so "acts by isometries" must be read on $M_t$ — is correct and is worth keeping. Fix: "...whose flow escapes in finite time at the points of the punctured $x$-axis, so $\theta_t$ is defined only on a proper open subset $M_t$".

2. [minor] example[Euclidean three-space], last paragraph (line 254): the computation is right — I confirm $[K_z, K_x] = x\partial_z - z\partial_x$ symbolically, and this is the $(i,j) = (1,3)$ member of part 4's basis, so "the third rotation of the basis" is accurate. But calling it $K_y$ clashes with the naming used for the other two in the same sentence: $K_z = x\partial_y - y\partial_x$ and $K_x = y\partial_z - z\partial_y$ follow the cyclic (angular-momentum) pattern, under which $K_y = z\partial_x - x\partial_z = -[K_z,K_x]$. Vector fields generating rotations satisfy $[L_i,L_j] = -\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$, the opposite sign to the quantum operators, which is exactly the trap a reader will fall into here. Fix: either drop the name ("$= x\partial_z - z\partial_x$, the remaining rotation of part 4's basis") or write $[K_z,K_x] = -K_y$ with $K_y = z\partial_x - x\partial_z$ and say the sign is the vector-field convention.
