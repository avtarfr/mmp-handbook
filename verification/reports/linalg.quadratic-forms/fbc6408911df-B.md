## Findings
1. [blocking] statement[Eigenvalue test for definiteness] (line 62, and frontmatter `role`, line 21): the strict half of the eigenvalue test is attributed to `hassani-2013` Cor 6.4.10, p. 181, but that corollary is not about positive *definiteness* in Hassani's own terminology. Hassani's Definition 4.3.9 (printed p. 117) defines "positive", written $A \ge 0$, by $\langle a|A|a\rangle \ge 0$ for all $|a\rangle \neq 0$, and reserves "strictly positive" / "positive definite", written $A > 0$, for the strict inequality. Cor 6.4.10 speaks of a hermitian operator being "positive", i.e. the non-strict notion, so it is the semidefinite statement, not the strict one. The unit's `role` line ("the strict half of the eigenvalue test — positive definiteness is equivalent to positivity of the spectrum") therefore translates Hassani's defined term into the one he explicitly distinguishes it from, and a reader who follows the citation to p. 181 and p. 117 will find the handbook mis-citing the book. Fix: cite `rhb-2006` §8.17, p. 290 for the strict half — that page states it outright ("Q > 0 for all x means that all the eigenvalues ... must be positive. A symmetric (Hermitian) matrix A with this property is called positive definite"), so a single RHB citation already carries both halves — and either drop the Hassani source or keep it for the semidefinite half with a one-clause remark that Hassani writes "positive" for $\ge 0$ and "strictly positive / positive definite" for $> 0$. The mathematics of the statement itself is correct and is not in question; only the attribution is.
2. [source] `hassani-2013` §6.4, Cor 6.4.10, p. 181: as printed the corollary is ambiguous. With Definition 4.3.9 (p. 117), "positive" means $\ge 0$, and the literal reading "all its eigenvalues are positive" then makes the corollary false ($\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ is positive in Hassani's sense and has a zero eigenvalue). Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add linalg.quadratic-forms hassani-2013`; not a reason to fail the unit, and it does not excuse finding 1, which is about which of Hassani's two defined terms the unit claims the corollary supplies.
