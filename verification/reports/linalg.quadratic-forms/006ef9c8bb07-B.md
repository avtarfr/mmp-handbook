## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Traps", bullet "**Conventions**" (line 110): the factor-of-two relation is stated backwards. The unit says

   > Some texts define the form with a factor $\tfrac12$, as $Q(x) = \tfrac12 x^{\mathsf T} A x$, so that $A$ is the Hessian of $Q$ rather than half of it; the eigenvalues then halve

   For one and the same form $Q$, the matrix that represents it under the $\tfrac12$ convention is *twice* the matrix that represents it under this unit's convention ($\tilde A = 2A$), so its eigenvalues **double**. Checked with sympy on the unit's own example form $Q = x_1^2 + 4x_1x_2 - x_2^2$ from the method block: our $A = \begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt5 \approx \pm 2.2360680$; the Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\4&-2\end{pmatrix}$, with eigenvalues $\pm 2\sqrt5 \approx \pm 4.4721360$. The surrounding clause is self-consistent and correct ("$A$ is the Hessian of $Q$ rather than half of it" — under this unit's convention the Hessian is $2A$), which makes the direction of the error unambiguous: it is the eigenvalue clause that is wrong, not the Hessian clause. The sentence also cannot be rescued by reading "eigenvalues" as the sum-of-squares coefficients: those are $\tfrac12\tilde\lambda_k = \lambda_k$, i.e. unchanged, not halved. This is in the one block whose purpose is to keep a reader from getting a convention factor wrong. Fix: "the eigenvalues then double".
2. [minor] statement "Principal axes" (line 46), and method step 5 (line 91): the geometric classification is false when no eigenvalue is positive. "if some $\lambda_k = 0$ it is a cylinder: unbounded along $u_k$" fails for $A = \operatorname{diag}(0,-1)$, where $\{x : x^{\mathsf T}Ax = 1\} = \{x : -x_2^2 = 1\} = \emptyset$ — an empty set is not a cylinder and is not unbounded in any direction; the same for $A = 0$. Method step 5 inherits it: "if $\lambda_k \le 0$ it does not meet that line at all and the set is unbounded in the direction $u_k$" is right whenever the level set is non-empty, and vacuous-to-wrong when it is not. Checked: for $A = \operatorname{diag}(-\sqrt5, \sqrt5)$ (the method's own example) the level set at $c = 2$ does meet the $u_2$ line at $0.945742$ with $x^{\mathsf T}Ax = 2.000000$, and never meets the $u_1$ line, exactly as stated — so the recipe is right in the mixed-sign case and only the all-non-positive case is uncovered. Fix: add "the level set is non-empty precisely when some $\lambda_k > 0$" to the statement, and the same proviso to step 5.
