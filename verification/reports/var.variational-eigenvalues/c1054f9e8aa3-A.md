## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, "Comparison" (line 89): the claim that decreasing $w$ raises every eigenvalue is false under the unit's hypotheses, because $q$ may be negative, so $D[u] < 0$ is possible and then a smaller weight makes $\mathcal{R}[u]$ more negative. Counterexample: $-u'' - 100u = \lambda w u$ on $[0, \pi]$ with Dirichlet conditions has $\lambda_1 = -99$ for $w \equiv 1$ but $\lambda_1 = -198$ for $w \equiv \tfrac12$. Also "raises" should be "does not lower": the min-max only gives non-strict inequalities. Fix: state the $w$-comparison only when $q \ge 0$ (so $D > 0$ on trial functions), or only for the eigenvalues that are positive, and replace "raises" with "does not lower" (or add the hypothesis needed for strictness). The claims for $p$, $q$ and shrinking the interval are correct.
2. [minor] proof step 6 (line 75): $\max_{V} \mathcal{R}$ is used without saying it exists. Fix: note that $\|\cdot\|_w$ is a norm on the finite-dimensional $V$ (because $w > 0$), so $\mathcal{R}$ is continuous on its compact unit sphere and attains its maximum.
3. [minor] statement Part 3, max-min form (line 51): the class of the functions $v_1, \dots, v_{n-1}$ is not specified, and for a general class the inner "minimum" should be an infimum. Fix: say, for example, $v_k \in C[a, b]$ and write "infimum" (or justify attainment).
4. [minor] idea (line 55): "the energy becomes $\sum \lambda_n c_n^2$" claims an identity, but the proof only establishes the inequality $D_\kappa[u] \ge \sum (\lambda_n + \kappa) c_n^2$, which is enough. Fix: say the energy is at least $\sum \lambda_n c_n^2$ (after the shift), or "behaves like".
5. [minor] application, Vibrations (line 119): "the fundamental frequency is the minimum of the ratio" - the minimum of the energy ratio is the square of the fundamental (angular) frequency. Fix: "the squared fundamental frequency".
