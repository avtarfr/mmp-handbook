## Findings
1. [blocking] application (line 247): "the identity (E) is then Poynting's theorem, with $e$ the field energy density and $\mathbf{S}$ the Poynting vector." With $u$ read as a Cartesian component of $\mathbf{E}$, the unit's $e = \tfrac12(u_t^2 + c^2|\nabla u|^2) = \tfrac12(\dot E_x^2 + c^2|\nabla E_x|^2)$ is not the electromagnetic energy density $\tfrac12(\varepsilon_0|\mathbf{E}|^2 + |\mathbf{B}|^2/\mu_0)$ — it is not even of the right dimensions — and $\mathbf{S} = -c^2u_t\nabla u$ is not $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}/\mu_0$; summing the componentwise $e$ over the six components of $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ does not repair this (using $\dot{\mathbf{E}} = c^2\nabla\times\mathbf{B}$ it gives $\tfrac12(c^4|\nabla\times\mathbf{B}|^2 + c^2\sum_i|\nabla E_i|^2)$). Poynting's theorem follows from Maxwell's equations, not from applying (E) to one component. Secondarily, the preceding clause is loose for the same reason: a cavity imposes $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\times\mathbf{E} = 0$ and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$, not componentwise Dirichlet data, so the uniqueness theorem does not transfer component by component without further argument. Fix: say that (E) has *the same form as* Poynting's theorem — a conserved density plus the divergence of a flux — and that for the true Maxwell fields that conservation law *is* Poynting's theorem with its own $e$ and $\mathbf{S}$; and either drop the cavity-uniqueness claim or say that it needs the field-energy identity rather than this scalar one.
2. [minor] proof "Conservation of energy on the line", Step 4 (line 125): "since both the equation and d'Alembert's formula are unchanged by $t\mapsto -t$, $E$ is constant on all of $\mathbb{R}$". (D) is not invariant under $t\mapsto -t$: the $\psi$ integral changes sign, so the reflected function is the solution for the data $(\varphi,-\psi)$. The conclusion is still right, because $\tilde u(x,t)=u(x,-t)$ solves the same equation with the compactly supported data $(\varphi,-\psi)$, so Steps 1–4 apply to $\tilde u$ on $[0,T]$, and $E$ is quadratic in the data so $E_{\tilde u}(t) = E_u(-t)$. Fix: give that reason, or simply say Steps 1–4 run unchanged on $[-T,0]$. (The same phrasing occurs in pde.dalembert's time-reversal bullet.)
