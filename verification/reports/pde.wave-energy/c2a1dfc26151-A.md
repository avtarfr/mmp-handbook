## Findings
1. [minor] :::proof "Conservation of energy on the line", Step 1 (line 96): the justification offered misses $t=0$. The open set invoked is $\{\lvert x\rvert > R+ct,\ t>0\}$, so the sentence "If $\lvert x\rvert \ge R_T$ and $0 \le t < T$ then ... $(x,t)$ lies in that open set" is false at $t=0$, which is excluded by the $t>0$ in the set. The conclusion is of course right, twice over: the step's own continuity device ("at $t=T$ the same holds by continuity") applies verbatim as $t\to0^+$, and directly $u_t(x,0) = \psi(x) = 0$ and $u_x(x,0) = \varphi'(x) = 0$ for $\lvert x\rvert > R$. Fix: write "for $0 < t < T$" and then "at $t = 0$ and $t = T$ the same holds by continuity of $u_t$ and $u_x$".
2. [minor] :::proof "Conservation of energy on the line", Step 3 (line 117): the parenthetical "(This is the only place where the compact support of the data is used.)" is not accurate and sits oddly against the unit's own remark block. Compact support is what Step 1 exploits to confine the integrand to the fixed interval $[-R_T,R_T]$, and that confinement is what makes $E$ finite at all and what lets Step 2 differentiate under the integral sign over fixed limits; the remark bullet "Compact support, or a boundary condition" says as much, giving $u=\sin(x-ct)$ as a solution of infinite energy. Fix: "(This is where the vanishing at $\pm R_T$ earns its keep; Steps 1 and 2 used the same compact support to make $E$ finite and its limits fixed.)"

## Checked
Verified the identity (E) by expanding both terms: $\partial_te = u_tu_{tt}+c^2\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_t$ (the mixed partials being interchangeable for $C^2$), $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{S} = -c^2(\nabla u_t\cdot\nabla u + u_t\Delta u)$, and the cancellation leaving $u_t(u_{tt}-c^2\Delta u)$; also checked that $\partial_te = c^2\nabla\cdot(u_t\nabla u)$ is the same statement, as the unit says of Strauss's form, and that the flux sign is the physically right one ($-\tau u_x$ is the transverse force, $u_t$ the velocity, so $-\tau u_xu_t$ is the power delivered rightward) and that dividing the string's $E$ by $\rho$ gives $\int e$ with $n=1$ because $\tau/\rho = c^2$. Went through the line proof step by step: the reduction to a fixed compact interval (see finding 1), the hypotheses for differentiation under the integral sign, the integration by parts with its vanishing bracket, and $dE/dt = \int u_t(\rho u_{tt}-\tau u_{xx}) = 0$; checked the $t<0$ half, where $\check u(x,t) = u(x,-t)$ solves the same equation with data $(\varphi,-\psi)$ of the same support and has the same energy because $E$ sees only $u_t^2$ and $u_x^2$, and the evaluation $E(0) = \frac12\int(\rho\psi^2+\tau\varphi'^2)$. On the bounded domain I checked $dE/dt = -\oint\mathbf{S}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}} = c^2\oint u_t\partial u/\partial n$, the sign in each of the three cases (Dirichlet, where differentiating $u\equiv0$ along the fixed boundary gives $u_t = 0$; Neumann; and Robin, where $\partial u/\partial n = -bu_t$ gives $dE/dt = -c^2b\oint u_t^2 \le 0$), and the regularity bookkeeping: $e$ and $\partial_te$ continuous on the compact $\overline V\times[0,T]$, and $\mathbf{S}$ of class $C^1$ on $\overline V$ for $u\in C^2$, so the divergence theorem applies. The unit's extension of the differentiation-under-the-integral theorem from $[a,b]$ to a bounded $V\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is correct as written (mean value theorem on the difference quotient, uniform continuity of $\partial_te$ on a compact set, error at most $\lvert V\rvert\varepsilon$). Checked the uniqueness proof: $\Gamma_T = \overline{V_T}\setminus V_T$ is indeed the bottom together with the side, $w = 0$ on the side gives $w_t = 0$ there, $E(0) = 0$ because $w(\cdot,0)\equiv0$ in $V$ forces $\nabla w(\cdot,0) = 0$ and $w_t(\cdot,0) = 0$ by hypothesis, the continuous-non-negative-with-zero-integral argument, and Step 4's integration in $t$ from the initial value. Also checked the remark's claims: $u = \sin(x-ct)$ has $e = c^2\cos^2(x-ct)$ with divergent integral; the Neumann wave problem is still unique because the constant left undetermined by $\nabla w\equiv0$ is pinned at $t=0$; and the failure of a maximum principle (with $\varphi\equiv0$, $\psi>0$ inside, $u\approx t\psi>0$ for small $t$, so the maximum over $\overline{V_T}$ is attained off $\Gamma_T$ while $\max_{\Gamma_T}u = 0$). In the example I re-derived the mode-by-mode energy by hand and then confirmed it symbolically with sympy for $N=3$ and general $A_n,B_n$: $E - \frac{\tau\pi^2}{4L}\sum_{n=1}^3 n^2(A_n^2+B_n^2)$ simplifies to exactly $0$, and the same superposition has $u_{tt}-c^2u_{xx} = 0$ identically, so the unit's parenthetical claim to have checked $N=3$ symbolically holds. The key cancellation $\rho\omega_n^2 = \tau(n\pi/L)^2$ and the Pythagorean collapse are right; equipartition ($\mathrm{KE} = E\sin^2\omega_nt$, $\mathrm{PE} = E\cos^2\omega_nt$, each with mean $E/2$, a full cycle twice per period) is right; the numbers reproduce ($c = 126.49$, $c/2L = 97.30$ Hz, $A_1 = 2.25$ mm, $A_3 = -0.75$ mm, $E = 3.0748\times10^{-3}$ J) and so does the observation that $n^2A_n^2$ is $5.0625$ for both $n=1$ and $n=3$, which is the claim that the third harmonic carries the same energy at a third of the amplitude; and the travelling-wave contrast checks ($\rho u_t^2 = \tau u_x^2 = \tau F'^2$, $E = \tau\int F'^2$, $S = \tau cF'^2 = ce$).
