## Findings
1. [minor] application, "Numerical analysis" (line 257): "symplectic or energy-conserving integrators are built to inherit the conservation law **exactly** rather than approximately". True of an energy-conserving (discrete-gradient, Gauss–Legendre-type) integrator; not of a symplectic one, which conserves a nearby modified energy and keeps the true energy within $O(\Delta t^p)$ over long times rather than exactly — which is in fact why the familiar leapfrog scheme for the wave equation shows a small bounded energy oscillation rather than none. Fix: "energy-conserving integrators inherit the conservation law exactly, and symplectic ones keep the energy within a bounded error for exponentially long times".
2. [minor] application, same paragraph (line 257): the stability condition described — "the numerical domain of dependence contain the true one" — is the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition, a statement about domains of dependence, whereas the cross-reference is to von Neumann stability analysis, which is the Fourier-mode calculation that happens to deliver the same inequality for the standard explicit scheme. The sentence as written attributes the geometric criterion to the Fourier method. Fix: name the CFL condition and say that von Neumann analysis recovers it for the standard scheme.
