**Counterexample search.** Attacked each clause of the Proposition.
- Part 1: $T^{-1}(X,Y) = (Y/b,\ X - 1 + (a/b^2)Y^2)$ verified symbolically; $T^{-1}\circ T = \mathrm{id}$ and $T\circ T^{-1} = \mathrm{id}$ in sympy. Fails only at $b = 0$, which the hypothesis excludes and the remark discusses.
- Part 2: $\det DT = (-2ax)\cdot 0 - 1\cdot b = -b$ at every point; the zero-area conclusion needs $|b| < 1$, which the remark correctly isolates.
- Part 3: product of the roots of $ax^2 + (1-b)x - 1$ is $-1/a < 0$, so the two fixed points always straddle $0$; $\mu^2 + 2ax\mu - b$ has product of roots $-b$, modulus $|b| < 1$, so no repeller -- no counterexample. The parenthetical "$(b>0)$" on the real radical is needed and is flagged in the remark; for $b<0$ I confirmed the pair is complex of modulus $\sqrt{|b|}$ when $a^2x^2 + b < 0$.
- Part 4: tried to break the hypothesis $|x_{n-1}| \le |x_n|$. It cannot be dropped as the unit says; I could not find a counterexample to the statement as written, and the step $|b|t_{m-1} \le |b|t_m$ is where it is used. $p(t) = at^2-(1+|b|)t-1$ has its vertex at $(1+|b|)/(2a) < R$, so $p$ is indeed strictly increasing and positive on $(R,\infty)$ as claimed.
- Part 5: the "reduction to the boundary" is sound -- the image of a vertical slice is a horizontal segment because $y\mapsto 1-ax^2+y$ is affine of slope $1$ and the second coordinate $bx$ is independent of $y$ -- and convexity of $Q$ then makes checking the two boundary graphs sufficient and (trivially) necessary.
- Degenerate cases: $b=0$ (two-to-one quadratic map), $|b|=1$ (area preserving, no attractor), $a<0$ (fixed points collide at $a = -(1-b)^2/4$, which I confirmed is where the discriminant $(1-b)^2+4a$ vanishes) -- all four are already covered by the hypotheses remark.
