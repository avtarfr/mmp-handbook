## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, second bullet (line 194): the inner-product convention flips. The handbook convention, stated in docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md ("inner products $(f,g)$, linear in the first slot") and used everywhere else in this unit — the Hellinger-Toeplitz proof needs $f_n(\varphi)=(\varphi,T\psi_n)$ to be *linear* in $\varphi$, and the worked example computes $(Tu,v)=\int u'\bar v$ — is linear in the first argument. The Reed and Simon Example 4 reported here is copied in Reed and Simon's convention (linear in the second argument, p. 252). Consequently, read in our notation, $T\psi = (f,\psi)\,\psi_{0}$ is conjugate-linear in $\psi$ and so is not a linear operator at all, and the stated adjoint $T^{*}\varphi = (\psi_{0},\varphi)\,f$ is the wrong formula: in our convention $(T\psi,\varphi)=(\psi,f)(\psi_{0},\varphi)=(\psi,(\varphi,\psi_{0})f)$, so $T^{*}\varphi=(\varphi,\psi_{0})f$. The conclusion drawn ($D(T^{*})=\{\psi_{0}\}^{\perp}$, not dense, $T$ not closable) is unaffected, since $|(\varphi,\psi_0)|=|(\psi_0,\varphi)|$, but the two displayed formulas as written are wrong in this unit's own notation. Fix: write $T\psi=(\psi,f)\,\psi_{0}$ and $T^{*}\varphi=(\varphi,\psi_{0})\,f$, and note that Reed and Simon's Example 4 uses the opposite convention.
2. [minor] prereqs (line 7) and proof "Hellinger-Toeplitz", Step 3 (line 140): the proof turns on the uniform boundedness theorem, and the idea block (line 122) turns on the closed graph theorem, but `funcan.uniform-boundedness-open-mapping` is not in `prereqs`. It is cited inline with `:ref` at the point of use, so the reader is not stranded, but it is a load-bearing result in the only full proof of the unit. Fix: add `funcan.uniform-boundedness-open-mapping` to `prereqs`.
3. [minor] definition "Closed, closable, closure" (line 65): "$T$ is closable if it has at least one closed extension. The smallest such extension, *when it exists*, is the closure." If a closed extension exists at all then the smallest one always exists — that is exactly part (b) of the theorem proved below ($\bar T = T^{**}$). The hedge suggests a third possibility that does not occur. Fix: "If a closed extension exists, there is a smallest one, the closure $\bar T$ (see the theorem below)."
4. [minor] application (line 268): "Example (i) above shows $X$ is unbounded, and example (iii) shows the same for $P$." Example (iii) is $-i\,d/dx$ on $L^{2}(0,1)$ with $u(0)=0$, not $P=-i\hbar\,d/dx$ on $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$. The conclusion is right but the pointer is not exact. Fix: say that example (iii) exhibits the unboundedness of differentiation (on an interval), and that the same sequence rescaled works on the line.
