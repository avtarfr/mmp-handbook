[blocking] remark "Where each hypothesis is used", first bullet: the unit says do Carmo's Exercise 10 curve "is regular and has $\kappa$ vanishing only at $t = 0$". do Carmo, Exercise 10(b), p. 26, says exactly the opposite of "only": $k(t) \neq 0$ for $t \neq 0$, $t \neq \pm\sqrt{2/3}$, and $k(0) = 0$ — the curvature vanishes at three points, not one. This is also false on its own terms: for the $t>0$ branch $(t,0,e^{-1/t^2})$ the plane curvature is $\propto f''(t) = -2(3t^2-2)e^{-1/t^2}/t^6$ (sympy), which vanishes at $t=\sqrt{2/3}\approx 0.8165$; symmetrically at $t=-\sqrt{2/3}$ on the other branch. Fix: drop "only", e.g. "has $\kappa(0)=0$ (and, as do Carmo notes, $\kappa$ also vanishes at $t=\pm\sqrt{2/3}$)". The point the bullet is making — that Part 3 needs $\kappa>0$ — survives unchanged.
2. [blocking] application "Kinematics, track design and elastic rods", "Charged particles" paragraph (provenance=original): "the gyroradius is $\rho = mv\sin\alpha/(qB)$ and $\kappa = 1/\rho$ is constant" is wrong, and it conflicts with the unit's own $\rho = 1/\kappa$ (radius of curvature) in the definition block. For the orbit $(a\cos\omega t, a\sin\omega t, v_\parallel t)$ with $a = v\sin\alpha/\omega$, $\omega = qB/m$, the curvature is $\kappa = a/(a^2+c^2) = \omega\sin\alpha/v = \sin^2\!\alpha\,/\,a$, not $1/a$. Numerically ($v=\omega=1$, $\alpha=60^\circ$): gyroradius $a=0.8660$, $1/a=1.1547$, but $\kappa=0.8660$; ratio $\kappa a = \sin^2 60^\circ = 0.75$. The two agree only at $\alpha=90^\circ$. Fix: either state $\kappa = \sin^2\!\alpha/\rho_{\text{gyro}} = qB\sin\alpha/(mv)$, or restrict the sentence to the perpendicular case $\alpha = \pi/2$ (a circle) and say separately that for a general pitch the radius of curvature exceeds the gyroradius by $1/\sin^2\!\alpha$.
