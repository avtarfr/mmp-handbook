## Findings
1. [minor] proof[Fixed limits], Step 2 (line 82): "This is the only place the *continuity* of $\partial f/\partial x_i$ is used" is misleading, because Step 6 re-invokes exactly that estimate ("the ball $K \subseteq U$, $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ of Steps 1 and 2", then "Step 2 bounds the integrands"). Continuity is invoked once but used twice. The same sentence's "its mere existence ... is used *again* in Step 3" also reads oddly, since existence has not been used earlier. Fix: "This is the only place where the continuity of $\partial f/\partial x_i$ is invoked directly; Step 6 reuses the same estimate. Its mere existence at every point of $U \times [a,b]$ is what Step 3 needs, to apply the mean value theorem."
2. [minor] proof[Variable limits], Step 2 (line 133): the fixed-limit statement is applied "with $[c,d]$ in place of $[a,b]$", but the conclusion drawn is about $\int_u^v$, not $\int_c^d$. The gap is one line, and $[c,d]$ is not what makes it work. Fix: apply the fixed-limit statement with $a = \min(u,v)$, $b = \max(u,v)$ (that interval is compact and contained in $J$, so $f$ and $\partial f/\partial x$ are continuous on $I' \times [a,b]$), then restore the orientation convention; or write $\int_u^v = \int_c^v - \int_c^u$ and apply it twice. $[c,d]$ is still needed in Step 3, for $M$.
3. [minor] application, third bullet (line 277): "the Hellmann-Feynman theorem $\partial E/\partial\lambda = \langle\psi|\partial H/\partial\lambda|\psi\rangle$ is the same interchange applied to $E(\lambda) = \int \overline{\psi} H_\lambda \psi$" overstates what the interchange gives. Differentiating that expression also produces the two terms in $\partial\psi/\partial\lambda$, which vanish only because $\psi_\lambda$ is a normalised eigenvector ($H_\lambda\psi_\lambda = E\psi_\lambda$ and $\langle\psi_\lambda|\psi_\lambda\rangle = 1$). Fix: say the interchange supplies the $\langle\psi|\partial H/\partial\lambda|\psi\rangle$ term and name the normalisation and eigenvalue equation that kill the rest.
4. [minor] note (line 59) and proof Step 4 (line 97): the theorem that a uniform limit may be passed under an integral sign is in :ref{to=found.termwise-operations}, not in found.uniform-convergence, whose scope is pointwise-versus-uniform, the M-test and continuity of a uniform limit. Nothing is actually *used* from either (the proof derives its own $(b-a)\varepsilon$ estimate), so the prerequisite list is not short of anything; only the pointer is aimed one unit away. Fix: point the "sufficient condition for a limit to pass under a sign of integration" clause at found.termwise-operations, keeping found.uniform-convergence for the vocabulary.
