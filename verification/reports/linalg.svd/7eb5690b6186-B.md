## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Spectral norm and best low-rank approximation" (line 71): the historical attribution is reversed. "This is the Eckart-Young theorem (with the Frobenius case due to Mirsky and Schmidt)" has it the wrong way round — Eckart and Young (1936) proved the *Frobenius* case (Schmidt (1907) having proved the operator analogue in the Hilbert-Schmidt norm), and it is Mirsky (1960) who extended it to the spectral norm and to every unitarily invariant norm. As written the unit credits Mirsky with the case he did not do and credits "the Eckart-Young theorem" with the one he did. The mathematics of the block is correct and verified; only the parenthesis is wrong. Fix: "This is the Eckart-Young theorem in the Frobenius norm (Schmidt's earlier result for operators), extended to the spectral norm and all unitarily invariant norms by Mirsky."
2. [minor] remark, bullet "**Full versus reduced**" (line 97): "identities such as $U U^\dagger = I$ hold only in the full form" is false when $m \le n$, because then $p = \min(m,n) = m$ and the thin $U$ is already $m \times m$ and unitary. Checked with numpy: for a $3\times5$ matrix the thin $U$ has shape $(3,3)$ and $UU^\dagger = I$; for a $5\times3$ matrix the thin $U$ has shape $(5,3)$, $U^\dagger U = I$ but $UU^\dagger \neq I$. The same asymmetry applies to $V$ with $m \ge n$. Fix: "hold in the full form, and in the thin form only for whichever of $U$, $V$ happens to be square".
3. [minor] statement "Existence and basic properties", part 4 (line 53): the uniqueness freedoms are listed as if the matched rotation were the only one available inside a group of equal singular values. For the group of *zero* singular values the remaining columns of $U$ and of $V$ are orthonormal completions that may be chosen entirely independently of one another — no matching change is needed, because those columns are constrained only by $Av_i = 0$ and $A^\dagger u_i = 0$. The sentence does not claim to exhaust the freedoms, so nothing stated is false; but part 1's "the two families cannot be chosen independently of each other" plus part 4 together suggest a tighter coupling than holds on the null spaces. Fix: add "for a group of equal non-zero singular values", and say that the completions beyond $r$ are free on both sides.
