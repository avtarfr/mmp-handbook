## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Traps and conventions", paragraph "Singular values are not eigenvalues" (line 93): "Only for Hermitian $A$ do the two lists agree up to absolute values and ordering" is false. The lists agree for every **normal** matrix: if $A = U\Lambda U^\dagger$ then $A^\dagger A = U|\Lambda|^2U^\dagger$, so the singular values are $|\lambda_k|$ sorted decreasingly. Verified numerically: $A = \operatorname{diag}(i,1)$ is not Hermitian and has eigenvalues $i,1$ with singular values $1,1 = |\lambda_k|$; the rotation $\begin{pmatrix}0.6&-0.8\\0.8&0.6\end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $0.6\pm0.8i$ and singular values $1,1 = |\lambda_k|$. The claim matters because the unit's own part 5 is stated only for Hermitian $A$ and this sentence asserts that the Hermitian hypothesis is necessary. Fix: replace "Hermitian" by "normal" here (and, if part 5 is to be sharp, state it for normal $A$). The companion clause "only for positive semidefinite $A$ do they coincide" is correct and should stand — equality of the two multisets forces $\|A\|_F^2 = \sum|\lambda_i|^2$, hence normality by Schur's inequality, hence $A \ge 0$.
2. [blocking] statement "Spectral norm and best low-rank approximation" (line 71): "This is the Eckart-Young theorem (with the Frobenius case due to Mirsky and Schmidt)" reverses the attribution. Eckart & Young (1936) proved the **Frobenius**-norm case (and E. Schmidt (1907) its Hilbert-Schmidt analogue for integral operators); Mirsky (1960) extended the result to all unitarily invariant norms, which is what yields the **spectral**-norm case. As written the sentence credits the spectral case to Eckart and Young and the Frobenius case to Mirsky, both wrong, in a block labelled `original` that we own. Fix: "The Frobenius case is the Eckart-Young theorem (Schmidt's theorem for integral operators); the spectral-norm case follows from Mirsky's extension to all unitarily invariant norms", or drop the attributions.
3. [minor] statement "Existence and basic properties", part 4 (line 53): the uniqueness description does not separate the zero block. Within a group of equal *non-zero* singular values the columns of $U$ and $V$ must be changed together, as the text says; but the trailing columns $u_{r+1},\dots,u_m$ and $v_{r+1},\dots,v_n$ belonging to $s=0$ may be rotated independently of one another (and the two blocks have different sizes, so "the matching change in $V$" is not even defined). Fix: restrict the sentence to non-zero singular values and add that the null-space blocks of $U$ and $V$ may be chosen independently.
4. [minor] remark, paragraph "Full versus reduced": "the thin factors are not unitary ... so identities such as $UU^\dagger = I$ hold only in the full form" is right for $m > n$ but vacuous when $m \le n$, where $p = m$ and the thin $U$ *is* $m\times m$ unitary. Fix: say "when $m > n$ (resp. $n > m$)".
5. [minor] frontmatter `sources`, reed-simon-1980 role: "singular values as the eigenvalues of $|A|$" is not stated on pp. 203-204; Thm VI.17 defines the singular values through the positive square roots of the eigenvalues of $A^*A$ without introducing $|A|$. The unit's own part 5 makes the $|A|$ statement, which is true. Fix: trim that clause from the role, or cite the page where $|A|$ is defined.
