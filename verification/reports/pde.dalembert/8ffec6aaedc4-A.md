## Findings
1. [minor] :::proof, Step 2 (lines 124-126): the terms "$\eta$-slice" and "$\xi$-slice" are used without being named. The preceding sentence defines the two slicings ("for each fixed $\eta$ the set of admissible $\xi$ is an interval, and for each fixed $\xi$ the set of admissible $\eta$ is an interval") but does not attach the labels, so a reader must infer that "$\eta$-slice" means the slice *along* $\eta$ (fixed $\xi$). The usage is consistent throughout and the argument is correct. Fix: say "the slice $\{\eta : (\xi,\eta)\in\Omega\}$ at fixed $\xi$" once, or name the two slicings where they are introduced.
2. [minor] :::method, step 5 (line 235): "no compatibility condition on $\varphi(0),\psi(0)$ is needed" is literally right, and $u_x(0,t)=0$ does hold for every $t>0$ even with a corner at the origin, but the parallel with step 1 is incomplete: the even extension is $C^1$ (resp. $C^2$) only when $\varphi'(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ (resp. also $\varphi'''(0)=0$), which is the Neumann analogue of the $\varphi(0)=\psi(0)=0$ of step 1 and of Evans's $g''(0)=0$ (p. 67, quoted in the second example). Fix: add a half-sentence noting that the smoothness conditions at the origin are on the *odd*-order derivatives here, so the solution is classical away from the characteristics $x=\pm ct$ in the same way as in the Dirichlet case.
3. [minor] :::example "A velocity pulse on the whole line" (line 268): the stated ratio is slightly out. $u(1,2)/u(1,\tfrac12) = 1.5426\times10^{-5}/0.1227105 = 1.257\times10^{-4}$, so "about $1.2\times10^{-4}$" should be $1.3\times10^{-4}$ (or $1.26\times10^{-4}$). The two displacements themselves are right.
4. [minor] :::statement "Causality", item 1 (line 199): the gloss "Strauss's open interval of dependence $(x-ct,x+ct)$ names the interval across which $\psi$ is averaged" is a fair reading but not quite Strauss's own bookkeeping: on p. 39 he says $u$ depends on $\psi$ on the *closed* $[x-ct,x+ct]$ and then names the *open* interval the interval of dependence. The two integrals agree, so nothing is wrong. Fix: say that Strauss names the open interval although the $\psi$-dependence he states is on its closure, which is harmless for the integral.
