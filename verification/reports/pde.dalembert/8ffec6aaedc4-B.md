## Findings
1. [minor] method "Reflection at a boundary", step 1 (line ~226): the compatibility discussion gives only the *necessary* conditions $\varphi(0)=\psi(0)=0$. Evans (p. 67) adds that the reflected solution is not $C^2$ unless $\varphi''(0)=0$, and the unit's second example in fact relies on exactly that. Read on its own, step 1 can be taken to mean that $\varphi(0)=\psi(0)=0$ is all a $C^2$ solution needs. Fix: add half a sentence in step 1 ("necessary; for a genuine $C^2$ solution one also needs $\varphi''(0)=0$, Evans p. 67 — see the second example").
2. [minor] method, step 5 (Neumann) (line ~243): the parallel necessary conditions for the even extension, $\varphi'(0)=\psi'(0)=0$, are not stated. The unit says only that "no compatibility condition on $\varphi(0),\psi(0)$ is needed", which is true, but the reader is left thinking the free end imposes nothing; the general rule the unit itself states in example 2 (odd-order derivatives of an even extension must vanish) gives $\varphi'(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ for a $C^2$ solution. Fix: say which derivatives must vanish instead of only which values need not.
3. [minor] method, step 5 (line ~249): "replacing the minus sign by a plus in the Dirichlet $\psi$-integral $\tfrac12\int_{t-x}^{t+x}1$" — the Dirichlet $\psi$-integral contains no minus sign; what is meant is flipping the sign in the $\varphi$-terms while *keeping* the Dirichlet $\psi$-integral. The arithmetic that follows ($x$ instead of $t$) is right. Fix: reword to "keeping the Dirichlet $\psi$-integral".
