## Findings
1. [blocking] method "Reflection at a boundary", item 5 (free end, Neumann) (line ~168): "the only change in the formulas is that the minus sign in the second case becomes a plus" is false whenever $\psi \not\equiv 0$. With the even extension, for $0<x<ct$ one gets $\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}\psi_{\mathrm{even}} = \int_0^{ct+x}\psi + \int_0^{ct-x}\psi$, which is *not* $\int_{ct-x}^{ct+x}\psi$ (the odd case produces that because the two half-integrals subtract; in the even case they add). Counterexample: $c=1$, $\varphi\equiv0$, $\psi\equiv1$ on $x>0$ (so $\psi_{\mathrm{even}}\equiv1$ and the compatibility of a free end is met). The true solution is $v(x,t)=\frac12\int_{x-t}^{x+t}1 = t$, which indeed has $v_x(0,t)=0$. The rule as stated gives, in the region $0<x<t$, $\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{t-x}^{t+x}1 = x$. At $(x,t)=(0.5,2)$: stated formula $0.5$, true value $2.0$; at $(0.1,1)$: $0.1$ against $1.0$. Fix: state the Neumann pair explicitly, $v=\frac12[\varphi(x+ct)+\varphi(x-ct)]+\frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}\psi$ for $x>c|t|$ and $v=\frac12[\varphi(ct+x)+\varphi(ct-x)]+\frac{1}{2c}\bigl[\int_0^{ct+x}\psi+\int_0^{ct-x}\psi\bigr]$ for $0<x<c|t|$; or restrict the sentence to the $\varphi$-term and say the $\psi$-contributions add instead of cancelling.

2. [blocking] example "A pulse reflected from a fixed end", first paragraph (line ~204): the parenthesis states the correct rule — the odd extension is $C^k$ when the **even-order** derivatives up to order $k$ vanish at $0$ — and then gives an odd-order derivative, $\varphi'''(0)=6\neq0$, as the reason the extension is not $C^4$. An odd-order derivative is never an obstruction (its two one-sided limits agree automatically). Verified symbolically for $\varphi=x^3e^{-x}$: $\varphi(0)=\varphi'(0)=\varphi''(0)=0$, $\varphi'''(0)=6$, $\varphi''''(0)=-24$. The conclusion ("not $C^4$") is right, but for the reason $\varphi''''(0)=-24\neq0$. Fix: replace "$\varphi'''(0)=6\neq0$" by "$\varphi''''(0)=-24\neq0$".
