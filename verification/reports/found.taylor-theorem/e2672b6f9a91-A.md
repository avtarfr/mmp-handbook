## Findings
1. [minor] proof, lemma "the weighted mean value theorem for integrals", last sentence (line 99): "This is the weighted form of the mean value theorem that Arfken uses at his Eq. (1.42)" invites the reading that Arfken's (1.42) is the weighted form. It is not: his (1.42) is the unweighted $\int_a^x g = (x-a)g(\xi)$, which he then applies $n$ times. The intended reading is supplied only by the next clause. Fix: "This is the weighted form of the mean value theorem; Arfken's Eq. (1.42) is the unweighted one, which he applies $n$ times in succession, and the weight is what lets it be applied once."

2. [minor] statement "Taylor's theorem", Lagrange form (line 52): "for some $\xi$ between $a$ and $x$" is proved only with $\xi$ in the *closed* interval $J$ (the lemma yields $\xi \in [c,d]$), whereas "between" is often read strictly, and the remark's second bullet contrasts this with a sharper hypothesis under which "$\xi$ may be taken strictly between $a$ and $x$". The unit is internally consistent, but a reader meeting the statement first cannot tell which is meant. Fix: write "for some $\xi \in J$" in the statement, leaving the strict version to the remark.
