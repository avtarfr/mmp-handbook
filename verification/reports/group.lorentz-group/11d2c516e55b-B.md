1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", paragraph "Non-compactness, and why there are no finite-dimensional unitary representations" (`provenance=original`, so this is our own material): the argument given is invalid. The text says that in a finite-dimensional unitary representation "every generator would be Hermitian, and then $[K_j,K_k]=-\mathrm{i}\epsilon_{jkl}J_l$ would make $J_l$ *anti*-Hermitian while $[J_j,J_k]=\mathrm{i}\epsilon_{jkl}J_l$ makes it Hermitian, forcing all $J_l=0$". No such clash exists. For Hermitian $A,B$ the commutator $[A,B]$ is *anti*-Hermitian. So with $K_j$ Hermitian, $[K_j,K_k]=-\mathrm{i}\epsilon_{jkl}J_l$ is anti-Hermitian, i.e. $(-\mathrm{i}J_l)^{\dagger}=+\mathrm{i}J_l$, i.e. $J_l^{\dagger}=J_l$: Hermitian, the very same conclusion the $[J,J]$ bracket gives. Both brackets are satisfied by Hermitian $J$ and Hermitian $K$, and nothing forces $J_l=0$. The argument is also sign-blind in exactly the place the unit relies on the sign: run it verbatim on $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, where the unit itself says two paragraphs earlier that the bracket is $[K_j,K_k]=+\mathrm{i}\epsilon_{jkl}J_l$ and the real splitting $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ holds, and one "proves" $J_l=K_l=0$ there too — yet $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ has a finite-dimensional unitary representation of every dimension. So the paragraph contradicts the unit's own neighbouring claim. Failure scenario: a reader who accepts the printed reasoning concludes that the sign of $[K,K]$ is what obstructs finite-dimensional unitarity, and will reproduce the same false argument for $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, $\mathfrak{so}(2,2)$ or any other real form. The *conclusion* of the paragraph is correct and the first sentence (the $K_i$ are anti-Hermitian in the defining representation, so entries grow like $\mathrm{e}^{|\rho|}$) is fine. Fix: replace the Hermiticity bookkeeping with a correct obstruction. The cheapest honest one for this unit: if $J,K$ are all Hermitian in a finite-dimensional representation, then $N^{\pm}=\tfrac12(J\pm\mathrm{i}K)$ of part 5 satisfy $(N^{+}_i)^{\dagger}=N^{-}_i$, so the representation is *not* a direct sum of two independent unitary $\mathfrak{su}(2)$'s but pairs them by adjoint, and the boost exponentials $\exp(-\mathrm{i}\rho\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf K)$ form a one-parameter group of unitaries with Hermitian unbounded-in-$\rho$ generator in a finite-dimensional space, which forces $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf K$ to have real spectrum and the orbit to be bounded — impossible unless $K=0$, and then $[K,K]=-\mathrm{i}\epsilon J$ gives $J=0$. Alternatively state the standard theorem (a connected non-compact simple Lie group has no non-trivial finite-dimensional unitary representation) and cite it, rather than offer a one-line proof.
