## Findings
1. [source] statement "Second-kind Volterra equations are always uniquely solvable" (line 79): the unit reports that Hassani's displayed operator-norm inequality on p. 544 "as printed, still carries the factor $\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}$, which for an operator norm has to be divided out". That is a faithful reading of the page — he writes $\lVert(\lambda K)^{n}\rVert\le\lvert\lambda\rvert^{n}\lvert M\rvert^{n}\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}(b-a)^{n}/n!$ — so under docs/contributing/source-authority.md it is not a defect in our text, and the unit draws the corrected form. A second slip on the next page belongs with it: Thm 18.1.2 writes the solution as $u(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{j}\int_{a}^{x}K^{j}(x,t)v(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$, but the $j=0$ term has no kernel ($K^{0}$ is the identity operator, not an integral kernel), so that term should be $v(x)$ and the sum over kernels should start at $j=1$ — which is exactly the form the unit writes, citing Riley–Hobson–Bence for it. I have recorded both with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`. No change to our text is called for; if anything, the unit could add four words noting that it starts the kernel sum at $m=1$ for the same reason.
2. [minor] statement "Abel's equation", part 2 (line 168), and proof, Step 2 (line 203): "Differentiating step 1 in $z$ gives the formula, at every point where the right-hand side of step 1 is differentiable" does not quite license the step it performs. Differentiating the left side of step 1 returns $y(z)$ only where $y$ is continuous at $z$ (or, in the integrable setting, at a Lebesgue point of $y$ and so only almost everywhere): differentiability of the right-hand side gives differentiability of $z\mapsto\int_{0}^{z}y$ at that point, but step 1 determines $y$ only up to a null set, so it cannot pin $y$'s value at one particular $z$. The proviso is therefore stated for the one case in which it is not sufficient. Note that in the case it *is* needed for — a continuous solution, which is what part 1's own parenthetical says any classical solution is — the conclusion is unconditional: $z\mapsto\int_{0}^{z}y$ is then differentiable everywhere with derivative $y(z)$, so the right-hand side is automatically differentiable and the inversion formula holds at every $z>0$. Fix: in part 2 and in Step 2 say "at every point where $y$ is continuous — in particular everywhere, for a continuous solution — and almost everywhere for a merely integrable one", and leave the right-hand side's differentiability where the remark already puts it (line 210), as the necessary condition on $f$ for a solution to exist at all.
3. [minor] statement "Abel's equation" (lines 153–167): the hypothesis under which part 1 is proved is given in prose after the display ("the proof below gets that from $\int_{0}^{z}\lvert y\rvert<\infty$ alone") rather than as a hypothesis of the block, and the opening "If $y$ is a solution" does not say in which class. Fix: open with "let $y$ be integrable on $[0,z]$ for every $z>0$", which is all the proof uses and is what the prose already concedes.
4. [minor] prerequisites: Step 1 of the Abel proof exchanges the order of a double integral with an integrable diagonal singularity and names Fubini for it (line 195). The justification given is the right one (absolute convergence, obtained by doing $\lvert y\rvert$ last and using the beta integral), but measure.fubini-tonelli is not in `prereqs`; it is reachable only transitively, via spec.beta-function $\to$ found.gaussian-integrals. Fix: list it, as found.change-of-variables, found.gaussian-integrals and fourier.tempered-distributions do.
5. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet (line 207): "so the *second*-kind equation with Abel's kernel is uniquely solvable for every $\lambda$ after all" is drawn from the summability of $\int_{0}^{x}\lvert K_{m}\rvert$, which gives convergence of the Neumann series and hence existence, but not uniqueness on its own. Uniqueness is one more line of the unit's own argument — the same bound reads $\lVert(\lambda\mathcal{K})^{m}\rVert\le(\lvert\lambda\rvert\sqrt{\pi b})^{m}/\Gamma(\tfrac{m}{2}+1)\to0$ on $C[0,b]$, so some power of $T$ is a contraction and steps 2–3 apply verbatim. Fix: add that half-sentence, since the point of the bullet is precisely that the conclusion survives where the hypotheses do not.
