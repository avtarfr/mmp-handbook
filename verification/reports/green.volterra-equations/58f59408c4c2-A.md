## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet (line 206): "What cannot be weakened is that the kernel be bounded *on the triangle*; Abel's kernel is not, and the theorem indeed fails for it" is false, and it contradicts a source this unit cites for exactly this point. arfken-2013 §21.3, p. 1067 says "In the Volterra case the Neumann series converges for all $\lambda$ as long as the kernel is square integrable" -- which is the role the unit's own frontmatter assigns to that page -- and square-integrable on the triangle is strictly weaker than bounded. The claim is also false on its own terms: for the second-kind Volterra equation with the Abel kernel $K(x,t)=(x-t)^{-1/2}$, the unit's *own* beta integral gives $K_{2}(x,t)=\int_{t}^{x}(x-s)^{-1/2}(s-t)^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}s=B(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)=\pi$, a bounded kernel, so the factorial estimate applies from the second iterate onward and the equation has exactly one continuous solution for every $\lambda$ -- unboundedness costs nothing here. The reason Abel's equation escapes the theorem is that it is of the **first** kind, which the bullet's own parenthesis says; what the bullet gets wrong is offering it as evidence that boundedness cannot be weakened. Fix: say that boundedness is what *this* proof uses (through $c=\max_{R}\lvert K\rvert$), that it can be relaxed to a weakly singular or merely square-integrable kernel because an iterate is then bounded -- citing arfken-2013 p. 1067 -- and that Abel's equation falls outside the theorem for being of the first kind, not for being singular.
