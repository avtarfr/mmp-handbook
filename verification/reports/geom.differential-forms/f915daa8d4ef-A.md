## Findings
1. [minor] proof "The shuffle formula…", Step 1: "Group the permutations by the *unordered* pair of sets $(\{\pi(1),\dots,\pi(k)\},\{\pi(k+1),\dots,\pi(k+l)\})$." The pair must be ordered — which block is the $\alpha$-block matters, and with the unordered reading a group would contain $2\,k!\,l!$ permutations and two shuffles when $k=l$, contradicting the very next sentence. The notation written is already an ordered pair; only the word is wrong. Fix: delete "unordered" (or write "ordered pair").
2. [minor] statement "Rules for the pullback", part 3 and proof Step 2: part 3 allows $u$ merely *continuous* (correctly following Lee's Prop. 14.20, p. 361), but Step 2 derives it from part 2, which is stated for $\omega \in \Omega^k(N)$, i.e. smooth forms. Nothing is actually wrong — the pullback identity is pointwise algebra and needs no smoothness of the coefficient — but as written the proof applies a smooth-coefficient rule to a continuous one. Fix: note in part 2 that the identity is pointwise and holds for continuous coefficients, as Lee's Lemma 14.16(c) is used on p. 362.
3. [minor] frontmatter `prereqs`: the second definition block uses the differential $dF_p$ (`geom.pullbacks-pushforwards`) and the vector-bundle language (`geom.vector-bundles`); both are `:ref`-ed at the point of use but neither is in `prereqs`. No *proof* depends on them, so this is presentational. Fix: add them, or state that the `:ref`s carry the dependency.
4. [minor] example, "Reading the answer": "The *triple* zero at $v = -u$ is not an accident of the algebra" — the explanation that follows (both coefficients $z$ and $x$ of $\omega$ vanish on the image of that line) accounts for the pullback vanishing there, but not for the order being three. Fix: either say "the zero at $v=-u$", or add that $z = u+v$ supplies one factor and $x = u^2-v^2 = (u+v)(u-v)$ a second, with the third coming from $dx\wedge dy$ and $dy\wedge dz$ themselves.

I checked the $\mathbb{R}^4$ counterexample symbolically: for $\alpha = dx^1\wedge dx^2 + dx^3\wedge dx^4$, $\alpha\wedge\alpha = 2\,dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3\wedge dx^4 \ne 0$, confirming both that the odd-degree rule does not extend and that $\alpha$ is not decomposable. I computed the example twice independently. Route 1: $dx\wedge dy = 4(u^2+v^2)du\wedge dv$, $dy\wedge dz = 2(v-u)du\wedge dv$, and $4(u+v)(u^2+v^2) - 2(u-v)(u^2-v^2) = 2(u+v)[2(u^2+v^2)-(u-v)^2] = 2(u+v)^3$, giving $54$ at $(1,2)$. Route 2: $F(1,2) = (-3,4,3)$, $dF(\partial_u) = (2,4,1)$, $dF(\partial_v) = (-4,2,1)$, $(dx\wedge dy)(a,b) = 20$, $(dy\wedge dz)(a,b) = 2$, $3(20) - 3(2) = 54$. The two tangent images along $v=-u$ are $(2u,-2u,1)$ and $(2u,2u,1)$, independent for $u \ne 0$, so the unit's reading of why the pullback vanishes there is correct.
