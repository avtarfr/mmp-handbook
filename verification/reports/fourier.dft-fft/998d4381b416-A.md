## Findings
1. [minor] method, step 2: "the frequencies it represents run $0,1,\dots$ up to $N/2$ and then *negative*: for $n>N/2$, bin $n$ carries the frequency $(n-N)/(N\tau)$". For even $N$ the bin $n=N/2$ is exactly the ambiguous one - it carries $+1/(2\tau)$ and $-1/(2\tau)$ at once, as the unit's own remark and part (c) of the example say - and the rule as worded silently assigns it to the positive side. Fix: say that for even $N$ the bin $N/2$ carries both, and that for real data it is real.
2. [minor] method, step 4: Fourier coefficients are called "dimensionless fractions of the signal". They carry the units of the signal (only their ratio to the signal is dimensionless); what is meant is that they are coefficients rather than a spectral density, which is what the rest of the step makes precise. Fix: "estimates of Fourier *coefficients* (in the units of the signal), not of the transform, which carries an extra factor $T$".
