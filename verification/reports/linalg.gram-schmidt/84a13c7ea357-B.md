Two slips. (a) The full factorization is already non-unique whenever `m > n`, rank hypothesis or not: the last `m - n` columns of `Q` can be replaced by any other orthonormal completion. What part 1 pins down is the *thin* factorization with positive diagonal, so the contrast "no longer unique" misattributes the loss of uniqueness to dropping the rank hypothesis rather than to passing from the thin form to the full one. (b) `R` here is `m x n`, but the definition block (line 53) defines "upper triangular" only for square `R` in `M_{n x n}`. Fix: say that the full factorization exists for any `A` (Householder gives it) and is never unique for `m > n`, and that dropping `rank A = n` additionally costs the uniqueness of the thin form; and extend the upper-triangular definition to rectangular matrices (`R_ik = 0` for `i > k`) or name it separately.
