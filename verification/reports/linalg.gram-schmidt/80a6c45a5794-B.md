## Findings
1. [minor] note "What is ours in that statement" (line 71): the sentence "Parts 1 and 3 are the content of the propositions cited on the block" overstates the sources for the *second* clause of part 3 ("any orthonormal basis of a subspace $W\subseteq V$ extends to one of $V$"). Lee p. 636 (Prop. B.40 with (B.12)-(B.13), and Exercise B.41) gives existence plus the nested spans only; Kreyszig pp. 157-158 and Hassani §2.2.2, pp. 33-35 give the construction and the nested spans only. None of the three records the extension of an orthonormal basis of a subspace — the unit derives it itself (the inductive check that the recipe reproduces an already-orthonormal initial segment, proof lines 117-118), and two later proofs in the handbook lean on it (QR part 3 here, and part 3/part 6 of linalg.hermitian-spectral-theorem). Fix: restrict the sentence to part 1 and the first clause of part 3, and add the extension clause to the list of what is the handbook's own.
2. [minor] statement "QR factorization", part 3 (line 190): "Without the rank hypothesis a factorization $A=QR$ ... still exists, but it is no longer unique" invites the reading that uniqueness holds in this wider form when $\operatorname{rank}A=n$. It never does: the diagonal of $R$ is not required to be positive here, so $(Q,R)\mapsto(QD, D^{*}R)$ with $D$ unitary diagonal always gives another factorization of the same form, and for $m>n$ the last $m-n$ columns of $Q$ are free as well. The two counterexamples given in the proof (line 232) are both rank-deficient, which reinforces the misreading. Fix: say that this form is not unique in any case, the rank hypothesis and the positive diagonal being what pin down the thin factorization of part 1.
