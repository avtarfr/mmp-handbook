## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, second bullet (line 136): the unit contradicts its own definition. The definition block (line 49) fixes "moderate decrease" as "$f$ is continuous **and** $\lvert f(x)\rvert \le A/(1+x^2)$", yet this bullet says of $f = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ that "It is of moderate decrease (it is bounded and compactly supported)" and then, three sentences later, that "continuity of $f$ fails too". Both cannot hold. The parenthetical justification only addresses the size bound and silently drops the continuity clause, so the defined term is used in a way the definition does not permit. The bullet's headline claim is also not demonstrated by this example: it is announced as showing that "dropping the second" hypothesis (decay of $\hat f$) breaks the formula, but the example violates the hypothesis on $f$ as well, so it does not isolate the role of $\hat f$. Fix: say plainly that $\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ satisfies the size bound but is not of moderate decrease because it is discontinuous, and that it shows the formula failing when the hypotheses are dropped, both the continuity of $f$ and the decay of $\hat f$ being lost; or keep the bullet's present claim and replace the example with one in which only the decay of $\hat f$ fails.
2. [minor] remark, second bullet (line 136): "Any repair must assign $f$ the average of its one-sided limits at the jumps, and this is the standard caution when the formula is applied to a function with a corner or a jump." A corner needs no such caution: for the triangle $f(x) = \max(0, 1-\lvert x\rvert)$ one has $\hat f(\xi) = (\sin\pi\xi/\pi\xi)^2 = O(\xi^{-2})$, both hypotheses hold and the formula is exact ($\sum_n f(n) = 1 = \sum_n\hat f(n)$, since $\hat f(n) = 0$ for $n \neq 0$). Fix: restrict the caution to jumps.
3. [minor] definition (line 49): the inline citation attaches the definition of moderate decrease — "$f$ is continuous and $\lvert f(x)\rvert\le A/(1+x^2)$" — to "Ch. 5, §3, p. 155". That page uses the notion and states that it suffices here, but the definition itself is at Ch. 5, §1.1, p. 131 ("A function $f$ defined on $\mathbb{R}$ is said to be of moderate decrease if $f$ is continuous and there exists a constant $A > 0$ ..."). Fix: cite p. 131 for the definition and keep p. 155 for the sufficiency claim, and add 131 to the frontmatter `pages`.
