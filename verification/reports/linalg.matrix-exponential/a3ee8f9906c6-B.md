## Findings
1. [blocking] note (line 271): the note says $\exp(tA)$ "carries the state at time $0$ to the state at
   time $t$, and it does so by doing nothing more than multiplying each eigendirection by
   $e^{\lambda t}$", and concludes that "Reading off the eigenvalues therefore reads off the dynamics
   — decay for $\operatorname{Re}\lambda < 0$, growth for $\operatorname{Re}\lambda > 0$, oscillation
   at frequency $\operatorname{Im}\lambda$". Both sentences are false for a defective $A$, and the
   unit refutes them itself twice. The `remark[Traps]` three paragraphs later (line 279) gives
   $A = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, whose only eigendirection is $(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$ with
   $\lambda = 0$: "multiplying each eigendirection by $e^{0 \cdot t}$" would give $\exp(tA) = I$,
   whereas the unit (correctly) computes $\exp(tA) = \begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, and the
   same remark says that this is growth even though every eigenvalue has
   $\operatorname{Re}\lambda = 0$. The unit's only worked example (lines 286-327) is also a defective
   matrix, whose $\exp(tA)$ has entries such as $(t - t^2)e^{t}$ that are not accounted for by the
   action on the one-dimensional eigenspace. The method's own step 3 (line 263) and the second
   application bullet (line 352) both state the correct picture. So an `original` block states as
   unconditional a claim that the rest of the unit exists to correct. Fix: restrict the sentence to
   the diagonalizable case and point forward, e.g. "...by multiplying each eigendirection by
   $e^{\lambda t}$ when $A$ has an eigenbasis; for defective $A$ each $e^{\lambda t}$ additionally
   carries a polynomial factor (step 3 of the method)", and hedge the dynamics trichotomy at
   $\operatorname{Re}\lambda = 0$, where the Jordan structure decides (as the trap says).

2. [minor] application, fourth bullet (line 356): "$\exp(t\Omega)$ is real orthogonal by the adjoints
   statement". That statement yields *unitary* for an anti-Hermitian exponent; orthogonality also
   needs that $\exp$ of a real matrix is real, which is immediate from the series but is not supplied
   by the statement invoked. Fix: add "and $\exp(t\Omega)$ is real because every term of its series
   is".

3. [source] hirsch-smale-devaney-2013, §6.4, p. 125: the displayed chain
   $\sum_{k\ge0}|a_{ij}(k)|/k! \le \sum_{k\ge0} n^{k-1}a^k/k! \le \dots \le \exp(na)$ does not hold
   term by term at $k = 0$ when $i = j$ and $n \ge 2$, since $|a_{ii}(0)| = 1 > 1/n$; the book's own
   bound $|a_{ij}(k)| \le n^{k-1}a^k$ is derived only for $k \ge 1$. The conclusion is unaffected and
   the unit does not inherit the flaw — its proof step 2 (line 99) splits off $k = 0$ explicitly and
   says in as many words that the book's route "bounds the entry by $e^{na}$ without separating the
   $k = 0$ term". Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add`; not a reason to fail the unit.
