## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Convergence", part 2 (line 40): the dominating bound is false. The unit says

   > Induction on $k$ gives $|(A^k)_{ij}| \le n^{k-1} a^k$ for $k \ge 1$, so each entry of $\sum_k A^k/k!$ is dominated by $\frac{1}{n}\sum_k (na)^k/k! = e^{na}/n < \infty$

   The induction is correct for $k \ge 1$, but the $k = 0$ term is then mis-weighted: the entry bound at $k=0$ is $|I_{ij}| \le 1$, while $\frac1n (na)^0/0! = \frac1n$. Summing the valid bounds gives $1 + (e^{na}-1)/n$, not $e^{na}/n$, and $e^{na}/n < 1 + (e^{na}-1)/n$ whenever $n \ge 2$. Concrete counterexamples, computed with `scipy.linalg.expm`:

   | $n$ | $a$ | $\max_{ij}|(\exp A)_{ij}|$ | claimed bound $e^{na}/n$ |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 2 | 0 | 1.000000 | 0.500000 |
   | 2 | 0.1 | 1.105171 | 0.610701 |
   | 3 | 0 | 1.000000 | 0.333333 |

   (taking $A = a I_n$, for which $a = \max_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ as the unit defines it). So the inequality fails not only at $A = 0$ but on an open set of matrices — every $A$ with $n \ge 2$ and $na$ small enough that $e^{na} < n$. The conclusion of part 2, that the exponential series always converges, is true and the argument is one line from being right; the displayed bound is simply wrong as printed. It also propagates: "The same bound shows that $\sum_k c_kA^k$ converges absolutely whenever $na < R$" rests on the same chain. Fix: bound the tail and the identity separately, $|(\exp A)_{ij}| \le 1 + \frac{e^{na}-1}{n}$, or state the weaker but clean $|(\exp A)_{ij}| \le e^{na}$ (true for all $n \ge 1$, since $1 + (e^{na}-1)/n \le e^{na}$).
2. [minor] definition "Power series in a matrix" (line 33): "The same construction defines $\sin A$, $\cos A$ and $(I - A)^{-1} = \sum_k A^k$" asserts an identity, not just a definition, and that identity needs $\rho(A) < 1$. The block's earlier "provided the series converges" does cover it (convergence of $\sum_k A^k$ forces $\rho(A) < 1$ and then the sum is indeed the inverse), but the reader meets the equation before meeting the proviso's consequence, and unlike $\sin$ and $\cos$ this one is a claim about an inverse. Fix: write "and, when $\rho(A) < 1$, $(I-A)^{-1} = \sum_k A^k$".
