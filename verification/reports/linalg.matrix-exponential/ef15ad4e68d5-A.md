## Findings
1. [blocking] idea (line 50, before the proof of "Convergence"): "On the circle itself the matrix contributes nothing either way, which is why two series of the same radius can behave differently at the same $A$." This is false, and it mis-explains the statement it introduces. Take $A = \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, so $A^k = \begin{pmatrix}1&k\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\rho(A) = 1$. With $c_0 = 0$, $c_k = 1/k^2$ (radius $R = 1$, the very coefficient sequence used in part 2) the $(1,2)$ entry of the matrix series is $\sum_k k/k^2 = \sum_k 1/k$, which diverges, although the scalar series converges at every $|z| = 1$; at $A = I$ the same coefficients converge. So on the circle the matrix structure is decisive, not inert: it is exactly the polynomial factor $k^{n-1}$ of step 1 that no longer has a geometric margin to absorb it. The idea also makes part 2 look weaker than it is: as written it suggests that at $\rho(A) = R$ the answer is read off the scalar series on the circle, whereas the statement's point is that $\rho(A) = R$ decides nothing. Fix: replace the sentence with one saying that at $\rho(A) = R$ the polynomial factor from the Jordan structure is no longer beaten by the geometric margin, so neither the spectrum nor the coefficients alone settle the question; $A = I$ is the degenerate case in which the matrix structure drops out entirely, which is why the two coefficient sequences of part 2 can be distinguished there.

2. [minor] proof of "When the product rule holds", step 4 (line 243): "The two differ, as part 2 requires they must." Part 2 only forces the identity to fail at some $(s,t)$ in every neighbourhood of the origin; it says nothing about the particular point $(s,t) = (1, \tfrac12)$, which lies far from $0$. The computation itself is correct (I reproduced both matrices), but the inference attributed to part 2 is not one part 2 makes. Fix: say that the failure at $(1,\tfrac12)$ shows directly that the identity is not an identity in $s$ and $t$, and that part 2 guarantees failure arbitrarily close to the origin as well.

3. [minor] idea (line 124, before the proof of "Properties of the exponential"): "Only the product rule needs more" than algebra on partial sums plus a limit. Property 5's proof needs more too — the quantitative tail bound $\bigl|\bigl(\tfrac{\exp(hA)-I}{h} - A\bigr)_{il}\bigr| \le n a^2 e^{na}|h|$, which comes from the entrywise estimate and not from continuity of a fixed linear map. Fix: name property 5 alongside the product rule as the second place where an estimate, not just a limit, is needed.

4. [minor] method, step 4 (line 264): "That is $n$ linear conditions on $n$ unknowns" does not by itself determine $\alpha_0, \dots, \alpha_{n-1}$; the recipe works because the confluent Vandermonde system built from the distinct eigenvalues with their multiplicities is nonsingular. Fix: add that clause, or point to where it is proved.

5. [minor] application, last bullet (line 358): the argument that $P(t) = \exp(tQ) = e^{-ct}\exp\bigl(t(Q+cI)\bigr)$ is entrywise non-negative uses that every term $t^k(Q+cI)^k/k!$ has non-negative entries, which needs $t \ge 0$. That is the only range of interest for a transition matrix, but the restriction is not stated. Fix: say "for $t \ge 0$".
