## Findings
1. [blocking] `remark`, bullet "Conjugacy is a relation, not a symmetry" (line 127): the reason given for the conclusion is a false identity. The text says "the pair $(v,u)$ satisfies $v_x = u_y$, which is the wrong sign". It does not: from the Cauchy-Riemann equations for $(u,v)$ we have $u_y = -v_x$, i.e. the pair $(v,u)$ satisfies $v_x = -u_y$, and what the Cauchy-Riemann equations would *require* of $(v,u)$ is $v_x = u_y$ — the condition that fails. The unit's own example confirms it: for $z^2$, $u = x^2-y^2$, $v = 2xy$, so $v_x = 2y$ and $u_y = -2y$, and $v_x = u_y$ would force $y = 0$. The bullet's conclusion ($u$ is the conjugate of $-v$, not of $v$) is correct, and the surrounding claims check out: the pair $(-v, u)$ does satisfy $(-v)_x = u_y$ and $(-v)_y = -u_x$. Fix: "the pair $(v,u)$ would have to satisfy $v_x = u_y$, whereas what it does satisfy is $v_x = -u_y$: the sign is wrong."

2. [minor] `proof`, part 2, after the two first-order expansions (line 101): "This is the only place continuity of the partials is used" is contradicted at the end of the same part, where "$f' = u_x + iv_x$ is continuous because $u_x$ and $v_x$ are" uses the same hypothesis again. The same overstatement reappears in the `remark`'s second bullet ("used once"). Fix: "This is the one place continuity of the partials is needed for differentiability; it is used once more below, for the continuity of $f'$."

3. [minor] `remark`, first bullet, the off-origin computation (line 117): the tangential increments are written with $\theta$ ($dz = ir_0e^{i\theta}d\theta$, $df = -3ir_0e^{-3i\theta}d\theta$) while the radial ones and both conclusions are written at $\theta_0$. The arithmetic is right — the two difference quotients are $e^{-4i\theta_0}$ and $-3e^{-4i\theta_0}$ — but the subscript is dropped mid-sentence. Fix: write $\theta_0$ throughout.

4. [minor] `statement`, part 3 (line 60): the hypothesis "if in addition $u$ and $v$ have continuous second partial derivatives" is ours, not the source's. Ahlfors (p. 25) gets harmonicity from the later theorem that $f'$ is analytic, so that $u, v$ are automatically $C^\infty$ and no extra hypothesis is needed; Arfken's Eq. (11.13) (p. 473) assumes nothing extra either. The statement as written is true, and assuming $C^2$ rather than forward-referencing an unproved theorem is a reasonable choice for a handbook, but nothing tells the reader that the hypothesis is removable. Fix: add one sentence to the `remark` saying that the $C^2$ hypothesis is avoidable once the analyticity of $f'$ is available (:ref to the Cauchy-integral-formula unit), and that it is imposed here only to keep the unit self-contained.
