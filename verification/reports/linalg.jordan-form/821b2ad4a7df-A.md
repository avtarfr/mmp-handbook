## Findings
1. [source] remark "Conventions" (line 227) / application (line 350): Grimmett and Stirzaker, §6.6, p. 270, display the Jordan form with exactly one block per distinct eigenvalue, of size equal to that eigenvalue's multiplicity, which is correct only when every eigenvalue has geometric multiplicity 1. The unit reports this accurately, flags it as the special case, and says it follows Dummit and Foote Thm 23 (p. 493) instead; this is a limitation of the source, not of our text. Already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (row for linalg.jordan-form / grimmett-stirzaker-2020), so no new entry was added.
2. [minor] example, step 4 (line 308): "The eigenvalue $-1$ is simple, giving one block of size $1$." The listed prerequisite linalg.eigenvalues-eigenvectors defines "simple" as $g(\lambda) = 1$ (geometric multiplicity), under which reading the conclusion "of size $1$" would not follow; the inference here needs $a(-1) = 1$, which step 1 of the example does establish. Fix: write "the eigenvalue $-1$ has algebraic multiplicity $1$", or avoid the word "simple" in this unit.
3. [minor] definition block, Jordan chains (line 79): "Written as the columns of a matrix in this order, a chain is exactly a basis on which $A$ acts as $J_s(\lambda)$" asserts the linear independence of the chain vectors before anything has established it. It is true (and follows from the rank-$t$ grading, and from step 3 of the proof), but at this point it is used without justification. Fix: add a clause, e.g. "the $x_t$ are independent because a vanishing combination killed by the lowest surviving power of $A - \lambda I$ forces its coefficients to vanish in turn".
4. [minor] method, "Why the rule is the right one" (line 250): the argument uses the quotient space $\ker T^{s'} / \ker T^{s'-1}$ and its dimension. Quotient spaces do not appear in the listed prerequisites (linalg.vector-spaces-bases and linalg.linear-maps-matrices do not introduce them) and are not uniformly part of a first linear-algebra course. Fix: either phrase it as a dimension count on $T^{s'-1}(\ker T^{s'})$, which has dimension $d_{s'} - d_{s'-1}$ by rank-nullity, or name the prerequisite.
5. [minor] statement "Powers and power series of a Jordan block" (line 203): "(and for all $k \ge 1$ when $A$ is invertible, the omission being vacuous)". The rewriting $\binom{k}{i}\lambda^{k-i} = (\binom{k}{i}\lambda^{-i})\lambda^{k}$ is valid for every $k \ge 0$ once $\lambda \neq 0$, so for invertible $A$ the formula in fact holds for all $k \ge 0$, $A^{0} = I$ included. The stated claim is weaker, not wrong.

## Checked
I re-derived every step of the main proof: the stalling of $\ker T^{j}$ and the Fitting splitting $V = \ker T^{d} \oplus \operatorname{im} T^{d}$ (step 1, including the directness argument and the invertibility of $T$ on the image); the induction of step 2 (that $K \neq \{0\}$, that $\chi_{A|_K} = (x-\lambda_1)^{\dim K}$ via the flag basis, the invertibility of $(\lambda_1-\lambda)I + T$ on $K$ with the displayed finite Neumann inverse, the inclusion $G_{\lambda_j} \subseteq R$, and the identification $\ker(A-\lambda_j I)^{\dim R} \cap R = G_{\lambda_j}$); the chain-basis induction of step 3 (the dimension count $\sum(s_i+1)+(k-r) = d$ and the two-stage independence argument, both of which are correct as written); step 4; and step 5, where I checked $\dim\ker N_s^{j} = \min(j,s)$, the resulting $d_j(\lambda) = \sum_{\text{blocks}}\min(j,s)$, the difference identity giving the "size $\ge j$" and "size exactly $j$" counts of the structure statement, and the minimal-polynomial argument $q(J_s(\lambda)) = \sum_i c_i N_s^{i}$. I also checked the whole correctness argument for the method's chain-selection rule (the equivalence "$v \notin \ker T^{s'-1} + U$ iff $T^{s'-1}v$ is outside the span of the bottoms", both directions; the "independent bottoms imply independent chains" argument with $q = \max_i(s_i - c_i)$; and the availability count), and I verified its $J_3(0)\oplus J_2(0)$ counterexample by hand ($d_1,d_2,d_3 = 2,4,5$; $v' = a_2+b_1$ passes the three weaker tests and produces a rank-4 list, and is rejected by the stated rule while $b_2$ is accepted). The binomial power formula, the entrywise form with its exclusion of $q-p > k$, the finite Taylor form $f(J_m(\lambda)) = \sum_{i<m} f^{(i)}(\lambda)N_m^{i}/i!$, and both limit criteria ($A^{k}\to 0$; boundedness, including the $\binom{k}{m-1}$ corner-entry argument) were checked line by line.

Numerically and symbolically with sympy I confirmed the whole worked example: $\chi_A = (x-3)^3(x+1)$, $\operatorname{tr}A = 8$, $\det A = -27$; the displayed $B$ and $B^{2}$ and their ranks 2 and 1, giving $d_1(3)=2$, $d_2(3)=3$ and blocks of sizes 2 and 1; $\mu_A = (x-3)^2(x+1)$ with the displayed non-zero $B(A+I)$ and $B^{2}(A+I)=0$; $\ker B = \operatorname{span}\{(-1,1,0,0),(1,0,1,0)\}$ and $\ker(A+I) = \operatorname{span}\{(0,0,1,1)\}$; $\det P = -2$ and $AP = PJ$ exactly; $\Pi^2=\Pi$, $\Pi'^2=\Pi'$, $\Pi+\Pi'=I$, $\operatorname{rank}\Pi = 3$ with $B^{2}\Pi = 0$ and $(A+I)\Pi' = 0$ (so the images are indeed $G_3$ and $G_{-1}$), and $M = B\Pi$ with $M^2 = 0$. The closed form $A^{k} = 3^{k}\Pi + k3^{k-1}M + (-1)^{k}\Pi'$ was verified symbolically in $k$ against $PJ^{k}P^{-1}$ (zero difference), and the stated checkable numbers reproduce: $A^{2}$ matches the displayed matrix, and $(A^{10})_{31} = 59049 - 393660 - 1 = -334612$ agrees with direct computation.

I read the cited pages. Dummit and Foote pp. 492-494 give the elementary Jordan matrix and Jordan canonical form as stated, Thm 23 with the hypothesis that $F$ contains all the eigenvalues, uniqueness up to a permutation of the blocks, Cor 24(1) and Cor 25; the unit's statements are faithful. Hirsch, Smale and Devaney p. 94 give the real canonical form with blocks (i) real Jordan blocks and (ii) $C_2 = \binom{\ \alpha\ \ \beta}{-\beta\ \ \alpha}$ on the diagonal with $I_2$ one place above, $\beta \neq 0$, exactly as the remark reports, and §5.6 p. 100 is the genericity discussion. Hassani p. 539 shows the superdiagonal convention and notes that different blocks may carry the same eigenvalue. Grimmett and Stirzaker pp. 269-270 give the Perron-Frobenius statement the application uses (eigenvalue 1 once for $d=1$, all others of modulus $<1$) and the bound $|p_{ij}(n)-\pi_j| \le c\mu^{n}$. Lee pp. 673-674 give the defective $2\times2$ case with $(A-\lambda I_2)Z_1 = Z_0$ and the second solution $e^{\lambda t}(tZ_0+Z_1)$, as quoted. Our own extension of the Markov bound to $c\,n^{s-1}\varrho^{\,n}$, the crossover $n \approx (s-1)/\log(1/\varrho)$, and the general chain solutions $u_r(t) = e^{\lambda t}\sum_{i<r}(t^{i}/i!)x_{r-i}$ I derived independently and they are correct.
