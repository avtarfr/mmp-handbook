## Findings
1. [blocking] method (line 239): the selection rule for chains of less than maximal length is insufficient, so the recipe can produce a dependent set. Step 4 says to choose, for each smaller size $s'$, a vector "$v \in \ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'}$ outside $\ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'-1}$ and outside the span of what has been produced". Those three conditions do not force the new chain to be independent of the chains already laid down, because the *lower* members $(A-\lambda I)^t v$ of the new chain may fall inside the span already obtained even when $v$ itself does not. Concrete failure (verified in sympy): take $\lambda = 0$ and the nilpotent $N$ on $F^5$ with $Ne_3 = e_2$, $Ne_2 = e_1$, $Ne_1 = 0$, $Ne_5 = e_4$, $Ne_4 = 0$ (block sizes $3,2$; nullities $d_1=2$, $d_2=4$, $d_3=5$, so step 3 correctly predicts one block of size 3 and one of size 2). Longest first, take $v = e_3$, producing $U = \operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_3)$. Now take $v' = e_2 + e_4$. Then $v' \in \ker N^2$, $Nv' = e_1 \neq 0$ so $v' \notin \ker N$, and $v' \notin U$ since $e_4 \notin U$ — every condition the method states is met. But its chain is $Nv' = e_1$, $v'$, and $e_1 \in U$: the five vectors $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_1,e_2+e_4$ span only a 4-dimensional space, so $P$ is singular. The accompanying justification ("step 3 of the proof guarantees that such choices exist in the numbers that step 3 of this recipe predicts") is therefore also wrong as stated: step 3 of the proof is an induction that builds a chain basis by lifting through $N(V)$, and it does not certify this greedy rule. Fix: require, as the method already does for a second chain of the *same* length, that the whole chain of $v$ be independent of the vectors already found — equivalently, choose $v \in \ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'}$ lying outside $\ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'-1} + \operatorname{span}(\text{vectors already produced})$ (in the example this correctly excludes $v' = e_2+e_4$, since $\ker N + U = \operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)$ contains it, and admits $v' = e_5$). The worked example is unaffected: its blocks at $\lambda = 3$ have sizes $2$ and $1$, and for $s' = 1$ the stated rule happens to be sufficient.

2. [minor] statement "Powers and power series of a Jordan block" (line 187): the entrywise formula $(J_m(\lambda)^k)_{pq} = \binom{k}{q-p}\lambda^{k-q+p}$ is ill-formed when $\lambda = 0$ and $q - p > k$, where the factor $0^{k-q+p}$ has a negative exponent; the stated convention $\binom{k}{i} = 0$ for $i > k$ makes the intended value $0$ but the product is $0 \cdot \text{undefined}$. The same gap affects the grouped form $(A^k)_{pq} = \sum_\lambda \varphi^\lambda_{pq}(k)\lambda^k$ at line 199, which cannot be obtained by pulling $\lambda^{-i}$ out of $\binom{k}{i}\lambda^{k-i}$ when $0 \in \sigma(A)$. Fix: say the entry is $0$ whenever $q - p > k$ before giving the closed form, and either exclude $\lambda = 0$ from the grouped formula or state it with $\lambda^{k-i}$ rather than $\varphi(k)\lambda^k$.

3. [minor] definition "Generalized eigenvectors..." (line 81): "the **minimal polynomial** $\mu_A$ of $A$ is the monic polynomial of least degree with $\mu_A(A) = 0$" presupposes that some non-zero polynomial annihilates $A$, which is not stated and is not among the listed prerequisites. The unit in fact supplies one only later (step 5 exhibits $\mu_J = \prod_\lambda (x-\lambda)^{s(\lambda)}$). Fix: add the one-line reason — $I, A, \dots, A^{n^2}$ are $n^2+1$ vectors in the $n^2$-dimensional space of matrices, so a non-trivial annihilating relation exists — or note that existence follows from the proof below.

Fix: add that $A^k x = \lambda^k x$ is already unbounded when $|\lambda| > 1$, reusing the eigenvector of the previous sentence.

5. [minor] frontmatter, sources (line 11): the locator gives "Cor 24, pp. 493-494". Corollary 24 and its proof are both wholly on p. 493; p. 494 opens with Corollary 25, which is cited separately. Fix: "Cor 24, p. 493".

6. [source] remark "Where the hypotheses are used" / application (lines 226, 337): Grimmett and Stirzaker, §6.6, p. 270, present the Jordan canonical form as one block $J_r$ per distinct eigenvalue $\lambda_r$, of size $k_r$ equal to that eigenvalue's multiplicity, which holds only when every eigenvalue has geometric multiplicity 1. The unit reports this accurately, labels it as the special case, and says it follows Dummit and Foote's several-block Thm 23 instead, which is the correct handling of conflicting sources. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`; not a fault of our text.
