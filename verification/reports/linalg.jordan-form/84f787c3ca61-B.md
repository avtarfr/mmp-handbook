## Findings

1. [blocking] :::method step 4 (line 237): the selection rule for the *shorter* chains is
   insufficient, and a reader who follows it literally can produce a linearly dependent list, hence
   a singular $P$. The text says: "each time choosing $v \in \ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'}$ outside
   $\ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'-1}$ and outside the span of what has been produced". Being outside the
   span of the vectors already produced is not enough, because what must be new is the *bottom* of
   the new chain, not its top.

   Explicit counterexample (verified in sympy, below): $\lambda = 0$, $A = J_3(0) \oplus J_2(0)$ on
   $\mathbb{R}^5$ with basis $a_1,a_2,a_3,b_1,b_2$, $Aa_3=a_2$, $Aa_2=a_1$, $Aa_1=0$, $Ab_2=b_1$,
   $Ab_1=0$. Steps 1-3 of the recipe give $d_1=2, d_2=4, d_3=5$, so block sizes $3$ and $2$ -- the
   right answer. Step 4: for $s=3$ take $v=a_3$, producing $a_1,a_2,a_3$. For $s'=2$ take
   $v' = a_2+b_1$. It satisfies every condition the recipe imposes: $A^2v'=0$, $Av'=a_1\neq 0$ so
   $v'\notin\ker A$, and $v'\notin\operatorname{span}\{a_1,a_2,a_3\}$. Its chain is
   $Av'=a_1,\ v'=a_2+b_1$, and the assembled list
   $a_1,a_2,a_3,a_1,a_2+b_1$ has rank $4$, not $5$. The sentence that follows ("step 3 of the proof
   guarantees that such choices exist in the numbers that step 3 of this recipe predicts") therefore
   asserts something false of the rule as stated. This is our own material: the rule is not in
   Hirsch-Smale-Devaney, who only ever back-solve a single chain.

   Note the unit already states the correct criterion two sentences earlier, for a *further chain of
   the same length* ("whose chain is independent of the vectors already found"), and then weakens it
   when moving to smaller sizes; so the defect is a local inconsistency as well as an error.

   Fix: use one criterion for both cases. Require
   $v \notin \ker(A-\lambda I)^{s'-1} + \operatorname{span}\{\text{vectors already produced}\}$,
   equivalently require the new chain's bottom vector $(A-\lambda I)^{s'-1}v$ to be linearly
   independent of the bottom vectors of the chains already built. In the counterexample this
   excludes $a_2+b_1$ (it lies in $\ker A + \operatorname{span}\{a_1,a_2,a_3\}$) and admits $b_2$,
   which gives the chain $b_1,b_2$ and a basis. The existence of such $v$ in the predicted numbers
   is exactly what step 3 of the proof delivers, so the sentence that cites step 3 then becomes true.

2. [minor] :::statement "Powers and power series of a Jordan block" (line 183): the entrywise form
   $\bigl(J_m(\lambda)^k\bigr)_{pq}=\binom{k}{q-p}\lambda^{k-q+p}$ is ill-defined in one degenerate
   case the stated conventions do not cover: when $\lambda=0$ and $k<q-p$ the factor is $0^{\text{negative}}$,
   multiplied by a vanishing binomial coefficient. (For $\lambda\neq 0$ the zero coefficient settles
   it; for $\lambda=0$, $k=q-p$ the convention $0^0=1$ settles it, correctly.) The same reading
   issue recurs in the $\sum_\lambda \varphi^{\lambda}_{pq}(k)\lambda^{k}$ display at $k=0$ with a
   zero eigenvalue. Fix: add "and the entry is $0$ whenever $q-p>k$" to the conventions.

3. [minor] :::proof step 2 (line 130): the argument uses "with $T$ nilpotent, hence triangular with
   zero diagonal in a suitable basis" as a known fact, with no justification at the point of use. It
   is not circular -- step 3 proves a chain basis for a nilpotent map and step 3 does not depend on
   step 2 -- but a self-contained proof that advertises itself as using "nothing beyond rank and
   nullity" should say where it comes from. Fix: either forward-reference step 3, or note in one
   clause that a basis adapted to the filtration $\ker T\subseteq\ker T^2\subseteq\cdots$ is strictly
   triangularising.
