## Findings
1. [blocking] example, part B, "Where the slack is" (line 248): "Averaged over a period the kinetic and potential parts are equal" is false for this solution. With $u=(1-\cos t)\sin x$ on $(0,\pi)$, $\int_0^\pi u_t^2dx = \tfrac\pi2\sin^2 t$ and $\int_0^\pi u_x^2dx = \tfrac\pi2(1-\cos t)^2$; averaging over $[0,2\pi]$ gives kinetic $\pi/4$ and potential $3\pi/4$, a ratio of $1:3$, not $1:1$ (checked symbolically with sympy). Equipartition holds for a free standing wave, but this solution is forced and carries a non-zero time-mean displacement. The conclusion drawn from it survives for a different reason — the time average of the *ratio* $\|u_t\|^2/\mathcal E = (1+\cos t)/2$ is exactly $1/2$, and the average of $\|u_t\|/\sqrt{\mathcal E} = |\cos(t/2)|$ over $[0,\pi]$ is $2/\pi$, which is precisely the reciprocal of the quoted slack factor $\pi/2$ — so the sentence is a false premise in front of a true conclusion. Fix: replace it with the ratio statement, e.g. "the ratio $\|u_t\|^2/\mathcal E = (1+\cos t)/2$ averages to $\tfrac12$ over a period, so $\|u_t\|$ is typically about $\sqrt{\mathcal E/2}$; in fact $\|u_t\|/\sqrt{\mathcal E}$ averages to $2/\pi$ over $[0,\pi]$, which is exactly the factor $\pi/2$ above".
2. [blocking] proof of the heat estimate, Step 2 (line 107) and remark, second bullet (line 200): "This is the only place the boundary condition is used, and a Neumann condition $\partial u/\partial n = 0$ would do just as well" is wrong, and it contradicts the unit's own Step 5. Step 5 asserts "for each fixed $t$ the function $u(\cdot,t)$ lies in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ — it is $C^1$ on $\overline\Omega$ and vanishes on $\partial\Omega$", which is a second, essential use of the Dirichlet condition, and it is exactly what part (d) rests on. Under a Neumann condition part (d) is false: on any bounded $\Omega$, $u\equiv1$ with $f\equiv0$ and $\partial u/\partial n = 0$ solves the problem, $N(t)=|\Omega|^{1/2}$ for all $t$, and (d) would force $N(t)\le e^{-k\lambda t}N(0)$ with $\lambda>0$. The same slip is repeated in the remark bullet, which says "Dirichlet and Neumann conditions do it" without qualification. Parts (a), (b) and (c) are indeed indifferent between Dirichlet and Neumann; (d) is not. Fix: in Step 2 write "this is the only place the boundary condition is used in (a)–(c), and a Neumann condition would do just as well for those three", and in the remark bullet add that (d) needs the Dirichlet condition, because the Poincaré inequality fails on $H^1(\Omega)$ (constants), with $u\equiv1$ as the witness.
