## Findings
1. [minor] proof (heat), Step 5, sub-step (5b) (line 135): the stated restriction $\sigma < \operatorname{dist}(K_\delta,\partial\Omega)$ is enough for the support claim but not for the justification that follows. The weak-derivative identity is applied with the test function $y\mapsto\eta_\sigma(x-y)$, whose support is $\overline{B(x,\sigma)}$; for $x$ in the closed $\sigma$-neighbourhood of $K_\delta$ this is contained in $\Omega$ only when $\sigma \le \tfrac12\operatorname{dist}(K_\delta,\partial\Omega)$. The conclusion is unaffected (only small $\sigma$ is used, and the limit $\sigma\to0$ is taken). Fix: require $\sigma < \tfrac12\operatorname{dist}(K_\delta,\partial\Omega)$, or note that the zero-extension of $w_\delta$ is Lipschitz with compact support on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so test functions in $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ are admissible and no constraint beyond smallness is needed.
2. [minor] proof (heat), Step 5, sub-step (5c) (line 163): "$\Omega$ is covered by countably many such neighbourhoods" is not what the argument needs or gets — the neighbourhoods are neighbourhoods of points of $\{w=0,\ \nabla w\neq0\}$ and do not cover $\Omega$. Fix: "the set $\{w=0,\ \nabla w\neq0\}$ is covered by countably many such neighbourhoods (Lindelöf), and in each of them $\{w=0\}$ is a $C^1$ hypersurface, hence null".
3. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 238): "Clause 3 fails in any pair of norms in which the data tend to zero" also needs the solution norms not to tend to zero; as written the claim quantifies over the solution norm without constraining it. Fix: "in any pair of norms for which $\|g_m\|\to0$ while $\|u_m(\cdot,0)\|$ does not", which the $L^2$ computation just given exhibits.
4. [minor] definition block (line 37): "with regularisation and with care" is attributed to Stakgold and Holst, but p. 66 says only that ill-posed problems "need careful physical interpretation" and that their solution "may be subtle", deferring to their Chapter 6; the word regularisation is ours. Fix: attribute only the caveat to them and mark regularisation as the standard response, or move it to the application block where it already appears as our own.
5. [minor] front matter, prereqs (line 7): the dominated convergence theorem is invoked by name in (5c), and Minkowski's integral inequality in (5b); neither is in the listed prereqs. Both are reachable transitively (measure.lp-spaces lists measure.dominated-convergence, and states Minkowski in its norm form), so this is at most a listing matter. Fix: add measure.dominated-convergence to prereqs, or cite it with :ref at the point of use.
