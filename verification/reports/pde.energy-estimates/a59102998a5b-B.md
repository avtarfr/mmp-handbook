1. [blocking] proof of the heat estimate, Step 5 (line 125): the displayed inequality
   $$\|w_\delta - w\|^2_{H^1} \;\le\; \int_{\{|w|\le\delta\}}\bigl(|w|^2+|\nabla w|^2\bigr)dx$$
   is false. With $w_\delta = (w-\delta)^+ - (-w-\delta)^+$ one has $w_\delta - w = -\delta$ on
   $\{w>\delta\}$ and $+\delta$ on $\{w<-\delta\}$, not $0$; only the gradient part is supported in
   $\{|w|\le\delta\}$. The $L^2$ part therefore carries an extra $\delta^2\,|\{|w|>\delta\}|$ that the
   right-hand side omits. Checked numerically on $\Omega=(0,\pi)$, $w=\sin x$ (which satisfies the
   block's hypotheses: $C^1$ on $\overline\Omega$, zero on $\partial\Omega$): at $\delta=0.1$ the true
   $\|w_\delta-w\|^2_{H^1} = 0.229747$ against a claimed bound of $0.200335$, the gap being exactly
   $\delta^2|\{|w|>\delta\}| = 0.029413$; same failure at $\delta=0.3$ ($0.837284$ vs $0.609385$) and
   $\delta=0.03$ ($0.062782$ vs $0.060009$). The *conclusion* $w_\delta \to w$ in $H^1(\Omega)$ is
   nevertheless true and the rest of Step 5 is sound, so the repair is local.
   Fix: bound instead by $\delta^2|\Omega| + \int_{\{|w|\le\delta\}}|\nabla w|^2dx$ (legitimate because
   $|w_\delta - w| \le \delta$ everywhere and $\Omega$ is bounded), and send both terms to zero — the
   first because $\Omega$ has finite measure, the second by dominated convergence to
   $\int_{\{w=0\}}|\nabla w|^2 = 0$, exactly as the block already argues.

   integration he then performs is over all of 3-space, with the divergence term discarded because the
   derivatives of $u$ decay as $|x|\to\infty$ — not over a bounded domain with a boundary term killed by
