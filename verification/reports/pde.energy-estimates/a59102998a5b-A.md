## Findings
1. [blocking] proof of the heat estimate, Step 5 (line 125): the displayed inequality
   $\|w_\delta - w\|^2_{H^1} \le \int_{\{|w|\le\delta\}}(|w|^2+|\nabla w|^2)\,dx$
   is false. With $w_\delta=(w-\delta)^+-(-w-\delta)^+$ one has $w_\delta-w=-\delta$ on $\{w>\delta\}$ and $+\delta$ on $\{w<-\delta\}$, so the $L^2$ part of the left-hand side is at least $\delta^2\,|\{|w|>\delta\}|$, which the right-hand side — an integral over the complementary set — does not bound. Only the gradient part obeys the stated bound, since $\nabla w_\delta-\nabla w=-\nabla w\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|w|\le\delta\}}$ a.e. Concretely, on $\Omega=(0,1)$ with $w=\sin\pi x$ and $\delta=0.1$ the left side is $0.63684$ and the claimed right side $0.62748$ (and $0.06293$ against $0.06283$ at $\delta=0.01$); the inequality fails for every $\delta$ here. The conclusion the step needs is nevertheless true, so this is a repairable gap, not a wrong theorem. Fix: split the two parts — $\|w_\delta-w\|_{L^2}\le\delta\,|\Omega|^{1/2}\to0$ because $|w_\delta-w|\le\delta$ pointwise, and keep the displayed bound for $\|\nabla w_\delta-\nabla w\|^2_{L^2}$ alone, which then tends to $\int_{\{w=0\}}|\nabla w|^2=0$ exactly as written.
