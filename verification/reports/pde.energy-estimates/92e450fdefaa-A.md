## Findings
1. [blocking] example, part B, third bullet "Where the slack is" (line 248): "Averaged over a period the kinetic and potential parts are equal, so $\|u_t\|$ is typically about $\sqrt{\mathcal{E}/2}$" is false for this solution, and the number it is offered to explain does not follow from it. With $u=(1-\cos t)\sin x$ the mean kinetic part over $[0,2\pi]$ is $\tfrac\pi2\langle\sin^2t\rangle=\pi/4$ and the mean potential part is $\tfrac\pi2\langle(1-\cos t)^2\rangle=3\pi/4$ — a ratio of $1:3$, not $1:1$, because the steady source gives the displacement a constant offset. Hence $\langle\|u_t\|^2\rangle=\langle\mathcal{E}\rangle/4$, so $\|u_t\|$ is typically $\sqrt{\mathcal{E}}/2$, not $\sqrt{\mathcal{E}/2}$ (I confirmed both averages numerically). Equipartition is a property of a free standing wave, which this is not. Fix: the exact statement is available and is nicer — on $[0,\pi]$, $\|u_t\|=|\sin t|\sqrt{\pi/2}$ and $\sqrt{\mathcal{E}}=\sqrt{\pi(1-\cos t)}$, so $\|u_t\|=\cos(t/2)\sqrt{\mathcal{E}}$ exactly, and the overshoot at $t=\pi$ is the reciprocal of its mean, $\bigl(\tfrac1\pi\int_0^\pi\cos(t/2)\,dt\bigr)^{-1}=(2/\pi)^{-1}=\pi/2$, which is precisely the factor reported in the previous bullet.
