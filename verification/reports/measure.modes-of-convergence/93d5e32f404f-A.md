## Findings
1. [minor] definition "The four modes" (the inequality note): the unit asserts that *both* Folland and Grimmett–Stirzaker write convergence/Cauchy in measure with the strict inequality $|f_n-f|>\varepsilon$. The Grimmett–Stirzaker half is right (Def. (1c), p. 352, clean text layer, strict $>$). The Folland half is not supportable from the corpus: the extraction of folland-1999 contains no $\ge$ or $\le$ glyph anywhere on pp. 45–64, rendering all of them as $>$ and $<$ — visible on p. 61, where the typewriter index condition comes out as "0 < j < 2^k" although the book has $0 \le j < 2^k$ (the unit itself writes $\le$ there). Folland's §2.4 definitions in fact use $\ge$, so the attribution is probably backwards. Nothing mathematical turns on it: the unit proves the two forms equivalent, and the only place the strict form is used, the sets $E_n(k)$ in the proof of part 4, is internally consistent (step 4 correctly concludes $\le 1/k$, not $< 1/k$). Fix: attribute the strict form to Grimmett–Stirzaker only, and say of Folland merely that conventions differ between books.
2. [minor] example "A one-parameter family", the "In measure" paragraph: "for $\varepsilon \ge n^{\alpha}$ it is empty" is false at $\varepsilon = n^{\alpha}$, where $\{f_n \ge \varepsilon\} = (0,1/n)$ under the unit's own $\ge$ convention; the boundary case belongs to the first clause. It also contradicts the correct boundary used eleven lines later, "$\mu(\{f_n \ge \varepsilon\}) = 10^{-4}$ for every $\varepsilon \le 100$" (with $\alpha = 1/2$, $n = 10^4$, $n^{\alpha} = 100$). The conclusion $\mu(\{f_n\ge\varepsilon\}) \le 1/n$ is unaffected. Fix: "for $\varepsilon > n^{\alpha}$ it is empty", and "for $0 < \varepsilon \le n^{\alpha}$ the set is $(0,1/n)$".
3. [minor] statement, part 3: "Consequently" hides one step. Parts 1 and 2 give part 3 only after noting that convergence in measure implies Cauchy in measure, which is the same two-set inclusion the unit already writes out in step 6 of the proof of part 2. Folland's Cor. 2.32 is equally terse ("Combine Proposition 2.29 and Theorem 2.30", p. 62), so this is the source's route and not an error, but the handbook's own standard asks for the half-sentence. Fix: "by part 1 the sequence converges, hence is Cauchy, in measure (same inclusion as in step 6 below), so part 2 applies".
4. [minor] remark, first bullet: "$\sup_n f_n$ ... equal to $1$ on $(1,\infty)$ in (ii)". For $f_n = \chi_{(n,n+1)}$ the supremum is $0$ at every integer, so it equals $1$ only almost everywhere on $(1,\infty)$. Non-integrability is unaffected. Fix: "equal to $1$ a.e. on $(1,\infty)$".
5. [minor] remark, opening sentence, and the last sentence of the statement: the four sequences block every remaining implication among {a.e., almost uniform, in measure, $L^p$} only when $p$ is read as fixed. Across different exponents they do not: none of (i)–(iv) converges in $L^1$ without converging in $L^2$ ((i) does the reverse, $\|f_n\|_p^p = n^{1-p}$), and it is the unit's own example with $\alpha = 1/2$ that supplies that separation. Fix: say "for each fixed $p$" in the remark, or point forward to the example for the $L^1$-but-not-$L^2$ case.
6. [minor] note "The same four modes in probability": "$\mathbb{E}|X_n^r| < \infty$" reproduces the book's notation exactly (Grimmett–Stirzaker Def. (1b), p. 352, and again in the norm on p. 353) and is harmless for the positive integral $r$ they restrict to, but it sits one clause away from our own $\mathbb{E}|X_n - X|^r$ and reads as a typo. Fix: write $\mathbb{E}|X_n|^r$ and add "(the book writes $\mathbb{E}|X_n^r|$)", or leave it and flag it as the book's notation.
