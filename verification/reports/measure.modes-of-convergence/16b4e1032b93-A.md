## Findings
1. [minor] proof "Part 4: Egorov's theorem" (line 118): "Discard the null set where convergence fails and redefine the functions to be $0$ there; this changes nothing in the conclusion" is asserted rather than justified. It is true, but the reason needs saying: the argument gives uniform convergence of the modified sequence off $E$, so the original sequence converges uniformly off $E \cup Z$, where $Z$ is the discarded null set, and $\mu(E\cup Z) = \mu(E) < \varepsilon$. Folland p. 62 elides this in the same way, but the handbook's rule is to justify at the step. Fix: add that clause.
2. [minor] definition (lines 29, 35): the unit defines convergence and Cauchyness in measure with $|f_n - f| \ge \varepsilon$, where Folland p. 61 uses $> \varepsilon$; and the note at line 49 reports Grimmett-Stirzaker's Thm (4c) (p. 354), stated there with $>\varepsilon$, as $\ge\varepsilon$. The two conventions are equivalent (quantifying over all $\varepsilon>0$), and the proofs here use the strict/non-strict inequalities consistently with the unit's own choice, so nothing is wrong. Fix: one sentence recording that the choice is immaterial, since the books differ.
3. [minor] statement, part 5 of the implication list and the closing sentence (line 64): "the remark below exhibits a counterexample to each" is slightly overstated. Three of the blocked implications — a.e. $\not\Rightarrow$ almost uniform, in measure $\not\Rightarrow$ almost uniform, $L^1 \not\Rightarrow$ almost uniform — are blocked not by an exhibited counterexample but by composing an exhibited one with a listed implication (almost uniform $\Rightarrow$ a.e. and $\Rightarrow$ in measure). Fix: "a counterexample to each, directly or by composition with the implications above".
4. [minor] example "A one-parameter family that separates the modes" (line 204): "The dominating function would have to exceed $n^{1/2}$ on $(0,1/n)$ for every $n$, that is to exceed $x^{-1/2}$" is loose — $\sup_n f_n(x) = \lceil 1/x - 1\rceil^{1/2}$, which is comparable to $x^{-1/2}$ rather than bounded below by it. The conclusion drawn ($x^{-1/2}\in L^1(0,1)$, $\notin L^2(0,1)$) is unaffected. In the same sentence, "part 6 explains the $L^1$ convergence and correctly declines to explain the $L^2$ failure" appeals to an $L^p$ version of part 6, which is stated only for $L^1$. Fix: "comparable to $x^{-1/2}$", and say explicitly that the $L^p$ analogue of part 6 needs $g \in L^p$.
