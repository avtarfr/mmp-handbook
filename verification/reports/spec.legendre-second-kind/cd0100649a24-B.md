1. [blocking] definition, "Outside the interval" paragraph (line 61), and the `hassani-2013`
   `loc` field in the frontmatter (line 20): the unit says Hassani obtains $Q_\lambda$ "by
   specialising the second-kind Jacobi functions to $\mu=\nu=0$", and the frontmatter locator
   repeats this as "their mu = nu = 0 specialisation". Hassani p. 478 parametrises the Jacobi
   functions as $Q^{(\alpha,\beta)}_\lambda$, eq. (15.33), and takes the Legendre case at
   $\alpha=\beta=0$ ("Another special case of the Jacobi functions is obtained when
   $\alpha=\beta=0$"). The symbols $\mu,\nu$ are not his Jacobi parameters, and $\mu$ is in
   active use on the same page for the Gegenbauer index $C^\mu_\lambda$ (15.34), where
   $\alpha=\beta=\mu-\tfrac12$ — so in the cited source $\mu=0$ names a *different* family
   (it gives $\alpha=\beta=-\tfrac12$), and the Legendre case is $\mu=\tfrac12$. A reader
   checking the citation is sent to the wrong specialisation. The displayed formula for
   $Q_\lambda$ itself is transcribed correctly and matches (the unnumbered equation following
   (15.35) on p. 478). Fix: write $\alpha=\beta=0$ in both the definition block and the
   frontmatter `loc`, matching Hassani's own notation.

2. [minor] remark, "Regularity at the ends is what selects $P_n$" (line 300): the obstruction is
   identified as "the survival of the boundary term
   $\bigl[(1-x^{2})(uv'-u'v)\bigr]_{-1}^{1}$", and then "for $u=P_{n}$, $v=Q_{n}$ the bracket is
   identically $1$, as far from zero as it could be". The bracketed *function* is indeed
   identically $1$ (part 5), but the quantity written, with its limits, is the difference
   $f(1)-f(-1)=1-1=0$ for this pair, so as literally written the illustration shows the boundary
   term vanishing — the opposite of the point being made. (For $u=P_n$, $v=Q_m$ the difference is
   $1-(-1)^{n+m}$, i.e. $2$ when $n+m$ is odd and $0$ when it is even; I checked the endpoint
   limits by hand and numerically.) The substantive claim — that $p(P_nQ_n'-P_n'Q_n)\to1\neq0$ at
   each endpoint separately, so symmetry fails there — is correct. Fix: drop the $[\;]_{-1}^{1}$
   limits and speak of the boundary form $p(uv'-u'v)$ evaluated *at each endpoint*, where it is
   $1$ at $x=1$. The same loose phrasing is in the application block, "the bilinear boundary form
   evaluates to exactly 1" (line 379).

3. [minor] example, Check 2 (line 358): the recurrence value is printed as $-0.1986547714$ and

   $-0.1986547715$; the two printed numbers agree only to nine significant figures. The exact
   value is $-0.198654771479\ldots$, so the recurrence result is exact and the trailing $4$ is an
   artefact of truncating the displayed intermediates ($-2.0466581700$ should be

   digits".

4. [minor] application, "Green's functions" (line 384): the kernel is written
   $G(x,s)=-u(x_{<})v(x_{>})$ with no prefactor, and the next clause then refers to "the usual
   $1/pW$ prefactor" as a constant to be computed once. As written the two halves of the sentence
   disagree about whether the prefactor is present in the displayed formula. Fix: display
   $G(x,s)=-u(x_{<})v(x_{>})/(pW)$ and then note that $pW$ is constant.
