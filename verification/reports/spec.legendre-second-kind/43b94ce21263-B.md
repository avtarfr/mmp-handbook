## Findings

1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", the paragraph "Two silent points in the
   reduction-of-order formula (R)" (line 313): the text claims that at a zero of $P_n$ "the quotient
   $Q_{n}/P_{n}$ nevertheless has a finite limit". This is false, and it contradicts the reason the
   same sentence gives for it. By (R), $Q_n/P_n$ *is* the integral $\int^x dt/((1-t^2)P_n(t)^2)$,
   which the sentence has just said acquires a simple pole at each zero of $P_n$; it is the product
   $P_n \cdot (\text{integral}) = Q_n$ whose limit is finite, because the prefactor $P_n$ cancels
   that simple pole. Counterexample: $n=1$, $x_0=0$, $Q_1/P_1 = \operatorname{artanh}x - 1/x$, which
   takes the values $-99.99$, $-9999.9999$, $-1.0\times10^{6}$ at $x = 10^{-2}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-6}$
   (it behaves like $-1/x$), while $Q_1(x)\to-1$. Same behaviour checked at the first zero of $P_2$
   ($x_0=-1/\sqrt3$: quotient $-50.2$, $-5000.2$, $-5.0\times10^{5}$ at offsets $10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6}$,
   with $Q_2\to 0.866025$) and of $P_3$. Fix: replace "the quotient $Q_{n}/P_{n}$ nevertheless has a
   finite limit at each" by "$Q_{n}$ itself nevertheless has a finite limit at each", leaving the
   explanation that follows unchanged — it is already the correct argument for $Q_n$.

2. [blocking] application, "Green's functions" (line 402): the displayed formula
   $G(x,s)=-u(x_{<})v(x_{>})/(p\,W[u,v])$ carries a minus sign that contradicts the prerequisite it
   cites in the same sentence. The unit's Legendre operator is (L) in the form
   $[(1-x^{2})y']'+\ell(\ell+1)y$, so its leading coefficient is $a_2=p=1-x^{2}$ (positive on
   $(-1,1)$), and green.green-function-bvp's boxed result for exactly that convention is
   $G(x,\xi)=u_1(x_{<})u_2(x_{>})/(a_2(\xi)W(u_1,u_2;\xi))$, with no minus. Checking the jump
   condition directly on the displayed formula: for $x>s$, $\partial_x G = -u(s)v'(s)/(pW)$ and for
   $x<s$, $\partial_x G = -u'(s)v(s)/(pW)$, so the jump is $-(uv'-u'v)/(pW) = -1/p(s)$, whereas
   condition 4 of green.green-function-bvp requires $+1/a_2(s)=+1/p(s)$. The minus sign belongs to
   the opposite convention $\mathcal{L}=-(pu')'+qu$ used in green.eigenfunction-expansion-green
   (leading coefficient $-p$), which is not the operator written here. Fix: drop the minus sign, or
   state explicitly that the Green's function displayed is that of $-\,$(L) and reconcile it with
   the cited unit.

3. [blocking] proof "Closed form, recurrences, parity and the singularity", parity paragraph
   (line 253): "For odd $n$, setting $x=0$ in the three-term recurrence gives
   $q_{n+1}(0)=-\frac{n}{n+1}q_{n-1}(0)$". With $n$ odd, $n+1$ and $n-1$ are both even, and the unit
   has just proved that $q_{m}$ of even index is an odd function, so both sides are $0$: the
   displayed relation is vacuous and does not connect $q_1(0)$ to $q_{2p+1}(0)$. The step that is
   needed takes $n$ **even**: $n=2p$ gives $(2p+1)q_{2p+1}(0)=-2p\,q_{2p-1}(0)$, which iterated from
   $q_1(0)=-1$ does give the stated $q_{2p+1}(0)=(-1)^{p+1}(2p)!!/(2p+1)!!$. The conclusion is
   correct (verified symbolically, see Checked), only the index parity named in the justification is
   wrong. Fix: write "For even $n$" (or "taking $n=2p$ in the three-term recurrence").

4. [minor] method, item 4 (line 332): "the Green's function of green.green-function-bvp is built
   from $P_{n},Q_{n}$ with denominator $1$". The denominator is $1$ only for the pair
   $u=P_n$, $v=Q_n$ themselves. For the combinations $u=aP_n+bQ_n$, $v=cP_n+dQ_n$ that the same
   sentence says satisfy the left and right conditions, $p\,W[u,v]=(ad-bc)\,p\,W[P_n,Q_n]=ad-bc$,
   a constant but not $1$. The point being made — that the prefactor is an $x$-independent number
   needing no determinant evaluation per point — survives; the wording does not. The application
   block states this correctly ("the prefactor $1/(pW)$ is a constant").

5. [minor] definition (line 41): "there are two solutions in powers of $x$, each with radius of
   convergence $1$". True for general $\ell$ (RHB p. 578 says exactly this), but the block is two
   sentences away from specialising to $\ell=n$, where one of the two series terminates and so has
   infinite radius of convergence. Fix: say "for general $\ell$" or "radius of convergence at least
   $1$".

6. [minor] statement (line 76 against line 119): the statement block opens "Let $n\ge0$ be an
   integer and $-1<x<1$", but part 7 is a claim about $x\in(1,\infty)$. Fix: move the range into
   parts 1-6, or say that part 7 steps outside the standing range.

7. [source] arfken-2013, §15.6, p. 767, the paragraph after eq. (15.160): the book says that
   continuing (15.160) past $x=+1$ makes the logarithm contribute $\pm i\pi$ "thereby making a
   contribution $\pm i\pi P_l$ to $Q_l$"; since (15.160) carries the logarithm with a factor
   $\tfrac12$, the contribution is $\pm i\pi P_l/2$. Our unit does not reproduce the factor — its
   definition block says only that the two conventions differ by a constant multiple of $P_n$, and
   its remark says the switch "differs by $\tfrac12\ln(-1)$", which is right. Recorded with
   `tools/discrepancy.mjs`; not a finding against the unit.
