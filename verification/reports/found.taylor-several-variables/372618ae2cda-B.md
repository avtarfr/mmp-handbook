[blocking] `application[Stable equilibria and transition states on a potential-energy surface]` (line 286): the third bullet says the rigid translations and rotations of an isolated molecule "give $3$ or $6$ exactly zero eigenvalues at every stationary point". That enumeration is wrong for linear molecules. In the $3N$-dimensional nuclear-coordinate space the three translations are always zero modes, but there are only *two* independent infinitesimal rotations of a linear molecule (rotation about the molecular axis moves no nucleus), so a diatomic or any linear molecule — an isolated molecule, covered by the sentence as written — has $5$ exactly zero eigenvalues, not $3$ or $6$. This is our own material in an `original` block, and the count is the operational one a reader uses (how many modes to project out before a vibrational analysis: the standard $3N-6$, and $3N-5$ for a linear molecule). Fix: say $6$ for a nonlinear molecule, $5$ for a linear one and $3$ for a single atom, or write "$3N-6$ vibrational modes, $3N-5$ if the molecule is linear".
