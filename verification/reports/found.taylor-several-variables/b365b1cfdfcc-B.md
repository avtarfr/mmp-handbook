1. [blocking] statement "Second-order Taylor expansion" (line 43): the provenance sentence misdescribes Lee's Thm C.15 and makes a false claim. It says "the display above is the case $k = 1$ of Lee's Thm C.15, whose remainder is an explicit integral of the second partial derivatives along the segment; that integral is $o(\lvert h \rvert^2)$ because those partials are continuous at $a$". The case $k = 1$ of Thm C.15 (p. 648, Eqs. (C.7)-(C.9)) gives $f(a+h) = f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot h + R_1(h)$ with $R_1(h) = \sum_{j,k} h_jh_k\int_0^1(1-t)\,\partial_j\partial_k f(a+th)\,dt$, and that integral is **not** $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$: it is $\tfrac12 h^{\mathsf T}H_f(a)h + o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$, i.e. exactly the quadratic term of the display plus a smaller error. Concretely, for $n=1$, $f(x)=x^2$, $a=0$: $P_1 \equiv 0$ and $R_1(h) = h^2\int_0^1 2(1-t)\,dt = h^2$, which is not $o(h^2)$. So (i) the display is not the case $k=1$ but a consequence of it, and (ii) the sentence attributes to Thm C.15 a remainder estimate it does not give. The sentence also misdescribes this unit's own route, since the proof at line 84 derives the $o$-form from the mean-value form, not from Lee's integral remainder. Fix: say that the case $k = 1$ of Thm C.15 gives an integral remainder in the second partials, that continuity of those partials at $a$ turns it into $\tfrac12 h^{\mathsf T}H_f(a)h + o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$, and that this last step (or, as actually carried out below, the reduction to the mean-value form) is ours.
