## Findings
1. [minor] :::statement[Second-order Taylor expansion] (line 34): the Peano form, with remainder $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$ for $f \in C^2$, is attributed to rhb-2006 Eqs. (5.18)/(5.20) and lee-2013 Thm C.15. RHB pp. 160-162 writes a formal series with no remainder estimate at all, and Lee's Thm C.15 at $k=1$ gives the integral remainder $\int_0^1 (1-t)\,\partial_I f(a+t h)\,dt$ in the *second* derivatives; getting from that to $\tfrac12 h^{\mathsf T}H_f(a)h + o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$ needs one further step (continuity of $H_f$ at $a$). The statement is true, but the step is ours. Fix: say so, as the mean-value block already does for its own form.
2. [minor] :::statement[The converse, and the cases the Hessian does not decide] (line 75): the positive-semidefinite-singular bullet offers only "may be a strict local minimum" and "may be no local extremum at all", omitting a non-strict local minimum, which also occurs: $f(x,y) = x^2$ has $\nabla f(0)=0$, $H = \operatorname{diag}(2,0)$ and a local (not strict) minimum at the origin. Likewise "if $H = 0$, all three possibilities occur" is correct, but the remark exhibits only two of the three. Fix: add the non-strict case, and a third example (e.g. $-(x^4+y^4)$) for $H=0$.
3. [minor] :::definition (line 20): the Hessian is written $H_f(a) = (\partial^2 f/\partial x_i\,\partial x_j(a))_{i,j}$ without saying which index differentiates first; it does not matter here because $f$ is $C^2$, but :ref[found.partial-derivatives-chain-rule] has just made a point of the convention, so one clause tying the two together would help.
