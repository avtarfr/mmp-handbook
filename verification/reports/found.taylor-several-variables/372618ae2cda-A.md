## Findings
1. [blocking] application (line 286): "the rigid translations and rotations of an isolated molecule ... give $3$ or $6$ exactly zero eigenvalues at every stationary point" is wrong. The zero modes are 3 translations plus 2 or 3 rotations, so the count is 6 for a nonlinear molecule and **5** for a linear one (the rotation about the molecular axis moves no nucleus) — the familiar $3N-6$ / $3N-5$ vibrational counts. The sentence omits 5, which is the case of every diatomic and of $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and offers 3 instead, a number that occurs only for a single atom and not for a molecule. This is an `original` block, so it is ours to get right. Fix: "3 translations and 2 or 3 rotations, so $5$ zero eigenvalues for a linear molecule and $6$ otherwise".
2. [minor] statement "Classification of a critical point by the Hessian" (line 136): the two-variable list says "$D = 0$: the test gives nothing", while part 5 of the next statement says the Hessian in the singular semidefinite case still "fixes at most the sign of the extremum that is still possible" — for $D = 0$ with $H \neq 0$ positive semidefinite it does exclude a local maximum. Not wrong if "the test" means the three-way classification, but the two sentences read as being in tension. Fix: "$D = 0$: the test does not classify the point (see the next statement)".
