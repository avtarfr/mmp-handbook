## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Second-order Taylor expansion" (line 43): the attribution paragraph is mathematically false in two ways. (a) "the display above is the case $k = 1$ of Lee's Thm C.15": with $k=1$ Lee's $P_k$ is the *first*-order Taylor polynomial, so that case reads $f(a+h) = f(a) + \nabla f(a)\cdot h + R_1(h)$ — it does not contain the quadratic term that our display writes out. (b) "that integral is $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$ because those partials are continuous at $a$" is wrong: Lee's remainder for $k=1$ (Eq. (C.9), p. 648) is $R_1(h) = \sum_{j,k} h_jh_k\int_0^1(1-t)\,\partial_j\partial_k f(a+th)\,dt$, and since $\int_0^1(1-t)\,dt = \tfrac12$ this is $\tfrac12 h^{\mathsf T}H_f(a)h + o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$, i.e. $\Theta(\lvert h\rvert^2)$, not $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$. The one-variable instance $n=1$, $f(x)=x^2$, $a=0$ makes it explicit: $P_1 \equiv 0$ and $R_1(h) = h^2\int_0^1 2(1-t)\,dt = h^2$. Taking $k=2$ instead does not rescue the sentence, since Lee then needs $f \in C^3$, which is not assumed. Fix: say that the display follows from the case $k=1$ by *splitting* the remainder — $R_1(h) = \tfrac12 h^{\mathsf T}H_f(a)h + \sum_{j,k}h_jh_k\int_0^1(1-t)\bigl[\partial_j\partial_k f(a+th) - \partial_j\partial_k f(a)\bigr]dt$, the second piece being $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$ by continuity of the second partials at $a$ — or simply point to the unit's own proof of the $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$ form, which is correct and self-contained, and drop the false sentence.
2. [minor] proof "Mean-value form" (line 75): "Its Lagrange remainder gives a point $\theta$ strictly between $0$ and $1$". The statement of the prerequisite :ref[found.taylor-theorem] says "for some $\xi$ between $a$ and $x$", but that unit's own proof delivers only $\xi$ in the *closed* interval (it applies the weighted mean value theorem for integrals, whose lemma concludes $\xi \in [c,d]$). Strictness is true but is not what the prerequisite establishes, and nothing in this unit needs it — the $o(\lvert h\rvert^2)$ proof uses only $\lvert\theta h\rvert \le \lvert h\rvert$. Fix: either weaken the statement's "$\theta \in (0,1)$" to $\theta \in [0,1]$, or justify the strictness.
3. [minor] proof "Mean-value form" (line 65): the step uses that a non-empty closed set disjoint from the compact set $[0,1]$ is at positive distance from it. That is point-set topology supplied by :ref[found.compactness], which is not in `prereqs`. It is also avoidable: $[0,1] \subseteq T$ with $T$ open gives an $\varepsilon$-neighbourhood directly from a finite subcover, or one may just apply the one-variable theorem on $[0,1]$ itself, where $g''$ is continuous. Fix: add the prerequisite or replace the argument by the shorter one.
