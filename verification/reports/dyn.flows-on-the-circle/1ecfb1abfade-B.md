## Findings
1. [minor] statement, "The bottleneck law": as written it fixes one $f$ and then takes $r=f(\theta_m)\to0^+$, which only makes sense for a family. The proof shows the intended reading ("fix a small $\eta>0$ independent of $r$"), and the two uses — the nonuniform oscillator with $r=\omega-a$, $c=a$, and the worked example with $r=\mu-\sqrt2$, $c=\sqrt2$ — are both genuine families with $c$ bounded away from $0$. Fix: state it for a family $f_r$ with $f_r(\theta_m)=r$, $f_r'(\theta_m)=0$, $f_r''(\theta_m)=c$ bounded above and below.
2. [minor] proof, bottleneck step: "replacing $f$ by $r+\tfrac c2\phi^2$ and then extending the limits to $\pm\infty$ changes the value by $O(1)$". The conclusion is right, but only because the cubic Taylor term is odd and integrates to zero over the symmetric interval $[-\eta,\eta]$; bounding $|f-(r+\tfrac c2\phi^2)|=O(|\phi|^3)$ term by term gives $O(\log(1/r))$, which is still $o(r^{-1/2})$ and so harmless. Fix: add the half-sentence about the odd term cancelling, or weaken "$O(1)$" to "$o(r^{-1/2})$".
3. [minor] definition block: $f$ is required to be continuously differentiable and $2\pi$-periodic, whereas Strogatz (p. 94) asks for $f$ smooth. The unit's hypothesis is the weaker and honest one (everything it proves needs only $C^1$), and nothing is attributed to Strogatz that he does not say, so this is not infidelity — but a word noting the difference would pre-empt the question.
