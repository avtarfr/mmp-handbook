## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", fourth bullet (line ~241): the illustration offered for "a closed subspace of an *incomplete* space need not be complete" is "$\mathbb{Q}$ is closed in $\mathbb{Q}$ and not complete". That is true but degenerate -- the subspace is the whole space, so it illustrates nothing about subspaces. Fix: use a proper subspace, e.g. $M = \mathbb{Q}\cap[0,2]$ inside $X = \mathbb{Q}$, which is closed in $X$ and is not complete (the decimal truncations of $\sqrt2$ already used two bullets earlier live in it).
2. [minor] proof "Part 7 for $C[a,b]$, and part 8", step 1 of the $d_1$ half (line ~153): "the bracket equals $\ln 2\,(n^{-1}-m^{-1})$ up to a term smaller than $e^{-2n}$". The error is $\ln(1+e^{-2m})/m - \ln(1+e^{-2n})/n$, whose modulus is at most $e^{-2n}/n$, so the stated bound is correct but an order of $n$ loose, and the sharper form is what the later example needs when it identifies the gap as $(2/n)\ln(1+e^{-2n})$. Fix: write "smaller than $e^{-2n}/n$" for consistency with the quantitative example.

The blocking point carried into this round -- "the displayed estimate is wrong -- recompute it and state what you get", against the example "How far from complete: a quantitative version" -- does **not** hold against the current text, which is titled "A quantitative version: the rate at which the missing limit is approached" and begins at line 216. I recomputed every displayed quantity in that block symbolically and numerically (mpmath, 30 digits) and every one is right; the values I obtained are listed below. In particular the two displays nearest the quoted line, $\inf_{h\in C[-1,1]} d_1(g_n,h) = 0$ (attained at $h=g_n$, since each $g_n$ is continuous) and $d_1(\operatorname{sgn},h_\eta) = 2\int_0^\eta(1-t/\eta)\,\mathrm dt = \eta$, are both exact: the second integral is $\eta-\eta/2=\eta/2$ and doubling for the symmetric half gives $\eta$.
