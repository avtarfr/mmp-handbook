   I recomputed it and the left-hand side is **identically $0$**, for every $n$: $g_n=\tanh(n\,\cdot)$ is itself an element of $C[-1,1]$, so $h=g_n$ is admissible and $\inf_{h\in C[-1,1]}d_1(g_n,h)=d_1(g_n,g_n)=0$. Two further things are wrong with it as written: (a) the witness used to bound the infimum, $\operatorname{sgn}$, is not a member of the set $C[-1,1]$ the infimum ranges over, so the inequality does not follow by the reason implied; and (b) the quantity it is supposed to stand in for is also zero — the continuous functions are $d_1$-dense in the larger space, so $\inf_{h\in C[-1,1]}\int_{-1}^1|{\operatorname{sgn}}-h|=0$ as well (take $h$ equal to $\operatorname{sgn}$ off $(-\eta,\eta)$ and linear across, giving $\int|{\operatorname{sgn}}-h|=\eta\to0$).
