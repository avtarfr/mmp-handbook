[minor] remark on secular terms (line 172): the second of the "two further observations" is attributed to Strogatz with the orders $\omega = \omega_0 + O(\epsilon)$ and an $O(1)$ phase error after $t \sim 1/\epsilon$. Strogatz's objection 2 (p. 218) is about his damped-oscillator example, where the frequency shift is $O(\epsilon^{2})$ and accumulates over $t \sim O(1/\epsilon^{2})$ -- he explicitly calls that "a third, super-slow time scale". The unit's version is correct mathematics and is the right statement for the Duffing example that follows, but the orders are ours, not his. Fix: present the $O(\epsilon)$ case as the general reading and note that Strogatz's own illustration has an $O(\epsilon^{2})$ shift on the $1/\epsilon^{2}$ scale. Objection 1 (the $e^{-\epsilon t} = 1 - \epsilon t + O(\epsilon^{2}t^{2})$ point) is reported exactly as he gives it.
3. [minor] example "A weakly nonlinear oscillator" (line 227): the frequency-shifted guess $\omega = 1 + \tfrac38\epsilon a^{2}$ is presented as a guess in an `original` block. It is in fact Strogatz Eq. (57), p. 227, obtained there by averaging. The block's route (regular perturbation on the Duffing equation, then a numerical check) is genuinely ours -- Strogatz treats the Duffing only by two-timing/averaging and treats $\ddot x + 2\epsilon\dot x + x = 0$ by regular perturbation -- so `original` is defensible, but a parenthetical citation to Eq. (57), p. 227, would be more honest about where the number comes from. Not a provenance failure in my judgement.
