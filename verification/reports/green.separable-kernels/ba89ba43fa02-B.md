## Checked
- Counterexample search: attacked the definition block's own counterexample, which is the risky claim in the unit — that a smooth bounded function of $x-t$ need not be separable. Confirmed: on $[0,2\pi]$, $\mathcal{K}e^{\mathrm{i}kt}=2\pi c_ke^{\mathrm{i}kx}$ with $c_k\neq0$ for every $k$, so the range contains infinitely many independent functions and the rank is infinite; the kernel is nevertheless in $C^\infty$ and bounded between $1/3$ and $1$. Also tested the statement at its boundary hypotheses: $\lambda=0$ is correctly excluded from part 2's "at most $n$ characteristic values" and handled separately (it is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$ of infinite multiplicity, as the unit says); the degree-$\le n$ claim is right because $\det(\mathrm{I}-\lambda A)$ has constant term 1 and so is not identically zero; dropping independence of $\{\psi_j\}$ would break only the rank-exactly-$n$ claim, which the definition block proves rather than assumes. Tried to break part 3 by making $A$ non-normal and $\lambda$ a defective eigenvalue: the alternative still holds, since it is stated through $\ker M$ and $\ker M^*$ rather than through multiplicities, and $\dim\ker M=\dim\ker M^*$ for a square matrix regardless of defect. No counterexample found.
- Calculations (`.venv/bin/python`, sympy and scipy):
  - Fourier coefficients of $1/(2+\cos\theta)$: computed exactly for $k=0,\dots,4$ and got $\sqrt3/3$, $1-2\sqrt3/3$, $-4+7\sqrt3/3$, $15-26\sqrt3/3$, $-56+97\sqrt3/3$, i.e. $0.577350269$, $-0.154700538$, $0.041451884$, $-0.011106999$, $0.002976111$, agreeing to 16 digits with the printed $(-1)^k(2-\sqrt3)^{|k|}/\sqrt3$. The operator identity was checked by quadrature at $x=0.7$ for $k=0,1,3$: $\mathcal{K}e^{\mathrm{i}kt}$ gave $3.627598728$, $-0.743435899-0.626187419\mathrm{i}$, $0.035231863-0.060241079\mathrm{i}$ against $2\pi c_ke^{\mathrm{i}kx}$ equal to the same to 14 digits.
  - Example, matrix: $A_{11}=1$, $A_{12}=0$, $A_{21}=0$, $A_{22}=1$, so $A=\mathrm{I}$ exactly, as printed. The parenthetical identification is right too: $\sum_{n}\frac{2n+1}{2}P_n(x)P_n(t)$ truncated at $n=1$ is $\frac12+\frac{3xt}{2}$.
  - Example, eigenfunctions: $\int_{-1}^{1}K(x,t)\cdot1\,\mathrm{d}t=1$ and $\int_{-1}^{1}K(x,t)\,t\,\mathrm{d}t=x$ exactly, so $\lambda=1$ with eigenspace $\operatorname{span}\{1,x\}$, and $\det(\mathrm{I}-\lambda\mathrm{I})=(1-\lambda)^2$ gives no other characteristic value.
  - Example, first branch: $b=(1/3,0)$, $c=(2/3,0)$, $y=x^2+1/3$; substituting $y$ back into the right-hand side gives residual exactly 0, and the intermediate value $\frac14(\frac23+\frac23)=\frac13$ printed in the unit is right.
  - Example, second branch (no solution): $b_1=1/3\neq0$ against the two conditions $b_1=b_2=0$; and $(x^2,1)=\int_{-1}^{1}x^2\,\mathrm{d}x=2/3\neq0$, both as printed.
  - Example, third branch: $(x^2-\frac13,1)=0$ and $(x^2-\frac13,x)=0$, so $b=0$; with $y=t^2-\frac13+\alpha+\beta t$ the integral is exactly $\alpha+\beta x$ for symbolic $\alpha,\beta$, and the residual $f+\mathcal{K}y-y$ is exactly 0 for all $\alpha,\beta$. The "two-parameter family" count matches $k=\dim\ker M=2$. The unit's "Every number in this example was checked symbolically" is justified.
  - Proof, part 3: re-derived the adjoint bookkeeping independently. $\overline{K(t,x)}=\sum_j\overline{\psi_j(x)}\,\overline{\phi_j(t)}$; the adjoint system matrix is $A^\sharp_{ij}=\int\overline{\phi_i\psi_j}=\overline{A_{ji}}=(A^*)_{ij}$; and $(f,g)=\lambda\sum_j\overline{d_j}b_j=\lambda(b,d)$. Every conjugate and index transposition in the printed chain is in the right place, which is the one thing in this unit that is easy to get wrong.
