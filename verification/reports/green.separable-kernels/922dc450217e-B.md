## Findings
1. [minor] application, "Separable potentials in nuclear and few-body physics" (line 244): the general-sign paragraph is correct in every assertion I could test, but the argument it offers does not reach its own conclusion. The attractive case is derived in full (eigenvalue $1$ of $\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}(H_0-E)^{-1}\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}$, rank at most $n$, then monotonicity in $E$). For general sign the text keeps the conclusion — "the count of at most $n$ bound states survives for either sign" — while the next clause withdraws the second of the two ingredients that produced it: "only the self-adjointness used for the monotonicity argument needs the attractive case". Rank alone bounds the eigenvalue count at each fixed $E$; it is monotonicity that turns that into a bound on the number of bound states. The conclusion is nevertheless true, and one extra clause closes the gap: run Birman-Schwinger on the negative part instead, $\lvert V_-\rvert^{1/2}(H_0-E)^{-1}\lvert V_-\rvert^{1/2}$, which is self-adjoint and still of rank at most $n$ since $V_-$ inherits the rank bound from $V$; equivalently, min-max on $\ker V_-$ (a subspace of codimension at most $n$ on which $H_0+V\ge H_0\ge0$). Fix: add that clause, or soften "survives" to a statement of what the rank bound alone gives.
