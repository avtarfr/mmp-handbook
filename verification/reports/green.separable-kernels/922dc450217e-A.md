## Findings
1. [blocking] application, first paragraph (Birman-Schwinger), final sentence: "The rank bound is unaffected by the extra factor, so the count of at most $n$ bound states survives for either sign; only the self-adjointness used for the monotonicity argument needs the attractive case." The inference does not follow, and the sentence's own second clause is why. In the attractive case the argument needs two ingredients: the rank bound, which gives at most $n$ nonzero eigenvalues of the Birman-Schwinger operator *at each fixed $E$*, and the counting principle (the monotonicity in $E$), which is what converts that into a bound on the number of bound states *over all $E$*. Without the second, the rank bound bounds only the multiplicity at a given energy, which is exactly the weaker conclusion the paragraph has already dismissed two sentences earlier ("a determinant of $E$-dependent entries can vanish at more than $n$ energies"). The text grants that the second ingredient needs $V\le0$ and then draws the full conclusion anyway. The conclusion is in fact true, but by a different route, so the repair is an addition rather than a retraction. Fix: justify the general-sign count by comparison instead of by the rank bound. Write $V=V_++V_-$ with $V_-\le0$ the negative part; then $V\ge V_-$, so by min-max $H_0+V\ge H_0+V_-$ and $H_0+V$ has no more negative eigenvalues than $H_0+V_-$; and $V_-$ is attractive of rank at most $n$, so the attractive argument just given bounds its bound states by $n$. Then say that the non-self-adjoint operator $-\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}(H_0-E)^{-1}U\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}$ still has rank at most $n$, which bounds the multiplicity at each $E$, but that it is the comparison and not that rank bound which gives the total count.

2. [minor] statement, part 2: "In addition, $\lambda=0$ is always an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$ itself" reuses the letter $\lambda$, which two lines above denotes a characteristic value (the reciprocal of an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$), for an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$. No contradiction follows, because $\lambda=0$ is excluded from the characteristic-value discussion and the phrase "of $\mathcal{K}$ itself" says which notion is meant, but the symbol is carrying two meanings in one item. Fix: drop the letter - "In addition, $0$ is always an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$ itself".

3. [minor] statement, closing line of part 3: "This is Theorem 6.2.1 of Stakgold and Holst" is true as to content but not as to parametrisation. Stakgold and Holst's (6.2.1) is $\lambda u-\mathcal{K}u=f$, so their $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$ and is the reciprocal of ours; their Remarks 1-3 on p. 360 are likewise phrased in that convention, which is also why their "$\lambda=0$" in Remark 2 matches our "$0$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{K}$" and not our $\lambda=0$. The unit never writes their $\lambda$, so nothing inside it is inconsistent, but a reader sent to p. 360 meets a differently normalised parameter with no warning. Fix: add "(their spectral parameter is the reciprocal of ours: they write $\lambda u-\mathcal{K}u=f$, following Arfken and Riley, Hobson and Bence we write $y=f+\lambda\mathcal{K}y$)".
