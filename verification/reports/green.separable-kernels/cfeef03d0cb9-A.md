## Findings
1. [blocking] definition (line 43): "and so is any kernel built from sums and differences of the arguments inside elementary transcendental functions" is false, and it strengthens the source. Arfken, Weber and Harris (p. 1057) claim only that the class "includes all polynomials and many of the elementary transcendental functions", with $\cos(t-x)$ as the example. Counterexamples to the universal version: $\tan(x-t)$ and $\log(x-t)$ are built exactly this way and are not separable — e.g. the functions $t\mapsto\tan(x-t)$ have poles at $t=x-\pi/2+k\pi$, so infinitely many of them are linearly independent and the operator is not of finite rank. Fix: keep the source's hedge, or name the property that makes it work (a finite addition theorem: polynomials, exponentials, and the trigonometric and hyperbolic sine and cosine).
2. [minor] definition (line 37): "has range contained in $\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\}$ and so has rank $n$ as a linear map" — the containment gives rank $\le n$; the equality needs the independence of the $\psi_{j}$ (which guarantees the $n$ functionals $u\mapsto\int\psi_{j}u$ are independent) as well as that of the $\phi_j$. Fix: add the reason for equality.
3. [minor] statement, part 2 (line 72): "of infinite multiplicity when the interval is infinite-dimensional" — an interval has no dimension. The condition is that $L^{2}(a,b)$ be infinite-dimensional, which it always is for $a<b$; Stakgold–Holst's Remark 2 (p. 360) simply says $\lambda=0$ can be an eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity. Fix: say "when $L^{2}(a,b)$ is infinite-dimensional", or drop the clause.
4. [minor] application, first paragraph: "a rank-$n$ separable potential can support at most $n$ bound states in each partial wave, because it has at most $n$ characteristic values" — the conclusion is right but part 2 does not give it, because the Lippmann–Schwinger kernel depends on the energy: the bound states are the energies at which $\lambda=1$ is a characteristic value, not the characteristic values of one fixed kernel. Fix: justify it by the finite rank of the potential (a rank-$n$ perturbation can push at most $n$ eigenvalues below the continuum), or drop the "because".
5. [minor] application (line 228): the projection $\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}\chi_{i}(x)\overline{\chi_{j}(t)}$ with $K_{ij}=(\mathcal K\chi_{j},\chi_{i})$, and the resulting system $(\mathrm I-\lambda K)c=b$ with $K$ itself as the matrix of step 2, hold for an *orthonormal* family $\{\chi_i\}$; "basis functions" does not say so. (A Nyström scheme is a quadrature rather than this Galerkin projection.)
