## Findings
1. [blocking] application "Separable potentials in nuclear and few-body physics" (line 244): the Birman–Schwinger step is stated for a potential of arbitrary sign, and in that generality it is false. The text says "a bound state at $E<0$ makes $1$ an eigenvalue of the Birman–Schwinger operator $\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}(H_{0}-E)^{-1}\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}$". Writing $V=U\lvert V\rvert$ with $U=\operatorname{sgn}V$ and $\varphi=\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}\psi$, the eigenvalue equation $H\psi=E\psi$ gives $\psi=-(H_{0}-E)^{-1}U\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}\varphi$ and hence $\varphi=-\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}(H_{0}-E)^{-1}U\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}\varphi$, so what has eigenvalue $1$ is $-\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}(H_{0}-E)^{-1}U\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}$. The $U$ collapses to $-\mathrm{I}$, and the displayed operator is recovered, only when $V\le0$. The block then leans on the stated form twice more — self-adjointness is what makes "monotonicity of those eigenvalues in $E$" available, and the counting of bound states below $E$ as the number of eigenvalues exceeding $1$ needs that monotonicity. This matters in exactly the setting the block invokes: rank-$n$ separable expansions of realistic nucleon–nucleon potentials are sign-indefinite (the repulsive core is one of the terms), so $V\le0$ cannot be assumed silently, and a rank-1 repulsive $V=+c\lvert g\rangle\langle g\rvert$ already has $\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}(H_{0}-E)^{-1}\lvert V\rvert^{1/2}$ reaching the eigenvalue $1$ at an energy where there is no bound state, so the displayed operator is not the one that counts bound states. The conclusion drawn ("at most $n$ bound states") is true and the fix is small. Fix: either restrict the sentence to an attractive $V$ ("for an attractive $V$, i.e. $V\le0$, a bound state at $E<0$ makes $1$ an eigenvalue of ..."), or keep the generality by adding the monotonicity step $N(V)\le N(-\lvert V\rvert)$ — since $-\lvert V\rvert\le V$ — and applying the displayed operator to $-\lvert V\rvert$, whose rank is still at most $n$.
