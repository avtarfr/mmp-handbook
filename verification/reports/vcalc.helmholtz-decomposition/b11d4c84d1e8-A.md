## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Scalar and vector potentials", part 1, second paragraph (line 35): the unit misreports its source. It says "*The restriction to a simply connected region is ours, not Arfken's.* He asserts that his three conditions, Eqs. (3.96), (3.99) and (3.100), 'are all equivalent', with no condition on the region (p. 172)". Arfken's "Scalar Potential" subsection opens (p. 171, the sentence that introduces Eq. (3.96)) with "If, over a given simply connected region of space (one with no holes), a force can be expressed as the negative gradient of a scalar function $\phi$", so simple connectivity is Arfken's own standing hypothesis for the whole discussion, including the equivalence paragraph on p. 172 that inherits it. The restriction is therefore the source's, not ours, and "with no condition on the region" is false. The same misreading is recorded as a discrepancy against Arfken in `verification/discrepancies.md` (the row for Eqs. (3.96), (3.99), (3.100), which opens "with no restriction on the region"), so that row has to go with the text; note also that it describes the unit as "present[ing] that hypothesis as part of what Arfken says", which is the opposite of what the unit's line 35 says, so the two are inconsistent with each other as well. Fix: delete the "ours, not Arfken's" paragraph and say instead that the hypothesis is Arfken's, stated at the head of his subsection (p. 171) though not repeated in the equivalence paragraph (p. 172), and that his argument for the direction needing it is local ("for a differential loop"). The counterexample on $\mathbb{R}^3$ minus the $z$-axis is worth keeping as the reason the hypothesis cannot be dropped; what must go is the attribution. The discrepancy row should be withdrawn or rewritten to record only the narrower point that Arfken's p.-172 paragraph does not repeat the hypothesis.
2. [source] statement "Scalar and vector potentials", part 2 (line 36): "A $C^1$ field $\mathbf{B}$ with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ admits a field $\mathbf{A}$ with $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$", with no condition on the domain. This is a faithful report of Arfken p. 173 ("subject only to the condition that $\mathbf{B}$ be solenoidal") and of Riley, Hobson and Bence p. 389 ("It may be shown that such a vector field $\mathbf{a}$ always exists"), so it does not fail the unit. Unqualified it is false: on $\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$ the field $\hat{\mathbf{r}}/r^2$ is smooth and divergence-free but has flux $4\pi$ through the unit sphere, while a curl has zero flux through every closed surface. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.
3. [source] application (line 309): "the gauge freedom $\mathbf{A}\mapsto\mathbf{A}+\nabla\psi$ remains, which amounts to a free choice of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}$", stated right after the time-dependent pair $\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{E}=-\nabla\varphi-\partial_t\mathbf{A}$. That replacement alone changes $\mathbf{E}$ by $-\partial_t\nabla\psi$; the gauge transformation is the pair $\mathbf{A}\mapsto\mathbf{A}+\nabla\psi$, $\varphi\mapsto\varphi-\partial_t\psi$. The sentence is a faithful report of Arfken p. 174 ("At this point $\mathbf{A}$ is still arbitrary to the extent of adding any gradient, which is equivalent to making an arbitrary choice of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}$"), whose own p. 175 then speaks of "transformations of $\mathbf{A}$ and $\phi$", so the unit passes on it; recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add`. Naming the companion shift in one clause would cost nothing.
4. [minor] remark, third bullet, Step 3 paragraph (line 221): "That term is bounded by $4\pi R^2\max_{r_2=R}\lvert\mathbf{c}\rvert/(R-r_1)$ … So it tends to zero if and only if $\mathbf{c} = o(1/r)$, and for $\lvert\mathbf{c}\rvert\sim 1/r$ … it does not". Only the "if" half is true: what fails for $\lvert\mathbf{c}\rvert\sim 1/r$ is the *bound*, not necessarily the term, and the term can vanish identically at that decay rate — take $\mathbf{c}$ azimuthal, $\mathbf{c} = (-y,x,0)/(x^2+y^2)$, which is divergence-free with $\lvert\mathbf{c}\rvert \sim 1/\rho$ and $\mathbf{c}\cdot d\mathbf{S}_2 = 0$ on every sphere. Fix: as in Step 3 of the proof, attach "only if" to the bound rather than to the term — "the bound tends to zero only if $\mathbf{c} = o(1/r)$, so $o(1/r)$ is what this route needs". The sufficient condition the proof actually uses is unaffected.
5. [minor] proof "Helmholtz's theorem", Steps 1 and 4 (lines 136–138, 174–176): $\nabla_1^2$ is moved inside the integral and evaluated through $\nabla^2(1/r_{12}) = -4\pi\delta^{(3)}$, which yields $\nabla^2\varphi = -s$ and $\nabla^2 A_j = -c_j$ in the distributional sense; that $\varphi$ and $\mathbf{A}$ are twice *classically* differentiable, which Steps 2 and 5 then use ($\mathbf{P}^\star$ is $C^1$, $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{A})$ is computed pointwise), is not argued. The preamble's "the second derivatives are never taken naively" acknowledges the move but does not supply the regularity. It is available cheaply from the declared hypotheses: the Newtonian potential of a $C^1$ compactly supported density is $C^2$, which is what makes the identity classical here. One sentence in the preamble would close it.
