## Findings
1. [blocking] statement[Uniqueness from divergence, curl and boundary data] (line 47) together with proof[Helmholtz's theorem], Step 5 (line 163). The uniqueness statement has no boundedness hypothesis on $V$ — "Let $V$ be a simply connected region with piecewise $C^1$ boundary" — yet Step 4 of its own proof applies Green's first identity, which `vcalc.green-identities` states only for a **bounded** domain with piecewise $C^1$ boundary. Step 5 of the Helmholtz proof then invokes the theorem with $V = \mathbb{R}^3$: "The region under study is all of $\mathbb{R}^3$, which is simply connected, and both fields vanish at infinity, so their normal components agree on the boundary at infinity." $\mathbb{R}^3$ has no boundary at all, so that hypothesis is vacuous there and the theorem as stated and proved would read "a field on $\mathbb{R}^3$ is determined by its divergence and its curl" — which is false: $\mathbf{Q} = $ any constant vector has $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{Q} = 0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{0}$, so $\mathbf{P}$ and $\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{Q}$ share both. Step 5 is therefore an unjustified step: the decay hypothesis is what rules the constant field out, and the statement being invoked offers no way to use decay. Fix, in two parts: (a) add "bounded" to the uniqueness statement, since that is what (G1) and hence Step 4 require; (b) in Step 5, run the limit explicitly rather than appealing to a "boundary at infinity" — apply the uniqueness theorem's proof on $V_R = \{r < R\}$, where the only surviving term of (G1) is $\oint_{r=R}\phi\,\partial\phi/\partial n\,dS = -\oint_{r=R}\phi\,Q_n\,dS$, bounded in modulus by $4\pi R^2 \max_{r=R}\lvert\phi\rvert\max_{r=R}\lvert\mathbf{Q}\rvert$, so that with $\mathbf{Q} = O(r^{-2})$ and $\phi = O(r^{-1})$ it tends to $0$ and $\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}\lvert\mathbf{Q}\rvert^2 = \lim_R\int_{V_R}\lvert\mathbf{Q}\rvert^2 = 0$. The existing `note` on line 81 is the right place to say which decay rate that needs, since it already discusses exactly this gap for the convergence of the integrals.
