## Findings
1. [minor] frontmatter (line 7): `prereqs` lists only `measure.monotone-convergence-fatou`, but the unit leans directly and repeatedly on `measure.lebesgue-integral` ($L^1$ as a space of a.e.-defined equivalence classes, cited in the statement at line 50 and in step 0 at line 90; claim 6, that redefining on a null set changes no integral; linearity on $L^1$ in steps 2-4) and on `measure.measurable-functions` (a pointwise limit of measurable functions is measurable, step 0 and the differentiation proof). Both are reachable transitively through the listed prerequisite, so nothing is out of the reader's reach. Fix: add `measure.lebesgue-integral` and `measure.measurable-functions` to `prereqs`.
2. [minor] statement (line 32): the unit has no `definition` block, so $L^1(\mu)$, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_1$ and "almost everywhere" are used from the first line with no local definition and with their defining unit not listed as a prerequisite (see finding 1). A two-line set-up block, as in the companion unit, would make the unit self-contained. Fix: add a short `definition` block fixing $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$, $L^1(\mu)$ as a.e.-defined equivalence classes, and a.e.
3. [minor] statement (line 56): "$\sum_{j\ge1} f_j$ converges almost everywhere, its sum is in $L^1(\mu)$". The sum is only defined off the null set $\{G = \infty\}$, so "its sum is in $L^1$" is meant in the a.e.-defined sense that the proof (and line 50) sets up, exactly as Folland's Thm 2.25 means it. The statement block does not say so, and a reader who has not yet reached the proof may read it as a pointwise claim. Fix: "converges absolutely almost everywhere, and its a.e.-defined sum is in $L^1(\mu)$".
4. [minor] example (line 207): "The bound is tight" is justified by numbers that make it tight only asymptotically — at $n=1$ the true supremum is $0.10907$ against $1/(6n^2) = 0.16667$, a factor $1.53$, while at $n = 50, 500$ the ratio is $0.991$ and $0.9991$. Fix: "The bound is asymptotically sharp".
5. [source] proof (line 135) and frontmatter (line 11): Folland's proof of Thm 2.27b (p. 56) bounds the difference quotient by the mean value theorem although Thm 2.27 is stated for complex-valued $f$, where the mean value theorem does not hold. The unit notices this, says exactly what Folland writes, and repairs it with the envelope $2g$, which is the right fix and costs nothing. Not a fault of ours. Already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (entry `measure.dominated-convergence` / `folland-1999` / §2.3, proof of Thm 2.27b, p. 56); no duplicate entry added.
