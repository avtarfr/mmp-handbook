## Findings
1. [minor] :::statement[Convergence as the number of nodes grows]: "at $11$ nodes it is $152921/49896 = 3.0648$, and the quantity grows without bound thereafter." I computed the closed Newton-Cotes weights exactly for $n = 2 \ldots 13$ nodes: $\sum_i|W_i|/(b-a)$ is $1$ for $n \le 8$, $6857/4725 = 1.45122$ at $n=9$, **$1$ again at $n=10$** (all weights positive there), $152921/49896 = 3.06479$ at $n=11$, $6921091/4354560 = 1.58939$ at $n=12$, $39581159/5255250 = 7.53174$ at $n=13$. So the sequence is unbounded, as the unit says, but it is not monotone and in particular does not "grow" at $n = 12$; the odd-$n$ subsequence is what runs away. Fix: "and the quantity is unbounded thereafter, though not monotonically -- the $10$-node rule again has all weights positive".
2. [minor] frontmatter, kreyszig-1978: `loc` names "§4.11 ..., pp. 276-284" while `pages` is "276-280". Everything actually cited (the process (3) and the norm (5) on p. 277, Requirement 4.11-2 and the Vandermonde argument on pp. 278-279, Thm 4.11-3 Polya on p. 279, Thm 4.11-4 Steklov on p. 280) lies inside 276-280, so nothing is miscited; the two ranges should just agree.
3. [minor] :::proof, Peano lemma: "the intermediate value theorem gives $\xi$ in the open interval unless $f^{(k)}$ is constant, in which case any interior point serves." This is right, but the IVT as invoked needs $f^{(k)}$ to attain the value strictly between its min and max at an interior point, which requires an argument when the extremes are attained only at the endpoints. The standard fix is one clause (if the value equals $m$ or $M$ then $f^{(k)} \equiv$ that value against the sign-definite kernel, so again any point serves).
