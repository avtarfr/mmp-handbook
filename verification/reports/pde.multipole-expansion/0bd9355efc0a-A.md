## Findings
1. [blocking] proof (line 104): "The series diverges at $h = 1$, that is at $r_1 = r_2$ ... so the restriction is a limitation of the expansion and not of the function" is false, and the inference drawn from it is the wrong way round. At $h = 1$ with $\chi \neq 0$ the series $\sum_\ell P_\ell(\cos\chi)$ converges — conditionally, since $P_\ell(\cos\chi) = O(\ell^{-1/2})$ with an oscillating factor — to $1/(2\sin(\chi/2))$, so (1) converges at $r_1 = r_2$ to the correct value $1/(2r\sin(\chi/2)) = 1/\lvert\mathbf{r}_1-\mathbf{r}_2\rvert$. I checked this by Legendre recurrence: partial sums over $3\times10^5$ terms give $1.00041$, $0.59496$, $0.70784$ against the targets $1$, $0.594198$, $0.707107$ for $\chi = \pi/3,\ 2,\ \pi/2$. The only genuine failure is $r_1 = r_2$ together with $\chi = 0$, and there the function itself blows up — the opposite of what the sentence claims. Note that Arfken (p. 737) says only that (15.63) "will converge everywhere except when $r_1 = r_2$", i.e. he claims nothing at $r_1 = r_2$; the assertion of divergence is the unit's own strengthening. Fix: say that the generating function gives the expansion for $\lvert h\rvert<1$, hence for $r_1 \ne r_2$, which is Arfken's statement, and either drop the gloss or replace it with the accurate one (at $r_1 = r_2$ the series still converges, but only conditionally, and the function is singular exactly when $\chi = 0$ as well).
2. [minor] proof (line 166): Step 4 derives the axial form (5) only for charges on the *positive* polar axis ($\theta_i = 0$), but (5) is stated for "charges on the polar axis, at $z = a_i$" with $a_i$ of either sign, and the second example uses a charge at $z = -a$. The negative-axis case needs one extra line: at $\theta_i = \pi$, $Y_\ell^m(\pi,\varphi_i) = (-1)^\ell\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/4\pi}\,\delta_{m0}$ and $r_i = -a_i$, so $r_i^\ell Y_\ell^0(\pi)^* = a_i^\ell\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/4\pi}$ and (5) comes out with the signed $a_i$ as written. Fix: add that line, or restrict (5) to $a_i > 0$ and treat the general case separately.
3. [minor] example (line 223): "the rotation through $\pi/2$ about the $z$-axis combined with $q\mapsto-q$, which kills the $m = 0$ terms" understates what that symmetry does. Under $\varphi \mapsto \varphi + \pi/2$ the moment $M_\ell^m$ picks up $e^{im\pi/2}$, and the configuration maps to minus itself, so $M_\ell^m = 0$ unless $e^{im\pi/2} = -1$, that is unless $m \equiv 2 \pmod 4$. This kills $m = 0, \pm1, \pm3, \pm4$ as well as $m = 0$, and is what guarantees that only $m = \pm2$ (and $m = \pm 6, \dots$) can ever appear — a stronger and more useful statement than the one made. Fix: state the condition $m \equiv 2 \pmod 4$; it also explains, together with the inversion symmetry, exactly which higher terms survive.
4. [minor] example (line 245): "The standard Cartesian quadrupole potential $\tfrac12 r^{-3}Q_{jk}\hat n_j\hat n_k$" is used as the cross-check but is not derived here and is not in any listed prerequisite (it is not in Arfken's treatment either, which works with $M_\ell^m$ and the Cartesian forms of the moments, not with the quadrupole tensor and its potential). The formula and all the numbers it gives are correct — I verified $Q_{xx} = 6qa^2$, $Q_{yy} = -6qa^2$, $Q_{zz} = 0$ and that $\tfrac12 r^{-3}Q_{jk}\hat n_j\hat n_k = 3qa^2\sin^2\theta\cos2\varphi/r^3$ — but the cross-check currently rests on an unsourced convention for $Q_{jk}$ and for the factor $\tfrac12$. Fix: say which normalisation of $Q_{jk}$ is meant and cite it, or recast the cross-check as a direct expansion of the exact potential in powers of $1/r$.
5. [source] statement (line 50) and remark (line 207): two places where the unit faithfully reports Arfken and I believe Arfken is imprecise. (a) "the series converging except when $r_1 = r_2$" is Arfken's Eq. (15.63) caveat verbatim, and it is stronger than necessary for the reason in finding 1. (b) The Cartesian table quoted in the remark — $M_1^{\pm1} = \mp(2\pi/3)^{1/2}(x \pm iy)$, $M_2^0 = (4\pi/5)^{1/2}\tfrac12(2z^2-x^2-y^2)$ — is Arfken's table on p. 802 exactly, but the complex entries appear to drop the conjugation that his own Eq. (16.73) requires: with $M_\ell^m = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}\sum_i q_ir_i^\ell Y_\ell^m(\Omega_i)^*$ and Condon–Shortley phases, a unit charge at $(x,y,z)$ gives $M_1^1 = -(2\pi/3)^{1/2}(x - iy)$ and $M_2^2 = (3\pi/10)^{1/2}(x^2-y^2-2ixy)$. The real entries, which are the ones the unit's argument actually uses, are unaffected and I checked them independently. Both recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`. The unit passes on these, but since the remark introduces the values as "the moments $M_\ell^m$ of (4)", a reader can read them as contradicting the unit's own (4); one clause saying the table is Arfken's and that it lists $r^\ell Y_\ell^m$ rather than $r^\ell Y_\ell^{m*}$ would remove the apparent contradiction.
