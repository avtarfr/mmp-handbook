## Findings
1. [blocking] proof of the multipole expansion, Step 4 (line 166): the step proves the axial reduction only for charges on the **positive** polar axis ("If every $\mathbf{r}_i$ lies on the positive polar axis, $\theta_i = 0$"), but statement (5) is for charges "on the polar axis, at $z = a_i$" with $\mu_n = \sum_i q_i a_i^n$, which allows $a_i < 0$ — and the unit itself uses that case twice (Arfken's dipole $\pm q$ at $z = \pm a$ in the definition block, and Example 2 with $+q$ at $z = -a$). A charge at $z = a_i < 0$ has $\theta_i = \pi$ and $r_i = |a_i| = -a_i$, which Step 4's formula $\sum_i q_i a_i^\ell$ does not cover as written. Fix: split the step — for $\theta_i = \pi$ use $Y_\ell^m(\pi,\varphi_i) = (-1)^\ell\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/4\pi}\,\delta_{m0}$, so $r_i^\ell Y_\ell^0(\pi,\varphi_i)^* = |a_i|^\ell(-1)^\ell\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/4\pi} = a_i^\ell\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/4\pi}$, and the two cases combine into the single formula $\mu_\ell = \sum_i q_i a_i^\ell$ for charges anywhere on the axis.
2. [blocking] proof of the origin-dependence statement (line 185): "It is enough to argue with the axial moments (5); the same computation with $\mathbf{r}_i\to\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{r}_p$ gives the general case." It is not the same computation and the reduction is not justified. Step 1's binomial theorem applies to the scalars $(a_i - z_p)^n$; the general moments involve $r_i^\ell Y_\ell^m(\Omega_i)^*$, i.e. the solid harmonics $\mathcal{Y}_\ell^m(\mathbf{r}_i)$, and shifting them needs the translation (addition) theorem for solid harmonics, $\mathcal{Y}_\ell^m(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_p) = \sum_{j\le\ell}\sum_{m'} c^{\,\ell m}_{j m'}(\mathbf{r}_p)\,\mathcal{Y}_j^{m'}(\mathbf{r})$ with $c = 1$ on the $j=\ell$, $m'=m$ term — not the binomial theorem. Nor does the axial argument cover an off-axis shift $\mathbf{r}_p$, which destroys the axial symmetry the argument assumes. The gap matters because the unit *uses* the general statement: Example 1 (line 265) invokes it for a planar, non-axial configuration under an arbitrary shift $\mathbf{d}$. Fix: either state the theorem only for axial distributions and axial shifts (and weaken Example 1's appeal accordingly), or prove the general case properly — the key fact being that $\mathcal{Y}_\ell^m$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $\ell$, so $\mathcal{Y}_\ell^m(\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}_p)$ expands in solid harmonics of degree $\le\ell$ with the degree-$\ell$ coefficient equal to $1$, whence $\tilde M_\ell^m = M_\ell^m + (\text{combination of } M_j^{m'},\ j<\ell)$.
3. [blocking] proof of the multipole expansion, Step 1 (line 150): "each term being $4\pi\varepsilon_0^{-1}q_i$ times the fundamental solution of :ref[Laplace's equation]{to=pde.laplace-fundamental-solution} centred at $\mathbf{r}_i$". The prerequisite unit normalizes $\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = 1/(4\pi|\mathbf{x}|)$ with $-\nabla^2\Phi = \delta_0$, so $\frac{q_i}{4\pi\varepsilon_0|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_i|} = \frac{q_i}{\varepsilon_0}\,\Phi(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_i)$. The stated factor is wrong by $4\pi$ on either reading of the ambiguous "$4\pi\varepsilon_0^{-1}q_i$" ($4\pi q_i/\varepsilon_0$, or $q_i/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)$). Fix: write "each term being $q_i/\varepsilon_0$ times the fundamental solution $\Phi(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_i) = 1/(4\pi|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_i|)$ of :ref[...]", and avoid the unbracketed $\varepsilon_0^{-1}$.
4. [blocking] remark, "What the expansion is not" (line 211): "Arfken's Eq. (15.62) is the companion statement for a field point inside a spherical shell of charge". Eq. (15.62) (Arfken p. 736) is the potential of a **single point charge $q$ at $z = a$** evaluated at $r < a$; there is no shell. The difference is not cosmetic: inside a uniform spherical shell the potential is the constant $q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 a)$, i.e. only the $n = 0$ term survives, whereas (15.62) is the full series $\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 a}\sum_n P_n(\cos\theta)(r/a)^n$. Fix: "Arfken's Eq. (15.62) is the companion statement for a field point closer to the origin than the charge — a point charge at radius $a$ seen from $r < a$ — where the roles of $r$ and $a$ in (1) are exchanged and the series runs in powers of $r/a$."
5. [source] statement, (1) (line 49) and proof Step 3 (line 104): "the series converging except when $r_1 = r_2$" / "The series diverges at $h = 1$". This faithfully reports Arfken's Eq. (15.63) ("will converge everywhere except when $r_1 = r_2$", p. 737), so it does not fail the unit, but the exclusion is wider than it needs to be: at $r_1 = r_2$ the series still converges, conditionally, for every $\chi \in (0,\pi)$ — $P_n(\cos\chi) \sim \sqrt{2/(\pi n\sin\chi)}\cos((n+\tfrac12)\chi - \tfrac\pi4)$, so the Dirichlet test applies, and Abel's theorem identifies the sum as $1/|\mathbf{r}_1-\mathbf{r}_2|$. I checked this numerically (partial sums to $n = 2\times10^5$ at $\chi = \pi/3,\pi/2,2\pi/3$ approach $1/\sqrt{2-2\cos\chi}$). Divergence at $r_1 = r_2$ happens only at $\chi = 0$. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add pde.multipole-expansion arfken-2013 "§15.3, Eq. (15.63), p. 737"`.
