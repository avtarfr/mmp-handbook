5. [minor] proof of the origin-independence statement, Step 5 (line 265): "whose second term is nonzero for every $z_p\ne0$. So moments above the lowest really do move" does not follow as stated. The full correction is $\sum_{j=N}^{n-1}\binom nj(-z_p)^{n-j}\mu_j$, and the other terms can cancel the $\mu_N$ one at isolated nonzero shifts — already at $n=N+2$ the correction vanishes at $z_p = \binom{n}{N+1}\mu_{N+1}\big/\bigl[\binom nN\mu_N\bigr]$ whenever $\mu_{N+1}\ne0$. What is true, and is what the statement's "in general" needs, is that the correction is a *polynomial in $z_p$ that is not identically zero* — its lowest-degree term, $\binom nN(-z_p)^{n-N}\mu_N$, has a nonzero coefficient — so it vanishes for at most finitely many $z_p$. Fix: replace the last clause with that argument.
6. [minor] application block, "Fast summation" (line 363): "truncating at the quadrupole gave three-digit accuracy at five cell radii" overstates the unit's own table. At $r=5a$ the table gives $0.02400000$ against the exact $0.02443440$ — a relative error of $1.8\%$, i.e. two significant digits at best; three-digit relative accuracy is first reached around $r=20a$ ($1.0\times10^{-3}$). Fix: "two-digit accuracy at five cell radii, and three-digit by twenty" or quote the relative error.
