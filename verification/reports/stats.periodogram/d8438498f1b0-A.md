## Findings
1. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 241): "$c(n)\sim\lvert n\rvert^{-\alpha}$ with $0<\alpha\le1$ goes with an integrable $\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\alpha-1}$ singularity of $g$ at $\lambda=0$" is stated over a range whose endpoint it does not fit. At $\alpha=1$ — which is exactly the unit's own example, $c(n)=\pi/\lvert n\rvert$ — the exponent gives $\lvert\lambda\rvert^0$, no singularity at all, whereas the true behaviour is the logarithmic one just displayed. Fix: restrict the power-law sentence to $0<\alpha<1$ and note that $\alpha=1$ is the logarithmic borderline case already exhibited.
2. [minor] statement, part 2 (line 107): "Among tapers that are heaviest at the centre the main lobe is narrowest with no taper at all" uses a class that does not contain its own extremal member — $w_t\equiv1$ is flat, not heaviest at the centre — so the sentence is not well formed as stated, although the content (the rectangular window has the narrowest main lobe, $4\pi/N$, and every sidelobe-suppressing taper widens it) is right. Fix: "No taper gives the narrowest main lobe of all: ...".
