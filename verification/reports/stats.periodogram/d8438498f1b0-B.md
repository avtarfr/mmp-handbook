## Findings
1. [minor] remark, third bullet (the long-memory bullet): "more generally $c(n)\sim\lvert n\rvert^{-\alpha}$ with $0<\alpha\le1$ goes with an integrable $\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\alpha-1}$ singularity of $g$ at $\lambda=0$". The power law is right for $0<\alpha<1$, but $\alpha=1$ is the borderline, where the exponent $\alpha-1=0$ names no singularity at all while the bullet's own worked case ($c(n)=\pi/\lvert n\rvert$, $g(\lambda)=\log2-\log\lvert2\sin(\lambda/2)\rvert$) has a logarithmic one. Fix: restrict the power law to $0<\alpha<1$ and say that $\alpha=1$ is the logarithmic borderline, which is the case displayed.
2. [minor] statement part 2: "every taper that lowers the sidelobes widens it" is asserted flatly. It is the right rule of thumb and the two cases the unit quotes obey it, but as stated it reads like a theorem, and the clause before it is already hedged ("among tapers that are heaviest at the centre"). Fix: hedge the second clause the way the first one is hedged.
3. [minor] remark, (T) bullet: "for the Hann taper it fails at two frequencies out of $M/2$" — the interior Fourier frequencies $0<j<M/2$ number $M/2-1$, not $M/2$. Harmless as an order-of-magnitude statement, inconsistent with the careful counting in part 4. Fix: "two out of the $M/2-1$ interior frequencies".
