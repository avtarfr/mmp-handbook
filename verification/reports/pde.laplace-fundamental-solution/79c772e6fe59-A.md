## Findings
1. [minor] example (line 351): "its second derivative jumps there by exactly the jump in $f$" has the wrong sign. Computing both sides at $r = a$: $u''(a^-) = -\rho_0/2$ and $u''(a^+) = +\rho_0/2$, so $[u'']$ read outwards is $+\rho_0$, while $[f]$ read outwards is $0 - \rho_0 = -\rho_0$. (Confirmed symbolically.) The correct statement is that $u''$ jumps by *minus* the jump in $f$, since $u'/r$ is continuous and $\nabla^2 u = -f$. Fix: "jumps there by exactly minus the jump in $f$", or "by $\rho_0$, the size of the jump in $f$".
2. [minor] proof (line 281) and definition (line 111): the proof's Step 6 and the definition both lean on :ref[pde.harmonic-functions] (for the shrinking-sphere average limit and for the $\alpha(n)$ conventions), but `pde.harmonic-functions` is not in `prereqs` — which lists only `vcalc.dirac-delta-basics`, `vcalc.green-identities`, `vcalc.cylindrical-spherical`. The borrowed fact (the average of a continuous function over $\partial B(x,\varepsilon)$ tends to $f(x)$) is elementary enough to count as standard calculus, so nothing is actually unsupported, but the cross-reference and the `prereqs` list disagree. Fix: add `pde.harmonic-functions` to `prereqs`, or give the two-line $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ argument inline in Step 6.
3. [minor] proof (lines 250, 260) and statement (line 137): Steps 4 and 5 apply Green's first identity on an $n$-dimensional annular region, but the statement block of :ref[vcalc.green-identities] is written for $V \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$; only its closing discussion records that Evans gives the identities in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (Appendix §C.2, for a bounded open $U$ with $C^1$ boundary and $u, v \in C^2(\overline U)$). Fix: name the $n$-dimensional form when invoking it, or generalise the prerequisite's statement.
4. [minor] idea (line 60) and statement (line 137): the symbol $v$ carries two meanings in one sentence of the idea block — the radial profile $v(r)$ of $u$, and the test function in "Green's identity with $v \equiv 1$" — and in (7) the normal derivative is written $\partial\Phi/\partial n$ while $n$ is the dimension throughout the unit (and appears as $n\alpha(n)$ two lines later). The proof of the theorem later switches to $\partial/\partial\nu$, which is clear. Fix: use $\partial/\partial\nu$ in (7) and its proof too, and rephrase the idea's clause as "Green's identity with the constant function $1$".
5. [minor] idea (line 189): "which is not integrable in $n$ dimensions, so the differentiated integral does not even converge" overstates Evans (p. 22), who says only that $D^2\Phi(x-y)$ "is not summable near the singularity" so that naive differentiation under the integral sign is unjustified and incorrect. The twice-differentiated integral fails to converge *absolutely*; as a principal value it does exist. Fix: "does not converge absolutely", or follow Evans's wording.
6. [minor] application (line 367): "as Stakgold and Holst set as their Exercise 2.5.3" is attached to the pair $e^{-qr}/(4\pi r)$ (three dimensions) and $K_0(q|x|)/(2\pi)$ (two dimensions), but Exercise 2.5.3 (p. 180) asks only for the two-dimensional $E = (1/2\pi)K_0(q|x|)$ for $L = -\Delta + q^2$; the three-dimensional Yukawa kernel is not in it. Also, p. 180 is outside the `pages: "175-177, 496-497"` declared for this work. Fix: attribute only the planar case to Exercise 2.5.3 and extend the `pages` range to include p. 180.
