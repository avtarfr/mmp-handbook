## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Sonine polynomials in kinetic theory" (first sentence): the Sonine indices are wrong for the two transport coefficients the paragraph then names. In the Chapman-Enskog solution the perturbation of the Maxwellian splits into a vector part expanded in $S^{(r)}_{3/2}(W^{2})$ and a traceless-tensor part expanded in $S^{(r)}_{5/2}(W^{2})$, with $S^{(r)}_{m}=L^{m}_{r}$; thermal conductivity comes from the first, viscosity from the second. The indices follow from the weight: the vector inner product is $\int\mathrm{e}^{-W^{2}}W^{2}\cdot W^{2}\,\mathrm{d}W$, and with $x=W^{2}$ this is $\tfrac12\int\mathrm{e}^{-x}x^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x$, giving $k=3/2$; the tensor one carries a further $W^{2}$ and gives $\tfrac12\int\mathrm{e}^{-x}x^{5/2}\mathrm{d}x$, so $k=5/2$. The unit writes "$k=\tfrac12$ or $\tfrac32$" and then says that viscosity and thermal conductivity come out of "this basis", which is wrong for viscosity under either reading. This is an `original` block with no source carrying the claim, so it is ours. Fix: write "$k=\tfrac32$ for the vector part that gives the thermal conductivity and $k=\tfrac52$ for the tensor part that gives the viscosity". The rest of the paragraph is right and should stand: half-integer $k$ is legal because only $k>-1$ is needed, the determinant-ratio structure is correct, and the hard-sphere first-approximation errors are the usual ones (about 1.6 per cent low for viscosity, about 2.5 per cent for thermal conductivity), which is what "under two per cent ... and somewhat more" says.
