## Findings

1. [blocking] remark "Two conventions for the associated polynomials" (line 275): the closing
   sentence — "A hydrogen radial function written with the wrong one acquires a sign
   $(-1)^{2L+1}=-1$ and, worse, the wrong degree, so the number of radial nodes comes out wrong;
   the symptom is a radial function with $n-1$ nodes instead of $n-L-1$" — is wrong twice over, and
   it contradicts the rest of the unit.
   (a) *Self-contradiction.* Everywhere else in this unit $n$ is the Laguerre lower index and $N$
   is the principal quantum number; the `application` block (line 350) says in so many words "the
   radial function has $n=N-L-1$ nodes". So in the unit's own notation the correct node count is
   $n$, and "$n-L-1$" is not it (it would be $N-2L-2$). Read instead with $n$ meaning the principal
   number, the sentence collides with the unit's established use of $n$ two blocks later.
   (b) *The arithmetic does not work either.* I computed the degrees. With $k=2L+1$ the correct
   factor is $L^{2L+1}_{N-L-1}$, of degree $N-L-1$. Carrying the other convention's subscript over
   to our symbol gives $L^{2L+1}_{N+L}$, of degree $N+L$ (checked: $N{=}2,L{=}1\to3$;
   $N{=}3,L{=}1\to4$; $N{=}4,L{=}2\to6$); swapping the object at fixed subscript gives
   $\mathcal L^{2L+1}_{N-L-1}=\mathrm{d}^{2L+1}/\mathrm{d}x^{2L+1}L_{N-L-1}$, which is identically
   zero whenever $N-L-1<2L+1$ (e.g. $N{=}2,L{=}1$; $N{=}3,L{=}1$; $N{=}4,L{=}2$) and of degree
   $N-3L-2$ otherwise. Neither is $N-1$ for any $L\ge1$. And at $L=0$ the sentence degenerates into
   "$N-1$ nodes instead of $N-1$", i.e. it asserts an error where there is none, which shows the
   claim cannot be rescued by any reading of $n$.
   Fix: either delete the sentence (the sign claim and the degree-$n$-versus-degree-$n-k$ test
   earlier in the same paragraph already make the point), or state the real consequence in the
   unit's notation — carrying the other convention's subscript over gives a Laguerre factor of
   degree $N+L$, hence $N+L$ radial nodes instead of the correct $N-L-1$.

2. [minor] definition (line 42) and remark (line 257): "nothing below uses integrality of $k$
   except the equivalent definition in item 2" / "Integrality of $k$ is needed only for item 2"
   overstate the case slightly. Item 1 *as written* uses $(n+k)!$ and $(k+m)!$, and
   $L^{k}_{n}(0)=\binom{n+k}{n}=(n+k)!/(n!\,k!)$ is a factorial identity; for real $\nu>-1$ these
   must be read with gamma functions ($\Gamma(n+\nu+1)/[\Gamma(\nu+m+1)m!(n-m)!]$, and Hassani's
   Problem 8.32 gives $L^\nu_n(0)=\Gamma(n+\nu+1)/[n!\Gamma(\nu+1)]$). The content of item 1
   survives, only its notation needs changing — which is worth one clause, since item 6 already
   does exactly this for the norm.

3. [minor] statement item 4 (line 77): the header "For $n\ge1$ and $k\ge0$" is needed for the
   first three relations but not the fourth: $L^{k+1}_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}L^{k}_{j}$ holds at $n=0$
   too (both sides are $1$), and the proof at line 170 derives it for all $n$.

4. [minor] application (line 364): "the first-order truncation already gives the Chapman-Enskog
   viscosity to within about one per cent for a hard-sphere gas" is an uncited quantitative claim
   in an `original` block. The figure usually quoted for the first Sonine approximation to the
   hard-sphere viscosity is about $1.6\%$ low (and nearer $2\%$ for thermal conductivity), so
   "about one per cent" is optimistic. Either soften to "a couple of per cent" or attach a source.
