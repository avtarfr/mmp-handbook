## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "The sign convention, stated before anything is proved" (line 65): the rule given for
   which formulas survive the change of convention is wrong in both directions, and the unit's own part 8
   contradicts it. The text says that what changes sign is "every table entry with odd $m$, every recurrence
   with an odd number of $P^{m}$'s of odd $m$, and the negative-$m$ relation if one is not careful".
   (a) False positive: the step-in-$l$ recurrence at odd $m$ contains three functions of odd $m$ (an odd
   number), yet it is *identical* in the two conventions — it is printed in the same form by Arfken, Weber
   and Harris (15.88) and by Riley, Hobson and Bence (18.42), and the unit itself prints that one form in
   part 8. Checked symbolically for $1\le l\le 4$, all admissible $m$, in both conventions: it holds in both.
   (b) False negative: the step-in-$m$ recurrence of part 8 at *even* $m$ contains two functions of odd $m$
   (an even number), yet it does change: the unit's form fails for the phase-free functions at
   $(l,m)=(3,2)$ and $(4,2)$ (verified symbolically), and Riley, Hobson and Bence's (18.41) differs from the
   unit's form by the sign of the $2mx$ term for every $m$.
   (c) The negative-$m$ relation is in fact form-identical in the two conventions — Arfken, Weber and Harris
   (15.81), Riley, Hobson and Bence (18.33) and Hassani (13.38) all print the same formula — so listing it
   among the things that change sign, even hedged with "if one is not careful", points the reader the wrong
   way. The correct criterion is parity of $m$ across the terms: a linear identity in the $P_{l}^{m}$ keeps
   its written form under $P\mapsto(-1)^{m}P$ exactly when all the upper indices appearing in it have the
   same parity. Fix: replace the clause with that criterion, and say explicitly that the step-in-$l$
   recurrence is convention-independent while the step-in-$m$ recurrence is not, for every $m$.
   This matters because the remark exists precisely to stop the reader making the sign error it describes.
2. [blocking] statement, part 6 (line 107): "Consequently any $f$ on $(-1,1)$ expands, at fixed $m\ge0$, as
   $f=\sum_{k\ge0}a_{m+k}P_{m+k}^{m}$" with $a_{l}$ given by an integral. This is false as written and does
   not follow from orthogonality. Counterexample: $f(x)=(1-x^{2})^{-1}$ is a function on $(-1,1)$ for which
   every coefficient integral diverges, so no expansion exists; more generally orthogonality alone never
   yields an expansion, which needs completeness of $\{P_{l}^{m}\}_{l\ge m}$ in $L^{2}(-1,1)$ at fixed $m$ —
   a separate theorem that the unit neither states nor cites. The source is weaker than our text here:
   Riley, Hobson and Bence (18.38), p. 591, says "any reasonable function". The style guide forbids hidden
   assumptions in a statement block, and the companion unit spec.legendre-polynomials does this correctly
   (it says $f\in L^{2}$, "the series converging in $L^{2}$", and proves completeness from Weierstrass).
   Fix: restrict to $f\in L^{2}(-1,1)$, say the series converges in $L^{2}$, and point to the completeness
   argument (or state it as a consequence of :ref spec.orthogonal-polynomials-general / funcan.orthonormal-bases).
3. [minor] statement, part 8 (line 123): the generating function is stated for the phase-free $\tilde P$ but
   the block src does not name the equation it comes from. It is Riley, Hobson and Bence (18.40), p. 592
   (equivalently Arfken, Weber and Harris (15.82), p. 743, whose $g_{m}$ generates the *polynomial* factors
   $\mathcal{P}_{l}^{m}$ and carries the $(-1)^{m}$ instead). Fix: cite (18.40) at that display and note the
   Arfken form, so the reader can see why the two look different.
4. [minor] proof, the normalisation (line 239): "Each boundary term retains at least one undifferentiated
   factor $R$ and so vanishes at $x=\pm1$." True, but for the terms in which $(1-x^{2})^{m}$ has been
   differentiated fewer than $m$ times the surviving factor comes from $(1-x^{2})^{m}=(-R)^{m}$, not from
   $R^{l}$; the single sentence hides a split into two ranges ($i\le m-1$ and $i\ge m$, where $i$ counts the
   derivatives landing on the first factor). Fix: one clause naming both ranges.
