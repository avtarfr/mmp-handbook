## Findings
1. [source] statement, part 2 (line 66), and proof, part 2 (line 125): Stakgold and Holst's Theorem 6.3.4 is printed as "these eigenfunctions $\{e_k\}$ must therefore form an orthonormal basis for $R_K$, but not necessarily for $H$". $R_K$ is in general not closed — their own Remark 1 to Thm 6.2.1, p. 360, says so and uses it — and the closed span of an orthonormal family is closed, so no orthonormal family can be a basis of a non-closed subspace. The true statement, and the one their proof delivers, is the one the unit states: a basis of $\overline{\operatorname{ran}\mathcal K}$. The unit reports the difference honestly in its own parenthetical, so this is not a defect in our text. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.
2. [source] statement, part 3 (line 71): Stakgold and Holst's Theorem 6.3.7, p. 377, is stated for "the $m$-th iterated kernel" with no restriction on $m$; read with $m=1$ allowed it contradicts the Remark printed immediately after it ("For $m=1$, (6.3.17) does not necessarily hold in the sense of uniform convergence even if $k(x,\xi)$ is continuous"), which is the whole reason Mercer's theorem is then needed. The unit states the uniform refinement for $m\ge2$ only, which is right. Recorded.
3. [source] remark, second bullet (line 175): Arfken, Weber and Harris's Exercise 21.4.4 says the equation $\varphi(x)=\lambda\int_0^\infty\mathrm e^{-xt}\varphi(t)\mathrm dt$ "has an infinite number of solutions, of which one is $\varphi(x)=x^{-1/2}$, $\lambda=\pi^{-1/2}$", while the Note on p. 1078 attributes the anomaly to there being "an infinite number of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions" — so the infinitude is over $\lambda$, not at the one displayed $\lambda$, which is not what the exercise's wording suggests. The unit quotes the printed sentence and adds "(They say nothing about how many solutions belong to that one value of $\lambda$.)", which is exactly the right amount of deference. Recorded.
4. [minor] remark, "A bonus from part 3" (line 179): "Setting $x=t$ in the expansion of $K_{m}$ and integrating gives $\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{-m}=\int_a^b K_{m}(x,x)\mathrm dx$ — valid for $m\ge2$, where part 3 supplies the expansion unconditionally". The conclusion is right, but the route is not: part 3 gives mean-square convergence of $K_m(\cdot,t)$ for a.e. fixed $t$, which licenses neither evaluation on the diagonal nor integration of the diagonal values. What does prove it at $m\ge2$ is the Hilbert–Schmidt trace formula: $\mathcal K$ and $\mathcal K^{m-1}$ are both Hilbert–Schmidt, so $\int_a^b K_m(x,x)\mathrm dx=\iint K(x,s)K_{m-1}(s,x)\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dx=\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal K\mathcal K^{m-1})=\sum_n\mu_n^m$, with no continuity anywhere. (Stakgold and Holst themselves derive their (6.3.19) "from (6.3.17)", i.e. from the continuous case.) Fix: give the trace-formula reason, or attribute the identity to the uniform case only.
5. [minor] proof, part 2 (line 118 vs line 120): the extremal principle named and quoted is Stakgold and Holst's Theorem 6.3.1, which is a statement about the whole space. The bound actually used one paragraph later, $\lVert\mathcal Kv\rVert\le\lvert\mu_{N+1}\rvert\lVert v\rVert$ for $v\perp e_1,\dots,e_N$, is their Theorem 6.3.3, eq. (6.3.5). Fix: name 6.3.3 at the step that uses it.
6. [minor] definition (line 37): "the case $K(x,t)=K(t,x)$ with $K$ real being the **symmetric** one that Arfken, Weber and Harris and Riley, Hobson and Bence treat" can be read as putting reality into the word *symmetric*, which `green.integral-equations` has just fixed to mean $K(x,t)=K(t,x)$ alone (and explicitly warns that a complex symmetric kernel is not self-adjoint). Fix: "the real symmetric case, $K(x,t)=K(t,x)$ with $K$ real, is the one Arfken … treat".
7. [minor] proof, part 4 (line 154): "because $\lambda_{n}\to\infty$ (or the family is finite)". The characteristic values are real but need not be positive, so this should be $\lvert\lambda_n\rvert\to\infty$, as part 1 states it. The argument that follows (boundedness of $\lvert\lambda_n/(\lambda_n-\lambda)\rvert$) needs only the modulus.
8. [minor] proof, part 4 (line 164): "$\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{-2}=\sum_{n}\mu_{n}^{2}\le\iint\lvert K\rvert^{2}$" is asserted. $\iint|K|^2=\operatorname{tr}\mathcal K^*\mathcal K$ is quoted from Reed and Simon through `green.integral-equations`, but turning that trace into $\sum\mu_n^2$ needs it computed in an orthonormal basis of $L^2$ containing the $e_n$ — which part 2 supplies, since the complement of the closed span of the $e_n$ is $\ker\mathcal K$. One clause would close it.
9. [minor] remark, "Mercer's hypotheses" (line 178): "automatic for a positive kernel such as a Green's function of a definite operator" uses "positive kernel" for "kernel of a positive operator". Stakgold and Holst warn on p. 375 that the two are different ("The fact that an operator is positive does not mean that its kernel is a positive function of $x$ and $\xi$"). Fix: "automatic for the kernel of a positive operator, such as …".
10. [minor] application, first paragraph (line 237): the translation of a self-adjoint boundary-value problem into $u=\theta\,\mathcal Gu$ silently drops two conditions Stakgold and Holst state at (6.3.13): that the order $p$ be even, and that $\theta=0$ be arranged not to be an eigenvalue (otherwise the Green's function does not exist and one shifts $L$ by $\theta'$). The claim "$\mathcal G$ has no null space" is then argued correctly. Fix: add the half-sentence about shifting $L$.
