## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet (line 170): the claim that **Hermitian symmetry** "is used in exactly two places in the proof: the reality-and-orthogonality computation of part 1, and the identification of the Fourier coefficients of $K_m$ in part 3" is false about our own proof. The first use is the self-adjointness computation at the head of part 1, which the proof itself flags ("The last step uses the Hermitian symmetry"); self-adjointness is then indispensable in part 2 (the extremal principle $\lVert\mathcal{K}v\rVert\le\lvert\mu_{N+1}\rvert\lVert v\rVert$ holds for compact *self-adjoint* operators, and $\mathcal{K}$ maps $M_N^{\perp}$ into itself only because it is self-adjoint) and in part 4, where relation (C) is obtained from $(\mathcal{K}y,e_{n})=(y,\mathcal{K}e_{n})$. So the hypothesis is used in at least four places, and "drop it and both fail" understates: dropping it collapses parts 2 and 4 as well. As written a reader concludes that parts 2 and 4 are independent of the hypothesis. Fix: say that Hermitian symmetry enters first as self-adjointness of $\mathcal{K}$, which every later part rests on (parts 2 and 4 through the spectral/extremal machinery), and that it is additionally used at the kernel level twice — the direct reality-and-orthogonality computation and the Fourier coefficients of $K_m$ in part 3.
2. [minor] proof, part 4, resolvent form (line 164): "insert $(f,e_{n})=\int f\overline{e_{n}}$ and exchange sum and integral, which the $L^{2}$ convergence permits" is thinner than the rest of the proof, because $L^{2}$ convergence of $\sum\frac{(f,e_n)}{\lambda_n-\lambda}e_n$ does not by itself say that $R(\cdot,\cdot;\lambda)$ is a function at all. It is one: the Hilbert-Schmidt condition gives $\sum\lambda_{n}^{-2}=\sum\mu_n^2<\infty$, hence $\sum\lvert\lambda_{n}-\lambda\rvert^{-2}<\infty$, so $R\in L^{2}([a,b]^{2})$ and is itself a Hermitian Hilbert-Schmidt kernel. Fix: add that half-sentence; it also justifies calling $R$ a kernel in the statement.
3. [minor] remark, second bullet (line 175), on Arfken's Exercise 21.4.4: "which has the non-normalisable solution $\varphi(x)=x^{-1/2}$ at $\lambda=\pi^{-1/2}$ and in fact infinitely many solutions" can be read as asserting infinitely many solutions *at that value of $\lambda$*, which the source does not say (arfken-2013, Exercise 21.4.4, p. 1077, says the equation "has an infinite number of solutions, of which one is" that pair). Fix: "and, the book adds, infinitely many solutions in all".
4. [minor] statement, part 2 (line 66): the unit says $\{e_{n}\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\overline{\operatorname{ran}\mathcal{K}}$ where stakgold-holst-2011 Thm 6.3.4, p. 373 says "a basis for $R_{K}$". The closure is the mathematically correct reading and the unit is right to take it, but the difference from the printed statement is worth a half-line in the proof, where Thm 6.3.4 is cited verbatim. Not an error.

5. [minor] remark, second bullet (line 171): "**The Hilbert-Schmidt condition and the finite interval** are what make $\mathcal{K}$ compact" credits the finite interval with something it does not do. The unit's own prerequisite green.integral-equations states (with reed-simon-1980 Thm VI.23, p. 210) that a Hilbert-Schmidt kernel generates a compact operator on any $L^2(M,\mathrm{d}\mu)$; stakgold-holst-2011 p. 358 even gives $\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}-\xi^{2}}$ on the whole line as a Hilbert-Schmidt kernel. Both warning examples in the bullet fail the Hilbert-Schmidt condition, not merely the finiteness of the interval. Fix: say the Hilbert-Schmidt condition alone gives compactness, and that the finite interval is what the rest of the unit needs (continuity on a compact square for Mercer, the trace integral, the Sturm-Liouville reading).
