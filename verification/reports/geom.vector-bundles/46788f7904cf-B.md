## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first bullet: "Dropping condition (ii) from the
   definition would leave a family of vector spaces with no way to compare neighbouring fibres."
   The unit's own definition block numbers nothing — it says "First, ... Second, ...". "(ii)" is
   Lee's numbering (p. 249-250), not ours, so the reference dangles. Fix: write "dropping the
   local-triviality clause".
2. [minor] remark, last bullet: "So $TS^{2}$ has no global frame and is not trivial ...; while
   $TS^{1}$ and $TS^{3}$ are trivial." Both halves are true, but only the first follows from the
   hairy ball theorem quoted immediately before it. A nowhere-vanishing field is one section, not a
   frame: $S^5$ also carries one and $TS^5$ is not trivial. Fix: mark the $S^1$/$S^3$ claim as a
   separate fact (they are Lie groups, hence parallelizable) rather than letting it read as a
   consequence of the quoted theorem.
3. [minor] example, closing paragraph: "because $\tau$ is nowhere constant and the pair of charts
   cannot be reduced to one, the two trivializations really do have to be glued. By the hairy ball
   theorem no third trivialization covers the whole sphere". $\tau$ being non-constant is not an
   argument for anything (a trivial bundle can have non-constant transition functions between two
   of its local trivializations), and "no third trivialization" has no antecedent — what the hairy
   ball theorem rules out is a *global* trivialization. The conclusion drawn, that $TS^2$ is not a
   product, is correct and does follow from the hairy ball theorem plus part 2. Fix: delete the
   $\tau$-non-constant clause and say "no global trivialization exists".
