## Findings

1. [source] application, "Oscillating reactions": the unit reports that Strogatz gives the large-$a$ limit of the CDIMA period as $2\pi/\sqrt{15}\approx1.63$ and adds that the value is $1.62231\ldots$. Confirmed at strogatz-1994 p. 260: the formula $T = 2\pi[(a^2+25)/(15a^2-625)]^{1/2}$ and the limit $2\pi/\sqrt{15}$ are both right, and $2\pi/\sqrt{15} = 1.6223114704\ldots$, which rounds to $1.62$, not $1.63$. The unit handles this correctly - it follows the source and states the number - so this is not a fault of the unit. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add dyn.hopf-bifurcation strogatz-1994 "Example 8.3.3, p. 260" ...`.

2. [minor] statement "A cycle is born whenever the cubic coefficient has the right sign" (line 103): the regularity hypothesis is given as "the functions $R,\Theta$ are continuous and $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$", but Step 5 of its proof invokes dyn.poincare-bendixson, whose trapping-region form requires "$f$ has continuous first partial derivatives on some neighbourhood of $R$". Mere continuity is not enough: without uniqueness of solutions the flow $\phi_t$ and hence "positively invariant" are not even well defined, and Poincare-Bendixson genuinely fails. The unit's definition block does impose "$f$ and its first partial derivatives in $x$ continuous in $(x,\mu)$" on every family it discusses, so under the intended reading the statement is fine; but the statement re-states regularity for $R$ and $\Theta$ and stops at continuity, which invites exactly the wrong reading at the one point where it matters. Fix: say in the statement that the Cartesian field is $C^1$ on the punctured disc (as the definition block already assumes), and keep the growth bounds as the extra hypotheses.

3. [minor] example "Enrichment destabilises a predator-prey equilibrium", Step 6 (line 285): "the cycle attracts at a rate of order $\lvert\tau\rvert/2 \approx 1.7\times10^{-4}$ at $K = 3.002$". The attraction rate of the newborn cycle is the Floquet exponent, which near a supercritical Hopf point is $\approx -2\alpha = -\tau$, not $-\tau/2$ ($\dot r = \mu r - r^3$ has $h'(\sqrt\mu) = -2\mu$ while $\alpha = \mu$). I computed the Poincare-map multiplier at $K=3.002$: $0.99488$ over $T = 15.396477$, giving an exponent of $-3.3319\times10^{-4}$, which is $-\lvert\tau\rvert = -3.3311\times10^{-4}$ to four figures and twice the figure quoted. The conclusion drawn - that plain forward integration is too slow and shooting is needed - is unaffected, and so is every number in the table. Fix: "$\lvert\tau\rvert \approx 3.3\times10^{-4}$".

4. [minor] proof "The subcritical model", Step 2 (line 174): "Since $r_-^2 = \tfrac12(1-\sqrt{1+4\mu}) < \tfrac12 < \tfrac12(1+\sqrt{1+4\mu}) = r_+^2$ whenever $\mu<0$ and $\sqrt{1+4\mu}>0$ ...". The qualifier "$\mu<0$" is needed only for the left inequality (so that $r_-$ exists as a positive radius); the right inequality $\tfrac12 < r_+^2$, which is what makes the outer cycle stable, holds for every $\mu > -\tfrac14$. As written the step does not cover the stability of $r_+$ in the $\mu>0$ case, which the statement's third bullet asserts. Fix: separate the two inequalities, noting $r_+^2 > \tfrac12$ for all $\mu > -\tfrac14$ and $r_+^2 > 1$ for $\mu > 0$.

5. [minor] statement "A cycle is born ..." (line 125), last sentence: "For $a > 0$ the mirror-image argument (run time backwards) produces an unstable cycle for $-\mu_1 < \mu < 0$". The time-reversed argument produces a *closed orbit* that repels forward-time trajectories crossing the annulus boundaries; it does not produce a *limit* cycle, for exactly the reason the preceding sentence gives ("The argument gives existence, not uniqueness: it does not by itself show the orbit is isolated"). Fix: "an unstable closed orbit".

## Checked
I verified the algebra of both models by hand. Supercritical: the conversion to Cartesian form is right term by term, the Jacobian at the origin is $\begin{pmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\\omega&\mu\end{pmatrix}$ with characteristic polynomial $(\lambda-\mu)^2+\omega^2$, and the unit's extra hypothesis $\omega+b\mu\neq0$ - which Strogatz does not state - is genuinely necessary and correctly explained: at $\mu=-\omega/b>0$ both $\dot r$ and $\dot\theta$ vanish on $r=\sqrt\mu$, so that circle is a curve of fixed points and the period $2\pi/\lvert\omega+b\mu\rvert$ is undefined. The claim that this can happen for at most one $\mu$, at a fixed positive distance from $0$, is right. Subcritical: $\rho^2-\rho-\mu=0$ with $\rho=r^2$ gives $r_\pm$ as stated; the root analysis (product $-\mu$, sum $1$) is right; $h'(r) = 2r^2(1-2r^2)$ at a positive zero is correct after substituting $\mu = r^4-r^2$; at $\mu=-\tfrac14$, $h(r) = -r(r^2-\tfrac12)^2$ exactly, so the two cycles coalesce half-stably at $r=1/\sqrt2$.

The original third statement is correct, and I checked each of the four bounds in $\mu_1$ against the job it does. Inner circle $\rho_-=\sqrt{\mu/(2|a|)}$: $\dot r \ge \rho_-(\tfrac12\mu - C\rho_-^3) > 0$ needs $\mu < 2|a|^3/C^2$. Outer circle $\rho_+=\sqrt{2\mu/|a|}$: $\dot r \le \rho_+(-\mu + C\rho_+^3) < 0$ needs $\mu < |a|^3/(8C^2)$. The quoted $|a|^3/(32C^2)$ is stricter than both and is exactly what makes $C\rho_+^3 < \mu/2$, as Step 2 claims. $\rho_+ \le r_0$ needs $\mu \le |a|r_0^2/2$, and $C\rho_+ < |\omega|$ needs $\mu < |a|\omega^2/(2C^2)$ - the other two bounds. Part 1 is right too: $\dot r \le -\tfrac12|a|r^3$ on $(0,r_1]$ with $r_1 = \min\{r_0,|a|/(2C)\}$, which gives both asymptotic stability and the exclusion of closed orbits meeting the disc. Finding 2 is the only gap I found in this block.

Against the sources: strogatz-1994 p. 251 confirms both rules of thumb and both "artefacts" (circular cycle, horizontal eigenvalue paths) in the form the unit reports them, as rules of thumb and not theorems; p. 252 confirms the subcritical model $\dot r = \mu r + r^3 - r^5$, the coexisting attractors, the hysteresis and the destruction at $\mu=-1/4$ "via another type of bifurcation"; p. 253 confirms that linearization cannot distinguish the cases, that the analytical criterion "can be difficult to use", the two-sweep computer test, and the caveat that computer experiments are not proofs - the unit's remark paraphrases all of this accurately; pp. 259-260 confirm $b_c = 3a/5 - 25/a$ and the period formula. hirsch-smale-devaney-2013 p. 182 confirms "no new equilibria arise" and pp. 270-272 the circuit family, the Jacobian $\begin{pmatrix}\mu&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$, the dead circuit for $\mu\le0$ including the centre at $\mu=0$, and the unique attracting $\gamma_\mu$ shrinking to the origin. The degenerate-case computation is ours and checks out: the pendulum's Jacobian is $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-\mu\end{pmatrix}$ with $\tau=-\mu$, $\Delta=1$, eigenvalues $\tfrac12(-\mu\pm\sqrt{\mu^2-4})$, and $E = \tfrac12y^2+1-\cos x$ is conserved at $\mu=0$.
