2. [minor] statement "A cycle is born whenever the cubic coefficient has the right sign" (line 105) and its proof, step 5 (line 198): the statement assumes only that "$R,\Theta$ are continuous and $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$", but step 5 invokes :ref[dyn.poincare-bendixson] in its trapping-region form, whose hypothesis 2 in that unit reads "$f$ has continuous first partial derivatives on some neighbourhood of $R$". Continuity alone does not license the appeal — without local uniqueness of solutions the limit-set argument behind Poincare-Bendixson is not available. I could not construct a counterexample to the conclusion, so I am not calling it blocking, but as written the last step of our own proof is not supported by our own cited theorem. The fix costs nothing, since both models and any normal-form reduction supply it: require $R$ and $\Theta$ to have continuous first partial derivatives (or state the hypothesis on the Cartesian field). Verifier A may rate this higher than I have.
3. [minor] statement "A cycle is born..." part 1 (line 120): "For $-\mu_0 \le \mu \le 0$ the origin is asymptotically stable". The proof's step 1 gives $\dot r \le -\tfrac12\lvert a\rvert r^3$ on $(0,r_1]$, which is asymptotic stability but at an algebraic, not exponential, rate at $\mu=0$ — worth saying, because the whole point of the $\mu=0$ case is that the linearisation predicts a centre. Fix: add "(only algebraically fast at $\mu=0$)", matching the parallel remark the unit already makes in the subcritical proof, step 3.
4. [minor] remark (line 203): "Transversality $\alpha'(\mu_c)\ne0$. Not used in any proof above". True of the three statements, but the definition (line 31) offers $\alpha'(\mu_c)\ne0$ only as "the usual sufficient condition" for condition 2, so a reader may not notice that condition 2 itself (sign change of $\alpha$) is also never used. Fix: say "neither condition 2 nor transversality is used in any proof above; the models are analysed at fixed $\mu$".
5. [minor] frontmatter `loc` for strogatz-1994 (line 11) attributes the degenerate example to "$\ddot x + \mu\dot x + \sin x = 0$, where a band of closed orbits replaces any limit cycle because the system becomes conservative at $\mu=0$ (p. 253)". Strogatz is slightly stronger than that on p. 253: he says that at $\mu=0$ "we do not have a true Hopf bifurcation", whereas the unit's definition (line 39) makes "degenerate" a third kind of Hopf bifurcation. Strogatz does himself write "a degenerate Hopf bifurcation" two sentences earlier, so the unit's usage is his and this is not infidelity; but the tension is worth one clause. Fix: note in the definition that Strogatz calls the degenerate case "not a true Hopf bifurcation" while using the name.
