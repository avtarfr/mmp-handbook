1. [blocking] `:::statement[The supercritical model]` (lines 44-58): counterexample. The statement says "Fix $\omega\ne0$ and $b\in\mathbb R$" and then asserts, for the system $\dot r = \mu r - r^3$, $\dot\theta = \omega + br^2$, that the Cartesian form has the origin as its **only** fixed point, that for every $\mu>0$ the circle $r=\sqrt\mu$ is a closed orbit onto which every other trajectory spirals, and that its period is exactly $2\pi/\lvert\omega+b\mu\rvert$. Take $\omega=1$, $b=-1$, $\mu=1$. Then the Cartesian field is $\dot x = x-y-(x^2+y^2)(x-y)$, $\dot y = x+y-(x^2+y^2)(x+y)$, which I checked symbolically vanishes identically on $x^2+y^2=1$: the whole unit circle is a curve of equilibria. So the origin is not the only fixed point, $r=\sqrt\mu$ is not a closed orbit, nothing spirals onto a periodic orbit, and $2\pi/\lvert\omega+b\mu\rvert = 2\pi/0$ is undefined. The proof is aware of this — step 1 says "we exclude this by taking $\omega+b\mu\ne0$, which the period formula below assumes in any case" — but the statement carries no such hypothesis, so the statement is false as written and the unit contradicts itself between statement and proof. (Hirsch, Smale and Devaney avoid it by taking $\theta'=1$ and saying explicitly, p. 181, "the origin is the only equilibrium point for this system, since $\theta'\ne0$"; Strogatz, pp. 250-251, never claims it for general $b$.) Fix: add $\omega + b\mu \ne 0$ to the hypotheses of the statement, or restrict it to $\lvert b\mu\rvert < \lvert\omega\rvert$, which is the regime the rest of the unit is about.
