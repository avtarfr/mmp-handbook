## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "The supercritical model" (and, identically, statement "The subcritical model"): a hypothesis is missing, and the statements are false without it. The supercritical statement fixes $\omega \ne 0$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}$ arbitrary and then asserts, for every $\mu > 0$, that the origin is the only fixed point, that $r = \sqrt\mu$ is a closed orbit and a stable limit cycle, and that the period is $2\pi/|\omega + b\mu|$. Take $\omega = 1$, $b = -1$, $\mu = 1$: then $\dot r = r - r^3$ and $\dot\theta = 1 - r^2$ both vanish on $r = 1$, and in Cartesian form $\dot x = \mu x - \omega y - r^2(x+by) = -(\omega+b\mu)y = 0$ and likewise $\dot y = 0$, so the whole circle $r = 1$ consists of fixed points. It is not a closed orbit, nothing spirals onto it, it is not a limit cycle, and $2\pi/|\omega+b\mu|$ is $2\pi/0$. The proof notices this — step 1 ends "we exclude this by taking $\omega + b\mu \ne 0$" — so the proof is carried out under a hypothesis the statement does not contain. The same gap is in the subcritical statement, which asserts the origin is the only fixed point and that $r_\pm$ are limit cycles for all $b$: choosing $b = -\omega/r_+^2$ fills the outer circle with fixed points. Fix: add $\omega + b\mu \ne 0$ (resp. $\omega + b r_\pm^2 \ne 0$) to each statement, or restrict to $|\mu|$ small enough that $|b\mu| < |\omega|$, which is the regime Strogatz actually treats (§8.2, pp. 250–252).
2. [blocking] example "Enrichment destabilises a predator-prey equilibrium", step 3: "For $K<3$ the trace is negative (stable spiral, since $\tau^2 < 4\Delta$ there)". With $\tau = (K-3)/2K$ and $\Delta = (K-1)/4K$, the inequality $\tau^2 < 4\Delta$ is $(K-3)^2 < 4K(K-1)$, i.e. $3K^2 + 2K - 9 > 0$, i.e. $K > (2\sqrt7 - 1)/3 = 1.4305009\ldots$. For $1 < K < 1.4305$ the equilibrium is a stable *node*, not a spiral, so the parenthetical is false on part of the stated range. Fix: write "for $1.431 < K < 3$", or drop the word "spiral" and say only that the trace is negative and the determinant positive.
3. [blocking] example, the numerical table, row $K = 3.002$: $A = 0.0632442$ and $A/\sqrt{K-3} = 1.414182$ are wrong in the fifth significant figure. Computing the limit cycle directly as a fixed point of the Poincare return map on the section $x = 1$, $\dot x > 0$ (Brent to $10^{-15}$, RK45 at rtol $10^{-13}$, atol $10^{-16}$) gives $A = 0.0632572$ and $A/\sqrt{K-3} = 1.414474$, with period $15.396477$ — the period in the table is right to six figures, only the amplitude is not. Every other row of the table reproduces exactly ($K=3.005$: $0.1000461$, $1.414865$, $15.405297$; $K=3.010$: $0.1415514$, $1.415514$, $15.420003$; $K=3.020$: $0.2003665$, $1.416805$, $15.449436$; $K=3.050$, $3.100$, $3.200$ likewise to all printed digits), so the one bad entry is almost certainly an under-converged transient: at $K = 3.002$ the transient decays at rate $\alpha = (K-3)/4K \approx 1.7\times10^{-4}$, so a run must reach $t \sim 10^5$ before the amplitude settles. This also undercuts the claim that "successive periods in each run agree to better than $10^{-11}$" for that row. Fix: recompute the row (and re-derive the $0.038\%$ figure in step 7 from the corrected number). Note the corrected value sits *above* $\sqrt2 = 1.4142136$ and on the straight-line trend of the other six rows, which strengthens the unit's own conclusion rather than weakening it.
