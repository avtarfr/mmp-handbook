## Findings
1. [minor] method, step 4: "If the value sets of $K$ differ, stop: no local isometry exists between the corresponding regions." Read as "no isometry carries region $A$ onto region $B$" this is correct, since an onto isometry preserves $K$ pointwise and so matches the two ranges. Read as "no local isometry of any part of $A$ into $\bar S$ exists" it is false: if $K \equiv 1$ on $A$ and $\bar S$ takes the values $\{1,2\}$, the ranges differ yet a local isometry of $A$ into $\bar S$ may well exist. Fix: say "no isometry carries one region onto the other", or sharpen to "if no value of $K$ on $A$ is taken anywhere on $\bar S$, no local isometry into $\bar S$ exists".
2. [minor] method, step 6: "a surface with $K$ constant and nonzero is locally isometric to a sphere ($K>0$) or to the pseudosphere ($K<0$)" is Minding's theorem, which the unit neither names, cites nor proves. It is true, and a method block may reasonably assert it, but it is the one claim in the unit that is neither derived here nor attributed. Fix: name Minding and point at a source, or mark it as quoted without proof.
3. [minor] remark, last bullet: "the sphere is locally conformal to the plane (Mercator's projection)". True, and it is in the cited book — do Carmo Exercise 16, p. 233, which constructs the Mercator parametrization and shows $E=G=\operatorname{sech}^2u$, $F=0$. The frontmatter lists Exercises 18 and 19 from that page but not 16, so a reader checking the remark has no locator for its first half while the second half (Exercise 19) is cited. Fix: add Exercise 16, p. 233 to the frontmatter locator.
4. [minor] proof, Part 4: the general orthogonal-chart formula is obtained by "Substituting into the Gauss formula and collecting terms", with the substitution deferred to do Carmo's Exercise 1 and to the worked example. The result is correct — I verified the reduction symbolically in full generality, see below — but the unit asserts rather than performs the one step. Fix: either show the two-line reduction or say explicitly that it is left to the cited exercise.
5. [minor] definition, conformal map: do Carmo's Definition 3, p. 229, asks that $\lambda^2$ be a nowhere-zero *differentiable* function; the unit says "a nowhere-zero function $\lambda$" and drops differentiability. Nothing in the unit uses conformality quantitatively, so this is cosmetic.
