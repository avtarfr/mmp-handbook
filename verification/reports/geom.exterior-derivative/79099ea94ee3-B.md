## Findings
1. [blocking] definition (line 43): "Part 4 of the statement below is what makes this independent of the chart, so the pieces agree on overlaps and define a single $(k+1)$-form on $M$." Part 4 of the statement is **Functions** ($df(X) = Xf$), which says nothing about charts and cannot make the chart recipe $d\omega = \varphi^{*}(d[(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\omega])$ well defined. The property that does it is Part 5, **Naturality**. The unit's own proof agrees and so contradicts this line: Step 5 ("the definition on a manifold is unambiguous") says "the transition map ... is a diffeomorphism between open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ ..., so step 4 applies to it", and the proof's step 4 is "naturality on open sets (property 5, Euclidean case)". The cited source agrees too: Lee's proof of Thm 14.24, p. 366, establishes well-definedness from Prop. 14.23(d), the pullback-naturality clause, not from the clause on functions. The unit's other numeric cross-references are correct ("Part 3 below says every exact form is closed"; "the right-hand side of property 6"), so the convention is unambiguous and this one is a genuine error, not a different numbering. Fix: change "Part 4" to "Part 5 (naturality)".
2. [minor] definition (line 24 ff.): the Euclidean paragraph is set up for "an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$" only, but the manifold paragraph admits a manifold "with or without boundary", whose boundary charts map onto open subsets of the half-space $\mathbb{H}^n$, which are not open in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The coordinate formula is therefore not literally defined on the sets the chart recipe feeds it. Lee states the Euclidean case for "an open subset $U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{H}^n$" ((14.19), p. 363), and the unit's proof is aware of the gap ("or of the half-space, at a boundary point", step 5). Fix: write "an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ or of $\mathbb{H}^n$" in the first line of the definition.
3. [minor] remark, bullet "Bump functions, in uniqueness only": "Uniqueness of $d$ is therefore a genuinely $C^{\infty}$ statement, while existence is not." What has been shown is that *this proof* of uniqueness needs bump functions; that the uniqueness assertion itself fails, or is unprovable, in the real-analytic category is a stronger claim and is not established here. (The preceding sentence also silently assumes the manifold is connected, since a real-analytic function vanishing on an open subset of a disconnected manifold need not be zero.) Fix: say that the proof of uniqueness, not uniqueness itself, is where the $C^{\infty}$ structure is spent.
4. [minor] remark, bullet "The sign cannot be dropped": the claim sentence is "Property 2 without $(-1)^{k}$ is inconsistent with property 3", but the worked check that follows contradicts the **coordinate definition** ("A signless Leibniz rule would give $+\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$ instead, contradicting the definition"), not property 3. The inconsistency with properties 1, 3, 4 alone is real but needs the extra step of regrouping $dx\wedge y\,dz$ as $(y\,dx)\wedge dz$, which gives $-dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$ from the same signless rule. Fix: either say "inconsistent with the coordinate definition" or add the second grouping.
5. [minor] statement (line 45) and remark: Hassani's Thm 28.5.3, p. 889, which is cited on the statement block, takes naturality as the **fifth defining axiom** of $d$ (and states existence and uniqueness only on a neighbourhood $U$ of each point), whereas this unit's statement characterises $d$ by four properties and demotes naturality to a derived one. Both are faithful to their own sources and the unit is right to follow Lee's Thm 14.24 for the global four-property version, but a reader comparing the two books will see a difference the unit does not mention. Fix: one clause in the remark noting that Hassani assumes naturality rather than deriving it.
