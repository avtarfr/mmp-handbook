[minor] application (line 243, bullet "Multipole expansions outside a source"): the text says Laurent's theorem "applies to the single-valued remainder, whose Laurent expansion is $\sum_{n\ge1}c_n z^{-n}$". The remainder is $\Phi$ minus the monopole term, i.e. $\operatorname{Re}\sum_{n\ge1}c_n z^{-n}$, which is real-valued and harmonic, not analytic, so Laurent's theorem does not apply to it; what has a Laurent expansion is the analytic function whose real part it is. The "Equivalently, write $\Phi = \operatorname{Re} w$ ... apply Laurent's theorem to $-w'$" clause two sentences later is the correct version, and the displayed formula and all the coefficient identifications ($c_n = p_n/(2\pi\varepsilon_0 n)$; $-w' = E_x - iE_y$; monopole in the $z^{-1}$ coefficient of $-w'$, $2^n$-pole in that of $z^{-n-1}$) are right, so nothing here is wrong in substance. Fix: say "the analytic function $\sum_{n\ge1}c_n z^{-n}$ whose real part is the single-valued remainder", or drop the first formulation and keep only the $-w'$ one.
