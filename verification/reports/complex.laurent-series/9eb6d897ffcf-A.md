## Findings
1. [source] remark (line 165): the bullet reports Arfken, Weber and Harris, p. 495, as saying a Laurent series "always diverges at $z = z_0$". The report is faithful (the book's sentence at the foot of p. 495 reads "will always diverge at least at $z = z_0$"), but the book's "always" is too strong: if $f$ is analytic at $z_0$, the series on $0 < \lvert z - z_0\rvert < R_2$ is the Taylor series and converges at the centre. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`. Fix: none required; the unit passes.
2. [minor] remark (line 165): the bullet's own heading, "A Laurent series diverges at the centre", states the above in our voice before attributing it, so a reader skimming the bold leads takes an unconditional claim away. Fix: "A Laurent series with a nonzero principal part diverges at the centre."
3. [minor] remark (line 164): the inline locator for "the coefficients need not be computed by integration" is given as Arfken p. 495, but p. 495 says nothing of the kind -- its only remark about Eq. (11.48) is the contour freedom. The point is made on p. 496, by demonstration in Example 11.5.1 (partial fractions plus the binomial series, with the integrals then only confirmed). "Arfken stresses the point" also overstates: he shows it, he does not say it, and "contour integrals for $a_n$ are often intractable" is our judgement, not his. The frontmatter's own locator (pp. 494-496, role "the practical remark that coefficients need not be found by integration") covers the right range, so this is the inline page alone. Fix: cite p. 496, Example 11.5.1, and attribute by "as Arfken's worked example shows" rather than "stresses".
4. [minor] proof, Step 3 (line 108): the termwise integration here is justified only by "applies verbatim" and a bare assertion of uniform convergence, while the exactly parallel Step 4 supplies an explicit geometric majorant. Fix: one clause giving the majorant $M_2 (\lvert z-z_0\rvert/r_2)^n / r_2$ on $C_2$, to match Step 4.
5. [minor] definition (line 47): the principal part is called analytic on $\lvert z-z_0\rvert > R_1$ by citing found.power-series, but what that prerequisite gives is analyticity of a power series in $w$; the composition with $w = 1/(z-z_0)$, analytic and nonvanishing off $z_0$, is the extra half-line. Fix: say "composed with the analytic map $w = 1/(z-z_0)$".
