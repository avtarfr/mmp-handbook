That assertion fails at $j = 1$, where $Z_1(1) = X(1) \ne 0$ almost surely and $\mathrm{var}(Z_1(\tfrac12)) = \tfrac12$ rather than $2^{-j-1} = \tfrac14$. This is a defect in the source, not in our text: the unit identifies it explicitly in the bracketed paragraph, explains why one level is harmless, and gives the repair (take $X_0$ to be the linear interpolant on $\mathcal{D}_0$). Already recorded in verification/discrepancies.md against grimmett-stirzaker-2020, §13.3, p. 588; no new entry added. Not a reason to fail.
2. [minor] definition (line 35): "it is enough to require this for two intervals $[u,v]$ and $[s,t]$ with $u \le v \le s \le t$ and then for finitely many at a time" is ambiguous. The intended reading (the ordered pair condition covers all disjoint pairs, and the requirement is imposed on finite families) is correct, but the sentence can also be read as saying that pairwise independence of increments suffices, which is false for a general process. Fix: say that the ordering $u \le v \le s \le t$ is no loss of generality and that the requirement is imposed on every finite family, as in Grimmett and Stirzaker's Definition (8.6.1)(b).
3. [minor] idea (line 113): "the Poisson and gamma laws, and compound Poisson laws generally, are infinitely divisible and have finite variance". Every compound Poisson law is infinitely divisible, but it has finite variance only when the jump distribution does. Fix: "compound Poisson laws with square-integrable jumps".
4. [minor] proof "Existence of a version with continuous paths" (line 176): the display for $\sup_{0\le t\le1}|Z_j(t)|$ is asserted for all $j \ge 1$ and then corrected for $j = 1$ in the paragraph that follows. Cleaner to state the display for $j \ge 2$ from the start and treat $j = 1$ separately.
