## Findings
1. [minor] definition (line 47): "Normality and the proportionality of the variance to $t-s$ are then *deduced* rather than assumed" overstates what Folland does. On p. 331 the subdivision argument only "suggests" normality and "strongly indicates" proportionality, and Folland then writes "we are led to the third assumption: (10.22)" - i.e. (10.22) is a postulate, exactly as the unit's own frontmatter locator records. Nothing mathematical is wrong (the unit's `idea` block correctly hedges with "very nearly a consequence"), but the attribution misdescribes the source. Fix: say Folland motivates (10.22) by the subdivision/CLT argument and then postulates it, rather than "deduced rather than assumed".
2. [minor] statement "Path properties", part 2 (line 96): the statement is "almost surely ... infinite total variation on every interval $[a,b]$", but step 4 of the third proof establishes it for one fixed interval, a.s. The quantifier exchange (a single null set for all intervals at once) is not supplied. It is easy - run the argument on all rational $a<b$, intersect the countably many full-probability events, and use monotonicity of total variation - but as written the proof is one interval short of the statement. Fix: add that sentence.
3. [minor] proof "Covariance, and the scaling limit", step 3 (line 133): the statement block allows $t_1 = 0$ (it says $0 \le t_1 < t_2 < \cdots$), but step 3 asserts $d_j = t_j - t_{j-1} > 0$ and $m_n \to \infty$, which fail for $j=1$ when $t_1 = 0$. The claim is trivially true there ($W_n(0) = W(0) = 0$), so this is a gap, not an error. Fix: either require $t_1 > 0$, or dispose of $t_1 = 0$ in one line.
4. [minor] frontmatter prereqs (line 7): the quadratic-variation proof uses Chebyshev's inequality and refs prob.probability-inequalities (line 209), and the application refs pde.heat-kernel, pde.harmonic-functions and num.monte-carlo, but prob.probability-inequalities is not in `prereqs`. The style guide asks that every prerequisite relied on be named. Fix: add prob.probability-inequalities.
5. [minor] remark (line 224): "Fractional Brownian motion ... is Gaussian with stationary increments and continuous paths". Grimmett and Stirzaker, p. 423, say the sample paths "may be taken to be a.s. continuous"; the unit drops the qualifier. Fix: match the source's wording.
