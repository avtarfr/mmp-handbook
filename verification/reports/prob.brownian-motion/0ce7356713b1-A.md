## Findings
1. [source] proof "Existence of a version with continuous paths", step 1 parenthetical (lines 180): the unit faithfully reproduces Grimmett and Stirzaker's dyadic-interpolation setup, in which $X_0 \equiv 0$ is incompatible with the assertion (p. 588) that $Z_j$ vanishes at every even dyadic: at $j=1$, $Z_1(1) = X(1) \neq 0$, and $\mathrm{var}(Z_1(\tfrac12)) = \tfrac12$, not the $2^{-j-1} = \tfrac14$ that step 2 gives. This is a defect of the source, not of the unit; the unit names it, shows it is harmless (one level contributes one almost surely finite term to the sum of step 4, and steps 2-3 are only needed for $j \ge 2$), and gives the repair. It is already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` against grimmett-stirzaker-2020, §13.3, p. 588. Not blocking; no change required.
2. [minor] definition block (line 35): "it is enough to require this for two intervals $[u,v]$ and $[s,t]$ with $u \le v \le s \le t$ and then for finitely many at a time" can be misread as saying that independence of ordered *pairs* of increments suffices, which is false in general. What is meant is only that the source states condition (d) (p. 579) for two ordered intervals while the condition imposed is mutual independence of any finite collection. Fix: "we state it, as Grimmett and Stirzaker do, for two ordered intervals $[u,v]$, $[s,t]$ with $u \le v \le s \le t$, but what is required is mutual independence of any finite collection of increments over disjoint intervals."
3. [minor] proof "Existence...", step 4 (lines 198-201): Borel-Cantelli gives that only finitely many $j$ have $\sup_t|Z_j(t)| > x_j$, and $\sum_j x_j < \infty$; concluding $\sum_j \sup_t|Z_j(t)| < \infty$ also uses that each of those finitely many exceptional suprema is almost surely finite. That is immediate (each is a maximum of finitely many normal variables, as the step 1 parenthetical in effect observes for $j=1$), but the guide's no-unjustified-step rule wants the clause. Fix: add "each $\sup_t|Z_j(t)|$ is a maximum of finitely many normal variables and so is almost surely finite, and only finitely many of them exceed $x_j$".
4. [minor] example, step 2 (lines 276-278): "exchanging expectation with the integrals" is an unjustified Fubini-Tonelli interchange. It is easy - $\mathbb{E}\int_0^1\!\int_0^1 |W(s)W(u)|\,ds\,du \le \int_0^1\!\int_0^1 \sqrt{su}\,ds\,du < \infty$ by Cauchy-Schwarz - but should be said in half a line.
5. [minor] method, move 3 (line 261): "the normal family is closed under limits in distribution" is true only if degenerate normals (point masses, $v = 0$) are counted as normal. Worth one parenthesis, since move 3 is offered as a general tool and a limit of Riemann sums can have variance tending to $0$.
