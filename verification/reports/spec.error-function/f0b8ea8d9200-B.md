## Findings
1. [blocking] statement, part 4 (line 128): "The series diverges for every $x$." The statement's preamble fixes only "$x>0$ and $a$ ... real", so this is asserted for every real $a$, and it is false whenever $a$ is a positive integer: then $c_a=(a-1)(a-2)\cdots(a-a)=0$, every later coefficient vanishes, and the sum is a finite sum. Part 5, two lines below (line 129), says exactly that — "Integer $a$: the expansion terminates and is exact" — so the statement block contradicts itself. The proof inherits the gap: part 4's divergence argument (line 204) concludes from "the ratio of consecutive terms has modulus $\lvert a-m-1\rvert/x$, which exceeds $1$ once $m>x+\lvert a\rvert$" that "the terms eventually increase without bound", which is wrong when the terms are all zero from $m=a$ onwards. I confirmed numerically: for $a=3$ the exact-truncation identity at $x=1.7$ and $x=5$ has remainder $0$ for every $N\ge3$. Arfken, Weber and Harris are explicit about this, introducing Eq. (13.76) with "For nonintegral $a$" (p. 634). Fix: add "for non-integral $a$" to part 4's divergence sentence and to the corresponding sentence of the proof (and to the idea block's "The series cannot converge", line 158).
2. [minor] example, last paragraph (line 297): "one then subtracts from $1$ and loses about ten digits to cancellation". The figure is high by about two digits. Fix: "about eight digits", or drop the number and keep the qualitative point, which is untouched.
