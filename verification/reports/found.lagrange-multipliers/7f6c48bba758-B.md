## Findings
1. [minor] method "Deciding which candidates are extrema", step 5 (line 123): "If $M$ is compact (closed and bounded) and $f$ is continuous on $M$, then $f$ attains a maximum and a minimum on $M$" is false for $M = \varnothing$, which is compact and on which every function is continuous. The proof at line 136 disposes of that case explicitly ("If $M = \varnothing$ there is nothing to extremise"), and found.compactness deliberately states its extreme value theorem for a *nonempty* compact space and says in its remark why. So the method text is out of step with both its own proof and the prerequisite it quotes. I have not treated this as blocking because the conclusion is vacuous in the one case that falsifies it and the proof names that case; but the recipe should carry the word. Fix: "If $M$ is non-empty and compact...".
2. [minor] example (a) (line 190): the multipliers are computed only for the second candidate. Since the whole point of step 5 is that the multipliers may then be discarded this is defensible, but the sentence reads as though it gave both. Fix: say "for the second point", or give $\lambda_1 = -1/\sqrt6$, $\lambda_2 = 1/3$ for the first as well (from $1 = 2\lambda_1(z-x)$ with $z-x = -3/\sqrt6$, then $\lambda_2 = -2\lambda_1 x = 1/3$).
3. [minor] application (line 230): "with $\beta$ then determined by $g_2 = 0$". A $\beta$ exists only when $\min_i E_i < \bar U < \max_i E_i$ (the map $\beta \mapsto \sum_ip_iE_i$ is a strictly decreasing bijection onto that open interval, strict because the energies are not all equal), and otherwise $M$ is empty or a single point. The worked instance satisfies it ($\bar U = 0.8\epsilon \in (0, 2\epsilon)$). Fix: one clause stating the range of admissible $\bar U$.
4. [minor] frontmatter `prereqs` (line 7) and map order: `node tools/check/index.mjs` warns that `found.compactness` comes after this unit in `content/map.yaml`. The dependency is genuine (step 5 and its proof need the extreme value theorem), so the fix belongs in the map ordering.

  - Regularity cannot be dropped: verified the unit's own cusp, $f = x$ on $\{y^2 = x^3\}$. The constraint forces $x \ge 0$, so the minimum is at the origin, where $\nabla g = (-3x^2, 2y) = (0,0)$ and $\nabla f = (1,0)$ is in no span - the multiplier equation genuinely has no solution at the minimiser. Correct, and it is why step 4 exists.
  - Necessary-not-sufficient: $f = y$, $g = y-x^3$; $\nabla g = (-3x^2,1)$ never vanishes, the system forces $\lambda = 1$, $x = 0$, and $f$ on $M$ is $x \mapsto x^3$, with no extremum there. Correct.
  - $m = n$: with the rank condition $g$ is a local diffeomorphism, so $M$ is locally $\{x_*\}$ and every point is vacuously an extremum - the unit's reason for requiring $m < n$ is sound; $m > n$ makes rank $m$ impossible. Correct.
  - Uniqueness of $\lambda$: fails exactly when the gradients are dependent, which the hypothesis excludes; the proof's step 7 is the right argument.
  - Second-order test (step 6): the definiteness is required only on $T_{x_*}M$, and example (b) exhibits an $H$ that is indefinite on $\mathbb{R}^2$ while positive on the tangent line, so the weaker requirement is not vacuous.   - Step 5 as written is falsified only by $M = \varnothing$ (finding 1); for non-empty compact $M$ the argument is airtight, since the extremiser is either regular (step 3 finds it) or not (step 4 lists it), an exhaustive dichotomy.
  - Proof of step 6: checked that $\phi(u) = (u,h(u))$ is a homeomorphism onto $M\cap(V_0\times W_0)$ with the projection as inverse, that $\operatorname{im}P = T_{x_*}M$ by dimension count, and that the second-derivative terms of $\phi$ drop out precisely because $\nabla f(x_*) - \sum_i\lambda_i\nabla g_i(x_*) = 0$ - the identity $H_F(u_*) = P^{\mathsf T}HP$ is right, and $w \neq 0 \Rightarrow Pw \neq 0$ because $\operatorname{rank}P = n-m$.
