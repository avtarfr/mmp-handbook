## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, bullet "Sign conventions and names": the unit says "Riley, Hobson and Bence and Arfken write $f + \lambda g$ and $f + \lambda g + \mu h$", but Arfken uses the *same* minus convention as this unit, not the opposite one. Arfken's Eq. (22.66) (printed p. 1108) is $(\partial f/\partial x)_{yz} - \lambda(\partial g/\partial x)_{yz} = 0$ in each of the three variables, and its $n$-variable, $k$-constraint generalisation Eq. (22.67) (printed p. 1109) is $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} - \sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j \dfrac{\partial g_j}{\partial x_i} = 0$ — identical in sign to the unit's $\nabla f = \sum_i \lambda_i \nabla g_i$. Only RHB (Eqs. (5.27)–(5.28), p. 168, and Eq. (5.31), p. 169) uses the plus convention, and the "$f + \lambda g + \mu h$" form the unit quotes is RHB's Eq. (5.31), not Arfken's. The frontmatter `role` for rhb-2006 ("the sign convention $f + \lambda g$") is correct; the body sentence extends it to a source that contradicts it. Fix: attribute the plus convention to RHB alone (Gelfand and Fomin's $F + \lambda G$, p. 43, could be added), and say that Arfken, Eqs. (22.66)–(22.67), pp. 1108–1109, uses the same minus convention as this unit.

2. [blocking] remark, bullet "The same hypothesis in a variational dress": "Evans's Theorem 2 in §8.4.1 assumes, in the same role, that the derivative of the constraint functional does not vanish identically" is not what Evans's theorem says. Theorem 2 (printed p. 460) has exactly one hypothesis — that $u \in \mathcal{A}$ minimises $I$ over $\mathcal{A}$ — and concludes unconditionally that a multiplier $\lambda$ exists. The non-degeneracy condition (13), "$g(u)$ is not equal to zero a.e. within $U$", appears only as a case assumption inside the proof ("Assume first (13)"), and step 3 of the proof (printed p. 462) treats the opposite case and shows the conclusion still holds there — in fact "for any $\lambda$". So Evans's result, unlike Gelfand and Fomin's Theorem 1 (whose statement genuinely assumes $y$ is not an extremal of $K$, p. 43), carries no such hypothesis, and the concluding sentence "Both are the infinite-dimensional form of $\operatorname{rank} J_g(x_*) = m$" is false for Evans. The contrast is sharper than the unit allows: in Evans's degenerate case a multiplier still exists, whereas the unit's own first remark bullet exhibits a finite-dimensional degenerate case where none does. Fix: attribute the stated non-degeneracy hypothesis to Gelfand and Fomin only; describe Evans's condition (13) as a case distinction in the proof of his Theorem 2, pp. 460–462, noting that his degenerate case admits a multiplier trivially.

4. ## Checked
Verified the statement's content and uniqueness claim: at a regular $x_*$ the rows of $J_g(x_*)$ are independent, so $\nabla f(x_*) \in \operatorname{span}\{\nabla g_i(x_*)\}$ determines $\lambda$ uniquely, and since $\operatorname{span}\{\nabla g_i(x_*)\} = (\ker J_g(x_*))^\perp = (T_{x_*}M)^\perp$ by rank–nullity, the multiplier form and the stated geometric form ($\nabla f(x_*)\cdot v = 0$ on $T_{x_*}M$) are equivalent; $\dim T_{x_*}M = n-m$ is right; $\nabla_\lambda L = -g$ and the count of $n+m$ equations in $n+m$ unknowns are right. Checked the sensitivity claim in the sign-convention bullet: differentiating $g(x^*(c)) = c$ gives $\nabla g_j \cdot \partial x^*/\partial c_i = \delta_{ji}$, hence $\partial v/\partial c_i = \nabla f \cdot \partial x^*/\partial c_i = \lambda_i$ with this unit's convention — confirmed on the concrete case $f = x+y$ on $x^2+y^2 = c$, where sympy gives $\lambda = 1/\sqrt{2c}$ and $df^*/dc = 1/\sqrt{2c}$, equal as claimed. Verified both remark counterexamples: for $f = x$, $g = y^2 - x^3$, the constraint set forces $x \ge 0$ so the minimum of $f$ on $M$ is at the origin, where $\nabla g = (0,0)$ (checked with sympy) has rank 0 and $(1,0)$ is not a multiple of it, so no multiplier exists at the true minimiser; for $f = y$, $g = y - x^3$, $\nabla g = (-3x^2,1)$ never vanishes, the multiplier system forces $\lambda = 1$, $x = 0$, and $f|_M : x \mapsto x^3$ has no local extremum there. Confirmed the method's step 6 against the standard constrained second-order sufficient conditions (Hessian of the Lagrangian positive definite on the tangent space, not on $\mathbb{R}^n$) — correct as stated.
