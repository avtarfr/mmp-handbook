2. [blocking] :::remark[Where the hypotheses are used] second bullet (line 269): "Without it one
   must subtract $v_1(x)u_1(\xi)$ instead" is wrong, and it makes the modified problem
   *inconsistent* — the very defect the construction exists to cure. The solvability condition is
   orthogonality of the source to $v_1$ **in $x$**, so the source
   $\delta(x-\xi) - a(x)b(\xi)$ is consistent iff
   $\int_a^b a\,v_1\,dx\;b(\xi) = v_1(\xi)$ for all $\xi$: the *second* argument must carry
   $v_1$, not $u_1$. Counterexample, taken from the very exercise cited (Stakgold & Holst
   Exercise 3.5.4, p. 220): $L = D^2$ on $(0,1)$, $B_1u = u(0)+u(1)$, $B_2u = u'(0)-u'(1)$. Then
   $u_1 = \sqrt3\,(1-2x)$ (normalised) and the adjoint conditions are $v(1)-v(0) = 0$,
   $v'(1)+v'(0) = 0$, so $v_1 = 1$. The block's source gives
   $\int_0^1\bigl[\delta(x-\xi) - v_1(x)u_1(\xi)\bigr]v_1(x)\,dx = 1 - \sqrt3\,(1-2\xi)$, which is
   nonzero for all but one $\xi$ — no $g_M$ exists. (Note also $(u_1,v_1) = 0$ here, so the
   "obvious" repair $u_1(x)v_1(\xi)/(u_1,v_1)$ is not available either.) Fix: subtract
   $v_1(x)v_1(\xi)/\lVert v_1\rVert^2$ (equal to $\delta(x-\xi) - 1$ in the example above), or
   state the general rule — any $a(x)$ with $(a,v_1)\ne 0$, paired with $v_1(\xi)/(a,v_1)$ — and
   keep the normalisation $\int g_M u_1\,dx = 0$ against the *direct* null space.
