## Findings

1. [blocking] remark (line 208): the stated reason for the quasi-Monte Carlo comparison is backwards.
   The text says a low-discrepancy sequence "will usually beat plain Monte Carlo, because the rate
   $n^{-1}(\log n)^{d}$ of the latter is better until $n$ is enormous." The comparison runs the other
   way: $(\log n)^{d}/n$ exceeds $n^{-1/2}$ for all moderate $n$ and only falls below it once $n$ is
   enormous. At $d=10$ I solved $(\log n)^{10}/n = n^{-1/2}$ numerically: the two are equal at
   $\log n \approx 90$, i.e. $n \approx 10^{39}$. Below that the quasi-random bound is worse
   (at $n=10^{4.3}$ the ratio of the two is $6.7\times10^{7}$; at $n=10^{34.7}$ it is $45.6$; at
   $n=10^{40}$ it is $0.46$). So the clause as written asserts the opposite of the truth, and it is
   also incoherent with the paragraph's own heading, "the crossover in part 2 is asymptotic and the
   constants matter" — the practical win of a sparse grid or a low-discrepancy sequence at $d=10$ is
   precisely *not* explained by that rate bound. This is an `original` block, so the claim is ours.
   Fix: replace with the correct direction, e.g. "even though the bound $n^{-1}(\log n)^{d}$ does not
   beat $n^{-1/2}$ until $n\approx10^{39}$ at $d=10$; the bound is pessimistic for a smooth integrand
   of low effective dimension, and that, not the rate, is why the sequence wins in practice."

2. [minor] statement, part 1 (lines 93-97): the non-degeneracy hypothesis $\sigma>0$ is missing. With
   $f$ constant on $[0,1]$ (allowed by "measurable with $\sigma^{2}<\infty$") the quantity
   $\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta)/\sigma$ is $0/0$, and the displayed limit
   $\Pr(\lvert\hat\theta_n-\theta\rvert\le1.96\,s_n/\sqrt n)\to0.95$ is false: $s_n=0$ and the
   probability is $1$ for every $n$. The first three identities (unbiasedness, variance, r.m.s. error)
   are fine as stated. Fix: add "and $\sigma>0$" where the normal limit is asserted.

3. [minor] statement, part 3 and proof, part 3 (lines 118-122, 170-176): the optimal density
   $p^{\ast}=\lvert g\rvert q/\int\lvert g\rvert q$ need not be "admissible" in the sense the
   definition fixed ("a density $p$ that vanishes exactly where $q$ does"): if $g$ vanishes on a set
   where $q>0$, then $p^{\ast}=0$ there while $q>0$. The conclusion survives, because the estimator
   only needs $p>0$ where $gq\neq0$, but the unit uses one word, "admissible", for two different
   conditions. Fix: state the weaker condition ($p>0$ wherever $gq\neq0$) when the optimum is
   introduced, or note that $p^{\ast}$ satisfies it.

4. [minor] method, step 2 and step 3 (lines 217-219): the block is `provenance=original`, yet the
   linear congruential recipe with its exact full-period conditions ($c$ odd, $a=4k+1$, $m$ a power
   of two) and the inverse-transform rule are Riley, Hobson and Bence's, p. 1017. The authors are
   named in prose, and the frontmatter `role` for rhb-2006 does cover "the generation machinery", so
   this is honest; but sibling units (e.g. asym.stationary-phase) attach a `:cite[...]{work= loc=}`
   directive in that situation and this unit uses no `:cite` at all. Fix: add
   `:cite[Riley, Hobson and Bence]{work=rhb-2006 loc="§27.4.4, p. 1017"}`.

5. [minor] application (line 329): "crude sampling would need $10^{8}$ paths to see it a few thousand
   times" — $10^{8}\times10^{-4}=10^{4}$, ten thousand, not a few thousand. Fix: say "about ten
   thousand times", or change the path count.
