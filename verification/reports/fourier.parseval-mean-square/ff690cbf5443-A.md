## Findings

1. [minor] definition "The mean-square setting" (line 41) and statement "Pythagoras, best approximation, Bessel" (line 56): the unit fixes its inner product to be linear in the first slot (correctly, and consistently with the style guide), and then cites Reed and Simon Thm II.1 and its corollary for part 1. Reed and Simon state on p. 37 that they take the inner product conjugate-linear in the first vector and linear in the second — the opposite convention — and their Thm II.6 (p. 45) accordingly writes the expansion coefficient as $(x_\alpha, y)$ where ours is $(f, e_n)$. Nothing is garbled, because the quantities the unit actually borrows ($\lvert (x,x_n)\rvert^2$ and $\lVert y\rVert^2 = \sum \lvert (x_\alpha,y)\rvert^2$) are unchanged under the swap. But the unit notes the three Fourier normalisations in a `note` and says nothing about this convention clash, which the style guide asks for. Fix: one clause in the definition block, e.g. "Reed and Simon use the opposite slot convention; the identities quoted from them are unaffected".

2. [minor] proof "Mean-square convergence, Parseval, Riemann-Lebesgue", step 3 (line 157): the conclusion "The constant $1+\sqrt{B/\pi}$ does not depend on $\varepsilon$, so part 1 of the theorem follows" compresses the final quantifier step. What has been shown is $\lVert f - S_N(f)\rVert < \varepsilon(1+\sqrt{B/\pi})$ for all $N \ge M(\varepsilon)$; the conclusion $\lVert f - S_N(f)\rVert \to 0$ then needs the remark that $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary and $B$ is fixed by $f$. Step 2 spells this out ("As $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary"); step 3 does not. Fix: add "and $\varepsilon > 0$ was arbitrary".

3. [minor] method, step 5 (line 192): "A partial sum to $\lvert n\rvert \le 100$ should match to the accuracy the decay rate allows; coefficients of size $1/n$ give only two or three digits at that point." I confirmed this against the unit's own example (the truncated sum $\sum_{\lvert n\rvert\le100}(1+n^2)^{-1} = 3.13345$ against $\pi\coth\pi = 3.153348$, so two digits), but the claim is stated with no indication of which quantity is being compared — the coefficient sum or the energy. Fix: say "two or three digits in the summed series".
