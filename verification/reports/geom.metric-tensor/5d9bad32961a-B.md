## Findings
1. [minor] proof, item 2-3 (line 99): "**This is the step that uses the nonsingularity of the coordinate system**, and nothing else in the proof does." Item 5 uses it too: the change-of-variables theorem invoked there (:ref to found.change-of-variables) needs the coordinate map to be a diffeomorphism, hence $\det J\neq 0$. The idea block's "Everything follows from two facts" carries the same slight overclaim. Fix: "nothing else in items 1-4, 6 and 7 does", or drop the clause.
2. [minor] remark, "A convention to state once" (line 133): "Hassani and Lee start from an abstract nondegenerate form and allow either sign". Hassani does (Definition 26.5.2, p. 805: an inner product is a *nondegenerate* symmetric bilinear form, definiteness only arriving at Definition 26.5.7). Lee does not start there: a Riemannian metric in his Chapter 13 is positive definite by definition, and the indefinite case appears only when he relaxes positivity to nondegeneracy under the heading "Pseudo-Riemannian Metrics" (p. 343, the last page of the cited range). Fix: attribute the abstract-nondegenerate starting point to Hassani and say that Lee reaches it by relaxing positivity.
3. [minor] statement item 5 (line 65) and proof item 5 (line 115): $\sqrt g$ is written bare. This is right for the embedded definition the unit follows, and the remark and the method block both switch to $\sqrt{|g|}$ for the indefinite case, but item 5 would read better with a parenthetical "($g>0$ here, by the embedding; see the remark)" so the reader does not carry $\sqrt g$ into the Minkowski application three blocks later.
4. [minor] statement item 1 (line 57): "the $g^{ij}$ the contravariant components of the same tensor". This is the standard convention and is RHB's (p. 960 makes $\delta^i{}_j$ the mixed components of the same $g$), but it is worth one clause saying that "the same tensor" means *after raising both indices with $g$ itself*, since the unit elsewhere is careful that raising is a metric-dependent operation.
