- Counterexample search: the Lemma is the only thing in the unit proved from scratch, so I attacked
  it. Its $2$-cycle $p,q = \tfrac12(\mu\pm\sqrt{\mu^2+4\mu})$ is genuinely of minimal period $2$:
  the fixed points of $f_\mu(x) = -(1+\mu)x + x^2$ are $0$ and $2+\mu$, and
  $p < \tfrac12(\mu + \mu + 2) = \mu+1 < \mu+2$ with $q<0<p$, so neither cycle point is a fixed
  point, for every $\mu>0$. Real and distinct requires $\mu^2+4\mu>0$, i.e. $\mu>0$ -- the stated
  hypothesis, used. In part 4 the contraction claim was checked on its own terms: $\mu \mapsto
  -2+\sqrt{6+\mu}$ has derivative $\tfrac12(6+\mu)^{-1/2} \le 0.2041 < 1$ on $[0,1]$ and maps
  $[0,1]$ into $[0.4495, 0.6458] \subset [0,1]$, so the monotone convergence is genuine. The
  spacing-ratio limit (line 196) is not a restatement of $T'(\mu^*)$ and had to be checked: with
  $\rho_k = e_{k-1}/e_k \to \rho$, the ratio is $(\rho_{k+1}-\rho_k\rho_{k+1})/(1-\rho_{k+1}) \to
  (\rho-\rho^2)/(1-\rho) = \rho$ -- correct, and it is the right thing to check, since $\delta$ is
  defined by spacings and $T'(\mu^*)$ is a rate of approach. (This is a cleaner route than the
  source's, which evaluates a $0/0$ by L'Hopital; our version is the one I verified.)
