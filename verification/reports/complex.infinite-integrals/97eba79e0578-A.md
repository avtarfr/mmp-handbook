## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "The resulting evaluations", part 1 (line 74): "Let $f$ be analytic on $\mathbb{R}$ and in the upper half-plane except for finitely many isolated singularities, with $R\,M(R)\to0$. Then $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx$ converges and [equals the residue sum]." The convergence claim does not follow from that hypothesis, and the proof does not prove it: Evaluation 1 (line 134) establishes the symmetric limit in general and then says "The hypothesis $R\,M(R)\to0$ also gives $\lvert f(x)\rvert = O(\lvert x\rvert^{-1-\epsilon})$ along the real axis **in the rational case**", so only the rational case is covered. The general claim is false. Take $f(z) = 1/\bigl((z+i)\operatorname{Log}(z+i)\bigr)$ with the principal branch: the pole $z=-i$, the zero $z = 1-i$ of the logarithm and the cut $\{x - i : x \le 0\}$ all lie in the lower half-plane, so $f$ is analytic on $\mathbb{R}$ and on the closed upper half-plane, and $M(R) \sim 1/(R\log R)$, so $R\,M(R) \to 0$; but $f(x) \sim 1/(x\log x)$ for large $x > 0$, whose integral diverges, so $\int_0^{\infty} f$ does not converge even though $\lim_R\int_{-R}^{R} f$ does. This is also the distinction the unit itself insists on in part 2 and in the idea block ("proving that the *symmetric* limit exists is not enough"). Fix: in part 1 either state the conclusion for the symmetric limit, or keep "converges" and strengthen the hypothesis to what the proof uses, e.g. $\lvert f(x)\rvert = O(\lvert x\rvert^{-1-\epsilon})$ on the real axis (automatic for rational $f$ with $\deg q \ge \deg p + 2$, which is the case Ahlfors p. 156 states as an iff).
2. [blocking] proof, "Evaluation 2" (line 136): the rectangle argument is run "Under the hypothesis that $\lvert zf(z)\rvert \le A$ for large $\lvert z\rvert$", which is not a hypothesis of statement 2 (that one assumes only $M(R)\to0$), and the final bound $\frac{A}{a}(1/X_1+1/X_2)$ depends on it. As written the proof therefore proves a weaker statement than the one above it. This follows Ahlfors p. 157, who is treating a rational $R$ with $\lvert zR(z)\rvert$ bounded, but our statement 2 is more general, so the gap is ours. Fix: the argument goes through unchanged with $\varepsilon(X) = \sup_{R \ge X} M(R)$ in place of $A/X$, since every $z$ in the upper half-plane with $\lvert z\rvert \ge X$ satisfies $\lvert f(z)\rvert \le M(\lvert z\rvert) \le \varepsilon(X)$ and $\varepsilon(X)\to0$ by hypothesis: the right vertical side is then at most $\varepsilon(X_2)\int_0^Y e^{-ay}dy < \varepsilon(X_2)/a$, the left side at most $\varepsilon(X_1)/a$, and the top at most $\varepsilon(\min(X_1,X_2))(X_1+X_2)e^{-aY} \to 0$ as $Y \to \infty$.
