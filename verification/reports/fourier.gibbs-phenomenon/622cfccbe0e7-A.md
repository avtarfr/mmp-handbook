## Findings
1. [minor] definition (line 41): "The partial sums $S_Nf$ converge to $f$ at every point other than $a$ and to $m$ at $a$" is not true of an arbitrary $f$ in class $D$, which the sentence's hypothesis allows: at any *other* jump the partial sums converge to that jump's midpoint, which need not be $f$'s value there. Fix: "converge to $f$ at every point of continuity and to the midpoint at each jump, $m$ at $a$".

2. [minor] statement, part 1 item 2 (line 70): "$x_1=\pi/M$ is the **global** maximum of $S_n$ on $[0,\pi]$" reads as a claim of uniqueness, but the symmetry of $S_n$ about $\pi/2$ established in Step 4 gives $S_n(\pi-\pi/M)=S_n(\pi/M)$, so the maximum value is attained at two points. The proof's own conclusion is stated correctly ("$\max_{[0,\pi]}S_n=S_n(\pi/M)$"). Fix: "$x_1=\pi/M$ attains the maximum of $S_n$ on $[0,\pi]$ (as, by symmetry, does $x_{M-1}$)".

3. [minor] idea (line 108): "the arches get smaller as $t$ grows because $1/\sin t$ decreases" is false on $(\pi/2,\pi)$, where $1/\sin t$ increases, and the proof does not argue this way: Step 4 first reflects $(\pi/2,\pi)$ onto $(0,\pi/2]$ using the symmetry of $S_n$ about $\pi/2$, and only then uses that $1/\sin t$ strictly decreases on $(0,\pi/2]$. Fix: add the reflection to the idea, e.g. "$S_n$ is symmetric about $\pi/2$, so it is enough to look at $(0,\pi/2]$, where $1/\sin t$ decreases and the arches shrink".

4. [minor] proof, Step 7 (line 212): "On $I'$ the function $g$ is continuous and piecewise continuously differentiable, hence absolutely continuous with a **bounded** derivative". Boundedness does not follow from the class $D$ of the sibling unit, which asks only for a continuous derivative *within* each subinterval plus existence of the one-sided derivatives at the ends ($x^2\sin(x^{-2})$ meets that with an unbounded derivative). Folland's Corollary 8.45(b) (p. 268) needs $g$ absolutely continuous with $g'\in L^p$, $p>1$, so the step wants the derivative to extend continuously to each closed arc — which is how the class is used everywhere else in these two units. Fix: say "with $g'$ extending continuously to each closed arc, hence bounded", or quote the weaker $g'\in L^p$ hypothesis directly.

5. [minor] remark (line 244): "$J_i$" carries a subscript that is defined nowhere in the unit; the jump is $J$ throughout (the $J_k$ indexing is Stakgold and Holst's, p. 136). Fix: $J$.

6. [minor] proof, Step 7 (line 220): "$u$ is continuous at $a$ with $u(a)=m$" holds only after $u$ is (re)defined at the single point $a$, since $g(a)$ and $\operatorname{sgn}0$ are unconstrained; Folland says this explicitly when he does the same subtraction (p. 268, "provided that we (re)define $g(a)$"). Nothing downstream is affected, because the partial sums do not see one point and the limit is taken along $a+c/N\ne a$. Fix: add "after setting $u(a)=m$, which changes no Fourier coefficient".
