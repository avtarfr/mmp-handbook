## Findings
1. [blocking] definition, "Overshoot, and the three ways it is quoted" (lines 41, 53, 57): the block introduces $J=f(a+)-f(a-)\neq0$, explicitly admitting either sign, and then states every consequence as though $J>0$. All three are false for a downward jump:
   - line 41: the spike "rises above $f(a+)$ and dips below $f(a-)$" - when $J<0$ it rises above $f(a-)$ and dips below $f(a+)$;
   - line 53: "$\max S_Nf$ tends to $m+\tfrac12 JP$, so it exceeds $f(a+)$ by $\tfrac12 J(P-1)=0.0894899\,J$" - when $J<0$, $m+\tfrac12 JP$ is the *minimum* of the limiting curve and lies below $f(a+)$, and the quoted excess is negative;
   - line 57: "All three are the one statement $\max S_Nf\to m+\tfrac12 JP$ ... the overshoot beyond each one-sided value is $0.0895\,J$".
   This contradicts part 2, item 3 of the statement (line 98), which is correct and says the extremes are $m\pm\tfrac12 JP$, that both lie beyond the corresponding one-sided value "whichever sign $J$ has", and that the overshoot is $\tfrac12\lvert J\rvert(P-1)=0.0894899\lvert J\rvert$. It also contradicts the unit's own worked Part C, where $g(x)=x$ gives $J=-2\pi$ and the computed limiting maximum is $+2\operatorname{Si}(\pi)=3.7038741$, whereas line 53 and line 57 predict $m+\tfrac12 JP=-\pi P=-3.7038741$. Fix: either state at the head of the block "replacing $f$ by $-f$ if necessary, take $J>0$", or carry $\lvert J\rvert$ through all three bullets and line 57, writing the limiting extremes as $m\pm\tfrac12\lvert J\rvert P$ and the per-side overshoot as $0.0894899\lvert J\rvert$. The summary's "overshoots each one-sided value by 8.95 percent of the jump" is already sign-safe if "the jump" is read as $\lvert J\rvert$, and should say so.
2. [minor] statement, part 1, item 2 (line 86): "the same value is attained at $x_{M-1}=\pi-\pi/M$ and at no other point" degenerates when $M=2$ (that is $n=0$, the single-term sum), where $x_1=x_{M-1}=\pi/2$ is one point, not two. Fix: add "for $n\ge1$", or "and, when $n\ge1$, also at".
