## Findings
1. [blocking] frontmatter `sources`, arfken-2013 entry (`loc` and `role`): the locator is wrong on both counts and the attribution over-claims. Printed p. 73 sits in §1.10, "Evaluation of Integrals" (running head on that page), not §1.9; printed pp. 229–230 sit in §4.4, "Jacobians" (running head, and p. 229's own text says "Computation of Jacobians will be treated in detail in Section 4.4"), not §3.10 — §3.10 is in the vector-analysis chapter and does not treat Jacobians at all. Separately, the `loc` attributes to these pages "the condition $\partial(x,y)/\partial(u,v) \neq 0$" and the `role` calls it "the non-vanishing Jacobian determinant as the practical invertibility test", but neither p. 73 nor pp. 229–230 states any non-vanishing or invertibility condition: p. 73 introduces the symbol $J = \partial(x,y)/\partial(u,v)$ and the polar example $J = r$, and pp. 229–230 give Eq. (4.74) and the subsection "Inverse of Jacobian" proving $JJ^{-1} = 1$ via the determinant product theorem. Fix: cite "§1.10, p. 73" and "§4.4, pp. 229–230", and narrow the attributed content to the Jacobian-determinant notation and the reciprocal relation $JJ^{-1}=1$ — which is all the unit's body actually uses — dropping the invertibility-test claim or sourcing it elsewhere.

2. [minor] statement[Inverse function theorem], part 2: the displayed formulas write $D(F^{-1})(y)$ and $\det J_{F^{-1}}(y)$, but $F$ is not assumed injective on $\Omega$ and the sentence immediately after the statement says so explicitly, so $F^{-1}$ does not exist as written; the object meant is $(F|_{\Omega_0})^{-1}$, which the preceding clause does name. Fix: write $D\bigl((F|_{\Omega_0})^{-1}\bigr)(y)$ and $\det J_{(F|_{\Omega_0})^{-1}}(y)$, or introduce $G = (F|_{\Omega_0})^{-1}$ once in part 1 and use $G$ in part 2.

3. [minor] statement[Implicit function theorem], final sentence: $V_0$ and $W_0$ are fixed by the theorem's conclusion, and then the closing sentence asserts invertibility of $\partial\Phi/\partial y$ "at every point of a small enough $V_0 \times W_0$", which silently re-chooses them. Fix: say that $V_0$ and $W_0$ may be shrunk so that $\partial\Phi/\partial y$ is invertible throughout $V_0 \times W_0$ (possible because $\det \partial\Phi/\partial y$ is continuous and non-zero at $(a,b)$), and state that the derivative formula holds for such a choice.

4. [minor] frontmatter `summary`: $k$ denotes the smoothness class in the first clause ("A $C^k$ map ... is a $C^k$ diffeomorphism") and then, in the implicit-function clause, the statement block uses $k$ for the number of dependent variables and $m$ for the smoothness class, so the summary's second "$C^k$ function of $x$" clashes with the body's notation. Fix: use a neutral phrase such as "of the same class" in the second clause.

## Checked

Lee's Thm C.40 (pp. 661–662) matches the implicit function theorem statement in hypotheses ($U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^k$ open, $\Phi$ of class $C^m$, $(a,b)$, $c = \Phi(a,b)$, the $k\times k$ block $\partial\Phi^i/\partial y^j(a,b)$ non-singular) and in conclusion (the graph characterisation on $V_0\times W_0$, from which uniqueness of $g$ and $g(a)=b$ follow); $V_0\times W_0 \subseteq U$ holds in Lee's construction. Checked the derivative formula's block shapes: $\partial\Phi/\partial x$ is $k\times n$, $\partial\Phi/\partial y$ is $k\times k$, $Dg$ is $k\times n$, so both displayed identities are dimensionally consistent and reduce to $g' = -\Phi_x/\Phi_y$ at $n=k=1$. Confirmed the note's claim about Lee's route: Lee's proof of C.40 does apply the inverse function theorem to $(x,y)\mapsto(x,\Phi(x,y))$ and does argue from block lower triangularity with diagonal blocks $I_n$ and $\partial\Phi^i/\partial y^j$, and the proof of C.34 is a contraction-mapping argument (Lemma C.35). Confirmed Folland printed p. 74 defines a $C^1$ diffeomorphism exactly as the unit's third definition bullet does ("G is injective and $D_xG$ is invertible for all $x$") and states $D_x(G^{-1}) = [D_{G^{-1}(x)}G]^{-1}$, so that bullet and the $C^1$ attribution are faithful. Verified all four remark counterexamples with sympy: $\det J = e^{2x}$ for $F(x,y)=(e^x\cos y, e^x\sin y)$, with $2\pi$-periodicity in $y$ killing injectivity; $f'(x) = 1 + 4x\sin(1/x) - 2\cos(1/x)$ for $f = x + 2x^2\sin(1/x)$, equal to $-1$ at $x = 1/(2\pi j)$ and $+3$ at $x = 1/((2j+1)\pi)$ for $j = 1,2,3$, with $f'(0)=1$ from the difference quotient, so differentiability with invertible $Df(0)$ genuinely fails to give local injectivity; $\partial\Phi/\partial y = 2y$ vanishes on the unit circle only at $(\pm1,0)$ where $\partial\Phi/\partial x = \pm 2 \neq 0$; and $\partial\Phi/\partial y = 5y^4+1 \ge 1$ with $g' = 1/(5g^4+1)$ for $y^5+y=x$ (whose non-elementarity is the standard Bring–Jerrard case, correct by Abel–Ruffini together with Ritt's theorem that an elementary algebraic function is expressible in radicals). Checked the final remark bullet's "precisely off the sets where those determinants vanish": correct, since a local diffeomorphism at a point forces an invertible total derivative there by Lee's Prop. C.4, and at $r=0$ (polar) and at $\sin\theta = 0$ (spherical) the maps are not locally injective. Verified Arfken pp. 229–231 do contain Eq. (4.74), the $JJ^{-1}=1$ derivation and the spherical example $\partial(r,\theta,\phi)/\partial(x,y,z) = 1/(r^2\sin\theta)$, so the body's "Arfken's reciprocal rule" claim is supported even though the frontmatter locator is not (finding 1).
