[blocking] prereqs (line 7) and proof "Inverse function theorem", step 4 (line 88): "Since $V_0$ is a ball, it is connected, and therefore so is its continuous image $\Omega_0 = G(V_0)$" is the whole justification of the clause "$\Omega_0$ may be taken connected" in part 1 of the statement, and it uses two results of point-set topology — a ball (convex set) is connected, and a continuous image of a connected set is connected. 2. [blocking] application (line 256): "So wherever $\Phi_V = 0$ the isotherm is flat, the isothermal compressibility is infinite, and the two-phase region begins." The last clause is wrong. $\Phi_V = 0$, i.e. $(\partial p/\partial V)_T = 0$, is the **spinodal**; the two-phase (coexistence) region is bounded by the binodal, obtained from the Maxwell construction, and the spinodal lies strictly inside it for every $T < T_c$, the two curves touching only at the critical point. Between binodal and spinodal the homogeneous phase is metastable, not absent, so the two-phase region has already begun well before $\Phi_V$ reaches $0$. The first two clauses are right. This is an `original` block. Fix: "and the homogeneous phase becomes unstable — this is the spinodal, which lies inside the two-phase region and meets its boundary only at the critical point".

[minor] proof "Inverse function theorem", step 3 (line 78): "$\sum_{m\ge0}\lvert x_{m+1}-x_m\rvert$ converges and the sequence is Cauchy; $K$ is closed and bounded in $\mathbb{R}^n$, so it converges to some $x \in K$." What is used is completeness of $\mathbb{R}^n$ plus closedness of $K$; boundedness plays no part in the step as written, and the Cauchy criterion is proved in found.limits-of-sequences only for $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$, which is not in `prereqs` either. 5. [minor] remark, second bullet (line 187): "So $f'$ takes both signs in every neighbourhood of $0$, and $f$ is injective on no neighbourhood of $0$."
