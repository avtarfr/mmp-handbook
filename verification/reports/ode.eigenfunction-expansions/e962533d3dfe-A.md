## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Forced problems and the Fredholm alternative", part 2 (line 169), with proof step 5 (line 210): part 1 carries an explicit caveat that existence is quoted from Strauss's Thm 1(a) and that the proof below establishes only uniqueness, the coefficients and $L^2_w$ convergence. Part 2 needs the same caveat and does not carry it. Step 5 opens "Suppose $\int F\varphi_N = 0$" and then argues that *adding* $C\varphi_N$ to a solution gives a solution and that $(\ast)$ fixes all the other coefficients, concluding "The displayed family is thus exactly the set of solutions, and it is infinite" — which presupposes that one solution exists. That is Strauss's Thm 1(b) (p. 318), not something the computation produces. Fix: attach the same "existence is quoted" note to part 2, and in step 5 say that *given* a solution the family is exactly the solution set.
2. [minor] method, step 6 (line 248): "The coefficients decay like $b_n/\lambda_n$, so the series converges faster than the expansion of $F$ itself". Two slips: the solution's coefficients are $b_n/(\lambda_n-\sigma)$, and $b_n$ are the coefficients of $F/w$ against $\{\varphi_n\}$, not of $F$ (the coefficients of $F$ in this inner product would be $\int w F\varphi_n$). Fix: "the coefficients are $b_n/(\lambda_n-\sigma)$, so the series converges faster than the expansion of $F/w$".
3. [minor] proof, step 1 (line 186): the step assumes a classical solution $u \in C^2[a,b]$, whereas the object part 1 delivers is an $L^2_w$ limit. The remark on line 222 says exactly this, but the proof does not flag at step 1 that the $u$ it starts from is a classical solution assumed to exist. Fix: one clause at step 1 ("let $u\in C^2[a,b]$ be a classical solution, whose existence is quoted").
4. [minor] note (line 142): "the eigenvalues are successive minima of the Rayleigh quotient $\lVert\nabla w\rVert^2/\lVert w\rVert^2$" uses $w$ for Strauss's trial function while $w$ is this unit's weight throughout. Strauss's own letter collides with ours. Fix: rename the trial function (he also writes $r_N$ for the remainder, which the note already adopts).
