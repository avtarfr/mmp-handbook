## Findings
1. [blocking] application, last bullet (line 334, `provenance=original`): "keeping the first $N$ modes is optimal among all $N$-dimensional subspaces spanned by eigenfunctions" is false, and the unit's own Part A is the counterexample. Part 1 of the least-squares statement says only that, for a *given* span $\{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_N\}$, the best coefficients are $c_1,\dots,c_N$; it says nothing about which $N$ eigenfunctions to keep. For a set $S$ of $N$ indices the squared error is $\|f\|_w^2-\sum_{n\in S}|c_n|^2$, so the optimal choice is the $N$ largest $|c_n|$, not the first $N$. With $f=1/x$ from Part A, $c_2 = 0$ and $c_3 = 0.249854$, so $\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_1,\varphi_2\}$ has squared error $0.693147-0.561844-0 = 0.131303$ while $\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_1,\varphi_3\}$ has $0.693147-0.561844-0.062427 = 0.068876$ — the first two modes are beaten by modes 1 and 3 by a factor of almost two. (The companion clause "the error is exactly the discarded tail $\sum_{n>N}|c_n|^2$" is correct.) Fix: state what is true — the error of keeping any index set is the sum of the discarded $|c_n|^2$, so the first $N$ modes are optimal exactly when the coefficient moduli decrease, which they do for smooth data because $c_n$ decays with $n$ — or drop the optimality-over-subspaces clause and keep part 1's actual content.

2. [minor] note, third bullet (line 142): "hence $\lVert r_N\rVert^2 \le \mathcal R[f]\,/\lambda_{N+1}\to 0$". Strauss's (12), p. 313 is $\|r_N\|^2 \le \|\nabla r_N\|^2/\lambda_N \le \|\nabla f\|^2/\lambda_N$: the bound is the *numerator* of the Rayleigh quotient, not the quotient, and the two differ by the factor $\|f\|^2$, which is not assumed to be $1$ anywhere in the bullet. (The bullet's own previous sentence correctly says "an energy identity bounds the numerator of $\mathcal R[r_N]$ by that of $f$", so the display contradicts it. Strauss writes $\lambda_N$ in (12) where this bullet writes $\lambda_{N+1}$; that difference is harmless, since both tend to infinity.) Fix: write $\|\nabla f\|^2/\lambda_{N+1}$, or "the numerator of $\mathcal R[f]$ divided by $\lambda_{N+1}$".

3. [minor] application, first bullet (line 330, `provenance=original`): the argument reproduces Strauss §11.5, eqs. (8)-(11), pp. 319-320 step for step — expand $u(\cdot,t)$ at fixed $t$ in the complete set, substitute, match coefficients, get $a_n' = -\lambda_n a_n$ and $a_n(t)=a_n(0)e^{-\lambda_n t}$. The text names Strauss and the frontmatter declares the role ("the separation-of-variables argument (8)-(11)"), so I do not call this mislabelled, but `adapted` with that `src` would be the honest label.

4. [minor] statement "Forced problems and the Fredholm alternative" (line 156): part 1 asserts existence ("there is exactly one solution"), while the proof establishes only uniqueness, the coefficients and $L^2_w$ convergence. The remark at line 218 says this plainly and attributes existence to Strauss's Theorem 1, so nothing is hidden; a half-sentence in the statement pointing at that remark would make the division visible at the point of reading.
