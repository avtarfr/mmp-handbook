[minor] remark, bullet "Self-adjointness, in every part" (line 148), and example 1, Step 5 (line 221): the momentum of a particle in a box is said to have deficiency indices $(1,1)$ and then, in the same breath as the half-line case with indices $(1,0)$, to be no observable at all, with "the model assigns this system no momentum distribution at all". For $(1,0)$ that is exact - no self-adjoint extension exists. For $(1,1)$ a circle of self-adjoint extensions does exist (the quasi-periodic realisations), so the accurate statement is that the minimal operator is not itself an observable and the model assigns no *canonical* momentum until an extension is chosen, none of whose domains sits inside the Dirichlet domain $\mathcal{D}(H)$ used here. The unit's own prerequisite, :ref[funcan.stone-theorem], makes exactly this point ("the circle of self-adjoint extensions is a circle of physically different theories"), so the two sit slightly uneasily together. Fix: distinguish the two cases in both places.
