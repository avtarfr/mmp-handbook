## Findings
1. [minor] definition, "Dynamics" (line 37): "By Stone's theorem there is exactly one self-adjoint operator generating it. That operator is the **Hamiltonian** $H$" sits awkwardly with the sign convention the same paragraph then adopts. :ref[funcan.stone-theorem] defines *the* infinitesimal generator as the self-adjoint $A$ with $U(t)=e^{itA}$; under $U(t)=e^{-itH}$ the generator in that sense is $-H$. The paragraph also displays Reed and Simon's $\frac{d}{dt}[U(t)\varphi]=iH[U(t)\varphi]$ and then disavows it two sentences later. Nothing downstream is wrong - the note immediately after states the choice and cites both pages, as the two-sources rule requires, and every later formula uses $U(t)=e^{-itH}$ consistently - but the wording asserts two incompatible things in one paragraph. Fix: "there is exactly one self-adjoint $A$ with $U(t)=e^{itA}$; the Hamiltonian is $H=-A$, so that $U(t)=e^{-itH}$ and $\frac{d}{dt}U(t)\varphi=-iHU(t)\varphi$", with the Reed-Simon display moved into the note that discusses it.
2. [minor] example "A particle in a box", (line 176): the completeness of $\{\sqrt2\sin(n\pi q)\}$ is attributed to :ref[ode.sturm-liouville-theory], which is not in `prereqs` (it precedes funcan in the map, so listing it raises no ordering warning). The example's arithmetic does not actually need completeness - orthonormality and $H\varphi_n=\tfrac12 n^2\pi^2\varphi_n$ are direct integrations, and $\psi$ lies in the span of three eigenvectors - so this is a naming gap rather than a hole. Fix: add `ode.sturm-liouville-theory` to `prereqs`, or say the completeness is the classical Fourier sine series and drop the ref.
3. [minor] statement F (line 73): the domain of $D$ is given as "the absolutely continuous functions with $D\psi \in L^2$". :cite[Kreyszig]{work=kreyszig-1978 loc="§11.2, p. 577"} asks for $\psi \in L^2(-\infty,+\infty)$ that are absolutely continuous *on every compact interval* with $D\psi \in L^2$; absolute continuity on all of $\mathbb{R}$ is strictly stronger. Fix: insert "locally" (or "on every compact interval") and "$\psi \in L^2$".
