## Findings
1. [minor] proof of "Simultaneous diagonalization", paragraph heading (line 166): "All four families of projections commute". There are two families, $\{P_j\}$ and $\{Q_\alpha\}$; what the paragraph establishes is that all four *pairings* commute ($PP$, $PQ$, $QP$, $QQ$). Fix: "All the projections commute with one another".
2. [minor] statement "Normality is exactly unitary diagonalizability" (lines 105-110): the block opens with its own derivation ("if $U^\dagger A U = \Lambda$ ... then $AA^\dagger = U\Lambda\overline\Lambda U^\dagger = \ldots = A^\dagger A$, because diagonal matrices commute"), which the following `proof` block then repeats. Correct, but proof material inside a statement block. Fix: state the equivalence and the classification, and leave the computation to the proof that follows it.
3. [minor] proof of "Simultaneous diagonalization", "A commuting family" (line 188): the step "since $A$ splits no summand, $W_m$ lies inside a single eigenspace of $A$" tacitly uses that $W_m$ is the direct sum of its intersections with the eigenspaces of $A$. That is true, but it needs one more application of the lemma than is named: $A$ commutes with every member, so each member commutes with each $P^A_\mu$, so each $P^A_\mu$ commutes with the projection onto $W_m$, so $W_m$ is $P^A_\mu$-invariant and $\sum_\mu P^A_\mu = I$ splits it. Fix: add that clause, or say that $A$ restricted to $W_m$ is normal (by the restriction lemma) and hence diagonalizable there.
4. [minor] application, "Why numerical analysts care whether a matrix is normal" (line 274): "a perturbation $A + \Delta A$ moves each eigenvalue by at most $\|\Delta A\|$". As a *matched* statement (each eigenvalue paired with its perturbed counterpart) this needs both matrices normal (Hoffman-Wielandt); for normal $A$ and an arbitrary $\Delta A$ what holds is that every eigenvalue of $A + \Delta A$ lies within $\|\Delta A\|_2$ of the spectrum of $A$ (Bauer-Fike with condition number $1$), together with the first-order statement that each simple eigenvalue of a normal matrix has eigenvalue condition number $1$. The conclusion drawn ("perfectly conditioned") is right; only the phrasing claims more than the inequality gives. Fix: "every eigenvalue of $A + \Delta A$ lies within $\|\Delta A\|$ of the spectrum of $A$".
