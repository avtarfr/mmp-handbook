- Counterexample search: tried to break each hypothesis. Injectivity: the polar map on $(0,\infty)\times(0,4\pi)$ has $\det DG = r \ne 0$ throughout, and I confirmed numerically that the formula then returns exactly twice the right answer ($\int_0^{4\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2} r\,dr\,d\varphi = 2\pi = 2 \times \pi$), so the first remark bullet is right, including "exactly twice" - the single-covered ray $\varphi = 2\pi$ is null and does not spoil the factor. Non-vanishing determinant: on $[0,\infty)\times[-\pi,\pi]$ the polar map fails both hypotheses (determinant zero at $r = 0$, endpoints identified), and the spherical map degenerates at $\theta \in \{0,\pi\}$, as the second bullet says; the removed sets' images (a closed half-line in $\mathbb{R}^2$, a closed half-plane and the $z$-axis in $\mathbb{R}^3$) are indeed Lebesgue null, so the stated conclusion "computes the integral over all of $\mathbb{R}^n$" holds.
