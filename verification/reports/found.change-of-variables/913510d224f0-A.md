[blocking] statement[Change of variables], sentence after the display: "Taking $f$ to be the indicator function of a measurable $E \subseteq \Omega$ gives the volume rule $\operatorname{vol}(G(E)) = \int_E \lvert \det D_uG\rvert\, d^nu$." The substitution named does not give the formula shown. In this statement $f$ is a function on $G(\Omega)$, while $E \subseteq \Omega$; putting $f = \chi_E$ makes the two sides $\int_{G(\Omega)}\chi_E = \operatorname{vol}(E \cap G(\Omega))$ and $\int_\Omega \chi_E(G(u))\lvert\det D_uG\rvert\,d^nu = \int_{G^{-1}(E)}\lvert\det D_uG\rvert\,d^nu$, which is a different (and generally unrelated) identity. The function that yields the displayed volume rule is $f = \chi_{G(E)}$, since then $f \circ G = \chi_E$. The displayed conclusion itself is correct and is Folland's Thm 2.47(b) (printed p. 74), so only the route is wrong — but as written the unit uses $f$ in a way its own hypotheses do not permit.
