[source] remark, doubling strategy (lines 161-165), against grimmett-stirzaker-2020 §12.1, Example (12.1.3), p. 534: the book states the staking rule as "if he loses the first $n$ plays, then he bets $2^n$ on the $(n+1)$th", so the stake on the $T$th (winning) bet is $2^{T-1}$, yet its displayed profit is $2^{T} - (1+2+4+\cdots+2^{T-1})$, which is that expression with $T$ shifted by one. Both evaluate to $1$, and the book's own later sum for the mean loss before the win, $\sum_n (\tfrac12)^n(1+2+\cdots+2^{n-2})$, uses the indexing consistent with the rule. Our unit uses the consistent form $Y_T = 2^{T-1} - (1+\cdots+2^{T-2}) = 1$, which is correct and is what the book's verbal rule gives. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`; not blocking.
2. [minor] remark, "Condition B1 is of a different kind" (line 178): the claim that a bounded, non-degenerate, mean-zero random walk oscillates ($\limsup_n M_n = +\infty$, $\liminf_n M_n = -\infty$ almost surely) is used without proof or citation, and it is the whole load-bearing step for "no value can be assigned to $M_T$ there by any limiting convention". It is true (Hewitt-Savage makes $\limsup M_n$ almost surely constant in $\{-\infty, c, +\infty\}$, and the strong law plus non-degeneracy rules out the finite and one-sided cases), but it is not elementary calculus and it is not in any listed prerequisite. Fix: either add the one-line Hewitt-Savage justification, or weaken to the fully elementary statement that $M_n$ is unbounded above and below on $\{T=\infty\}$ because $S_n \to -\infty$ there while $M_n = S_n + (q-p)n$ has no limit, or cite the oscillation result.
3. [minor] definition block, stopping time (line 41): "$\{T = n\} \in \mathcal{F}_n$ for every $n \ge 0$, equivalently if $\{T \le n\} \in \mathcal{F}_n$" is right, but the "equivalently" is asserted rather than shown, and the one direction that is not immediate ($\{T=n\} = \{T\le n\}\setminus\{T\le n-1\}$, and conversely $\{T\le n\} = \bigcup_{m\le n}\{T=m\}$) is two symbols. Worth adding, since proof A step 1 leans on $\{T \ge n\} = \{T \le n-1\}^{c}$.
4. [minor] application, "Elsewhere" (line 266): "the Wiener process and its compensated square $W(t)^2 - t$ are martingales" is true for the *standard* Wiener process; with the free variance parameter $\sigma^2$ that :ref[Brownian motion]{to=prob.brownian-motion} carries, the compensated square is $W(t)^2 - \sigma^2 t$. Insert "standard", or write $\sigma^2 t$.
5. [minor] example, step 1 (line 200): "a stake of $s$ on a correctly guessed letter returns $2s$" fixes the fair-odds convention for the fair coin, and step 6 silently replaces it by $s/p$ and $s/q$. The generalisation is correct, but saying at step 1 that $2s$ is the $p=\tfrac12$ case of $s/p$ would save the reader the re-derivation.
