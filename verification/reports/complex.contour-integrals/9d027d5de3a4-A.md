## Findings
1. [blocking] `proof`, part 2, the reversal sentence (line 104): the sign bookkeeping as written does not give the stated conclusion. "the derivative is $-z'(-t)$ and substituting $s = -t$ reverses the limits, producing one sign change from each, hence a net sign change" counts two sign changes, whose product is $+1$, not a net sign change. There are in fact three factors of $-1$: the derivative $-z'(-t)$, the differential $dt = -ds$, and the exchange of the limits $\int_b^a \to -\int_a^b$; their product is $-1$, which is the claimed result. The companion sentence for $\lvert dz\rvert$ gets it right ("the two cancellations leave the integral unchanged" — those two being $dt = -ds$ and the limit exchange), so the two halves of the same paragraph count differently. Fix: "the derivative contributes one sign; the substitution contributes two more, from $dt = -ds$ and from exchanging the limits, which cancel each other; the net effect is a single sign change. With $\lvert dz\rvert$ the derivative factor $\lvert -z'(-t)\rvert = \lvert z'(-t)\rvert$ carries no sign, so only the two cancelling changes remain and the integral is unchanged."

2. [minor] `proof`, part 5 (line 143): "For $n = -1$ no primitive is available — that is exactly the content of part 3 of :ref[branch points and branch cuts]" is a backwards reference that inverts the logical order. In `complex.branch-points-cuts` part 3, the non-existence of a single-valued branch of $\operatorname{Ln}(z-a)$ on an annulus is *deduced from* $\oint dz/(z-a) = 2\pi i$ — the computation performed here. The argument in this unit is self-contained and correct (direct computation gives $2\pi i \ne 0$, so part 4 forbids a primitive), so nothing is circular mathematically, but the cross-reference presents the dependency the wrong way round, and `complex.branch-points-cuts` is not among this unit's `prereqs`. Fix: drop the clause, or replace it with "so part 4 shows no primitive exists on any annulus about $a$; this is the obstruction behind the branch structure of the logarithm (:ref...)".

3. [minor] `application`, last sentence of the first paragraph (line 186): "part 5 is the reason a point vortex ... gives a circulation independent of the contour that encloses it." Part 5 evaluates the $n = -1$ integral only on a circle centred at $a$; independence of the contour, for an arbitrary closed curve enclosing the vortex, needs contour deformation (`complex.cauchy-theorem`) or the winding number (`complex.winding-number`). Fix: "part 5 gives the circulation of a point vortex on any circle about it, and Cauchy's theorem extends that to any contour enclosing it".
