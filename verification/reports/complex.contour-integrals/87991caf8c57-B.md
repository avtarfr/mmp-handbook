## Findings
1. [minor] example "A primitive, and path dependence", item 2 (line 161): "$\bar z$ satisfies no Cauchy-Riemann equation anywhere". The second equation does hold everywhere for $\bar z$: $u=x$, $v=-y$ give $u_y = 0 = -v_x$. It is the first, $u_x = v_y$, that fails ($1\ne-1$). The substance (no derivative anywhere, hence path dependence is allowed) is right; the sentence as written is false on a literal reading. Fix: "$\bar z$ does not satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations anywhere".
2. [minor] same item (line 161): "around the closed triangle the integral is $(1+i)-1 = i$" is the value for the orientation $0\to1\to1+i\to0$ (counterclockwise); the opposite orientation gives $-i$. Fix: name the orientation, as the rest of the unit does for closed contours.
3. [minor] definition block (line 49): "For a piecewise $C^1$ path that is a small theorem rather than a restatement of the definition, and one line proves it" — the one-line proof bounds the difference by $L(\gamma)$ times the largest oscillation of $f\circ z$, which is the right estimate, but it silently uses that $\lvert\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_j} (\cdot) z'\,dt\rvert \le \sup\lvert\cdot\rvert\int\lvert z'\rvert$, i.e. part 3 of the statement that has not been proved yet at that point. Fix: say "by the modulus inequality proved in part 3 below", or move the sentence after the statement.
