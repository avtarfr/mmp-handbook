## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Conventions and traps", bullet "**Preserving the norm requires all $x$**" (line 85): the claim is false for the matrix given. The unit says

   > $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ preserves the length of $e_2$ but kills $e_1$

   but $A e_2$ is the second column, $(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$, so $\|A e_2\| = \sqrt{2} \neq 1 = \|e_2\|$. This matrix preserves *no* vector's length except multiples of $e_1$ (which it sends to $0$), so it does not illustrate "preserves one length, fails on another" at all. Computed with numpy: $A e_1 = (0,0)$, $\|A e_1\| = 0$; $A e_2 = (1,1)$, $\|A e_2\| = 1.4142135623730951$. Fix: use a matrix that really does preserve one basis vector and kill another, e.g. $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, for which $\|A e_2\| = \|e_2\| = 1$ and $A e_1 = 0$; or keep the present matrix and change the claim to "scales $e_2$ by $\sqrt 2$ and kills $e_1$".
2. [minor] remark, bullet "**'Orthogonal matrix' is not 'matrix with orthogonal columns'**" (line 83): "A matrix with merely orthogonal columns satisfies $Q^{\mathsf T} Q = D$ with $D$ diagonal and positive" needs the columns to be non-zero — the zero vector is orthogonal to everything, and then $D$ has a zero on the diagonal and $Q$ is not even injective. And "and scales lengths" understates the point: such a $Q$ scales by a *different* factor in each column direction, which is why it is not a rigid motion. Fix: say "non-zero pairwise orthogonal columns", and "stretches different directions by different factors".
3. [minor] statement "Characterisation" (line 44): "That 4 implies 3 is the polarization identity" is right, but only the real polarization identity is displayed, and over $\mathbb{C}$ the real one is not enough — the complex case needs the four-term (or $1, i$) form, because $\|Ux\| = \|x\|$ for all $x$ fixes only the real part of $(Ux,Uy)$ through that formula. The statement is made for complex $U$ first and the real case second, so the displayed identity belongs to the second half. Fix: add the complex form, or say explicitly that the displayed identity is the real case and name the complex one.
