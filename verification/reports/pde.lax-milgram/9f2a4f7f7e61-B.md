1. [blocking] example, item 5 "The contraction parameter" (line 271): the closing sentence "The true optimum sits at the edge of the range the general bound can certify" is false. With $A$ diagonal in the basis $e_k=\sin kx$ and $c_k = k^2/(1+k^2)$, the spectrum of $A$ has closure $\{c_k\}\cup\{1\}$, so $\|I-\sigma A\| = \max\{\,1-\sigma/2,\ |1-\sigma|\,\}$ for $0<\sigma<2$. That is minimised at $\sigma = 4/3$, where $1-\sigma/2 = \sigma-1 = 1/3$; I confirmed this numerically (sup over $k\le 2\times10^5$): $\|I-\sigma A\| = 0.75$ at $\sigma=1/2$, $0.5$ at $\sigma=1$, $0.4$ at $\sigma=1.2$, $1/3$ at $\sigma=4/3$, $0.5$ at $\sigma=3/2$, with the numerical argmin at $\sigma=1.3333$. So the true optimum lies strictly *outside* the certifiable range $(0,2m/M^2)=(0,1)$, not at its edge, and it is better than the best the range contains. The two numbers quoted just before ($3/4$ at $\sigma=1/2$, $1/2$ at $\sigma=1$) are both correct.
