1. [minor] statement "Existence, uniqueness and stability for the elliptic Dirichlet problem"
   (line 185): the paragraph lists every way the unit's theorem differs from Stakgold and Holst's
   Theorems 10.4.1/10.4.2 — the constants, the lift, the $H^{-1}$ data hypothesis — but not the domain.
   S&H assume throughout §10.4 that $\Omega$ is a **bounded Lipschitz** domain (p. 711), because their
   space $H^1_{0,D}(\Omega)$ is cut out by a trace; the unit asks only that $\Omega$ be open and of
   finite width. The unit's own statement is true at that generality (its proof uses $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and
   the slab lemma and never touches a trace), so this is an unflagged *strengthening*, not an error, but
   "This is the pure Dirichlet case of ..." reads as though S&H's theorem already covered an unbounded
   slab with a rough boundary. Fix: add "and with their bounded Lipschitz domain relaxed to an open set
   of finite width, which the $H^1_0$ formulation allows".
2. [minor] example, item 5 (line 275): "its supremum over those numbers is reached at those two
   extremes". Since $c_k = k^2/(1+k^2) < 1$ for every $k$, the supremum is attained only at
   $c_1 = \tfrac12$ and merely approached at the upper end. The displayed formula for $\|I-\sigma A\|$ is
   right as a supremum (and the text is self-consistent two sentences later, "without reaching it").
   Fix: "attained at $c_1$ and approached at the other end".

  (i) The $\ell^2$ counterexample in the remark is correct as given: $a(u,v)=\sum k^{-1}u_kv_k$ is
  bounded with $M=1$ and positive but not coercive, $F(v)=\sum k^{-1}v_k$ is bounded because
  $\sum k^{-2}<\infty$, and $a(u,v)=F(v)$ for all $v$ forces $u_k \equiv 1 \notin \ell^2$ — no solution,

  (ii) The lemma $\|u\|_{L^2}\le d\|\partial_1u\|_{L^2}$ on $H^1_0$: tested on $\Omega=(0,\pi)$, $d=\pi$,
  $u=\sin x$ gives $1.2533 \le \pi\cdot1.2533$ — holds with the stated slack, and the remark's claim that
  the sharp constant there is $1$ (not $\pi$) is confirmed by the sine expansion,
  $\|u'\|^2 = \tfrac\pi2\sum k^2b_k^2 \ge \tfrac\pi2\sum b_k^2$, equality at $k=1$. Degenerate cases
  (constants, $u$ not vanishing on the boundary, $\Omega$ unbounded in the other $n-1$ directions) break
  nothing: the lemma's proof uses only the slab direction, as it says.

  by that route; with $b<0$ allowed, $a(u,u)$ can be negative, confirming "this is the only use of that
  hypothesis".
