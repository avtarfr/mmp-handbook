## Findings
1. [minor] remark "The two sources state Poincaré differently" (line 89): "In general the best constant is $\lambda_1^{-1/2}$, where $\lambda_1$ is the lowest Dirichlet eigenvalue of $-\Delta$". This is true for $p=2$ and $\Gamma=\partial\Omega$, but the sentence sits in a block whose statement of Poincaré ranges over $1\le p<\infty$, so "in general" can be read as a claim for every $p$. Fix: say "for $p=2$ and $\Gamma=\partial\Omega$ the best constant is ...".
2. [minor] example, step 2 (line 257): "the consistency theorem of :ref[weak derivatives and Sobolev spaces]" — the result actually invoked is that unit's statement *Weak and classical solutions agree where both make sense*, part 1 (a $C^2\cap H^1_0$ pointwise solution is a weak solution), not its *consistency* clause about classical versus weak derivatives. The name points the reader at the wrong statement.
3. [minor] example, step 5 (line 277): $\|I-\sigma A\| = \sup_{k\ge1}|1-\sigma c_k| = \max\{|1-\tfrac\sigma2|,|1-\sigma|\}$. The second equality is right, but the branch $|1-\sigma|$ is a limit value, not attained at any $k$; it enters because the spectrum of $A$ is the closure $\{c_k\}\cup\{1\}$. The convexity sentence just above ("its supremum over those numbers is reached at those two extremes") therefore overstates: the supremum is reached only at $c=\tfrac12$. Fix: say the closure of $\{c_k\}$ has extremes $\tfrac12$ and $1$.
4. [minor] statement "Existence, uniqueness and stability", line 185: "The special case $a_{ij}=a\delta_{ij}$ with $a\ge1$ and $b\ge1$ on a smooth bounded plane domain is their Exercise 8.5.4." Exercise 8.5.4 (p. 553) also asks $a,b,f\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega;\mathbb{R})$ and $\Omega$ simply connected. Not wrong as a description of the special case, but the omitted hypotheses are worth a word since the whole point of the theorem is that no smoothness of the coefficients is needed.
