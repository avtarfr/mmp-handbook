## Findings
1. [blocking] example, item 3 "How good is the bound?" (line 263): "The remaining factor $\sqrt2$ is the cost of splitting the seminorm in half in Step 4 of the proof" is wrong, and the rest of the same sentence refutes it. With the sharp Poincaré constant the chain is $m\|u\|_{H^1}^2\le a(u,u)=F(u)\le L\|u\|_{H^1}$; for $u=\sin x$ the first inequality is an *equality* ($\tfrac12\pi=\pi/2$), exactly as the sentence itself says and as item 4 restates, so the splitting contributes no slack whatever. The whole factor $\sqrt2$ sits in the last inequality, i.e. in Step 3's $|F(v)|\le\|f\|_{L^2}\|v\|_{L^2}\le\|f\|_{L^2}\|v\|_{H^1}$: here $F(u)=\pi/2=1.5708$ while $L\|u\|_{H^1}=\sqrt{\pi/2}\cdot\sqrt\pi=\pi/\sqrt2=2.2214$, a ratio of exactly $\sqrt2$, which is $\|u\|_{H^1}/\|u\|_{L^2}$ for this $u$. Fix: attribute the remaining $\sqrt2$ to bounding $\|v\|_{L^2}$ by $\|v\|_{H^1}$ when $L$ is computed (equivalently, to measuring the solution in the full $H^1$ norm when the data are only paired against its $L^2$ part); the observation that $m=1/2$ is sharp then stands, and item 4 becomes consistent with item 3.
2. [minor] example, item 5 (line 271): "The true optimum sits at the edge of the range the general bound can certify" is not true as written. Minimising $\|I-\sigma A\|=\max_{k\ge1}|1-\sigma k^2/(1+k^2)|$ over all $\sigma$ gives $\sigma=4/3$ with value $1/3$ (I checked numerically over $k\le10^6$); $\sigma=1$, value $1/2$, is only the best point of the certified interval $(0,1)$, whose endpoint is excluded anyway. Fix: "within the certified range the best choice is at its edge, and the true optimum, $\sigma=4/3$ with factor $1/3$, lies outside it."
