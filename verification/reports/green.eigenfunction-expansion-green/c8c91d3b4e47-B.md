## Checked
- Counterexample search: tested the statements at the places they could break.
  (i) $\lambda$ an eigenvalue: the series and the resolvent both fail there, as the unit says, and
  Hassani states the equivalence ("the GF exists if and only if $(L_x-\lambda)[u]=0$ has no
  nontrivial solution") on p. 630 — so the hypothesis in the statement is not merely sufficient.
  (ii) $0$ an eigenvalue: the $\lambda=0$ series needs $0\notin\{\lambda_n\}$, and Arfken flags the
  same gap on p. 450.
  (iii) Degenerate eigenvalues: the single-term numerator would be wrong for a double eigenvalue;
  the unit states the $\sum_k$ form and restricts the simple case to unmixed conditions, which is
  where Stakgold and Holst prove simplicity (p. 412).
  (iv) The most promising attack was part (i)'s "the zeros of $C$ are exactly the eigenvalues". It
  is false for an arbitrary pair of homogeneous solutions, and the unit's own example 7 exhibits
  the failure: the convenient pair gives $C=-k\sin k$ with a spurious simple zero at $\lambda=0$. I
  checked this independently — $-k\sin k = -\lambda+\lambda^2/6-\dots$, a simple zero at $\lambda=0$;
  $\lambda=0$ is not an eigenvalue of this problem; and $G$ has no pole there, since
  $\lim_{k\to0}\sin(kt_<)\sin(k(1-t_>))/(k\sin k) = t_<(1-t_>)$, which sympy confirms ($4/25$ at
  $t=2/5$, $\tau=3/5$) and which is exactly item 3's $G$. With part (ii)'s $\lambda$-independent
  data the trap disappears: $C_{\mathrm{ii}} = -e\sin k/k \to -e \neq 0$. The unit anticipates this
  and makes it a warning in method step 4. No counterexample survives against the statements as
  written.

    in the definition is right. Lagrange's identity as used in Step 3 checks out term by term:
    $\overline{u_n}\mathcal{L}G - G\overline{\mathcal{L}u_n} = -\overline{u_n}(pG')'+G(p\overline

    Holst's (7.1.8) multiplied by their $s$, consistent with their (7.1.9).
  - Part (ii): $B_av = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha-\cos\alpha\sin\alpha = 0$ and
    $B_bz = \sin\beta\cos\beta-\cos\beta\sin\beta = 0$, so the prescribed data do enforce the
    conditions; $A\,W[v,z;\xi] = -1/p(\xi)$ with $pW$ constant gives $A=-1/C$, the printed sign.
    Abel's formula for $-(pu')'+qu=\lambda su$, i.e. $pu''+p'u'+(\lambda s-q)u=0$, gives
    $W'/W=-p'/p$ and hence $pW$ constant, as Step 3 asserts.
  - Part (iii): from $-u_nu_n = -k_nz_nz_n/C'$ one gets $u_n=\pm\{k_n/C'\}^{1/2}z_n$, and since
    $v_n=k_nz_n$ this is the same as $\pm v_n/\{k_nC'\}^{1/2}$ — the two printed forms agree.
  - Example, item 1–2: under $t=\ln x$, $(xu')' = u_{tt}/x$ and $\delta(x-\xi)=\delta(t-\tau)/x$

    Dirichlet data on $[0,1]$: $\lambda_n=n^2\pi^2$. Since $s\,dx = dt$, normalising in
    $(\cdot,\cdot)_s$ is normalising in $L^2(0,1)$, giving $u_n=\sqrt2\sin(n\pi\ln x)$.

    x$ at $\xi$ is $-1/\xi = -1/p(\xi)$.
