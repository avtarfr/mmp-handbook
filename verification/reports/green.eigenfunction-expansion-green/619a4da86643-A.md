## Findings

1. [blocking] proof "The bilinear expansion", Step 3 (lines 178–182): the Lagrange identity is displayed with the wrong sign, twice. For $\mathcal{L}u=-(pu')'+qu$ and the unit's own Wronskian convention $W(f,g)=fg'-f'g$ (the one used in part (ii), $W[v,z]=vz'-zv'$),
   $$\overline{u_n}\,\mathcal{L}G-G\,\overline{\mathcal{L}u_n} \;=\; +\bigl[p\,W(G,\overline{u_n})\bigr]' ,$$
   because $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[p(G\overline{u_n}'-\overline{u_n}G')\bigr]=G(p\overline{u_n}')'-\overline{u_n}(pG')'$. The unit writes $-\bigl[pW(G,\overline{u_n})\bigr]'$, and then the integrated display likewise carries a spurious minus: $\int(\overline{u_n}\mathcal{L}G-G\overline{\mathcal{L}u_n})\,dx = +\bigl[p(G\overline{u_n}'-G'\overline{u_n})\bigr]_a^b$. The source agrees with the plus sign: Stakgold and Holst's (7.1.8) (checked on the page image of p. 411, where the OCR turns the factor $1/s$ into a minus) reads $\bar vLu-uL\bar v=\frac1s[p(u\bar v'-u'\bar v)]'$, and their (7.1.9) $\langle Lu,v\rangle_s-\langle u,Lv\rangle_s = p(b)W(u,\bar v;b)-p(a)W(u,\bar v;a)$ has no minus. The conclusion of the step (the boundary term is $0$) is unaffected, but the displayed identity is false as written. Fix: drop both minus signs.

2. [blocking] example, item 7 (line 322): "they differ from the exact initial-value normalisations of part (ii) by constant factors" is wrong, and the error has a visible consequence. The part-(ii) solutions are $v=\sin(k t)/k$ (from $v(1)=\sin\alpha=0$, $v'(1)=\cos\alpha=1$, the derivative in $x$) and $z=(e/k)\sin k(1-t)$ (from $z(e)=0$, $z'(e)=-1$), so the example's $v=\sin kt$, $z=\sin k(1-t)$ differ from them by the **$\lambda$-dependent** factors $k$ and $k/e$. That is exactly why the example's $C(\lambda)=-k\sin k$ has a zero at $\lambda = 0$, which is *not* an eigenvalue — contradicting statement (ii) ("the zeros of $C$ are exactly the eigenvalues") and method step 4 ("find the eigenvalues as the zeros of $C$"). The properly normalised pair gives $C(\lambda)=-e\sin k/k$, which tends to $-e\neq0$ as $\lambda\to0$ and whose zeros are exactly $k=n\pi$. The parenthesis at the end of item 7 notices the spurious zero but excuses it with the same wrong reason. Fix: either use the part-(ii) normalisation, or say that the factors depend on $\lambda$ and that $G$ and the residue formula are invariant under rescaling $v,z$ by any functions of $\lambda$ that are nonzero at $\lambda_n$ (which is true: $C\mapsto abC$, $C'(\lambda_n)\mapsto a(\lambda_n)b(\lambda_n)C'(\lambda_n)$, $k_n\mapsto (a/b)k_n$, $z_n^2\mapsto b^2z_n^2$, so $k_nz_n^2/C'$ is unchanged) — while noting that such a rescaling can introduce zeros of $C$ that are not eigenvalues.

3. [blocking] `prereqs` (line 7): the whole second half of the unit is complex analysis and none of it is a listed prerequisite. Statement (i) asserts meromorphy and uses residues; (ii) uses entire functions; (iv) and proof Step 7 evaluate a contour integral by summing residues; proof Step 4 infers the order of a zero of $C$ from the order of a pole of $G$; Step 5 uses the residue formula $\operatorname{Res}(f/C)=f/C'$ at a simple zero. That is `complex.singularities` and `complex.residue-theorem` (and the notion of an entire function), none of which is standard undergraduate calculus or linear algebra. Fix: add `complex.residue-theorem` (and, if wanted, `complex.singularities`) to `prereqs`.

5. [minor] remark "Conventions" (line 272): "Arfken writes $L$ for the self-adjoint $(pu')'+qu$ … the sign of every $\lambda_n$ differs accordingly" is exact only when $q\equiv0$. In general Arfken's $L=(pu')'+qu$ is not $-\mathcal{L}=(pu')'-qu$, so the two spectra are not related by a sign flip; what is true is that his eigenvalues decrease to $-\infty$ and ours increase to $+\infty$. Fix: say the two operators differ by a sign only when $q\equiv0$, and otherwise only the direction of the spectrum is reversed.
