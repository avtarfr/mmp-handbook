[source] definition and remark, the Arfken "central moments" sentence (lines 44, 204): Arfken, Weber and Harris, §23.2, p. 1141, write of the moments generated by $\langle e^{tX}\rangle$ that "the moments here are not relative to the expectation value, but are relative to $x=0$; they are called central moments". That is the standard term used backwards - a central moment is a moment about the mean, which is what their own Eq. (23.31) on the same page defines, while $\langle e^{tX}\rangle$ generates the raw moments. The unit reports the sentence accurately, does not adopt the usage, and flags the clash in the remark, which is the right handling. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add prob.generating-functions arfken-2013 ...`; not a reason to fail.
2. [minor] statement, rule 12 (line 97): "If $M_X = M_Y$ and both are finite on a neighbourhood of $0$, then $X$ and $Y$ have the same distribution. Riley, Hobson and Bence state this and say a rigorous proof lies outside their scope." RHB's uniqueness paragraph (p. 1165) carries **no** hypothesis at all - it reads simply "If the MGF of the random variable $X_1$ is identical to that for $X_2$ then the probability distributions of $X_1$ and $X_2$ are identical", with the "rigorous proof ... beyond the scope of this book" footnote. The finiteness-near-zero condition is ours (and Grimmett and Stirzaker's, Remark (5.7.10), p. 206), not theirs, so "state this" attributes a more careful statement to RHB than they make. Our version is the correct one, so nothing is mathematically wrong. Fix: "Riley, Hobson and Bence assert the uniqueness without conditions and say a rigorous proof lies outside their scope; the finiteness hypothesis is Grimmett and Stirzaker's (Remark (5.7.10))."
3. [minor] proof "Extinction", the $\mu>1$ bullet (line 185): "$G(s) < s$ for $s$ slightly below $1$, while $G(0) = \mathbb{P}(Z_1=0) \ge 0$; so $G(s)-s$ changes sign on $[0,1)$". When $p_0 = 0$ the function $G(s)-s$ vanishes at $s=0$ and is negative near $1$, so it never changes sign - it only touches zero at the endpoint. The conclusion ("there is a root $\eta<1$") still holds, with $\eta=0$, and the rest of the proof is unaffected; only the word "changes" is wrong in that boundary case. Fix: "so $G(s)-s$ is non-negative at $s=0$ and negative near $s=1$, and by the intermediate value theorem has a root $\eta<1$ (which is $\eta=0$ when $p_0=0$)".
