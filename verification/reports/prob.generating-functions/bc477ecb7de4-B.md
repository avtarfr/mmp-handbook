## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, second bullet (line 199): the counterexample to rule 5 computes the wrong distribution for $S$. With $X_1,X_2,\dots$ independent Bernoulli$(\tfrac12)$ and $N=1+X_1$, the four equally likely outcomes of $(X_1,X_2)$ give: $X_1=0\Rightarrow N=1,\ S=0$ (probability $\tfrac12$); $X_1=1\Rightarrow N=2,\ S=1+X_2\in\{1,2\}$ (probability $\tfrac14$ each). So $\mathbb{P}(S=0)=\tfrac12$, $\mathbb{P}(S=1)=\tfrac14$, $\mathbb{P}(S=2)=\tfrac14$ and $G_S(s)=\tfrac12+\tfrac14 s+\tfrac14 s^2$. The unit asserts $\mathbb{P}(S=1)=0$, $\mathbb{P}(S=2)=\tfrac12$ and $G_S(s)=\tfrac12(1+s^2)$, which would require $S=2$ whenever $X_1=1$ - that is, it silently sets $X_2=1$. I confirmed the correct distribution by enumerating all four outcomes exactly in rationals. The bullet's *conclusion* survives: the unit's own $G_N(G_X(s))=\tfrac12\bigl(\tfrac{1+s}{2}+\tfrac{(1+s)^2}{4}\bigr)$ expands to $\tfrac18 s^2+\tfrac12 s+\tfrac38$, which is still different from the true $G_S$, so rule 5 does fail here - but the stated mass function and the stated $G_S$ are wrong, and they are our own material. Fix: replace the three probabilities by $\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac14$ and $G_S(s)$ by $\tfrac12+\tfrac14s+\tfrac14s^2$, then note that $\tfrac12+\tfrac14s+\tfrac14s^2 \ne \tfrac38+\tfrac12s+\tfrac18s^2$ (they differ at every $s\ne1$).
