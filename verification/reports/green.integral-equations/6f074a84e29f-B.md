## Findings
1. [blocking] method "Turning a second-order linear ODE into an integral equation", step 2 (line 145): the claim that the two-point-boundary-data reduction "produces a **Fredholm** equation of the second kind on $[a,b]$, whose kernel is continuous, symmetric, and has a jump of $-1$ in $\partial K/\partial t$ as $t$ crosses $x$" is false for the general equation $y''+A(x)y'+B(x)y=g$ that the method is set up with. Carrying $y'(a)=C$ through the two integrations of step 1, imposing $y(b)=0$ and splitting the range gives the total kernel
   $\tilde K(x,t) = K(x,t)\mathbf{1}_{t<x} - \frac{x-a}{b-a}K(b,t)$ with $K$ the step-1 kernel. Two counterexamples, both verified symbolically:
   - $A\equiv1$, $B\equiv0$, $g\equiv1$, $[a,b]=[0,1]$: $K\equiv-1$, so $\tilde K(x,t)=x-1$ for $t<x$ and $\tilde K(x,t)=x$ for $t>x$. This kernel jumps by $1$ across the diagonal (not continuous), is not symmetric ($\tilde K(x,t)-\tilde K(t,x)=x-t-1$ for $t<x$), and $\partial\tilde K/\partial t\equiv0$, so there is no jump of $-1$. (The reduction itself is right: the resulting Fredholm equation does reproduce the exact solution of $y''+y'=1$, $y(0)=y(1)=0$ — residual $0$ — so only the three asserted properties fail.)
   - $A\equiv0$, $B(x)=x$, $[a,b]=[0,1]$: $\tilde K(x,t)=t^{2}(1-x)$ for $t<x$ and $tx(1-t)$ for $t>x$. Continuous, but not symmetric (difference $t^{2}(t-x)$), and the jump in $\partial\tilde K/\partial t$ is $-x$, not $-1$. In general $A\equiv0$ gives $\tilde K(x,t)=B(t)G(x,t)$ with $G$ the symmetric Green's function of $\mathrm{d}^2/\mathrm{d}x^2$, so symmetry needs $B$ constant.
   Arfken, Weber and Harris establish the three properties (p. 1052, properties 1–3) only for their Eq. (21.16), the case $A\equiv0$, $B\equiv\omega^{2}$ constant. Fix: restrict the sentence to that case — e.g. "for $A\equiv0$ the kernel is $B(t)G(x,t)$ with $G$ continuous and symmetric and $\partial G/\partial t$ jumping by $-1$; when in addition $B$ is constant the whole kernel is symmetric, as in Arfken, Weber and Harris's oscillator example" — or attribute the three properties to that example rather than to the general step.
