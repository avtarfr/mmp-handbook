## Findings
1. [source] method, step 2 (line 153): the unit reports Arfken, Weber and Harris's summary on p. 1052 that two-point boundary data give "a Fredholm-type integral equation with a kernel that will be the Green's function appropriate to the given boundary conditions". That is exact only for $A\equiv0$ with $B$ constant; for variable $B$ the kernel is $B(t)\Gamma(x,t)$, and for $A\not\equiv0$ it is not even continuous across the diagonal. The unit reports the source faithfully and corrects the generality itself in the `note` that follows, so this is not a defect in our text. The imprecision in the source is already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (entry for green.integral-equations against arfken-2013, §21.1 summary p. 1052); I confirmed it independently rather than re-recording it.
2. [source] statement "Boundedness and compactness" (line 124): the unit cites hassani-2013 p. 544 for the distribution of the characteristic values. The same page defines a Hilbert–Schmidt kernel by finiteness of the interval ("when the interval of integration $(a,b)$ is finite, $K(x,t)$ is called a Hilbert-Schmidt kernel") and deduces compactness from that. Finiteness of the interval is not the condition — $|x-t|^{-1}$ on $[0,1]^2$ is a counterexample, and $\mathrm{e}^{-x^2-t^2}$ on the plane is Hilbert–Schmidt — but the unit takes its definition from Stakgold and Holst and does not report Hassani's, so nothing in our text is wrong. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.
3. [minor] note "What the Fredholm kernel of step 2 looks like in general", closing paragraph (line 182): "Symmetry of the kernel is a property of the *operator* — it needs the operator to be formally self-adjoint, which $\mathrm{d}^{2}/\mathrm{d}x^{2}+A\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x+B$ is only when $A\equiv0$". Read as a necessary condition this is consistent with the bullets above it, but it sits one line after the bullet showing that for $A\equiv0$ the kernel is symmetric only when $B$ is constant, so on a first pass the two look to disagree; and the necessity itself is asserted with no argument. (It is true at least for constant coefficients: with $A\equiv\alpha$, $B\equiv\beta$ on $[0,1]$ the total kernel of step 2 is $[(t-x)\beta-\alpha]\mathbf 1_{t<x}-x[(t-1)\beta-\alpha]$, whose symmetry forces $\alpha=0$, the $\beta$ parts cancelling identically.) Fix: "formal self-adjointness is necessary — it fails as soon as $A\not\equiv0$ — but not sufficient: even for $A\equiv0$ the kernel $B(t)\Gamma(x,t)$ is symmetric only when $B$ is constant."
4. [minor] prereqs (line 7): the `note` derives the total kernel $K=B(t)\Gamma(x,t)$ from the Green's-function representation of the Dirichlet problem, and the closing example uses $\min(x,t)$ as the Green's function of $-\mathrm{d}^2/\mathrm{d}x^2$ with $y(0)=y'(1)=0$. Both are results of `green.green-function-bvp`, which is `:ref`'d at the point of use but is not in `prereqs`; the style guide asks for the units relied on to be listed there. Not blocking, because the definitions, the statement, the proof and the method do not depend on it, and both Green's functions are written out explicitly with the properties the text uses. Fix: add `green.green-function-bvp` to `prereqs` (it precedes this unit in the map, so no ordering warning).
5. [minor] definition "The integral operator and its kernel classes" (line 74): "Let $-\infty\le a<b\le\infty$. A kernel $K$ on the square $[a,b]\times[a,b]$" uses closed-interval notation for endpoints that may be infinite — the parenthetical two lines later takes care of the image but not of the domain. Fix: write the square as $(a,b)\times(a,b)$, or say "on $(a,b)\times(a,b)$, the endpoints being irrelevant".
6. [minor] definition "Linear integral equations: the four kinds" (line 53): "absorbing $\lambda$ into $K$ for the equations of the first kind, where it may be divided out". With $g\equiv0$ the general form reads $0=f+\lambda\int Ky$, so what is absorbed is $-\lambda$ and the displayed $v$ is $-f/\lambda$, not $f$. The four displayed equations are exactly Hassani's, so only the bridging clause is loose. Fix: "absorbing $-\lambda$ into $K$ … so that $v=-f/\lambda$".
7. [minor] front matter, stakgold-holst-2011 locator (line 23) and application (line 285): the locator promises "the four listed advantages of the integral formulation, p. 351", and the application paragraph reports three of them (boundedness/compactness, the kernel as Green's function, the reduction in dimension), silently dropping the third of the book's four (variational principles and approximation schemes). Fix: either name all four or change the locator.
