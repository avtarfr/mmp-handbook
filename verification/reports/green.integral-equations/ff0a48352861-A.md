## Findings
1. [blocking] method step 1 hypotheses (line 131), proof (line 200) and remark "Where the hypotheses are used", third bullet (line 211): the stated hypotheses are "$A$ differentiable and $A,B,g$ continuous", which is too weak for three things the unit does with them.
   (a) The kernel is $K(x,t)=(t-x)[B(t)-A'(t)]-A(t)$, so the remark's claim that continuity of $A,B,g$ "also makes $K$ continuous on the closed triangle $a\le t\le x\le b$" is false: continuity of $K$ needs $A'$ continuous, which mere differentiability of $A$ does not give. The claim is load-bearing, because the sentence goes on to say that this continuity "is the hypothesis under which the Volterra theory of green.volterra-equations applies" (kreyszig-1978 Thm 5.4-2, p. 320, asks for a kernel continuous on the triangle). As stated, the unit's own hypotheses do not deliver the hypothesis of the theory it hands the equation to.
   (b) The proof's justification for exchanging the order of integration — "the integrand is continuous on the closed triangle" — is applied to $\Phi=-[B-A']y$, whose continuity again needs $A'$ continuous.
   (c) The integration by parts $\int_a^x Ay'=A(x)y(x)-A(a)y_0-\int_a^x A'y$ needs $A'y$ integrable on $[a,x]$; a function differentiable at every point of a compact interval can have a non-integrable derivative (e.g. $x^{2}\sin(1/x^{2})$ extended by $0$), so the step is not justified under the hypothesis as written.
   These hypotheses are ours, not the source's: arfken-2013 eqs. (21.7)-(21.11), pp. 1049-1050, states none at all. Fix: require $A$ **continuously** differentiable and $B,g$ continuous, and adjust the first bullet of the remark ("$A$ differentiable is used only in the integration by parts") to match; everything else in the method, proof and note then stands unchanged.
2. [minor] definition "The integral operator and its kernel classes" (line 99): "The kernel is **symmetric** if $K(x,t)=K(t,x)$ and **Hermitian** if $K(x,t)=\overline{K(t,x)}$; for a Hermitian kernel $\mathcal{K}^{*}=\mathcal{K}$ ... the words self-adjoint and symmetric being used interchangeably for bounded operators." The unit has just taken care to separate symmetric from Hermitian kernels, and then imports the operator-theoretic usage in which "symmetric" means self-adjoint. For a complex kernel with $K(x,t)=K(t,x)$ but $K\neq\overline{K(t,\cdot)}$ the operator is *not* self-adjoint, so within one paragraph "symmetric" carries two different meanings; green.hilbert-schmidt depends on exactly this distinction. stakgold-holst-2011 can use the words interchangeably (p. 371 and §6.3) because its kernels are real. Fix: add "for real kernels the two notions coincide, which is why Stakgold and Holst use symmetric and self-adjoint interchangeably; for a complex kernel only the Hermitian condition gives $\mathcal{K}^{*}=\mathcal{K}$".
3. [minor] definition, iterated kernels (line 104): "If $K$ is symmetric, so is every $K_m$" is true, but green.hilbert-schmidt's part 3 uses the Hermitian version ("Because $K$ is Hermitian, so is every $K_m$") and takes it from this unit, where it is not stated. Fix: state both, or state the Hermitian case, which has the same one-line proof.
4. [minor] definition (lines 74, 77): "Let $-\infty\le a<b\le\infty$" followed by "$a\le x\le b$" admits $x=\pm\infty$ as an argument of $\mathcal{K}u$. Fix: "for almost every $x\in(a,b)$".
