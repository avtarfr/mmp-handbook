## Findings
1. [minor] proof of "Separation into stationary states", Step 1 (line 103): the step divides (1) by $\psi T$ without saying where that is legitimate. $\psi$ may vanish in the interior — the unit's own $\psi_2(x) = \sqrt{2/L}\sin(2\pi x/L)$ has a node at $x = L/2$, and the current calculation later in the unit uses exactly that point. Fix: carry out the division on the open set where $\Psi \ne 0$, conclude $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + V\psi = E\psi$ there, and extend to the rest of the domain by continuity of both sides; or note that the direction actually used elsewhere (if $\psi$ solves (3) then $\psi e^{-iEt/\hbar}$ solves (1)) needs no division at all. While rephrasing, the statement's "Separable solutions ... exist exactly when $\psi$ solves (3)" could add the standing assumption $\psi \not\equiv 0$, $T\not\equiv0$.
2. [minor] example "The harmonic oscillator", the paragraph "No other $\Lambda$ is admissible" (line 328): two links in the growth argument are asserted rather than argued. (a) "A series whose tail has the coefficient ratio of $e^{s^2}$ grows like $s^pe^{s^2}$ for some power $p$" — matching coefficient ratios asymptotically does not by itself give the growth of the sum. The cheap honest version: from the recursion, $a_{k+2}/a_k = (2k+1-\Lambda)/((k+2)(k+1)) \ge (2-\epsilon)/(k+2)$ for all $k \ge k_0(\epsilon)$, which dominates the coefficient ratio of $e^{(1-\epsilon)s^2}$ term by term, so $|v(s)| \ge c\,e^{(1-\epsilon)s^2}$ along its parity for large $s$ and $\tilde\psi = ve^{-s^2/2}$ is unbounded. (b) The case where *neither* series terminates is not separated from the case where one does: a general solution is $c_ev_e + c_ov_o$, and the argument needs that these cannot cancel — they cannot, because for $k \ge k_0$ all the coefficients of each series have one sign, so both are eventually positive and increasing on $s > 0$. Fix: add those two clauses. (The conclusion is right and the unit is already explicit that Kreyszig leaves this as his Problem 5, p. 588.)
