## Findings
1. [source] statement "The probability current and the continuity equation" (line 140) / hassani-2013 §14.8.1, after Eq. (14.50), p. 452: Hassani's claim that the polar pair is "completely equivalent" to the Schrödinger equation is false at the nodes of $\psi$ (where $A=0$, the phase is undefined, and his first equation divides by $A$) and ignores that the decomposition fixes $S$ only modulo $2\pi\hbar$. The unit reports the claim as his, italicizes it, attributes it by `:cite`, and states both caveats in its own voice — exactly the right handling. Already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (two entries against this unit and locator); no new entry added.
2. [minor] statement, line 63–69 and Step 3 of the proof (line 99): (5) is called "the general solution of (1)", but the only convergence Step 3 establishes is mean-square, which does not by itself let the series be differentiated term by term in $t$ or twice in $\mathbf r$, i.e. does not make the sum a solution of (1) in the classical sense the rest of the unit uses. The honest statement — and the one the Reed–Simon remark at line 219 already sets up — is that (5) is the solution in the sense of the unitary group $e^{-i\hat Ht/\hbar}$, and is a classical solution when the coefficients decay fast enough (e.g. $\sum\lvert c_n\rvert^2E_n^2<\infty$ for $f$ in the domain of $\hat H$). Fix: name the sense in Step 3, in one clause.
3. [minor] statement, line 53: "Separable solutions of (1) of the form $\Psi = \psi(\mathbf r)T(t)$ exist exactly when $\psi$ solves (3) for some constant $E\in\mathbb{C}$" should say *nontrivial* separable solutions; as written the forward direction is read off a $\Psi\equiv0$ that carries no information about $E$, and the proof's Step 1 (which divides by $\psi T$) silently assumes both factors are not identically zero.
4. [minor] proof of the oscillator's quantization, line 370: "term by term they dominate a positive multiple of those of $\mathrm{e}^{cs^2}$" is the one step of that argument left implicit. What makes it work is that $a_{k+2}/a_k \ge b_{k+2}/b_k$ with both sides positive for $k\ge k_0$, so $a_k/b_k$ is nondecreasing and $a_k \ge (a_{k_0}/b_{k_0})\,b_k$ for all even $k\ge k_0$. Fix: one sentence; everything else in that paragraph (including the choice $\tfrac12<c<1$ and the equivalence $2k+1-\Lambda\ge 2c(k+1)$) is correct and well set up.
