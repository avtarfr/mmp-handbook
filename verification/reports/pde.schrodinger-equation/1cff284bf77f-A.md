## Findings
1. [blocking] :::statement[Separation into stationary states] (lines 53-59): the reality of the separation constant $E$ is nowhere a hypothesis, but the statement's conclusion needs it. "Separable solutions ... exist exactly when $\psi$ solves (3) for some constant $E$, and then $\Psi = \psi e^{-iEt/\hbar}$" is true for every $E\in\mathbb{C}$; the next sentence, "Such a $\Psi$ is a stationary state: its probability density $\lvert\Psi\rvert^2 = \lvert\psi\rvert^2$ does not depend on $t$, because the time factor has modulus one", is false unless $E$ is real. (3) as the statement poses it is a differential equation with no normalizability or boundary condition attached — the unit's own remark, third bullet, insists on exactly that distinction — so complex $E$ is not excluded. Concretely, with $V\equiv0$ in one dimension and $E = i$, $\psi = e^{\alpha x}$ with $\alpha^2 = -2mi/\hbar^2$ solves (3), and $\Psi = \psi e^{-iEt/\hbar} = \psi e^{t/\hbar}$ solves (1) with a density that grows without bound: a separable solution that is not stationary. The proof's Step 2 does supply the missing clause ("Since $E$ is real whenever $\hat H$ is self-adjoint ... which holds for the real potentials considered here"), so the fix is to carry it into the statement: say "with $E$ real" and note there, as Step 2 does, that reality is automatic when $\psi$ is a normalizable eigenfunction of a self-adjoint $\hat H$ rather than merely a solution of the differential equation.
2. [minor] :::example[The free particle], last sentence of the second paragraph (line 211): "here the phase velocity $\omega/k = \hbar k/2m$ depends on $k$, so a packet spreads". Spreading is governed by the $k$-dependence of the *group* velocity, equivalently by $\omega''\ne0$: a medium with $\omega = ck + a$ has a $k$-dependent phase velocity $c + a/k$ and no spreading whatever, since every component of the envelope travels at $c$. The conclusion is right here only because $\omega'' = \hbar/m \ne 0$. Fix: argue from $d\omega/dk = \hbar k/m$, or from $\omega'' = \hbar/m$, and keep the phase-velocity value as a separate remark.
3. [minor] :::example[The harmonic oscillator] (lines 314-318): "It has a polynomial solution — and therefore $\tilde\psi$ decays at infinity — exactly when $\Lambda-1 = 2n$." The "exactly when" is correct as a statement about polynomial solutions of Hermite's equation, but what the block needs, and asserts by implication when it writes down $E_n$ as *the* energy levels, is the other direction: that no other $\Lambda$ gives an admissible $\tilde\psi$. That needs the one extra fact that a non-terminating Hermite series grows like $e^{s^2}$, so $\tilde\psi = ve^{-s^2/2}$ grows like $e^{s^2/2}$ and is not in $L^2$. Kreyszig leaves this to his Problem 5 (p. 588) rather than proving it, which is his privilege; ours says "Normalizability as the quantization condition" is the whole point of the unit, so it should carry the clause. Fix: one sentence after (11) giving the growth of the non-terminating solution.
4. [source] hassani-2013 §14.8.1, Eqs. (14.49)-(14.50), p. 452, quoted at line 120 as "equation (1) is *exactly equivalent* to the pair": Hassani's "These two equations are completely equivalent to the Schrodinger equation" is an overstatement at the nodes of $\Psi$, where $A = 0$, the phase $S$ is undefined, and the first equation of (14.50) divides by $A$; the polar form also determines $S$ only modulo $2\pi\hbar$. The equivalence holds on any region where $\Psi$ is nowhere zero. The unit reports Hassani faithfully, so this does not count against it; recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`.
