## Findings
1. [source] statement "Nonlinear centres in a conservative system" (line 63): the theorem is taken verbatim in substance from Strogatz Thm 6.5.1, and with its stated hypotheses (E only continuous, minimum not required to be strict) it is false. Counterexample: $f(x,y)=(0,\,x^2+y^2)$ is polynomial with $(0,0)$ as its only fixed point, and $E(x,y)=|x|$ is continuous, constant along every orbit ($\dot x=0$) and nonconstant on every open set, with $E(0,0)=0\le E$ everywhere, so the origin is a local minimum of $E$; yet every nearby orbit is a vertical half-line travelled upward ($\dot y=x^2+y^2>0$ off the origin) and nothing near the origin is closed. The gap is exactly the step the book declines to prove ("Near a local maximum or minimum, the contours are closed", p. 163). The unit reports the source faithfully, says in step 2 of its proof that Strogatz "states and does not prove" that step, and then proves and uses the $C^2$/positive-definite-Hessian version in the remark at line 105, so our text is sound. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add dyn.conservative-reversible strogatz-1994 "§6.5, Theorem 6.5.1, p. 163"`. No fix needed; optionally the statement could add "and the hypothesis under which we prove it" as a forward reference to the remark.

[minor] proof "Nonlinear centres in a reversible system", step 6 (line 139): $T_{\max}=2\pi/\mu$ is only enough to turn $\theta$ by $\pi$ at the guaranteed rate $\mu/2$ (step 2 says so explicitly), so running backwards for time $\le T_{\max}$ guarantees reaching *the $x$-axis*, not the *positive* $x$-axis; a point starting just past the negative $x$-axis needs up to $2T_{\max}$. Fix: take the backward time budget to be $2T_{\max}$ and the neighbourhood $0<|z|\le r_1e^{-2KT_{\max}}$, or say "reaches the $x$-axis, and if it reaches the negative half, continue for at most a further $T_{\max}$".
