## Findings
1. [blocking] application (line 255): "At a local maximum of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the theorem applied to $-E$ gives a centre as well" is false, and contradicts the clause that immediately follows it. If $r_\ast$ is a local maximum of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ then $E = p_r^2/2m + V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)$ has a *saddle* at $(r_\ast,0)$ — a minimum in the $p_r$ direction, a maximum in the $r$ direction — and so does $-E$; the centre theorem needs a local extremum of $E$, not of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The linearization there has eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{-V_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_\ast)/m}$, real and of opposite sign, so the equilibrium is exactly the "saddle of the radial system" that the next half of the same sentence calls unstable. Fix: delete the clause and say that a local maximum of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ gives a saddle of the radial system, hence an unstable circular orbit; a local minimum of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the only case the centre theorem reaches, because only there is $(r_\ast,0)$ a local minimum of $E$.
2. [blocking] application (line 258): "the level set through a saddle of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the escape threshold. In the Kepler problem with $V = -k/r$ it is the parabolic orbit." For Kepler with $\ell \neq 0$, $V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r) = -k/r + \ell^2/(2mr^2)$ has exactly one critical point, $r = \ell^2/(mk)$, and it is a minimum; there is no saddle anywhere, so the sentence has no referent in the example it names. The escape threshold is $E = 0 = \lim_{r\to\infty}V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)$, a non-critical level reached only in the limit. The sentence also conflates "saddle of $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$" (a function on a half-line, which has no saddles) with "saddle of the radial system". Fix: say the separatrix between bound and unbounded motion is the level $E = \lim_{r\to\infty} V_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which in Kepler is $E=0$, the parabolic orbit; keep the saddle-level picture for potentials that really do have a local maximum, such as the cubic hump of the worked example.
3. [blocking] proof[Nonlinear centres in a reversible system], step 6 (line 139): the time bound is wrong, and the chain of radius estimates breaks. $T_{\max} = 2\pi/\mu$ was defined in step 2 as a bound for advancing $\theta$ by $\pi$. Running backwards from an arbitrary $z$ whose polar angle may be anywhere in $[0,2\pi)$, the angle must change by up to $2\pi$ before the *positive* $x$-axis is reached, which takes time up to $4\pi/\mu = 2T_{\max}$. With the stated $|z| \le r_1 e^{-KT_{\max}}$ the bound $r(t) \le |z|e^{K|t|}$ over $|t| \le 2T_{\max}$ gives only $r \le r_0$ at arrival, whereas step 3 needs the arrival point to have modulus at most $r_1$. Fix: take $|z| \le r_1 e^{-2KT_{\max}}$ and allow time up to $2T_{\max}$; then $r \le r_1$ on arrival and steps 3-5 apply unchanged. (Alternatively define $T_{\max} = 4\pi/\mu$ as the time to advance $\theta$ by $2\pi$ and keep the rest as written.)
4. [minor] proof[Nonlinear centres in a conservative system], step 2 (line 94), with the statement block (line 64): the statement is made for a merely continuous conserved quantity, but from step 2 onwards the proof assumes $E \in C^2$ with positive definite Hessian at $p$. The restriction is declared in the text and Strogatz (p. 163) does not prove the contour fact at all, so this is a faithful route rather than an error; but a reader meeting the statement first is not warned. Fix: add one clause to the statement block saying that the proof below establishes the theorem under the extra hypothesis that $E$ is $C^2$ with nondegenerate minimum, the general continuous case being the contour fact that Strogatz states without proof.
5. [minor] method, step 4 (line 155): "If the system is reversible and the fixed point lies on the symmetry set, it is a centre" is false read on its own — the saddles $(0,\pm1)$ of this unit's own second example lie on the symmetry set $x=0$ of $R(x,y)=(-x,y)$. The linear-centre hypothesis is carried only by the step's title "Promote the predicted centres". Fix: write "a fixed point *predicted by the linearization to be a centre*, lying on the symmetry set, is a centre".
6. [minor] example (line 206): $x_+$ is printed as $1.3305410\ldots$, but the value is $1.330540951770\ldots$, so the final digit is a rounding while the companion $x_- = 0.5670689\ldots$ (true value $0.567068922852\ldots$) is a truncation. The trailing "$\ldots$" after a rounded digit reads as further digits. Fix: print $1.3305409\ldots$, or round both.
7. [minor] application (line 256): "it is in general different from the angular period $2\pi$". $2\pi$ is an angular advance, not a period in time; the angular period is $\int_0^{T_r}\! \ell/(mr^2)\,\mathrm dt$ worth of revolution. Fix: say the radial period is in general not commensurate with the time taken for $\theta$ to advance by $2\pi$, and that the ratio of the two decides closure versus precession.
8. [minor] sources, role of hirsch-smale-devaney-2013 (line 17): the role claims the unit uses "the constraint on the eigenvalues at an equilibrium of a planar Hamiltonian system" ($\pm\lambda$ or $\pm i\lambda$). Nothing in the unit uses or states it. Fix: drop it from the role, or use it in the remark on the two meanings of "conservative", where it would strengthen the point that a planar Hamiltonian equilibrium is never a node or a spiral.
