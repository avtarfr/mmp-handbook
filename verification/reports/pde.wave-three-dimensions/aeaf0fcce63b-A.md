## Findings
1. [minor] :::proof "Kirchhoff's formula", Step 3 (the "Therefore" after the uniqueness lemma): the step closes one gap in the sources and leaves a smaller one of the same kind open. Having proved that the half-line Dirichlet problem has at most one $C^2$ solution, the unit concludes that $\widetilde U$ equals the two-case reflection formula — but that identification also needs the reflection formula to *be* a $C^2$ solution, and the reflection method of `pde.dalembert` is stated there under the compatibility $\widetilde G(0) = \widetilde H(0) = 0$ only, which is necessary and not sufficient (Evans notes on p. 67 that the reflected solution is not $C^2$ unless $g''(0) = 0$). Nothing is actually wrong: $G(x;r) = \mathcal{M}_r\varphi(x)$ is even in $r$, so $\widetilde G = rG$ is odd and $\widetilde G''(0) = 0$ automatically. Better still, the unit's own odd-extension lemma already does the whole job without the detour: applied to $\widetilde U$ itself it shows the odd extension $W$ of $\widetilde U$ is $C^2$ on the whole line, solves the equation there with data the odd extensions of $\widetilde G,\widetilde H$, and therefore equals (D) by the uniqueness half of d'Alembert's theorem — and (D) unwound on $0<r<ct$ *is* the two-case formula. Fix: run the lemma forwards on $\widetilde U$ rather than backwards on the difference of two solutions, or add the half-sentence that $\widetilde G$ is odd, hence $\widetilde G''(0)=0$, so the reflected data are $C^2/C^1$.
