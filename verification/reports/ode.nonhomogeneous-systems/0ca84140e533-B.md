## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Variation of constants: the Duhamel integral" (line 75): "this solution being the
   only one with $x(0) = x_0$ (part 1 of the existence statement in :ref[linear systems] applies to
   the nonhomogeneous system as well)". Part 1 of that statement is the claim that the solution set is
   *a vector space of dimension $n$*, and it is explicitly introduced there by "Three consequences,
   for the homogeneous system $b \equiv 0$"; read literally it does **not** apply to the
   nonhomogeneous system, whose solution set this unit's own first statement shows to be a coset
   $\Phi c + z$. What does apply is the unnumbered lead theorem of that statement, which is stated for
   $x' = A(t)x + b(t)$ and does give existence and uniqueness. The claim being justified (uniqueness)
   is correct; only the pointer is wrong. Fix: cite the existence-and-uniqueness theorem of
   ode.linear-systems rather than its part 1.
2. [minor] remark "Comparison with scalar variation of parameters" (line 190): the sentence
   "Coddington makes the same observation for the scalar formula" is attached to a compound claim —
   that the formula still defines a continuous function, still satisfies the equation at continuity
   points, and that $x'$ jumps by the jump of $g$. Coddington's Exercise 5 (p. 90) supports the middle
   part ("the formula (10.3) ... makes sense for some discontinuous functions $b$. Then $\psi_p$ will
   be a solution of $L(y) = b(x)$ at the continuity points of $b$") and asks for a continuously
   differentiable solution in a particular case; it does not state the jump rule, which is ours.
   Fix: narrow the attribution to the continuity-points sentence, or say the jump rule is ours.
3. [minor] proof of "Time-dependent coefficients", step 6 (line 157): the displayed Leibniz expansion
   is written $\frac{d}{dt}B^k = \sum_j B^j B' B^{k-1-j}$ without saying that $j$ runs from $0$ to
   $k-1$. It is unambiguous from context but the range should be printed, since the whole point of the
   step is that the $k$ terms cannot be collected.
