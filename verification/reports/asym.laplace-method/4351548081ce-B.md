## Findings

1. [blocking] method, step 6 (line 174): the large parameter $x$ is missing from the exponential
   being expanded. The exponent of the integrand is $x f(t_0+u)$, so the factor left over after the
   Gaussian is $\exp\{x(\tfrac16 f'''u^3 + \tfrac1{24}f''''u^4 + \cdots)\}$, not
   $\exp\{\tfrac16 f'''u^3 + \tfrac1{24}f''''u^4\}$. As displayed the expansion is wrong and the
   conclusion drawn from it in the same sentence does not follow: integrating the displayed
   $\tfrac1{24}f''''u^4$ against $e^{-Axu^2/2}$ with the stated moment formula gives relative order
   $x^{-2}$, not $x^{-1}$. The surrounding prose and the worked example (step 5 of the example,
   where $e^{x\ln(1-4u^2)} = e^{-4xu^2}(1-8xu^4+\cdots)$ correctly carries the $x$) show the $x$ is
   simply dropped from the display.
   Fix: write $\exp\{x(\tfrac16f'''u^3+\tfrac1{24}f''''u^4)\} = 1 + \tfrac{x}{6}f'''u^3 +
   \tfrac{x}{24}f''''u^4 + \tfrac{x^2}{72}(f''')^2u^6 + \cdots$. With the $x$ restored both the
   $u^4$ term ($x\cdot x^{-2}$) and the $u^6$ term ($x^2\cdot x^{-3}$) give relative order $1/x$,
   which is what the sentence then asserts.

2. [blocking] definition (line 41): "Hassani and Grimmett and Stirzaker both call the complex-plane
   version the method of steepest descent" misreports Grimmett and Stirzaker. Their Exercise 5.9.6
   (p. 216) applies the name "Laplace's method of steepest descent" to a purely real integral
   $\int_0^\infty e^{f_n(x)}dx$ with $f_n$ a real differentiable function with a global maximum at
   $a>0$; no contour and no complex plane appear there. The cited page therefore contradicts the
   attribution, and the following clause ("the real case treated here is the one in which no contour
   has to be moved") compounds it by implying their usage excludes the real case.
   Fix: attribute the complex-plane usage to Hassani alone, and say separately that Grimmett and
   Stirzaker use the same name for the real-axis statement -- which is in fact the better support
   for the sentence's point that the two names overlap.

3. [minor] statement (line 67): the correction series is attributed to Hassani's (12.33), but on
   p. 386 Hassani says of (12.33) that "that infinite sum is in reality inconsistent" (only the
   first power of $t$ was kept in $z(t)$), and replaces it by (12.36) on p. 387. The powers
   $\alpha^{-k-1/2}$ are the same in both, so the structural claim the unit makes is supported, but
   the pointer sends the reader to the series its own author disavows.
   Fix: cite (12.36), or cite (12.33) and add "as corrected in (12.36)".

4. [minor] remark, correction-series bullet (line 150): "$\Phi$ has an expansion in powers of
   $s^{1/2}$ ... so Watson's lemma applies with $\lambda = \tfrac12$ and returns a series in
   $x^{-1/2-n}$" states the conclusion before the sentence that earns it. Watson's lemma as this
   handbook states it (asym.watson-lemma, (W2)) takes an expansion in $t^{n+\lambda-1}$, i.e. in
   $s^{n-1/2}$ for $\lambda=\tfrac12$; a general expansion "in powers of $s^{1/2}$" contains the
   intermediate powers $s^{0}, s^{1}, \ldots$ too, and it is exactly the two-branch cancellation of
   the following sentence that removes them.
   Fix: put the cancellation first, then apply the lemma to the surviving powers $s^{n-1/2}$.
