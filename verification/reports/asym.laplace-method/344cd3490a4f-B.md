## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, bullet on (L2) (line 141): "If $g$ vanishes to order $k$ at $t_0$, the true order is $x^{-(k+1)/2}$." This is false for odd $k$, and it is our own material (the block is `original`). The leading Gaussian moment $\int u^{k}e^{-xAu^{2}/2}du$ vanishes identically for odd $k$, so the first surviving contribution comes from the order-$(k+1)$ term of $g$ (or from the cubic term of $f$) and the true order is $x^{-(k+2)/2}$.
   Failure scenario: take $a=-\infty$, $b=+\infty$, $f(t)=-t^{2}/2$ (so $t_{0}=0$, $A=1$), $g(t)=t$, which vanishes to order $k=1$. The unit predicts order $x^{-1}$. In fact $I(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}te^{-xt^{2}/2}dt=0$ for every $x$ — I confirmed this numerically at $x=10,100,1000$ (value $0$ to 25 digits). Taking $g(t)=t+t^{2}$ instead, again $k=1$, gives $I(x)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,x^{-3/2}$ exactly: $0.079266546$, $0.0025066283$, $7.9266546\times10^{-5}$ at $x=10,100,1000$, matching $x^{-3/2}$ and not the claimed $x^{-1}$ ($0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$).
   Fix: restrict the sentence to even $k$ and state the odd case, e.g. "If $g$ vanishes to order $k$ at $t_0$, the true order is $x^{-(k+1)/2}$ for even $k$; for odd $k$ the leading Gaussian moment vanishes by symmetry and the order is $x^{-(k+2)/2}$ (and can be smaller still when $f$ and $g$ are both even about $t_0$)."

[minor] remark, bullet on (L3) (line 142): "$f(t) = -t^{2}$ replaced by $+t$ for $t > 10$ has none" — the replacement makes $f$ discontinuous at $t=10$ and the phrase "has none" is doing double duty (no global maximum, hence no gap). The conclusion ($I(x)$ diverges) is right. Fix: say "a function equal to $-t^2$ for $t\le10$ and continued so that $f(t)\to+\infty$ as $t\to\infty$ still has a local maximum at $0$ but no global one, and $I(x)$ diverges."
3. [minor] statement (line 61) says the boxed formula is Hassani's (12.34) "where his phase $\theta_1$ is $0$". Hassani's convention fixes $0\le\theta_1<\pi$ with $z=z_0+(t/\sqrt{r_2})e^{i\theta_1}$ and $r_2=|f''(z_0)|/2$; for a real maximum traversed left-to-right on the real axis $\theta_1=0$ is indeed the right branch, but the unit does not say why the other admissible branch is excluded. Fix: add "because the steepest-descent direction at a real maximum is along the real axis in the direction of increasing $t$".
