1. [blocking] :::example, "One step by hand": "in the ratio $1 : 1.5\times10^{-1} : 5.9\times10^{-5}$". The three errors quoted just above are $-1.0905\times10^{-2}$ (Euler), $-7.0774\times10^{-4}$ (Heun) and $-6.4404\times10^{-7}$ (RK4), which I reproduced at 40 digits as $-0.0109055$, $-0.000707739$, $-6.44043\times10^{-7}$. The Heun-to-Euler ratio is $7.07739\times10^{-4}/1.09055\times10^{-2} = 6.49\times10^{-2}$, not $1.5\times10^{-1}$; the RK4-to-Euler ratio is $5.906\times10^{-5}$, which is right. (The reciprocal is $15.4$, so the quoted figure looks like a mis-signed exponent.) Fix: "$1 : 6.5\times10^{-2} : 5.9\times10^{-5}$".
2. [blocking] :::example, "Cost at a fixed accuracy": "To reach an error of $10^{-5}$ at $x = \tfrac12$, the table gives $h \approx 3.2\times10^{-4}$ for Euler ($1560$ steps, $1560$ evaluations of $f$)". From the unit's own table the Euler error constant is $|{-6.40362\times10^{-3}}|/0.003125 = 2.04916$, so $10^{-5}$ needs $h \approx 4.88\times10^{-6}$ and about $102\,460$ steps, not $1560$. (At $h = 3.2\times10^{-4}$ the Euler error is about $6.6\times10^{-4}$, two orders too large.) The Heun and RK4 entries are right: I got $h = 2.451\times10^{-3}$, $204$ steps (printed $2.5\times10^{-3}$, $200$) and $h = 0.0769$, $6.5 \to 7$ steps (printed $0.079$, $7$). The following sentence, "RK4 is some $56$ times cheaper than Euler here", is derived from the wrong figure; with the correct count the factor is about $3660$. Fix: $h \approx 4.9\times10^{-6}$, $\approx 102\,000$ steps and evaluations, and RK4 about $3.7\times10^3$ times cheaper -- which incidentally makes the point far better.
3. [minor] :::example, last paragraph: "it assumes the worst case $|y''| = 9.149$ at every step, whereas $y''$ is below $3$ over the first half of the interval". $y''(x) = 2y^3\cos^2 x - y^2\sin x$ at the exact solution takes the values $2.0000$ at $x=0$, $2.9711$ at $x=0.15$, $3.4240$ at $x=0.2$, $3.9679$ at $x=0.25$ and $9.1492$ at $x=0.5$. So $y''$ is below $3$ only on roughly $[0, 0.152]$, less than a third of the interval, not over the first half (where it reaches $3.97$). The qualitative point survives. Fix: "below $4$ over the first half of the interval", which is true.
