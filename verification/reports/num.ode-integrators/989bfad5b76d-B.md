2. [blocking] method "Running an integrator on a real problem", step 7 (line 236): "the difference of the two answers is about $(2^{p}-1)/2^{p}$ times the error of the *finer* one" — it is $(2^{p}-1)/2^{p}$ times the error of the **coarser** one, equivalently $(2^{p}-1)$ times the error of the finer one. With local error $Ch^{p+1}$: $E_h = Ch^{p+1}$, $E_{h/2} = 2C(h/2)^{p+1} = E_h/2^{p}$, so $A_{h/2}-A_h = E_h - E_{h/2} = E_h(2^{p}-1)/2^{p} = E_{h/2}(2^{p}-1)$. Measured on this unit's own problem ($y'=y^2\cos x$, step from $x=0.2$, $h=0.05$): for Euler, difference/$E_{\text{fine}} = 0.9172$ and difference/$E_{\text{coarse}} = 0.4784$ against $(2^{p}-1)/2^{p} = 0.5$; for RK4, difference/$E_{\text{fine}} = 14.71$ and difference/$E_{\text{coarse}} = 0.9363$ against $0.9375$. As written the recipe under-estimates the error of the finer answer by a factor $2^{p}$ ($16$ for RK4), which then feeds the step-size update in the same sentence. Fix: say "coarser", or state the standard form, error of the finer answer $\approx (A_{h/2}-A_h)/(2^{p}-1)$.
