## Findings
1. [minor] frontmatter summary (line 6): the summary says the unit "proves that the solution operators of such an equation form a contraction semigroup whose generator is densely defined and closed...". Contractivity is a *hypothesis* of the statement block ("Let $\{S(t)\}_{t\ge0}$ be a contraction semigroup..."), not a conclusion; nothing in the unit proves that the solution operators of a general linear evolution equation are contractive, and in general they are not. The body is consistent; only the summary overstates. Fix: reword to "takes the solution operators of such an equation to form a contraction semigroup and proves that its generator is densely defined and closed, with resolvent the Laplace transform of the semigroup".
2. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used" (first bullet): "Contractivity is used exactly once in the proof, in the backward difference quotient of Step 2 ...; and once more in Step 5". This is self-contradictory as written ("exactly once ... and once more"), and the count is wrong either way: Step 4 also uses the uniform bound, in "$\lVert S(s)Au_k - S(s)v\rVert\le\lVert Au_k-v\rVert$ uniformly in $s$", and the proof's own preamble says $\lVert S(t)\rVert\le1$ "is used freely". Fix: list Steps 2, 4 and 5.
3. [minor] remark, last bullet: "by the uniqueness in part 4 this is the semigroup generated by $A$". Part 4's uniqueness is stated for a semigroup with a *given* generator, so applying it presupposes what is being shown. The clean route is part 3: the computed Laplace transform of this $S$ equals $(\lambda I-A)^{-1}$, and by part 3 it also equals $(\lambda I - B)^{-1}$ for $B$ the generator of $S$, whence $B=A$. The conclusion is correct; only the citation of part 4 is loose.
4. [minor] remark, second bullet: "It cannot be dropped from Hille-Yosida: if $A$ is not closed, $(\lambda I-A)^{-1}$ need not be a bounded operator and (HY) does not even make sense as stated." If $\lambda I-A:D(A)\to X$ is a bijection whose inverse is bounded and defined on all of $X$, then $\lambda I-A$, and hence $A$, is automatically closed, so closedness is implied by the resolvent condition rather than independent of it. The unit is internally consistent, because its definition block defines $\rho(A)$ only for closed $A$, but "cannot be dropped" overstates the role of the hypothesis. Fix: say that Evans carries closedness as a standing hypothesis so that $\rho(A)$ is defined, and note that it is in fact forced by the bound.
