## Findings
1. [minor] proof "Deformation and multiply connected regions", Part 2 (line 173) and Part 3 (line
   181): "the region it encloses is simply connected and contained in the region of analyticity, so
   $\oint_\Gamma f\,dz = 0$". With analyticity alone that step is the *quoted* homology form of the
   theorem (or else it needs the continuous-partials hypothesis of the Green's-theorem proof, since
   $\Gamma$ is a simple closed contour whose inside lies in the region of analyticity). Part 1 is
   scrupulous about saying which version it leans on; Parts 2 and 3 are silent. Fix: name the
   version used, as Part 1 does — the Green's proof suffices here if $f'$ is assumed continuous.
2. [minor] statement "Deformation of contours...", part 1 (line 56): the unit puts "The region
   enclosed between them" in quotation marks as "the wording of Riley, Hobson and Bence"; p. 850
   actually reads "the region enclosed by the two paths". The substance is identical. Fix: quote it
   as printed, or drop the quotation marks.
3. [minor] proof, Part 2 (line 173): "Now let $\lambda$ and $\lambda'$ approach each other. Their
   contributions tend to the integrals of $f$ along the same arc in opposite directions, which
   cancel" is the source's picture rather than an estimate. Given how carefully the rest of the unit
   separates proved from quoted, a clause saying that the limit is taken from Arfken's figure (or an
   appeal to uniform continuity of $f$ on a compact neighbourhood of the cut) would match.

## Checked
Goursat's proof verified step by step: the cancellation of interior edges under bisection; the
quarter-choice giving $|\eta(R_n)| \ge 4^{-n}|\eta(R)|$; $d_n = 2^{-n}d$, $L_n = 2^{-n}L$; the
nested-compact-sets step (which is what prereq `found.compactness` is for); the use of
differentiability at the single point $z_*$; the vanishing of the affine part via part 5 of
`complex.contour-integrals`; and the final comparison of the two $4^{-n}$ rates, which gives
$|\eta(R)| \le \varepsilon dL$ for every $\varepsilon$. The disk proof: I checked the geometric claim
that a disk contains the whole axis-parallel rectangle determined by its centre and any of its
points ($(x'-x_0)^2 + (y'-y_0)^2 \le |z-z_0|^2$), both staircase derivatives, $F_x = f = -iF_y$, and
that $F_x$, $F_y$ are continuous so the sufficiency half of `complex.cauchy-riemann` applies. The
Green's-theorem proof: checked the split into $\oint(u\,dx - v\,dy) + i\oint(v\,dx + u\,dy)$ and
that $P = u, Q = -v$ and $P = v, Q = u$ give integrands $-v_x - u_y$ and $u_x - v_y$, both zero by
the Cauchy-Riemann equations; the $A \subseteq \Omega$ gap is explicitly flagged in the unit and
offered as an alternative hypothesis, which I consider adequate. Compared with the printed sources
(`tools/corpus/pages.mjs`): Arfken p. 479 defines simply connected by contractibility, as the unit
says; Ahlfors Definition 1 and Theorem 14, p. 139 give the complement-in-the-extended-plane
definition and its winding-number equivalent, and Definition 2 with Theorem 15 and Corollary 1,
p. 141 are the homology form the note quotes — all four citations check out, as do Ahlfors Theorem 2,
pp. 109–111 (bisection) and Theorem 4, p. 113 (staircase from the centre). RHB Eq. (24.40), p. 849
indeed states the theorem with "$f'(z)$ is continuous" and p. 850 records that analyticity within
and on $C$ suffices but the proof is longer; Arfken's footnote 3, p. 488 flags the same point. So
the unit's account of what each book assumes is faithful. Remark checked: $1/z$ on
$\{0<|z|<2\}$ with $\oint_{|z|=1}dz/z = 2\pi i$; the claim that the unit circle lies in no simply
connected subregion of that annulus on which $1/z$ is analytic (a contractible loop would have
winding number $0$ about the origin, but it is $1$); and the orientation bookkeeping in part 3.
Numbers: $\oint_S dz/z$ over the square with vertices $\pm2\pm2i$ computed by direct quadrature
along the four sides gives $6.2831853071i$ against $2\pi i = 6.2831853072i$, confirming the stated
six digits, and the same integral over the square $3\pm i$, $5\pm i$ comes out $1.5\times10^{-12}i$,
i.e. zero. The two-hole example checks out: the partial fractions are right, $\sigma_\pm$ are
mutually exterior inside $|z|=3$, each contributes $\pm\pi i$, the total is $0$, and the $ML$
consistency check $2\pi R/(R^2-1) \to 0$ with $R$-independence by part 2 is a correct argument.

`vcalc.stokes-theorem` and `found.connectedness-homotopy` are all used; `complex.winding-number` is
forward-referenced but only for results the unit explicitly quotes with a book citation.
