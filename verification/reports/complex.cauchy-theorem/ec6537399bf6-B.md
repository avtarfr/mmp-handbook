## Findings

1. [minor] proof "Deformation and multiply connected regions", part 1 (line ~185): "Let $\gamma$ be $\gamma_1$ followed by the reverse of $\gamma_2$: a closed path. The region it encloses lies in $\Omega$ by hypothesis, so the Cauchy-Goursat theorem applies to it." The Cauchy–Goursat statement proved and quoted above is about a closed path in a *simply connected region*, and the $\Omega$ of this statement block is an arbitrary region. What is actually being used is the quoted general (homology) form, or else a simply connected open subset of $\Omega$ containing $\gamma$ and its interior. The `note` block is admirably explicit about which cases are proved and which are quoted, so nothing is hidden, but this proof should say which of the two it is invoking. Fix: add "the closed region it bounds lies in $\Omega$, so $\gamma$ is homologous to zero in $\Omega$ and the general form quoted in the note applies" (or thicken the enclosed region to a simply connected neighbourhood).

2. [minor] statement "Deformation of contours and multiply connected regions", part 1: "the region enclosed between them lies in $\Omega$" is not defined when $\gamma_1$ followed by $-\gamma_2$ is not a simple closed curve. RHB p. 850 state it the same way, so this is inherited, not invented; but one clause ("assume $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ meet only at their endpoints") would make it precise.

3. [minor] `idea` block, Goursat paragraph: "Near $z_*$ the function is its own affine approximation up to an error $o(\lvert z-z_*\rvert)$". The proof uses the $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ form, which is the same thing, but the $o(\cdot)$ notation is used nowhere else in the unit; spelling it out would match the proof's Step 3.
