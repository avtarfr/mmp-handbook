- Counterexample search. The two statements were tested against the standard traps. Simply connectedness: $1/z$ on $0<\lvert z\rvert<2$ with $\oint_{\lvert z\rvert=1}dz/z = 2\pi i$ is a genuine counterexample to dropping it, and the remark's second bullet correctly observes that the circle $\lvert z\rvert=1$ lies in no simply connected subregion of that annulus on which $1/z$ is analytic. Deformation across a non-analyticity: the remark's "which precludes moving the contour across a point where $f$ fails to be analytic" is the needed caveat and is present. Orientation in part 3: with every contour positively oriented the inner integrals are added, and the remark's last bullet correctly notes the sign flip when the $\sigma_k$ are read as parts of one boundary. Goursat's proof: checked the two competing rates ($\lvert\eta(R_n)\rvert\ge4^{-n}\lvert\eta(R)\rvert$ against $\varepsilon d_nL_n = \varepsilon 4^{-n}dL$) — they cancel exactly, so the conclusion $\lvert\eta(R)\rvert\le\varepsilon dL$ for every $\varepsilon$ is right; the choice of $\delta$ depends on $\varepsilon$ but $n$ is chosen after $\delta$, so there is no circularity. Disk proof: the assertion that a disk contains the whole axis-parallel rectangle determined by its centre and any of its points is true, since every point $\zeta$ of that rectangle has $\lvert\zeta-z_0\rvert\le\lvert z-z_0\rvert$. No counterexample found.
