[minor] example, part (c) (line 232): "$\gamma$ is homotopic in $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a\}$ to the circle $\lvert z-a\rvert = 1/2$ traversed once counterclockwise, by the straight-line homotopy from the square to that circle, which stays in the region between them and so avoids $a$." The parenthetical justification does not hold for an arbitrary pairing of parametrisations: the parameter-wise segment from a square point $p$ to a circle point $q$ enters the disc $\lvert z-a\rvert<1/2$ (so it does not stay in the region between the curves), and if $q$ happens to be the point of the circle diametrically opposite $p$ as seen from $a$, the segment passes through $a$ itself. What does work is the radial homotopy centred at $a$ (move each point along its ray from $a$), whose track lies in the closed region between the two curves and stays at distance at least $1/2$ from $a$. The conclusion $n(\gamma,a)=1$ is right either way. Fix: "by the radial homotopy centred at $a$, which moves each point along its ray from $a$ and so stays in the region between the two curves".
