## Findings
1. [blocking] example "Green's theorem: the area inside an astroid", step 4 (line 205): the sanity check is wrong in both of its claims, and its arithmetic conclusion is false.

   The text says the astroid region "contains the square with vertices $(\pm a, 0), (0,\pm a)$, of area $2a^2$", and concludes that "$3\pi/8 = 1.17810$ lies between $2$ and $4$ — closer to the lower bound, as the four cusps make the region concave."

   The containment runs the other way. That square is $\{|x|+|y|\le a\}$. Its boundary point $(a/2, a/2)$ satisfies $(a/2)^{2/3} + (a/2)^{2/3} = 2^{1/3}a^{2/3} \approx 1.2599\,a^{2/3} > a^{2/3}$, so it lies **outside** the astroid $x^{2/3}+y^{2/3} = a^{2/3}$. Along the diagonal the astroid boundary is at $(2^{-3/2}a, 2^{-3/2}a) \approx (0.3536a, 0.3536a)$ while the square's is at $(0.5a, 0.5a)$: the astroid is inside the square, not the reverse. And the number does not work either — $3\pi/8 = 1.17810$ is **less** than $2$, so it cannot lie between $2$ and $4$, and "closer to the lower bound" is meaningless for a value below it. The only claim in the step that survives is the upper bound $4a^2$ from the circumscribing square $[-a,a]^2$.

   The area itself, $3\pi a^2/8$, and the value $3\pi/2 = 4.71239$ for $a=2$, are both correct — I verified the whole computation symbolically — so the defect is confined to the check. Fix: reverse the containment and use it as an upper bound, e.g. "the astroid lies inside the square $[-a,a]^2$ of area $4a^2$ and inside the diamond $|x|+|y|\le a$ of area $2a^2$ (its diagonal boundary point $(2^{-3/2}a,2^{-3/2}a)$ is well inside the diamond's $(a/2,a/2)$), and $3\pi/8 = 1.17810 < 2$, which is consistent — the four cusps make the region markedly smaller than either square."
