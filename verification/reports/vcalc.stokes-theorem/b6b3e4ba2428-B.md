## Findings

1. [minor] statement[Stokes' theorem], "Surface independence" bullet (line 53): two wording defects. (a) "provided $\mathbf{F}$ is $C^1$ on one open set containing *both* of them" puts the emphasis on a distinction that cannot occur: if $\mathbf{F}$ is $C^1$ on an open $U_1 \supseteq S_1$ and on an open $U_2 \supseteq S_2$, then $U_1\cup U_2$ is an open set containing both, so the italicised condition is equivalent to "$C^1$ on a neighbourhood of each". (b) The forward pointer says the last remark "exhibits a field, and two surfaces spanning one circle, for which it fails and the two fluxes are not even both defined"; the remark exhibits one field and one circle and asserts that *every* surface spanning that circle meets the $z$-axis — it names no two surfaces, and the failure is that no spanning surface has a defined flux at all. Fix: write the hypothesis as "$C^1$ on a neighbourhood of each of them", and reword the pointer to "exhibits a field and a circle for which no spanning surface has a defined flux".

2. [minor] proof[The two companion identities] (line 187) and statement[Stokes' theorem] (line 62): the route used for the scalar identity (set $\mathbf{F} = \varphi\mathbf{a}$ with $\mathbf{a}$ constant, expand $\nabla\times(\varphi\mathbf{a}) = \nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{a}$, move the flux side through the scalar triple product, strip $\mathbf{a}$) is carried out by Riley, Hobson and Bence on p. 407, §11.9.1, where both identities appear as their Eqs. (11.24)–(11.25) and (11.24) is proved from (11.26). That page is inside the frontmatter range for rhb-2006 but is not in this block's `src`, and the text at line 62 ("Arfken states these without derivation ... the third proof below carries that technique out") is true of Arfken yet leaves the impression that the derivation is unsourced. Our Step 1/Step 4/Step 5 index work is genuinely ours and the vector identity is not derived by either source. Fix: add `rhb-2006:§11.9.1, Eqs. (11.24)–(11.26), p. 407` to the block's `src` and say in a clause that RHB carry out the scalar case, the vector one being ours.

3. [minor] statement[Stokes' theorem], "Closed surfaces" bullet (line 52): presented as an immediate consequence, but the theorem as stated in this unit hypothesises a perimeter curve $C = \partial S$ and a traversal, which a closed $S$ does not have, so the stated theorem does not literally apply to it. The honest one-line route is to cut $S$ along a closed curve into two pieces, orient both by the outward normal (their common boundary is then traversed oppositely) and add. Arfken's Eq. (3.93), p. 167, takes the same shortcut, so this is inherited; it costs one clause to close.

4. [minor] note[What the proof above covers...] (line 123): "suppose $R = \bigcup_{k=1}^N R_k$ were a finite decomposition into two-way graph regions" mixes an open $R$ (the statement's bounded plane domain, and the $R$ of this paragraph) with closed pieces of the form $\{c_k\le y\le d_k,\ a_k(y)\le x\le b_k(y)\}$, as Step 5 of the proof also does; a finite union of closed sets cannot equal a nonempty bounded open set, so the sentence as written is true for a trivial reason beside the intended one. The argument is unaffected — it applies verbatim with "cover" in place of "equal", and also to $\overline R = \bigcup_k R_k$, since the sets $\{x\in I_j,\ 2 < y \le f(x)\}$ are still pairwise disjoint and relatively open in $\overline R\cap\{y>2\}$ — so only the phrasing needs fixing ("cover $R$", or state it for closures).

5. [minor] proof[Stokes' theorem for a $C^2$ patch] (line 135): "it carries the interior of $D$ onto the points of $S$ with a neighbourhood in $S$ and $\partial D$ onto the rest". As written this says nothing, since every point of $S$ has a neighbourhood in $S$ (namely $S$). The intended notion is the manifold interior: points with a neighbourhood in $S$ homeomorphic to an open disc. Fix: say that, or appeal directly to invariance of the boundary under a homeomorphism of compact surfaces-with-boundary.

6. [minor] remark, "$\mathbf{r}\in C^2$, used twice" bullet (line 239): "What is unproved here is the smoothness of the surface and nothing else" is narrower than it reads. Two further links in the chain are not proved in the unit: Green's theorem for a general bounded plane domain (taken on authority in the note at line 115, and needed as soon as the parameter domain $D$ is not a finite union of two-way graph regions — the proof at line 133 restricts $D$ to exactly that case), and the per-patch injectivity the proof adds at line 133 but the statement does not require. The sentence is accurate if "here" means the gap between the statement's *surface* hypotheses and the proof's, which is how the following clause reads it; a four-word qualifier ("beyond the Green's-theorem gap recorded above") would remove the collision with the note.

7. [minor] statement[Stokes' theorem] (line 50): "Riley, Hobson and Bence note that the theorem holds for simply and multiply connected open surfaces alike, provided they are two-sided; for a multiply connected $S$ the right side becomes a sum over all the boundary curves, each taken in the sense attached to $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$." The first clause is on p. 406; the second is not — RHB state the sum-over-all-boundaries rule only for Green's theorem (p. 385 with Fig. 11.4 on p. 386), not for Eq. (11.23). The second clause is correct and consistent with vcalc.surface-integrals, but reads as attributed. Fix: start a new sentence so the reading is plainly ours, or cite p. 385–386 for the analogous rule.

8. [source] rhb-2006, §11.9, Eq. (11.23), p. 406 (and arfken-2013 §3.8, p. 167): both state Stokes' theorem with no smoothness hypothesis on $S$ or on the field, and neither estimates the per-cell error before the mesh limit. The unit reports this faithfully, states the theorem in the sources' generality and says in the remark (line 239) exactly what is proved here, so this is deference, not a defect in our text. Already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md`; no new entry added.

9. The unit supplies that disambiguation at line 44 and says explicitly that it is ours and not Arfken's, which is the right handling. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.
