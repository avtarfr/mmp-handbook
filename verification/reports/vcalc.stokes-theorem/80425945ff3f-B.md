## Findings
1. [minor] proof "Green's theorem", Step 4 (line 107): "A general region with piecewise $C^1$ boundary is cut by horizontal and vertical lines into finitely many pieces of this kind". The finiteness is not justified and is not in general true as stated: a boundary made of finitely many $C^1$ arcs may still have infinitely many horizontal or vertical tangencies (the arc $y = x^3\sin(1/x)$, $y(0)=0$, is $C^1$ and its tangent is horizontal at infinitely many points accumulating at $0$), so the cutting need not terminate. Everything the unit actually uses — the examples, and Step 1 of the Stokes proof, which only needs a parameter domain of the two-way graph form — is unaffected. Fix: state the reduction as the standard one that the handbook does not prove (as the `note` already does for the $C^1$ surface case), or add "whose boundary has finitely many points of horizontal or vertical tangency", which covers every region used here.
2. [minor] application (line 269): "Reversing the sense of the loop reverses both $d\mathbf{r}$ and $d\mathbf{S}$, so the physical prediction is unambiguous — this is Lenz's law". The sign-consistency under reversing the loop is Arfken's "no sign ambiguity" remark (p. 168), not Lenz's law; Lenz's law is the content of the minus sign, that the induced emf drives a current opposing the change of flux. Fix: "— which is Arfken's point that there is no sign ambiguity. The minus sign itself is Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the change in flux."
