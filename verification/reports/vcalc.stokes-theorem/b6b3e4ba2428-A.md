## Findings
1. [minor] note "What the proof above covers, and what is taken on authority" (line 125): the
   displayed identity
   $R_k \cap \{y>2\} = \{\max(c_k,2) < y \le d_k,\ a_k(y)\le x\le b_k(y)\}$
   is false when $c_k > 2$: the slice at height $y = c_k$ belongs to $R_k \cap \{y>2\}$ but is
   excluded by the strict lower bound. The case is not vacuous (a piece may lie entirely above the
   line), and the conclusion is unaffected — the omitted slice $[a_k(c_k),b_k(c_k)]\times\{c_k\}$ is
   connected and meets the left edge, so the same argument gives connectedness of the true set. Fix:
   write the set as $\{(x,y)\in R_k : y>2\}$, whose heights range over the interval
   $[c_k,d_k]\cap(2,\infty)$, and run the slice/left-edge argument over that interval.
2. [minor] same note (line 121): "any two of which are separated by the line $y=2$ in the sense that
   no connected subset of $R\cap\{y>2\}$ meets two of them" is the hinge of the argument and is
   asserted without its reason. (It is true: the $B_j$ are open, pairwise disjoint, and their union
   *is* $R\cap\{y>2\}$ because $f>2$ exactly on $\bigcup_j I_j$, so each $B_j$ is relatively closed
   as well as open there, i.e. they are its connected components.) Fix: add the clause
   "$\bigcup_j B_j = R\cap\{y>2\}$, so each $B_j$, being open, is also relatively closed in it".
   The wording "separated by the line $y=2$" also misdescribes what separates them: they are
   separated from each other above the line, not by it.
3. [minor] proof "Green's theorem" (line 75) and the note (line 123): the open/closed convention
   clashes with the statement. The statement's $R$ is a bounded plane *domain* (hence open, with
   $\overline R = R\cup C$), but the proof sets $R = \{a\le x\le b,\ y_1(x)\le y\le y_2(x)\}$, a
   closed set, and the note then supposes "$R = \bigcup_{k=1}^N R_k$" with closed graph pieces —
   which is impossible for a nonempty open bounded $R$ for the trivial reason that a finite union of
   closed sets is closed, and that would trivialize the very claim the paragraph is at pains to
   prove. The intended reading is plainly up to closure, and the argument survives verbatim with
   $\overline R$ in place of $R$ (with $\overline R\cap\{y>2\} = \bigcup_j\{x\in I_j,\ 2<y\le f(x)\}$
   still a separated family, since $f = 2$ at every endpoint of every $I_j$). Fix: say once, where
   the graph form is introduced, that $R$ there denotes the closed region and the integrals are
   unchanged; and state the note's hypothesis as a decomposition of $\overline R$.
4. [minor] statement "Stokes' theorem", third bullet (line 53): the forward reference does not
   describe the remark it points to. It promises "two surfaces spanning one circle, for which it
   fails and the two fluxes are not even both defined", whereas the remark's fifth bullet exhibits
   one field and one circle for which *every* spanning surface meets the $z$-axis, so no spanning
   surface has a defined flux; its second sentence then changes the circle, not the surface. Fix:
   say that the remark exhibits a field and a circle whose circulation is $2\pi$ while no spanning
   surface has a defined flux at all.
5. [minor] remark, fifth bullet (line 241): "every surface spanning $C$ meets the $z$-axis" carries
   the counterexample and is asserted without justification. It is true, but it is a linking
   statement, not a computation; one clause naming the reason would stop the reader looking for an
   elementary argument.
6. [minor] proof "Stokes' theorem for a $C^2$ patch" (line 135): "A regular injective $\mathbf r$ on
   the compact set $D$ is a homeomorphism of $D$ onto $S$, so it carries the interior of $D$ onto the
   points of $S$ with a neighbourhood in $S$ and $\partial D$ onto the rest." A homeomorphism alone
   gives this only through invariance of the boundary, which is neither a listed prerequisite nor
   undergraduate material. Under the hypotheses in force it is elementary and the proof should say
   so: at an interior parameter, regularity and the inverse function theorem present $S$ locally as a
   graph over a coordinate plane, so that image point has a neighbourhood in $S$; injectivity on the
   compact $D$ then keeps images of boundary parameters out of that neighbourhood.
7. [minor] remark, "$\mathbf r \in C^2$, used twice" bullet (line 239): the bullet now correctly
   names both uses (Step 2 for $P,Q\in C^1$, Step 4 for $\mathbf r_{uv}=\mathbf r_{vu}$), but its
   closing sentence "What is unproved here is the smoothness of the surface and nothing else"
   overstates the coverage. The patch proof also requires $\mathbf r$ to be injective and the
   parameter domain to be one of the restricted class for which Green's theorem is actually proved
   above — and the note two blocks earlier says in terms that the general plane-domain case is taken
   on authority. Fix: "unproved here are the smoothness of the surface and, through Green's theorem,
   the general form of the parameter domain".
8. [minor] statement, companion-identities paragraph (lines 55–62) and proof "The two companion
   identities" (line 187): the two identities are Riley, Hobson and Bence's Eqs. (11.24)–(11.25) as
   well as Arfken's (3.94)–(3.95), and RHB p. 407 — a page this unit already cites — carries the
   arbitrary-constant-vector derivation of the first one out in full, including the step
   $[\nabla\times(\varphi\mathbf c)]\cdot d\mathbf S = \mathbf c\cdot(d\mathbf S\times\nabla\varphi)$

   derivation), but attributing the identities to Arfken alone, and presenting the technique as one
