## Findings

[blocking] proof "Green's theorem", Step 4 (line 107): "A general region with piecewise $C^1$ boundary is cut by horizontal and vertical lines into finitely many pieces of this kind" is asserted without justification, and it is false as stated, so the proof does not reach the theorem as stated (line 29, any bounded plane domain with piecewise $C^1$ boundary). Counterexample: take $f(x) = 2 + x^3\sin(1/x)$ for $x > 0$, $f(0) = 2$, which is $C^1$ on $[0,1]$ since $f'(x) = x\bigl(3x\sin(1/x) - \cos(1/x)\bigr) \to 0$, and let $R$ be the open region under its graph over $(0,1)$. Then $\partial R$ is piecewise $C^1$ (three segments and one $C^1$ arc), but $f'$ changes sign at infinitely many points accumulating at $x = 0$, so for $y$ just below $2$ the slice $\{x : f(x) > y\}$ is a union of infinitely many intervals: $R$ is not of the two-way-graph form, and no *finite* family of horizontal and vertical cuts makes every piece of that form, since every neighbourhood of $(0,2)$ still contains infinitely many oscillations. Note that Riley, Hobson and Bence do not claim this reduction — on p. 385 they only say of the multiply connected extension that "it may further be shown" — so this is our own material. Fix: handle it the way the sibling unit `vcalc.divergence-theorem` handles the same situation. (11.4); or add the hypothesis "with finitely many horizontal and vertical tangencies" to the elementary version.
