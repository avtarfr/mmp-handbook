## Findings
1. [blocking] note "What the proof above covers, and what is taken on authority" (lines 117–121): the justification does not establish the claim it is offered for. The claim is that a bounded plane domain with piecewise $C^1$ boundary "need not admit a finite decomposition of that kind", where "that kind" is the one Step 5 of the Green's-theorem proof covers — *any* finite family $R_1,\dots,R_m$ of two-way graph regions meeting only along arcs of their boundaries. The argument given rules out only decompositions by finitely many vertical and horizontal *cuts* ("Finitely many vertical cuts therefore always leave a piece …, and finitely many horizontal cuts always leave a band …; the cell where the two meet …"). Step 5 nowhere requires the pieces to be cells of a grid, so a reader who grants the whole paragraph still has no reason to believe the claim, and the conclusion drawn from it in the following paragraph ("We therefore take the general statement unproved") rests on nothing. Fix: the claim is true and can be proved for arbitrary decompositions by a slice argument, which should replace the cut argument. With $I_j$ the maximal intervals on which $\sin(1/x) > 0$ (so $f > 2$ exactly on $\bigcup_j I_j$, infinitely many of them), the bumps $B_j = \{x \in I_j,\ 2 < y < f(x)\}$ are infinitely many disjoint nonempty subsets of $R$. For a two-way graph region $R_i \subseteq \overline R$ with slices $[x_1(y), x_2(y)]$, $x_1, x_2$ continuous: at any height $t > 2$ the slice is a connected subset of $\{x : f(x) \ge t\} \subseteq \bigcup_j \overline{I_j}$, and for fixed $t>2$ only finitely many $\overline{I_j}$ meet $\{f \ge t\}$ and they are pairwise separated, so the slice lies in a single $\overline{I_{j(t)}}$; continuity of $x_1$ makes $j(t)$ locally constant, hence constant on the interval of heights $t > 2$ that $R_i$ reaches. So each $R_i$ meets at most one bump above height 2, and finitely many of them cannot cover infinitely many bumps.
2. [minor] statement "Stokes' theorem", third bullet (line 53): the forward reference does not describe the remark it points to. It promises "two surfaces spanning one circle, for which it fails and the two fluxes are not even both defined", but the last remark's fifth bullet (line 235) exhibits no such pair: it says that *every* surface spanning the unit circle meets the $z$-axis, so no spanning surface has a defined flux at all, and its second sentence changes to a different circle rather than a second surface. Fix: say instead that the remark exhibits a field and a circle for which no spanning surface has a defined flux, while the circulation is $2\pi$ — so the conclusion would be contradicted if the hypothesis could be dropped.
3. [minor] proof "Stokes' theorem for a $C^2$ patch" (line 129): "A regular injective $\mathbf{r}$ on the compact set $D$ is a homeomorphism of $D$ onto $S$, so it carries the interior of $D$ onto the points of $S$ with a neighbourhood in $S$ and $\partial D$ onto the rest." A homeomorphism alone gives this only via invariance of the boundary, which is neither in `prereqs` nor undergraduate material. The hypotheses in force make it elementary and the proof should say so: at an interior point of $D$ regularity plus the inverse function theorem represents $S$ locally as a graph over a coordinate plane, so that point of $S$ has a neighbourhood in $S$; and injectivity on the compact $D$ keeps images of boundary parameters off that neighbourhood.
4. [minor] remark, fifth bullet (line 235): "every surface spanning $C$ meets the $z$-axis" is the load-bearing step of the counterexample and is asserted without justification. It is true, but it is a linking/degree statement, not a computation; a sentence naming the reason (a surface spanning a circle that winds once about the axis must meet it) would stop the reader hunting for an elementary argument.
