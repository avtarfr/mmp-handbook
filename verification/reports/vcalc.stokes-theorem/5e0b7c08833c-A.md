## Findings
1. [blocking] note "What the proof above covers, and what is taken on authority" (line 122): the key
   step of the no-finite-decomposition argument is given a reason that does not work. The text says
   "Every horizontal slice of a two-way graph region is a single interval, so each $R_k$ meets at
   most one $B_j$: if it met two, its slice at some height $y > 2$ would be disconnected." The two
   meetings need not occur at the same height, and in fact *no* slice is ever disconnected in that
   scenario: a two-way graph region is $R_k=\{c\le y\le d,\ x_1(y)\le x\le x_2(y)\}$ with $x_1,x_2$
   continuous, so for each $y>2$ its slice is an interval contained in $R$'s slice
   $\{f>y\}\subset\bigcup_j I_j$, hence (being connected) inside a single $I_{j(y)}$ -- a single
   interval, never disconnected. What actually rules out two $B_j$'s is that $j(y)$ cannot change: it
   is locally constant in $y$ (both endpoints move continuously and the $I_j$ are open) on the
   interval $(2,d]$, hence constant. So the conclusion is true but the stated reason is not the
   reason. Fix: use the fact the preceding sentence already sets up. $R_k\cap\{y>2\} =
   \{\max(c,2)<y\le d,\ x_1(y)\le x\le x_2(y)\}$ is *connected*, and $R\cap\{y>2\}$ is the disjoint
   union of the open sets $B_j$, which are therefore its connected components; a connected subset of
   it lies in a single $B_j$. One added clause ("and $R_k\cap\{y>2\}$ is itself connected") closes it.
2. [minor] same note, the sentence beginning "The number of those components grows without bound as
   $\eta \to 0$": $\eta$ is never introduced anywhere in the unit, and "those components" has no
   antecedent (the preceding sentences speak of the $B_j$, not of components indexed by a parameter).
   I reconstructed the intended meaning -- the number of connected components of
   $\{x\in(0,1): f(x) > 2+\eta\}$ -- and the three counts are right (numerically: 2 at
   $\eta=10^{-3}$, 8 at $10^{-5}$, 35 at $10^{-7}$). Beyond the undefined symbol, these two closing
   sentences are the *old* cut-based argument, which only rules out decompositions made by finitely
   many vertical and horizontal cuts and so does not cover arbitrary finite decompositions; now that
   the general $B_j$ argument precedes them they are redundant and invite the reader to think the
   conclusion rests on them. Fix: delete the two sentences, or define $\eta$ and present them
   explicitly as illustration of how the oscillation accumulates, not as part of the argument.
