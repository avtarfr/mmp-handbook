## Findings
1. [minor] :::example[A planar dust], last paragraph of the count: "Below $\varepsilon \approx 10^{-5}$ the count saturates at $10\,077\,696$ ... and including those scales drags the fitted slope down to $1.05$." I reproduced the box count exactly (see below) and the saturation is a little later than stated: at $\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$ the count is $6\,921\,416$ (69% of the point total), at $\varepsilon = 10^{-4.5}$ it is $2\,030\,193$, and full saturation at $10\,077\,696$ is first reached at $\varepsilon = 10^{-5.5}$, consistent with the stage-9 cell side $4^{-9} = 3.81\times10^{-6}$. The $1.05$ is also cutoff-dependent and the text does not say where it stops: extending the fit to $10^{-5}$ gives $1.2681$, to $10^{-5.5}$ gives $1.1951$, to $10^{-6}$ gives $1.0927$, to $10^{-6.5}$ gives $0.9872$. So $1.05$ corresponds to stopping around $10^{-6.2}$ and is not reproducible as written. Fix: "saturates at $10\,077\,696$ once $\varepsilon$ drops below the stage-9 cell size $4^{-9} = 3.8\times10^{-6}$; extending the fit down to $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$ already drags the slope to $1.09$, and to $10^{-6.5}$ to $0.99$".
2. [minor] :::definition[Self-similarity, and three dimensions]: the correlation sum is defined with denominator $n(n-1)$ and numerator the count of *ordered* pairs $(i,j)$ with $i\ne j$ -- consistent as written -- but the preceding sentence says it comes from "averaging over $x$" the pointwise counts $N_x(\varepsilon)$, which would give $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i\frac{N_{x_i}(\varepsilon)}{n-1}$; these are the same thing only because the ordered count is used. One clause making that explicit would keep a reader from recomputing with unordered pairs and getting a factor of two (which does not change the slope, but does change $C$).
3. [minor] :::statement part 4 and :::proof: the uncountability argument says the coding map is "at most $2^D$-to-one (a point lies in at most $2^D$ cubes of a grid)". The parenthetical justifies the bound at one stage; the step from "at most $2^D$ stage-$k$ cubes for each $k$" to "at most $2^D$ words" needs the observation that two distinct words first differ at some finite stage and so give distinct cubes there. True, and one clause long.
4. [minor] :::example: "a dust of points scattered along a bent diagonal with two outlying clumps at each end" -- with the chosen squares $(0,0),(0,3),(1,1),(2,2),(3,0),(3,3)$ there is one outlying square at each end of the diagonal, namely $(0,3)$ and $(3,0)$, plus the diagonal squares $(0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)$. "Two outlying clumps at each end" overcounts; the picture is one off-diagonal clump per corner.
