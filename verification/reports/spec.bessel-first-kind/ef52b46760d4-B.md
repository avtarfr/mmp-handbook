## Findings
1. [blocking] definition, third bullet "Behaviour at the origin" (line 63): "$J_\nu$ is unbounded at $0$ for $\nu < 0$" is false at negative integer order, and it contradicts the bullet immediately above it. The bullet above (line 58) defines the series at $\nu = -n$ by the convention that $1/\Gamma$ vanishes at the poles, concludes $J_{-n}(x) = (-1)^{n}J_n(x)$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and so brings negative integer orders inside the scope of "$\nu < 0$". Counterexample: $J_{-1}(0.001) = -4.99999\times10^{-4}$ (scipy), bounded, and $J_{-1}(0) = 0$; likewise $J_{-2}(0.5) = J_2(0.5) = 0.0306040$. The derivation offered in the bullet also fails exactly there: the claimed leading term $(x/2)^{\nu}/\Gamma(1+\nu)$ is $0$ when $\nu$ is a negative integer, so it is not the leading term. Fix: restrict the clause to "$\nu < 0$ and not an integer" (and, if wanted, add that at negative integer order $J_{-n} = (-1)^{n}J_n$ is regular, which is what the previous bullet shows).
2. [blocking] definition, second bullet heading (line 58): "**It is defined unless $\nu$ is a negative integer.**" is contradicted by its own body, which shows the series *is* defined there (the Gamma poles are "instead *helpful*") and evaluates it to $(-1)^nJ_n$. The unit therefore carries both conventions at once — Strauss p. 283 ("the series is not defined because $\Gamma(s)$ is infinite for negative integers") in the heading, and the $1/\Gamma(\text{pole}) = 0$ convention in the body — without saying which it adopts, and the rest of the unit (the generating-function proof's case $n<0$, line 122; the remark, line 235) relies on the body's convention. Fix: state the convention once, e.g. "**It is defined for every $\nu$, with the usual reading $1/\Gamma = 0$ at the poles.** Arfken Eq. (14.6) and Strauss Eq. (4) exclude $\nu = -1,-2,\dots$; with the pole convention the series still makes sense and reduces to $(-1)^nJ_n$." Then finding 1 follows as a consequence.
3. [minor] remark, last bullet (line 241): "forward recursion amplifies rounding error into a spurious multiple of the growing solution $Y_\nu$" is correct in substance, but $Y_\nu$ is only introduced in a forward reference; a half-clause ("the second solution, :ref[]{to=spec.bessel-second-kind-hankel}") would keep the unit self-contained. No source is cited for the numerical-stability claim, which is `original` and is standard (Miller's algorithm); it is right.
4. [minor] example (a) (line 254): the route $J_{1/2}$ from the series, then $J_{3/2}$ by the three-term recurrence, is the route of RHB's two worked examples (pp. 605 and 612), and the block is labelled `provenance=original`. I did not treat this as a mislabelling, because the Gamma-factor step used here ($\Gamma(\tfrac32+m) = \frac{(2m+1)!}{m!4^{m}}\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}$ in closed form) is not RHB's telescoping, and because everything with a number in it — $J_{5/2}$, the check at $x=3$, parts (b) and (c) — is ours; the frontmatter also credits RHB's role for "the half-integer closed forms". Worth a `src` on the block all the same.
5. [minor] proof "The recurrences from the series" (line 179): the unit's single `idea` block (line 93) describes only the generating function and the derivative route; the series proof opens with one orienting sentence instead. Not a style-guide violation as I read it, but an idea sentence for the series route (where the asymmetry comes from) would match the other proof.
