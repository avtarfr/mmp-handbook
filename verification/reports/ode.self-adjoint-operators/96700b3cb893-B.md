## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Symmetric boundary conditions and self-adjoint problems" (line 161): the aside "although Strauss attaches the word [Robin] to the whole separated family (his Case 4, p. 119)" overstates the source. On p. 119 Strauss lists Robin as Case 4 *alongside* Dirichlet (Case 1) and Neumann (Case 2), and his Robin conditions (§4.3, p. 92, eqs. (1)-(2)) have the form $X' - a_0X = 0$, $X' + a_lX = 0$, which cover Neumann ($a=0$) but cannot produce Dirichlet. So Strauss's "Robin" is the separated family with $\beta_i \neq 0$, not the whole of it. Related: the statement block "Separated conditions are symmetric" is correspondingly a mild generalisation of the cited Strauss §11.4 Exercise 6 ("Show that the Robin BCs are symmetric for any Sturm-Liouville operator", p. 317), since it also admits $\beta_i = 0$. The generalisation is harmless because the proof in this unit is `original` and correct, and §5.3 Case 1 covers Dirichlet anyway. Fix: replace the clause with something like "although Strauss's Robin conditions $X' \pm aX = 0$ already absorb the Neumann case", and note in the statement block that it extends Strauss's Exercise 11.4.6 to $\beta_i = 0$.
