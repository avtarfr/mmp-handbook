## Findings
1. [minor] idea (line 90): the idea block describes the derivation in the opposite order from the proof that follows. It says the two halves "come from integrating by parts twice" and that "Lagrange's identity is then just the differentiated form: the integrated identity holds for every upper limit, so the two sides may be differentiated with respect to it" — that is Stakgold and Holst's route (their (2.5.7) then (2.5.10), p. 167). The proof, however, never integrates by parts: it establishes the pointwise identity first by the product rule (steps 1-3) and gets Green's formula by one application of the fundamental theorem of calculus. Not wrong, but it previews a different argument. Fix: in the last two sentences of the idea, describe the route actually taken ("the pointwise identity comes out of the product rule; integrating it once over $[a,b]$ gives Green's formula"), or add "either order works" and say which the proof uses.
2. [minor] remark, periodic non-example (line 209): the stated value of the surviving boundary term has the opposite sign to this unit's own convention. With $J(u,v)=p(vu'-uv')$ and $f,g$ real the boundary term is $[f'g-fg']_a^b$, and under $X(a)=X(b)$, $X'(a)=2X'(b)$ with $p\equiv 1$ it evaluates to $-\bigl(f'(b)g(b)-f(b)g'(b)\bigr)$, not $f'(b)g(b)-f(b)g'(b)$. The quoted expression is exactly what Strauss displays (§5.3, p. 119), but his Green's second identity (3) carries the opposite sign ($-X_1'X_2+X_1X_2'$), so transplanting his expression into our convention flips it. The conclusion ("generally nonzero") is unaffected and the magnitude is right. Fix: write the value as $-[f'(b)g(b)-f(b)g'(b)]$, or say "equal, up to sign, to ...", noting Strauss's opposite sign convention.
3. [minor] definition, Symmetric boundary conditions (line 164): the list of what makes a Sturm-Liouville problem singular is attributed to Strauss §11.4, p. 316, but goes beyond that page. Strauss's list there is (i) $p$ vanishes at an endpoint, (ii) $p$, $q$ or $m$ becomes infinite at an endpoint, (iii) an endpoint is infinite. The unit adds "$w$ vanishes" and "a coefficient is discontinuous". Both additions are correct and are in the library — they are items 2 and 3 of Hassani's four conditions (§19.2, p. 572) — but Hassani p. 572 is not among this unit's cited pages. Fix: cite Hassani §19.2, p. 572 alongside Strauss for this sentence (and add p. 572 to the hassani-2013 `pages`), or trim the list to Strauss's three items.
4. [minor] method, step 5 (line 243): "If $p$ vanishes at two points, those two points are the natural interval" — the two points are the endpoints of the natural interval, not the interval. Fix: "the interval between them is the natural interval".
