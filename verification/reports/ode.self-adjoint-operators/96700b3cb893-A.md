## Findings

1. [minor] definition "Symmetric boundary conditions and self-adjoint problems" (Separated/Robin bullet): the aside "although Strauss attaches the word to the whole separated family (his Case 4, p. 119)" misdescribes Strauss's usage. His Robin condition (§4.3, eqs. (1)–(2), p. 92) is $X'-a_0X=0$ at the left end and $X'+a_lX=0$ at the right, i.e. the coefficient of $X'$ is normalised to 1, so the family contains Neumann ($a=0$) but not Dirichlet; and in §5.3 he lists Dirichlet and Neumann as Cases 1 and 2, separately from Robin (Case 4). Fix: say that Strauss's "Robin" is the family $X'\pm aX=0$ at each end, which covers Neumann but not Dirichlet, so his Cases 1, 2 and 4 together exhaust the separated family.

2. [minor] statement "Separated conditions are symmetric" (hypotheses): the hypotheses "$Lu=(pu')'+a_0u$ with $p,a_0$ real" are never used. The displayed conclusion $[\,p(vu'-uv')\,]_a^b=0$ follows from the two boundary conditions alone and holds for an arbitrary (even complex) $p$ and with no reference to $a_0$ or to $L$; the proof correctly uses only $(\alpha_i,\beta_i)\neq(0,0)$. Fix: either state it as a lemma about the bracket $p(vu'-uv')$, or add one clause saying that $L$ and the reality of $p,a_0$ are carried along only to connect the bracket to the definition of "symmetric" above (where reality of the coefficients is what makes $L\bar g=\overline{Lg}$). Relatedly, the statement admits $u,v\in C^1$ while $\mathcal M$ in the preceding definition is $C^2$; worth a word so the two are visibly compatible.

3. [minor] front matter, `prereqs` (line 6): `ode.linear-ode-theory` is listed but no result from it is invoked anywhere in the unit — the two proofs use only the product rule, the fundamental theorem of calculus, and the fact that a nonzero linear functional on $\mathbb R^2$ has one-dimensional kernel. Fix: either drop it or cite it where the standing setting (second-order operator with $C^2$ coefficients, $C^2$ solutions on $[a,b]$) is fixed in the first definition.

4. [minor] definition "Formal adjoint and conjunct" (first paragraph): the text attributes the hypothesis "coefficients lie in $C^2[a,b]$" to Stakgold and Holst, but they write $C^2(\mathbb R)$ at §2.5, p. 166 and $C^2(a,b)$ at §3.2, eq. (3.2.17), p. 197 — an open interval in the second place. The closed interval is the right choice for us (Green's formula evaluates $J$ at $a$ and $b$), but it is ours, not theirs. Fix: say "on the closed interval, as the endpoint evaluations below require", rather than presenting $C^2[a,b]$ as the source's hypothesis.
