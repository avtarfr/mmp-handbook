## Findings
1. [blocking] application (line 314): "Legendre's, Chebyshev's, Hermite's and Laguerre's equations are not self-adjoint as written" is false for Legendre and contradicts this unit's own material. In the form the bullet's weight list belongs to — the form whose Sturm-Liouville data the method block reads off from Table 17.1, $(1-x^2)y'' - 2xy' + \ell(\ell+1)y = 0$ — one has $a_2' = -2x = a_1$, so step 1 of the method ("if $a_2' = a_1$, the operator is already formally self-adjoint") passes, and the integrating factor is $h \equiv 1$, which is exactly why the weight is $1$. Hassani p. 572 states this explicitly of the Legendre equation ("is already self-adjoint. Thus $w(x)=1$"), and the method block on line 251 agrees ("Legendre gives $p = 1-x^2$, $w=1$"). The claim is true only of Hassani's *normal* form (Example 14.5.5(a), p. 436), in which the coefficient multiplying $\lambda$ is $1/(1-x^2)$ rather than $1$, so that form is not the one whose weight is being quoted. Fix: exclude Legendre from the list — "Chebyshev's, Hermite's and Laguerre's equations are not self-adjoint as written, and Legendre's already is, with $h\equiv 1$ and weight $1$" — or say that the normal form is meant and that the weight $1$ is then $h\rho$ with $\rho = (1-x^2)^{-1}$.
2. [minor] remark, "Sign conventions in the Sturm-Liouville form" (line 221): "Arfken writes $L = D(p_0D) + p_2$ with $L\psi=\lambda\psi$, so that his $p_2$ is $-q$". Once the weight is present (his eqs. (8.14)–(8.19), p. 385–386) the comparison is $p = w p_0$ and $q = -w\,p_2$, so his $p_2$ is $-q/w$; the identification $p_2 = -q$ holds only for $w \equiv 1$. Fix: say "his $p_2$ is $-q$ in the unweighted case, and $-q/w$ once the weight factor is included".
3. [minor] method, step 4 (line 248): "For an eigenvalue problem $Ly = \lambda y$ with no weight in the original equation, take $\rho \equiv 1$". The method's displayed equation is $a_2y''+a_1y'+a_0y+\lambda\rho y = 0$, so $\rho\equiv1$ is the problem $Ly = -\lambda y$, not $Ly = \lambda y$; the sentence silently adopts the opposite $\lambda$ sign that the remark on line 221 warns about. Fix: write $Ly = -\lambda y$, or note that this is Arfken's sign.
4. [minor] statement "Separated conditions are symmetric" (lines 170–182): the hypotheses "$Lu = (pu')' + a_0u$ with $p,a_0$ real" are not used anywhere in the proof. The conclusion $[p(vu'-uv')]_a^b = 0$ follows from the boundary conditions alone, for any $p$ whatever and with no operator in sight. Fix: state it as a fact about the separated conditions and the bracket, mentioning $L$ only as motivation.
5. [minor] definition "Symmetric boundary conditions and self-adjoint problems" (line 166): $q$ and $w$ appear in the four singular conditions before they have been introduced; they are first defined in the method block (line 242). Fix: forward-reference the Sturm-Liouville form, or name them where they are first used.
6. [minor] definition (line 166): "so a bounded jump in $q$ leaves his problem regular while ours is singular". Strauss defines only *singular* (his (i)–(iii), p. 316) and his §11.4 hypotheses already require $q$ continuous, so a $q$ with a bounded jump falls outside his framework rather than sitting inside it as a regular problem. Fix: "is not singular in his sense".
7. [minor] prereqs: `ode.linear-ode-theory` is listed but nothing in the unit uses it; the two proofs need only the product rule, the fundamental theorem of calculus and two-dimensional linear algebra.
