## Findings
1. [minor] definition block: the unit sets $C(j_1j_2J|m_1m_2M)=\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2|J,M\rangle$, while the cited `arfken-2013` Eq. (16.44), p. 789 prints $C=\langle J,M|j_1,m_1;j_2,m_2\rangle$ — the conjugate orientation, and the one that does *not* follow from his own Eq. (16.43) unless the coefficients are real. Arfken establishes reality in the next sentence, so the two agree and the unit's version is the self-consistent one; but a reader checking the cite against the page will see a bra and ket swapped with no comment. Fix: add a half-clause ("the two orders agree because the coefficients are real, as the source notes immediately after its Eq. (16.44)").
2. [minor] method, step 4: "In the row $M=J-1$ there is one more uncoupled state than coupled states built so far." That is true only while $J-1\ge|j_1-j_2|$; below the triangle floor the rows stop growing and there is no leftover direction. Step 5 does say to stop at $|j_1-j_2|$, and step 1 says the row lengths give the answer in advance, so the recipe is not wrong — only the sentence, read alone, is. Fix: "while $J-1\ge|j_1-j_2|$, the row $M=J-1$ has one more uncoupled state than coupled states built so far".
3. [minor] statement 3 and method step 4 state the Condon–Shortley sign rule in two different-looking forms ("$C(j_1j_2J|j_1,J-j_1,J)>0$" and "the coefficient of the state with the largest $m_1$ is positive") without saying they are the same rule. They are: in the row $M=J$ the largest admissible $m_1$ is $\min(j_1,J+j_2)=j_1$, because $J\ge|j_1-j_2|$ gives $J+j_2\ge j_1$, and then $m_2=J-j_1$. Fix: note the identification once, in the method.
