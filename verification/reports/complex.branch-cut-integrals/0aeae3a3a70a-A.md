## Findings
1. [minor] method "Dogbone recipe", step 4 (line 95): "the circle contributes $2\pi i$ times the coefficient of $1/z$, which is the residue at infinity and must be kept". The formula is right, but the name is not: under the standard convention $\operatorname*{Res}_{z=\infty} f = -a_{-1}$, chosen precisely so that $\oint_{\lvert z\rvert = R} f\,dz = -2\pi i \operatorname*{Res}_{\infty} f$, so the quantity named here is the negative of the residue at infinity. The handbook has no unit defining it (`complex.singularities` treats the *type* of singularity at infinity, not a residue there), so a reader who looks the term up elsewhere will pick up the opposite sign. Fix: write "$2\pi i$ times the coefficient of $1/z$ in the expansion at infinity", and either drop the name or state the sign convention.
2. [minor] `prereqs` (line 7): neither listed prerequisite is ever cited in the unit, although both are used throughout -- `complex.residue-theorem` in every example and in keyhole steps 4-5, `complex.branch-points-cuts` for the branch and cut language of the whole unit. Meanwhile `complex.contour-integrals`, which *is* cited (keyhole step 2, dogbone step 4, example 3 step 4, all for the $ML$ estimate), is listed only transitively. Fix: cite the two listed prerequisites where they are used and add `complex.contour-integrals` to the list.
3. [minor] example "Dogbone across a two-branch-point cut", step 1 (line 197): the branch verification checks continuity across $(1,\infty)$ and the sign change across $(-1,1)$, but not continuity across $(-\infty,-1)$, which is equally needed for the claim "the cut is exactly $[-1,1]$". It holds -- for $x < -1$ both $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ pass through $\pi$ and neither jumps -- and one clause would complete the argument.
