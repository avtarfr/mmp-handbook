## Findings
1. [blocking] method "Keyhole recipe", step 2 (line 82) and frontmatter source hassani-2013 (lines 23-25): the unit attributes a specific estimate to Hassani that is not on the cited page. Step 2 says "A logarithm is harmless at the origin because $\rho\ln\rho \to 0$, which is the estimate Hassani uses (p. 371)", and the frontmatter role claims hassani supplies "the vanishing of the small circle because $\rho\ln\rho \to 0$". Printed p. 371 carries Example 12.2.6's Eq. (12.5) and then the single sentence "The contributions from the circles tend to zero" -- no estimate, no $\rho\ln\rho$, no $ML$ bound. (Hassani's set-up is also not a keyhole: Example 12.2.6, which begins on p. 370, uses "the zeroth branch of the logarithmic function, in which $-\pi < \theta < \pi$", so his cut is the negative axis and his contour is two real segments closed by semicircles -- a different contour from the one this unit's role describes as "the logarithm-on-a-cut computation".) The estimate itself is correct and is ours; only the attribution is wrong. Fix: drop the attribution from step 2 and state the estimate as ours ("the small circle contributes at most $2\pi\rho\cdot\rho^{-1}\lvert\ln\rho\rvert$ times a bounded factor"), and reword the hassani role to "cross-check of a logarithmic integral evaluated by a cut-plane contour (Example 12.2.6, pp. 370-371)".
2. [minor] method "Dogbone recipe", step 4 (line 95): "the circle contributes $2\pi i$ times the coefficient of $1/z$, which is the residue at infinity". Under the standard convention the residue at infinity is *minus* that coefficient, $\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty}f = -a_{-1}$, chosen precisely so that $\oint_{\lvert z\rvert=R}f\,dz = 2\pi i\,a_{-1} = -2\pi i\operatorname{Res}_{\infty}f$ and all residues including the one at infinity sum to zero. The operative quantity in the recipe ($2\pi i a_{-1}$) is right; only the name is off by a sign, and the phrase "residue at infinity" occurs nowhere else in the handbook, so nothing defines it. Fix: say "$2\pi i$ times the coefficient of $1/z$ in the expansion of the integrand at infinity (that is, $-2\pi i\operatorname{Res}_{z=\infty}$)", or drop the name.
