## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Keyhole and dogbone contours" / example "Dogbone across a two-branch-point cut" (lines 207-211): the Morera step says "Cut $\gamma$ at its crossings of the diameter $U \cap \mathbb{R}$" and then groups finitely many arcs. This presupposes finitely many crossings, which a piecewise-$C^1$ closed contour in $U$ need not have (an arc following $y = x^3\sin(1/x)$ crosses the diameter infinitely often, with the crossings accumulating), and the Morera statement this appeals to — part 4 of the consequences in `complex.cauchy-integral-formula` — is the all-closed-contours form, whose own remark stresses "Morera needs all closed contours". The conclusion ($w$ analytic across $(1,\infty)$) is correct; only this step's generality is short. Fix: either reduce to a family for which the finiteness is automatic (triangles or axis-parallel rectangles in $U$, noting that vanishing on those already gives analyticity), or add the one line that for a closed contour in $U$ the integral is unchanged when $\gamma$ is replaced by a polygonal approximation in $U$, by uniform continuity of $w$ on $\overline U$.
2. [minor] same block (line 211): "each of those integrals vanishes by Cauchy's theorem ... applied on a slightly shrunken half-disc and passing to the limit, using the uniform continuity of $w$ on the compact $\overline U$" compresses two steps (shrinking a closed contour that may touch the diameter, then the limit) into one clause. Fix: say which shrinking map is used, e.g. $z \mapsto x_0 + (1-\epsilon)(z-x_0)$ composed with a vertical shift off the diameter, and that the integrand converges uniformly.
3. [minor] application, "Dispersion integrals" (line 303): "the jump across the cut, $f(x+i0)-f(x-i0) = 2i\operatorname{Im}f(x+i0)$" holds only for a function real on a real interval of its domain (Schwarz reflection); the hypothesis is not named. Fix: add "for an $f$ real on the real axis off the cut, so that $f(\bar z) = \overline{f(z)}$".
