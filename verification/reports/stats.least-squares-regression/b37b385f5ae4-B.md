1. [minor] example, "The normal matrix" (line ~318): the displayed determinant reads $\Delta = S_0S_2 - S_1^2 = 18\,148\,066\,234 - 17\,557\,500\,103$. With the table's weights taken as exact rationals, $S_1^2 = 17\,557\,500\,102.4997$, which rounds to $\ldots102$, not $\ldots103$; $S_0S_2 = 18\,148\,066\,234.2147$ rounds correctly. Nothing downstream is affected - the quoted $\Delta = 5.90566\times10^8$ is right (exact value $590\,566\,131.715$), and rounding $S_1^2$ up is in fact what makes the displayed integer subtraction land on it. Fix: print $17\,557\,500\,102$ and $\Delta \approx 5.9057\times10^{8}$, or carry one decimal in both terms.
2. [minor] remark, "$R$ of rank $M$" (line ~255): "Riley, Hobson and Bence ... recommend replacing $1,x,x^2,\dots$ by polynomials orthogonal over the data points, which makes $A$ diagonal." What they write (p. 1276) is that $R^{\mathsf T}R$ is then diagonal, and their orthogonality condition is the unweighted $\sum_i h_l(x_i)h_m(x_i) = 0$. In this unit's notation $A = R^{\mathsf T}\mathsf{N}^{-1}R$, which is diagonal only when $\mathsf{N}$ is a multiple of the identity; for a general $\mathsf{N}$ one needs orthogonality in the $\mathsf{N}^{-1}$ inner product. Fix: "which makes $R^{\mathsf T}R$ - and hence $A$, when $\mathsf{N} = \sigma^2 I$ - diagonal", or state the weighted orthogonality condition.
3. [source] remark, "A difference in how the optimality is stated" (line ~265) and rhb-2006 §31.6.1, p. 1273: the unit correctly reports that Riley, Hobson and Bence claim the LS estimators are "unbiased and minimum-variance, irrespective of the probability density function from which the measurement errors are drawn", and correctly observes that the unrestricted form is false (minimum-variance among all unbiased estimators needs normality; without it only the Gauss-Markov, linear-estimator statement survives, and for Cauchy errors the non-linear ML estimator beats LS outright). This is a source overstatement handled exactly as the project asks - the unit states the restricted theorem, proves it, and flags the difference in a remark - so it is not a defect in our text.
