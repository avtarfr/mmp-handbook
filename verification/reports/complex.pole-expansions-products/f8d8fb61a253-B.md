## Findings
1. [minor] remark (line 239): the Liouville bullet writes "$F - G + 1$ and $F - G + \sin 2\pi z$ both have the same poles and singular parts as $F$". Since the unit has just proved $F - G \equiv 0$, the functions named are $1$ and $\sin2\pi z$, which have no poles at all. The intended competitors are $G + 1$ and $G + \sin 2\pi z$ (equivalently, the candidate values $g = 1$, $g = \sin 2\pi z$ for the entire remainder). Fix: replace "$F - G + 1$ and $F - G + \sin 2\pi z$" by "$G + 1$ and $G + \sin 2\pi z$", or phrase it as "$g = 1$ and $g = \sin 2\pi z$ are both consistent with the pole data".
2. [minor] statement "Weierstrass: prescribing the zeros" (line 120) and proof Part D (line 216): both say a constant exponent $h$ serves "precisely when" / "converges precisely when" $\sum_n\lvert a_n\rvert^{-h-1} < \infty$. Ahlfors p. 195-196 asserts only the sufficiency ("provided that $\sum 1/\lvert a_n\rvert^{h+1} < \infty$"), and the displayed estimate (19)-style bound in Part D is an upper bound, so it shows one direction only. The "precisely" is true for *absolute/unconditional* convergence of the product but not for convergence in a chosen order: the nonzero integers have $\sum 1/\lvert n\rvert = \infty$, yet the paired product $\prod_{n\ge1}(1-z^2/n^2)$, which the unit itself displays, converges with no exponential factors. Fix: say "converges absolutely precisely when", or follow Ahlfors and say the condition is what makes the construction work and what the smallest such $h$ is defined by.
3. [minor] proof Part A (line 153): "the bound Stein and Shakarchi isolate on p. 247". Lemma 1.8(iii), $\lvert y\rvert \le 2\lvert x\rvert$, is *stated* on p. 246; only its (two-line) proof is on p. 247. The frontmatter locator "Lemma 1.8 and Proposition 1.9, pp. 246-248" is correct; only the in-text page is slightly off. Fix: "pp. 246-247".
4. [minor] statement "The sine product" (line 128): the second display ends in a comma, but the next line begins a new sentence ("The first product is ..."). Fix: end the display with a full stop.
