## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used, and conventions", third bullet (line 212): "Strict definiteness is used in exactly three places: the clause '$\lVert\cdot\rVert$ is a norm' in part 3, and the two *equality* characterisations in parts 2 and 3." Read as a claim about what the results require, this is correct — Cauchy–Schwarz and the triangle inequality do hold for a positive semi-definite hermitian form. But the remark's job in this handbook is to say which step needs which assumption, and the proof of the part-2 *inequality* now added does use strict definiteness twice: "let $y\neq 0$, whence $(y,y)>0$ by strict positivity" and "Multiplying by $\lVert y\rVert^2>0$" (line 117–125). Under semi-definiteness the case $y\ne 0$ with $(y,y)=0$ falls outside the argument and needs its own line (there $(x,y)=0$ for every $x$, so the inequality is $0\le 0$). Fix: either add that line to the proof, or change the remark to say that the inequality itself needs only $(x,x)\ge 0$ while the route taken here divides by $(y,y)$ and so uses strict definiteness as well.
2. [source] kreyszig-1978, §3.2, Lemma 3.2-1(a), p. 137: Kreyszig's equality condition for the Schwarz inequality is that "$\{x,y\}$ is a linearly dependent set", which read as a set fails at $x=y\ne 0$ (the singleton is independent, yet equality holds). The unit states the pair form in part 2 and flags the difference in its remark, so nothing here fails; this is already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (entries for this unit), so I have not added it again.
