## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Superposition and thinning", Step 4 (line 256): the assertion is **false**. The text says "Events of the form $\{N(B_1)=n_1,\dots,N(B_r)=n_r\}$, for $B_1,\dots,B_r$ a finite partition of $A_i$, are closed under intersection, because two partitions have a common refinement". A common refinement makes the generated *$\sigma$-fields* nest; it does not make these *events* close under intersection, because the intersection of two such events is in general a finite **union** of events of that form on the refinement, not one of them. Explicit counterexample: let $A_i=B_1\cup B_2=B_1'\cup B_2'$ with all four cells of the refinement $C_{jk}=B_j\cap B_k'$ nonempty and of positive mean measure. Put $E=\{N(B_1)=1,N(B_2)=1\}$ and $E'=\{N(B_1')=1,N(B_2')=1\}$. The refinement count vectors $(c_{11},c_{12},c_{21},c_{22})$ consistent with both have $c_{11}+c_{12}=1$, $c_{21}+c_{22}=1$, $c_{11}+c_{21}=1$, $c_{12}+c_{22}=1$, whose solutions are $(1,0,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1,0)$ — two of them. So $E\cap E'$ is the disjoint union of two events of the stated form and is not itself one, and both have positive probability here, so the union is proper. The family is therefore not a $\pi$-system, and the sentence that follows — "Independence on a generating family closed under intersection is independence of the $\sigma$-fields" — is applied to a family that does not satisfy its hypothesis. The conclusion (the configurations $\Pi\cap A_i$ are independent across $i$) is true, but the argument for it as written is not valid, and this is our own addition: Grimmett and Stirzaker's proof of the Colouring theorem (6.13.14), p. 324, stops at independence of $N_g$ and $N_s$ within a single $A$ and never argues independence across disjoint sets, so there is no source to defer to here. Fix: take the $\pi$-system one level up. Either (a) for each finite Borel partition $P$ of $A_i$ let $\mathcal F_P=\sigma(N(B):B\in P)$; refinement makes $P\mapsto\mathcal F_P$ increasing and directed, so $\bigcup_P\mathcal F_P$ is an algebra, hence a genuine $\pi$-system, it generates $\sigma(\Pi\cap A_i)$, and clause 2 applied to a common refinement of the partitions chosen in $A_1,\dots,A_k$ (whose cells are disjoint across $i$ because the $A_i$ are) gives independence on it; or (b) take the family of all finite intersections $\bigcap_{j=1}^r\{N(B_j)=n_j\}$ with $B_j\subseteq A_i$ arbitrary Borel, which is closed under intersection by construction, generates the same $\sigma$-field, and on which independence again follows from clause 2 on a common refinement. Either repair is two sentences; the present one-sentence justification has to go.
