## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Superposition and thinning", Thinning Step 4 (line 256): the claim "Events of the form $\{N(B_1)=n_1,\dots,N(B_r)=n_r\}$, for $B_1,\dots,B_r$ a finite partition of $A_i$, are closed under intersection, because two partitions have a common refinement" is **false**, and with it the pi-system argument that the whole step rests on. Having a common refinement is not enough: the intersection of two such events is in general a *union* of refinement cylinders, not one of them. Explicitly, if $\{B_1,B_2\}$ and $\{C_1,C_2\}$ are two partitions of $A_i$ in general position, write $D_{jk}=B_j\cap C_k$; then
   $$\{N(B_1)=1,N(B_2)=1\}\cap\{N(C_1)=1,N(C_2)=1\}=\{N(D_{11})=1,N(D_{12})=0,N(D_{21})=0,N(D_{22})=1\}\ \cup\ \{N(D_{11})=0,N(D_{12})=1,N(D_{21})=1,N(D_{22})=0\},$$
   because the nonnegative integer matrices $(k_{jk})$ with all row and column sums equal to $1$ are the two permutation matrices. That is a disjoint union of two distinct cylinders and is not itself a cylinder over any partition. So the family named is not a pi-system, and "Independence on a generating family closed under intersection is independence of the sigma-fields" is being applied to a family that does not satisfy its hypothesis. Fix: replace the family by one that is a pi-system by construction, namely
   $$\mathcal{C}_i=\Bigl\{\textstyle\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}\{N(B_j)=n_j\} \;:\; r\ge1,\ B_1,\dots,B_r\in\mathcal{B}^d,\ B_j\subseteq A_i,\ n_j\ge0\Bigr\},$$
   the finite intersections of single-cell count events over *arbitrary* (not necessarily disjoint, not necessarily partitioning) Borel subsets of $A_i$. This is closed under intersection trivially — an intersection of two finite intersections is a finite intersection — and it still generates $\sigma(\Pi\cap A_i)$. The factorisation across $i$ then has to be checked on $\mathcal{C}_i$ rather than asserted: given $E_i\in\mathcal{C}_i$ for each $i$, take the finite Borel partition $\mathcal{P}_i$ of $A_i$ generated by the finitely many $B_j$ appearing in $E_i$; each $E_i$ is a countable disjoint union of cylinders over $\mathcal{P}_i$, the cells of $\mathcal{P}_1,\dots,\mathcal{P}_k$ are pairwise disjoint across $i$, so clause 2 of the region-based definition makes all those cell counts independent, and summing the product over the disjoint decompositions gives $\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_i E_i)=\prod_i\mathbb{P}(E_i)$. Dynkin then upgrades this to independence of the $\sigma$-fields, which is what the rest of step 4 needs. (The same correction should be reflected in the back-reference "the point already used at step 3 of the conditional-property proof": that step, at line 219, is sound as it stands, because it only invokes *uniqueness of a measure determined on the algebra of finite disjoint unions of partition cylinders*, not closure under intersection — but once step 4 is reworded the cross-reference should point at the pi-system actually used.)
