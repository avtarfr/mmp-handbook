## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Superposition and thinning", Thinning Step 4 (lines 289 and 297): the decomposition of a $\pi$-system element into cylinders is asserted to be **finite**, and it is not. Line 289 reads "let $\mathcal{P}_i$ be the finite Borel partition of $A_i$ generated by the cells appearing in $E_i$ ... $E_i$ is a **finite** disjoint union of $\mathcal{P}_i$-cylinders", and the same sentence then says "here the total count over $A_i$ is **not** conditioned, so the union simply runs over all count vectors $(n_D)$ consistent with $E_i$". Those two halves contradict each other. In step (c) of the conditional-property proof, which this step cites as its authority, finiteness came entirely from the constraint $\sum_D n_D = n$ imposed by the conditioning; with that constraint removed the count in any cell of $\mathcal{P}_i$ not contained in some $B_j$ is unconstrained and the union is countably infinite. Concretely: take $E_i = \{N(B) = 1\}$ with $B \subsetneq A_i$ and $\Lambda(A_i\setminus B) > 0$. Then $\mathcal{P}_i = \{B,\ A_i\setminus B\}$ and
   $$E_i = \biguplus_{n\ge0}\{N(B)=1,\ N(A_i\setminus B)=n\},$$
   a countably infinite disjoint union, every term of positive probability since $N(A_i\setminus B)$ is Poisson with parameter $\Lambda(A_i\setminus B) > 0$. So $E_i$ is not a finite disjoint union of $\mathcal{P}_i$-cylinders. Line 297 then rests the interchange of sum and product on the false claim: "the middle step is the distributive law, legitimate **because all the sums are finite**". The sums are infinite, so that justification does not apply.
   The conclusion is nevertheless true and the repair is small, which is why this is a defect of the written argument rather than of the result. Fix, either way round: (a) take $\mathcal{P}_i$ to be the finite Borel partition of $\bigcup_j B_j$ (not of $A_i$) generated by the sets $B_1,\dots,B_r$ appearing in $E_i$ -- the leftover cell $A_i\setminus\bigcup_j B_j$ is then absent, every $n_D$ is bounded by $\max_j m_j$, the union really is finite, and the cells of $\mathcal{P}_1,\dots,\mathcal{P}_k$ are still pairwise disjoint so clause 2 still applies; or (b) keep the partition of $A_i$, say "countable disjoint union", and replace the justification at line 297 by the observation that all terms are non-negative, so the interchange of the countable sums with the finite product is Tonelli (equivalently, absolute convergence of each $\sum_{c_i}\mathbb{P}(c_i) = \mathbb{P}(E_i) \le 1$). Option (a) is the smaller edit and keeps the sentence's "finite".
