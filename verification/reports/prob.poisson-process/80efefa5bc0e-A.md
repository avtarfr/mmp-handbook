## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "The counts and the gaps", part 4 (line 71): part 4 (conditional uniformity) is stated as part of the theorem but never proved. The unit has exactly three proof blocks — part 1, parts 2-3, and superposition/thinning — and none of them establishes the order-statistics property, which the source proves (Theorem (6.8.11), pp. 280-281). Fix: add a proof of part 4 (the conditional density computation of pp. 280-281, rewritten), or demote part 4 to a cited remark and say explicitly that it is quoted without proof.
2. [blocking] proof "Superposition and thinning", Thinning step 1 (line 157): the step invokes "the conditional property of the Poisson process" in its general form — the $n$ points of $\Pi\cap A$ are an independent sample from $Q(B)=\Lambda(B)/\Lambda(A)$ for a non-constant intensity (G&S Theorem (6.13.11), p. 323). That statement is strictly stronger than the unit's part 4, which covers only the homogeneous one-dimensional case with uniform points on $[0,t]$; it is neither stated in the unit nor derivable from anything in `prereqs` (`prob.random-variables`, `prob.expectation-variance`, `prob.generating-functions`). The thinning proof therefore rests on an unstated result. Fix: state the general conditional property as a numbered part of the statement block (with the mean-measure $Q$) and prove it, or prove it inline in step 1 from the partition calculation (13) of p. 323.
3. [blocking] proof "2 and 3: the gaps, and the converse", step 4 (lines 143): the statement of part 3 is that the constructed $N$ *is a Poisson process*, i.e. that it satisfies clauses 1-3 of the definition. The proof derives only the marginal law of $N(t)$; clauses 1-3 are then disposed of in one sentence, "That the resulting process satisfies clauses 1-3 ... is the content of the lack-of-memory property of the exponential distribution ... so the future of $N$ after any fixed time has the same description as the whole of $N$." That is an assertion, not an argument: what is needed is that the residual gap $T_{N(t)+1}-t$ at a *deterministic* time $t$ is exponential($\lambda$) and independent of the arrival pattern on $[0,t]$, and that the subsequent gaps are untouched. The source sets exactly this as an exercise (p. 280, "exercise: use the lack-of-memory property"); our unit promotes it to a proved part of the theorem, so the handbook owns the argument. Fix: prove the residual-lifetime claim (condition on $N(t)=n$ and $T_n$, use $\mathbb{P}(U_n>u+v\mid U_n>u)=e^{-\lambda v}$), then verify clauses 1-3 from it.
4. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first paragraph (line 183): "It is used exactly twice in the proof of part 2, at steps 2 and 3, and nowhere else" is false. Clause 3 is also used, explicitly and essentially, in the proof of part 1, step 1 (line 95: "Clause 3 of the definition lets us replace $\mathbb{P}(N(t+h)=j\mid N(t)=i)$ by the unconditional probability"), and again in Thinning step 4 in its region form. Fix: say that clause 3 is used in the proof of part 1 (to decouple the increment over $(t,t+h]$ from $N(t)$) and twice in the proof of part 2, and drop "nowhere else".
5. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first paragraph (line 183): the counterexample offered for dropping clause 3 is not a counterexample. "A process whose arrivals come in pairs satisfies clause 1, can be arranged to satisfy clause 2 on average" — a paired-arrival process has $\mathbb{P}(N(t+h)=n+2\mid N(t)=n)$ of order $h$, so it violates the $m>1$ line of clause 2 outright; satisfying a clause "on average" is not satisfying it. The unit says so itself two paragraphs later (line 187: "a process that jumps by $2$ at the times of a Poisson process satisfies the other clauses", offered there as the counterexample for the $o(h)$ line), so the same process is used as a counterexample for two different clauses and the two uses contradict each other. Fix: for clause 3 use a process that genuinely satisfies clauses 1 and 2 but has dependent gaps — e.g. a mixed (doubly stochastic) process whose rate is drawn once from a distribution with mean $\lambda$, or a renewal process with unit jumps and non-exponential gaps — and keep the paired-jump process for the $o(h)$ clause only.
6. [minor] definition "Poisson processes on a region" (line 58): "Grimmett and Stirzaker record that for $d=1$ this definition and the previous one describe the same object" is ambiguous, and false under the reading in which "this definition" is the non-homogeneous definition just given: for a genuinely varying $\lambda(x)$, $M(t)=N([0,t])$ does not satisfy the infinitesimal definition, whose clause 2 has a constant rate. The source's equivalence remark (p. 319, after Definition (6.13.1)) is about the constant-intensity process. Fix: "in the constant-intensity case with $d=1$, this definition and the previous one describe the same object".
7. [minor] application "Shot noise, dead time and counting statistics" (line 246): "the recorded process is a renewal process whose gaps are $\tau$ plus an exponential variable, with variance smaller than the mean" — attached to the gaps this is dimensionally incoherent ($\operatorname{var} = \lambda^{-2}$ in $\mathrm{s}^2$, mean $\tau+\lambda^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{s}$). The intended and correct claim is about the counts, as the following sentence confirms. Fix: "...whose gaps are $\tau$ plus an exponential variable; the resulting counts are under-dispersed, with variance below the mean".
8. [minor] frontmatter `pages` and two locators (lines 11-12, 146, 182): the conditional property (6.13.11), used in Thinning step 1, is stated and proved on pp. 323-324, but the locator places §6.13 at "pp. 319-320 and 324-325" and `pages` omits p. 323; the dyadic-box coincidence argument quoted in Superposition step 3 runs onto p. 321, also omitted; Theorem (6.8.17), quoted in the remark, is on p. 282, which `pages` omits although the remark's own `src` says "pp. 281-282". Fix: extend `pages` to 276-282, 286-287, 319-326 and correct the §6.13 locator.
