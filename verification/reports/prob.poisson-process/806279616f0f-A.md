## Findings
1. [blocking] frontmatter `prereqs` (line 7): the proofs use results that are handbook units but are
   not listed. Proof "4: conditional uniformity", Step 1 transforms the joint density of
   $(U_0,\dots,U_{n-1})$ into the joint density of $(T_1,\dots,T_n)$ by a Jacobian; that is the
   multivariate density-transformation rule, which is `prob.joint-distributions` (and
   `found.change-of-variables` behind it), not undergraduate calculus. The same proof's Step 2 uses a
   conditional density given a continuous vector, $\mathbb{P}(N(t)=n,T\in C)=\int_C
   \mathbb{P}(N(t)=n\mid T=t)f_T(t)\,dt$, and proof "2 and 3" Steps 2-3 condition on the null events
   $\{U_0=t_0\}$, $\{U_0=t_0,\dots,U_{n-1}=t_{n-1}\}$; those belong to
   `prob.conditional-expectation`. Note that `prob.multivariate-normal` and
   `prob.conditional-expectation` in this part already list `prob.joint-distributions` for exactly
   this Jacobian step, so the omission is against the handbook's own convention.
   Fix: add `prob.joint-distributions` and `prob.conditional-expectation` to `prereqs` (both are
   earlier units in the same part), and name the density-transformation rule where Step 1 uses it.
2. [minor] proof "1: the counts are Poisson", Step 2 (line ~110): the sum
   $\sum_j p_j'(t)s^j$ is identified with $\partial G/\partial t$ without justifying the
   term-by-term differentiation of the power series. Fix: one clause noting that
   $|p_j'(t)|\le 2\lambda$ and $|s|\le 1$ give local uniform convergence of the differentiated
   series, so the interchange is legitimate. (Grimmett and Stirzaker's Method B, p. 278, makes the
   same step silently, so this is presentation rather than error.)
3. [minor] proof "Superposition and thinning", Thinning Step 4 (line ~259): the statement claims
   $\Gamma$ and $\Sigma$ are *independent* Poisson processes, i.e. that the whole family
   $\{N_g(A_i)\},\{N_s(A_i)\}$ over disjoint $A_i$ is independent. Step 3 gives
   $N_g(A)\perp N_s(A)$ for one $A$, and Step 4 as written only asserts that the green counts in
   disjoint sets are independent "and likewise the scarlet ones", which is weaker than what is
   claimed. The argument of Step 4 does deliver independence of the *pairs*
   $(N_g(A_i),N_s(A_i))$ across disjoint $A_i$, which together with Step 3 gives the full result.
   Fix: say that the pairs, not just the same-colour counts, are independent across disjoint sets.
4. [minor] definition "Poisson processes on a region" (line ~46), clause 1: $\lambda$ is only assumed
   to make $\Lambda$ finite on bounded sets, yet clause 1 asserts a Poisson law with parameter
   $\Lambda(A)$ for *every* $A\in\mathcal{B}^d$, where $\Lambda(A)$ may be $+\infty$. The convention
   $\mathbb{P}(N(A)=\infty)=1$ (Grimmett and Stirzaker, p. 319) appears only at the end of the
   closing remark. Fix: carry the convention into the definition, or restrict clause 1 to bounded
   $A$.
