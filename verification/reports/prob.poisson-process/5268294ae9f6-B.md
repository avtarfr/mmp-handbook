## Checked

- **The new hypothesis in the quoted $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem.** Part (ii) as now stated ($\pi$-systems
  $\mathcal{C}_1,\dots,\mathcal{C}_k$ each containing $\Omega$, full-intersection identity) is the correct
  classical statement: with $\Omega$ available, the identity on any subcollection $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$
  follows from the full one by setting $E_i=\Omega$ for $i\notin S$, which is what the standard proof needs.
  Part (i) needs no $\Omega$, both measures being probability measures.
- **The counterexample in the quoted theorem, recomputed exactly** (fractions, $\Omega=\{1,\dots,8\}$ uniform,
  $A=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $B=\{1,2,3,5\}$, $C=\{1,5,6,7\}$): $\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(B)=\mathbb{P}(C)=1/2$;
  $A\cap B\cap C=\{1\}$ so $\mathbb{P}(A\cap B\cap C)=1/8=(1/2)^3$; $A\cap B=\{1,2,3\}$ so
  $\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)=3/8\neq1/4$. (Also $\mathbb{P}(A\cap C)=1/8$, $\mathbb{P}(B\cap C)=1/4$.) The
  counterexample is correct and does show the hypothesis is load-bearing for $k\ge3$.
- **Does step 4(a) discharge the hypothesis where part (ii) is applied?** Yes. $\emptyset$ is a Borel subset
  of $A$, $N(\emptyset)=\lvert\Pi\cap\emptyset\rvert=0$ identically, so $\{N(\emptyset)=0\}=\Omega$ is the
  member of $\mathcal{C}(A)$ with $r=1$, $B_1=\emptyset$, $m_1=0$. Part (ii) is applied only at step 4 of the
  thinning proof, to $\mathcal{C}(A_1),\dots,\mathcal{C}(A_k)$, and (a) is invoked there "with $A_i$ in place
  of $A$", so each family contains $\Omega$. I also checked that the step's own verification of the
  full-intersection identity survives the degenerate choice $E_i=\Omega$: with $B_{i1}=\emptyset$ the
  partition $\mathcal{P}_i$ of $B_{i1}\cup\cdots$ is empty (the $+$ cell is empty, the all-minus cell is
  discarded), the single qualifying count vector is the empty one, the constraint
  $\sum_{D\subseteq B_{i1}}n_D=m_{i1}=0$ holds as an empty sum, and the one cylinder is $\Omega$ itself — so
  the displayed product identity holds for every choice of $E_i$, including the padded ones, which is exactly
  the hypothesis part (ii) consumes. No circularity: part (ii) is not used in establishing the identity.
- **Clause 2's uniformity against what the proofs need.** Proof 1, step 1 re-reads the relation at base point
  $t-h$ to get the left-hand derivative; proof 2, step 2 transports the $\{N=0\}$ computation to the window
  $(t_0,t_0+t]$. Both need exactly uniformity in the base time, and nothing more (uniformity in $n$ is not
  claimed and not needed: only the finitely many $i\le j$ contribute, since $N$ is non-decreasing).
  I checked the converse direction too: step 7 derives
  $\mathbb{P}(N(t+h)=n+m\mid N(t)=n)=\mathbb{P}(N(h)=m)$, whose right side contains no $t$ or $n$, so the
  strengthened clause 2 — and not only the printed one — is what the converse establishes. The definition and
  the converse are therefore consistent, and the region-based comparison on p. 319 is unaffected, the
  constant-intensity spatial process having stationary increments.
