- Counterexample search. Definitions: "compact as a subset of $X$ iff compact as a space in the subspace topology" verified in both directions from the definition given (a relatively open cover lifts to an ambient open cover and back), so the independence-of-ambient-space claim is right. Total boundedness with centres in $M$ rather than in $E$ is Folland's convention (p. 15) and the unit uses it consistently; "strictly stronger than boundedness" is witnessed by the closed unit ball of $\ell^2$ and holds in the trivial direction by Folland's own estimate on p. 15; the empty set satisfies both definitions vacuously. Permanence properties: item 2 fails without closedness ($(0,1) \subseteq [0,1]$, with $(1/n, 1)$, $n \ge 2$, covering it - I checked that the union over $n \ge 2$ is exactly $(0,1)$ and that $(1/N,1)$ omits $1/(2N)$, computed for $N = 2, 3, 10$: $0.25, 0.1\overline{6}, 0.05$, none of them in the respective interval); item 3 fails without Hausdorff ($\{a\}$ in the trivial topology on $\{a,b\}$ is compact, being finite, and not closed, since $\{b\}$ is not open); item 4 and item 5 checked against Lee A.45(c), (g). Three-way equivalence in a metric space: checked each implication's standard route and the two hypotheses' necessity - $\mathbb{Q} \cap [0,1]$ is closed in $\mathbb{Q}$, bounded and totally bounded but not complete, hence not compact, which is exactly the unit's $\mathbb{Q}$ remark; the decimal truncations of $1/\sqrt 2$ ($7/10$, $707/1000$, $7071/10000$, ...) all lie in $\mathbb{Q} \cap [0,1]$ and converge in $\mathbb{R}$ to $0.70710678\ldots \notin \mathbb{Q}$, so no subsequence converges in $\mathbb{Q}$ - verified. Heine-Borel: $\mathbb{R} = \bigcup_n (-n,n)$ has no finite subcover; $(0,1] \subseteq \bigcup_n (1/n, 2)$ has none either, and the stated witness is right - $1/(2N) \in (0,1]$ and $1/(2N) \notin (1/N, 2)$, computed for $N = 1, 2, 5, 100$ ($0.5$, $0.25$, $0.1$, $0.005$). The $\ell^2$ counterexample: $S = \{e_n\}$ is bounded ($\|e_n\| = 1$), has no accumulation point in $\ell^2$ and so is closed, and no subsequence of $(e_n)$ is Cauchy - I verified the spacing numerically, $\|e_n - e_m\| = 1.4142135623730951 = \sqrt 2$ for $n \neq m$. Extreme value theorem: the "nonempty" hypothesis is exactly what the empty space breaks (finding 3); boundedness and attainment both checked on $[0,1]$, on a two-point discrete space and on $S^1$. Uniform continuity on compact sets: the hypothesis cannot be dropped, witnessed by $1/x$ on $(0,1)$ - for $\varepsilon = 1$ and each $\delta$, the pair $x = \delta$, $y = \delta/2$ has $|x-y| < \delta$ and $|1/x - 1/y| = 1/\delta$, computed as $10^2, 10^4, 10^8$ for $\delta = 10^{-2}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-8}$. Closed map lemma: the Hausdorff counterexample is correct - the identity from $\{a,b\}$ discrete to $\{a,b\}$ trivial is a continuous bijection, $\{a\}$ is closed in the domain and not in the codomain, so the map is neither closed nor a homeomorphism. Finite intersection property: the equivalence is Folland Prop 4.21 and I checked the De Morgan translation both ways.
