1. [minor] proof[The three kernel properties...], Step 1 (line 165): "The middle expression in (9) is Stein and Shakarchi's split; the right-hand one is Strauss's, and it is the sharper of the two." The two are the *same number*: $2ar(1-\cos\theta) = 4ar\sin^2(\theta/2)$ identically. Neither can be sharper than the other. Fix: "and the right-hand one is the more convenient here, because $\sin^2(\theta/2)$ is monotone in $\lvert\theta\rvert$ on $[0,\pi]$".

2. [minor] proof[The three kernel properties...], Step 3, display (11) (line 178): the bound $P(r,\theta)\le (a^2-r^2)/(4ar\sin^2(\delta/2))$ is stated for all $0\le r<a$ (that is the range fixed in (2)), but its right-hand side is undefined at $r=0$. Strauss states his version only "for $r$ sufficiently close to $a$", which is all the proof uses. Fix: attach "for $0 < r < a$" (or "for $r \ge a/2$") to the display.

3. [minor] example[A jump on the boundary circle] (line 319): "By the uniqueness argument of Step 8, applied on the disc minus two boundary points (or simply by checking against the integral), $u = U$." Step 8 as proved needs $w$ continuous on $\overline D$ and zero on all of $C$, which fails here at $\varphi = 0,\pi$; uniqueness for bounded harmonic functions whose boundary values agree off a finite set is a genuine theorem but is not in this unit. Fix: drop the appeal to Step 8 and say instead that the two agree because $U$ is bounded and harmonic with the same boundary limits off two points, citing where that uniqueness is proved — or just present the numerical/closed-form check as the verification.

4. [minor] frontmatter source `ahlfors-1979` (lines 19–20): `pages: "168-170, 243"` stops one page short.
