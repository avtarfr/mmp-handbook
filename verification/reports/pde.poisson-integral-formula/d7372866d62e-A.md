## Findings
1. [minor] example (line 319): "By the uniqueness argument of Step 8, applied on the disc minus two boundary points (or simply by checking against the integral), $u = U$" invokes Step 8 outside its hypotheses. Step 8 needs $w = u_1 - u_2$ continuous on $\overline D$ and zero on all of $C$; here the data have jumps at $z = \pm 1$ and neither $u$ nor $U$ extends continuously there, so the weak maximum principle does not apply as stated (uniqueness for *bounded* harmonic functions with prescribed boundary values off a finite set is a different theorem). The identification is nonetheless correct, and the four-point table confirms it numerically. Fix: replace the appeal with the exact check — $\log\frac{1+z}{1-z} = 2\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}$ gives $U = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{r^{2k+1}\sin((2k+1)\theta)}{2k+1}$, which is term by term the series (7) built from the Fourier coefficients of $h$ ($A_0 = 1$, $B_n = 2/(\pi n)$ for odd $n$, all other coefficients zero).
2. [minor] proof (line 165): "The middle expression in (9) is Stein and Shakarchi's split; the right-hand one is Strauss's, and it is the sharper of the two for what follows" — the two are algebraically identical, since $2ar(1-\cos\theta) = 4ar\sin^2(\theta/2)$, so neither is sharper than the other. Fix: "and it is the more convenient of the two for what follows" (the $\sin^2$ form is what makes Step 3 immediate).
3. [minor] proof (line 102): "The solutions of $\Theta'' + \lambda\Theta = 0$ are periodic with period $2\pi$ exactly when $\lambda = n^2$ with $n = 0,1,2,\dots$" is not literally true at $n = 0$: for $\lambda = 0$ the solutions are $A + B\theta$ and only the constants are periodic. The line that follows ($\Theta = A$ for $n = 0$) already makes the right choice. Fix: "exactly when $\lambda = n^2$ with $n \ge 1$, together with $\lambda = 0$, where the periodic solutions are the constants", which is also how Strauss (p. 166) and Stakgold & Holst (Eq. (8.3.5), p. 492) phrase it.
4. [minor] proof (line 178): the bound (11) divides by $4ar$ and so is vacuous at $r = 0$, although the statement is made "for $0 \le r < a$" in Step 1 and the conclusion of Step 3 is only ever used for $r$ near $a$. Fix: state (11) for $0 < r < a$, or restrict to $a/2 \le r < a$ as Stein & Shakarchi do (p. 56, where they assume $1/2 \le r \le 1$).
5. [minor] frontmatter (line 15): the locator attributes to Stein & Shakarchi's Theorem 5.7 that $u = f * P_r$ "is twice (indeed infinitely) differentiable"; part (i) of that theorem (p. 57) claims only that "$u$ has two continuous derivatives in the unit disc and satisfies $\Delta u = 0$". The parenthetical is not theirs. Fix: drop "(indeed infinitely)" from the locator, or attribute the stronger statement to Strauss p. 170 / Evans Thm 6 instead.
6. [minor] example (line 319) and prereqs (line 7): the example's proof that $U$ is harmonic rests on "the imaginary part of an analytic function is harmonic", and the application block (line 369 area) uses the same fact; neither is in `prereqs`, which lists only `pde.harmonic-functions`, `vcalc.cylindrical-spherical`, `fourier.fourier-series`. Fix: add the complex-analysis unit that carries the Cauchy–Riemann equations, or verify $\nabla^2 U = 0$ directly.
7. [minor] remark (line 212): "a Möbius transformation ... produces (5), with the kernel appearing as the Jacobian $d\theta^*/d\theta$" runs two of Ahlfors's maps together. In his derivation of (63)–(64) (p. 167) the kernel appears as $d\arg\zeta/d\theta$ for the Möbius map $\zeta = S^{-1}(z)$; the Jacobian $d\theta^*/d\theta$ of the chord map $\theta \mapsto \theta^*$ is the separate geometric interpretation of pp. 170–171. The two happen to equal the same kernel, so nothing is false, but they are different maps. Fix: name the Möbius Jacobian in the first clause and keep $d\theta^*/d\theta$ for the picture that follows.
