3. [minor] :::statement, part 5, second bullet (line 78): "$\ell^p$ and $L^p$ for $p\ne2$ are Banach spaces and **not** Hilbert spaces". True for $\ell^p$ and for every $L^p(\mu)$ whose measure algebra contains two disjoint sets of positive finite measure, but false in the degenerate case: if $\mu$ is a single point mass then $L^p(\mu)\cong\mathbb{K}$ with $\lVert\cdot\rVert_p = \lvert\cdot\rvert$, which satisfies the parallelogram law for every $p$. The proof block establishes only the $\ell^p$ half, so the $L^p$ clause is both unproved and over-general. Fix: "$\ell^p$, and $L^p(\mu)$ whenever $\mu$ has two disjoint sets of positive finite measure, for $p\ne2$".
4. [minor] :::proof[Part 5: the test, and its two applications] (line 119): the unit says one checks "in turn that it is continuous in $v$, additive in $u$, and homogeneous". Stakgold and Holst's Exercise 4.5.4 (p. 273) runs (a) continuity in $v$, (b) homogeneity $f(\alpha u,v)=\alpha f(u,v)$, (c) additivity $f(u_1+u_2,v)=f(u_1,v)+f(u_2,v)$. "In turn" therefore misreports the book's order. (The exercise is also stated there for a *real* Banach space, which the unit does not say.) Fix: "continuous in $v$, homogeneous in $u$, and additive in $u$", and note the real-case framing.
