The square root $0.3084904$ that follows is correct. Fix: print $0.0951663$.
2. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 133): the sentence says the existence statements "each fail in a pre-Hilbert space", and then offers as the counterexample that in the finitely supported sequences $S$ the vector $(1/j)$ has no nearest point. That is true but does not show what it is offered for: $(1/j)\notin S$, so this is a non-closed subset of the *complete* space $\ell^2$, not a closed set in a pre-Hilbert space with no nearest point. A counterexample of the advertised kind: in $S$, the kernel $M$ of the bounded functional $y\mapsto\sum_j y_j/j$ is closed in $S$, and no $y\in S\setminus M$ has a nearest point in $M$. Fix: either state the functional example or reword to "where to look is the gap between a dense subspace and its completion".
3. [minor] front matter, `prereqs` (line 7): the completeness proof for finite-dimensional inner product spaces invokes Gram-Schmidt (:ref to linalg.gram-schmidt) and step 3 of the $\ell^2$ proof invokes Minkowski (:ref to measure.lp-spaces); neither is in `prereqs`. Both are arguably standard undergraduate material, so this is not a gap in the mathematics, but the unit's own rule is to name what it relies on. Fix: add linalg.gram-schmidt, or replace the appeal by "take any orthonormal basis, which exists in finite dimensions".
4. [minor] statement part 2, third bullet (line 66): "The polynomials on $[a,b]$, with any of the inner products above" — of the two inner products displayed above only $\int_a^b x\overline y$ applies to polynomials on $[a,b]$. Fix: "with the integral inner product".
