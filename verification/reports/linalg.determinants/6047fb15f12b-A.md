1. [blocking] idea before the properties proof (line 71): it describes a proof the unit does not give. It says "Everything else follows from one lemma" and then "Uniqueness, the product rule and cofactor expansion are all that lemma applied to a well-chosen function". The lemma is Step 6. Step 7 (uniqueness) and Step 11 (product rule) do apply it, but Step 10 (cofactor expansion, part 5) never touches it: it uses Step 1 (linearity in one column) to write $\det A = \sum_i A_{ij}\det Y_{ij}$, then the deletion lemma 10(a) and the interchange/transpose signs of Steps 3 and 5. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used, and conventions", third bullet (line 205): "Dummit and Foote do the opposite, $\det A = \sum_\sigma \varepsilon(\sigma)A_{\sigma(1)1}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)n}$, and Arfken writes the same sum with the Levi-Civita symbol $\varepsilon_{ij\ldots}$ in place of $\operatorname{sgn}\sigma$." The only sum displayed is Dummit and Foote's, so "the same sum" reads as the row-permuted one — which is not Arfken's. Arfken's eq. The sentence is at best ambiguous and on its natural reading misreports the source. Fix: state that Arfken's eq. (2.10) uses the convention adopted here, with $\varepsilon_{ij\ldots}$ in place of $\operatorname{sgn}\sigma$, and that only Dummit and Foote permute the row index; then "all three give the same number, by part 2" applies to the one genuine difference.
3. [minor] definition "Sign of a permutation, and the determinant" (line 41): "For $n=0$ the empty sum convention gives $\det A = 1$." The sum is not empty — $S_0$ has one element — and an empty-sum convention would give $0$. What gives $1$ is the empty *product* over the single (empty) permutation. Fix: "for $n=0$ the sum has the one empty permutation, whose empty product is $1$, so $\det A = 1$."
