## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Determinant as a signed volume", part 2 (line 76): the part is true but neither cited page supports it, and it reaches outside the unit's prerequisites. The block's `src` is `rhb-2006:section 8.9, eq. (8.48), p. 261` and `lee-2013:Ch. 16, p. 401`; RHB p. 261 treats only the 3x3 scalar triple product, and Lee p. 401 says only that `det(v_1, v_2)` is, up to sign, the area of the parallelogram and that "the analogous fact is true in all dimensions" (he defers the proof to Problem 16-1). Neither mentions Lebesgue measure, measurability of `T(E)`, or the volume-scaling identity for an arbitrary measurable set. `lambda_n` is also used without a definition and with no prerequisite supplying it (`prereqs` are the two linear-algebra units), and measure theory is not "standard undergraduate calculus". Fix: either cite a measure-theory source for part 2 (`folland-1999` or `stein-shakarchi-2003` are both in `bibliography/works.yaml`) and add the prerequisite, or label part 2 `original` and state it for the elementary case the unit actually needs (images of boxes), leaving the general change-of-variables scaling to the unit that owns Lebesgue measure.
2. [minor] remark, bullet "Which 'alternating'" (line 85): "We take alternating to mean *vanishing whenever two arguments coincide*, as Dummit and Foote do", cited at `section 11.4, p. 437`. Dummit and Foote's definition (p. 436, one page earlier and outside the frontmatter page range `437-439`) is the *consecutive*-arguments version: "alternating if phi(v_1, ..., v_n) = 0 whenever v_i = v_{i+1} for some i". The any-two-equal condition the unit ascribes to them is their Proposition 22(3), also p. 436, derived from it. The two are equivalent for a multilinear function over any commutative ring, so the mathematics is fine; the attribution and locator are not. Fix: cite `section 11.4, Prop. 22(3), p. 436`, and either say "as Dummit and Foote's Prop. 22(3) shows is equivalent to their definition" or quote their definition as the consecutive one. Add p. 436 to the frontmatter page range.
3. [minor] definition, "Sign of a permutation, and the determinant" (line 37): "For n = 0 the empty sum convention gives det A = 1". The value 1 is right, but it comes from the empty *product*, not an empty sum: `S_0` has exactly one element (the empty bijection), and the product `A_{1 sigma(1)} ... A_{n sigma(n)}` over no factors is 1. An empty sum would give 0. Fix: "For n = 0 the sum has the single empty permutation, whose empty product is 1, so det A = 1."
4. [minor] definition (line 39): the unit defines the **minor** `M_ij` as the submatrix, whereas both sources that are credited with the term define it as that submatrix's determinant - RHB p. 259 ("The minor M_ij ... is the determinant of the (N-1) x (N-1) matrix obtained by removing ...") and Lee p. 634 ("Each determinant det M_ij is called a minor of A"). The unit is internally consistent (`C_ij = (-1)^{i+j} det M_ij` is then the same number as RHB's `C_ij`), so nothing is wrong, but this is exactly the kind of clash the phase file asks a remark to record, and the frontmatter credits RHB with "the terms minor, cofactor". Fix: add a sentence to the conventions remark saying that RHB and Lee call `det M_ij` the minor while we reserve the name for the submatrix.
5. [minor] whole unit (lines 28-90): 792 prose words with math stripped (1153 counting formulas), against the "usually 150 to 450 words plus formulas" guidance in `docs/contributing/phase-concepts.md`.
