## Findings
1. [minor] proof "Matrices represent linear maps faithfully", Part 2 (line 85): the spanning clause reads "$A = \sum_{i,j}A_{ij}$ times the $(i,j)$ one", which is not a sentence and not an equation. Fix: name the basis matrices, e.g. let $E^{(pq)}$ have a single $1$ in position $(p,q)$, and write $A = \sum_{i,j}A_{ij}E^{(ij)}$.
2. [minor] definition "Linear map, kernel, image" (line 28, bullet at line 37): the block's `src` cites lee-2013 at p. 622 only, but the two claims in that bullet — that $\ker T$ and $\operatorname{im}T$ are subspaces, and that $T$ is injective iff $\ker T=\{0\}$ — are Lee's Exercise B.14, on p. 623. The frontmatter `loc` already spans pp. 622–623; the block `src` should too.
3. [minor] idea (line 69): "Only part 3 needs a calculation" understates parts 1 and 2 — the existence half of part 1 constructs $T$ and checks its linearity, and part 2 computes $\dim M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F}) = mn$ from an explicit basis. Fix: "Only part 3 needs a calculation with indices", or name the two short ones.
4. [minor] remark "Conventions that differ between books", last bullet (line 157): "Parts 2–4 of the representation statement use finite bases and fail without them" overshoots for part 3. With infinite (Hamel) bases every $Te_j$ is a finite combination, so the matrix is column-finite, each entry of the product $\sum_i B_{ki}A_{ij}$ is a finite sum, and "composition is multiplication" survives verbatim; what fails is that the matrix is no longer an element of $M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{F})$. Parts 2 and 4 do fail, and the right-shift counterexample given for part 4 is genuine. Fix: say parts 2 and 4 fail, and that part 3 needs reinterpreting because the matrix is then infinite.
