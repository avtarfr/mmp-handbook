## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Characteristic polynomial and algebraic multiplicity" (line 55): "The $\mu_k$ are the eigenvalues listed with repetitions, each $\lambda_j$ occurring $g(\lambda_j)$ times" is a theorem, not part of the definition, and it is asserted with no justification and no forward pointer. It is true — for a diagonalizable $A$ any eigenbasis has exactly $k_j=g(\lambda_j)$ members in $E_{\lambda_j}$, which follows from $k_j\le g(\lambda_j)\le m(\lambda_j)$ and $\sum_jk_j=\sum_jm_j=n$ — but that chain is only available in the second statement's part 2 (proof lines 147-153). Fix: add a pointer ("by part 2 of the second statement below") or move the sentence into that statement.

## Checked
- Counterexample search: tried to break each part. Part 1: the degree argument needs $n\ge1$, which is hypothesised; a non-identity permutation does drop the degree to $\le n-2$, so the leading coefficient is exactly $(-1)^n$ and the collected factorization has $c=1$. Part 2 is used later in the stronger-looking form "a union of eigenspace bases is independent" (proof line 155); the reduction there is legitimate, since the non-zero grouped terms are one eigenvector per distinct eigenvalue with coefficients $1$, and the $p=1$ case of part 2 covers the single-term case. Part 3 tested on non-diagonalizable, nilpotent and degenerate matrices (e.g. $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, $I_n$): determinant and trace identities hold with *algebraic* multiplicities as stated, and would fail with geometric ones, so the statement is correctly worded. Part 4 of the second statement: the $g\le m$ cofactor iteration survives $g=n$ (the last minor is $1\times1$) and $\lambda=\mu$ (it is a polynomial identity). The "sufficient but not necessary" claim in part 3 is witnessed by $I_n$. The remark's two counterexamples check out: $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ has $\chi=\lambda^2+1$, no real eigenvector, eigenvalues $\pm i$; $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has $m=2$, $g=1$. The remark's claim that "repeated" and "degenerate" come apart is exactly realised by the unit's second example, as it says.
- Calculations (sympy, exact): Example 1, $A=\begin{pmatrix}4&1&-1\\2&5&-2\\1&1&2\end{pmatrix}$. The three first-row brackets are $\lambda^2-7\lambda+12$, $6-2\lambda$ (contributing $2\lambda-6$) and $\lambda-3$ (contributing $3-\lambda$), giving $\chi_A=-\lambda^3+11\lambda^2-39\lambda+45$, which sympy factors as $-(\lambda-5)(\lambda-3)^2$ — matches. $\operatorname{tr}A=11=5+3+3$; $\det A=45=5\cdot3\cdot3$, and the independent cofactor computation $4(12)-1(6)-1(-3)$ also gives 45. Eigenspaces: sympy returns $E_5=\operatorname{span}\{(1,2,1)\}$ with multiplicity 1 and $E_3=\operatorname{span}\{(-1,1,0),(1,0,1)\}$ with multiplicity 2 — the unit's vectors exactly. $\det S=2$ confirmed, and $S^{-1}AS=\operatorname{diag}(5,3,3)$ confirmed symbolically; the three products $A(1,2,1)=(5,10,5)$, $A(-1,1,0)=(-3,3,0)$, $A(1,0,1)=(3,0,3)$ are as printed. Example 2, $B$ with the $(2,2)$ entry changed to 6: the changed bracket is $(6-\lambda)(2-\lambda)+2=\lambda^2-8\lambda+14$ and the changed cofactor $\det\begin{pmatrix}2&6-\lambda\\1&1\end{pmatrix}=\lambda-4$, both confirmed; $\chi_B=-\lambda^3+12\lambda^2-45\lambda+54=-(\lambda-6)(\lambda-3)^2$; $\operatorname{tr}B=12=6+3+3$, $\det B=54=6\cdot9$. Eigenspaces: sympy gives $E_6=\operatorname{span}\{(1,3,1)\}$ and $E_3=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,1)\}$ with algebraic multiplicity 2 — so $g(3)=1<2=m(3)$, $\sum_jg=2<3$, defective, exactly as claimed, and $m-g=1$. $B(1,3,1)=(6,18,6)$ and $B(1,0,1)=(3,0,3)$ check. The physics claim in the application also checks: $\ddot u+2\gamma\dot u+\omega_0^2u=0$ has companion matrix with roots $-\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^2-\omega_0^2}$, defective precisely at $\gamma=\omega_0$, solution $(c_1+c_2t)e^{-\gamma t}$.
