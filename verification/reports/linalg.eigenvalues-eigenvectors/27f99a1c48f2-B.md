## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Characteristic polynomial and algebraic multiplicity" (line 55): notation collides inside the block. Four lines earlier $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_p$ are introduced as the *distinct* roots; here $S^{-1} A S = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$ uses the same symbols for the list *with repetitions* (and $p \neq n$ unless every eigenvalue is simple). The meaning is recoverable from "each eigenvalue repeated according to its geometric multiplicity", but the reader has to do it. Fix: write the repeated list with a different symbol, as `linalg.normal-matrices` already does with $\lambda_k'$.
2. [minor] definition "Eigenvalue, eigenvector, eigenspace" (line 37): "$\lambda$ is **simple** when $g(\lambda) = 1$" is faithful to Hassani Def 6.2.3 (p. 173, checked: "An eigenvalue is called simple if its geometric multiplicity is 1"), so it is not a defect. But most of the literature calls an eigenvalue simple when its *algebraic* multiplicity is 1, and under the unit's definition the eigenvalue $\lambda = 1$ of $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ — the very matrix the unit's own remark calls defective — counts as "simple". The Conventions remark (line 87) already separates "degenerate" from "repeated"; it should separate "simple" too. Fix: add one clause to the Conventions remark saying which multiplicity "simple" refers to here and that other texts use the algebraic one.
3. [minor] statement "Spectrum, trace and determinant" (line 58): the src cites `arfken-2013:§6.4, p. 313` for part 3, but p. 313 derives det $=\prod\lambda_i$ and tr $=\sum\lambda_i$ for a *Hermitian* matrix ("we see that the determinant of a Hermitian matrix can be identified as the product of its eigenvalues"), whereas the block states them for a general $n \times n$ complex $A$. The general claim is carried by the other two cites (Hassani Eq. (6.9) p. 174 and RHB Eq. (8.87) p. 280), so nothing is wrong; only the Arfken attribution is broader than the page. Fix: note in the `role`/`src` that Arfken gives the Hermitian case.
