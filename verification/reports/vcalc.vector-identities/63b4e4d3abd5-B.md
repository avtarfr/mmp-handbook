1. [minor] frontmatter `sources`, rhb-2006 `loc` (line 14): the gloss says "Eqs. (10.37)–(10.42), pp. 356–357 (the second-derivative identities, **the three meaningless combinations**, ...)". Riley, Hobson and Bence discard **four** of the nine combinations (p. 355–356: "we may immediately discard four of the nine obvious combinations", named on p. 356 as grad(grad φ), div(div a), curl(div a), grad(curl a)); the number three belongs to the *remaining* combinations, Eqs. (10.39)–(10.41). The body of the unit has this right ("four are meaningless"), so only the metadata misreports the source. Fix: change "the three meaningless combinations" to "the four meaningless combinations" (or "the three remaining combinations, Eqs. (10.39)–(10.41)"). Eq. and page numbers themselves check out.
2. [minor] `proof`, Product rule 6 (line 130): "which is precisely the combination **step 6 of the method** warns must be made before translating". Step 6 of the method is "Check before trusting" (free/dummy index bookkeeping); the warning about combining $a_j\partial_ib_j$ with its partner is in the method's closing paragraph, "When this is not enough". The remark's last bullet (line 158) points to that paragraph correctly, so the proof's pointer is the slip. Fix: "which is precisely the combination the closing paragraph of the method warns must be made before translating."
3. [minor] `example[A solenoidal field from two potentials]`, second paragraph (line 206): "the Clebsch representation $\mathbf{v} = \nabla\phi\times\nabla\psi$ in fluid mechanics". This is our own comment (the block says so) and the name is misapplied: in fluid mechanics the Clebsch representation is $\mathbf{u} = \nabla\phi + \lambda\nabla\mu$, which is not solenoidal in general; the two-potential cross-product form $\nabla\alpha\times\nabla\beta$ for a divergence-free field is usually called the Euler-potential (or Clebsch-coordinate) representation, and is standard in MHD/plasma physics rather than in fluid mechanics. The mathematics of the sentence is right — $\nabla\phi\times\nabla\psi$ is solenoidal and is used to parameterise divergence-free fields by two potentials — only the name and the field of use are wrong. Fix: call it the Euler-potential (two-potential) representation of a solenoidal field, as used for magnetic fields $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\alpha\times\nabla\beta$, and drop "in fluid mechanics" or replace it with the stream-function pair for incompressible flow. The companion claim in the same paragraph (the field is tangent to both families of level surfaces, so its field lines are their intersection curves) is correct where $\nabla\phi\times\nabla\psi\ne\mathbf{0}$.
4. [minor] `application`, third paragraph (line 226): "giving Bernoulli's theorem along streamlines in steady irrotational flow" conflates two different results. Dotting $\nabla(\tfrac12|\mathbf v|^2 + p/\rho + \Phi) = \mathbf v\times\boldsymbol\zeta$ with $\mathbf v$ kills the right-hand side because $\mathbf v\times\boldsymbol\zeta \perp \mathbf v$, so the head is constant *along streamlines* in any steady flow, rotational or not; irrotationality gives the stronger conclusion that the head is the same constant *everywhere*. As written, the weaker conclusion is attributed to the stronger hypothesis. Fix: "constant along streamlines in any steady flow, and constant throughout the region when the flow is also irrotational".
5. [minor] `application` (line 209): the block's `src` is `arfken-2013:§3.6, Example 3.6.2 and Eqs. (3.70)–(3.71), pp. 155–156`, which supports only the first of the three paragraphs. The gauge-freedom paragraph and the fluid-mechanics paragraph are not on those pages and are not attributed to anything. Fix: either add sources for them or mark them as ours, as the preceding example block does with "Two comments of our own".
6. [minor] `statement[Product rules]` (line 45): "$(\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla)$ is the scalar differential operator $a_i\partial_i$ **of** :ref[]{to=vcalc.gradient}" over-attributes: `vcalc.gradient` defines $\nabla$ with $(\nabla)_i = \partial_i$ and the operator $\phi\nabla$, but never $a_i\partial_i$ or $(\mathbf a\cdot\nabla)$. The present unit does define it, in the same sentence, so nothing is missing mathematically. Fix: "the scalar differential operator $a_i\partial_i$ built from the $\nabla$ of :ref[]{to=vcalc.gradient}".
7. [minor] `application`, second paragraph (line 220): "in conservative mechanics, lets an irrotational force field be written as $-\nabla V$" is the converse direction and needs the domain to be simply connected (or the claim to be local). The statement block states that caveat correctly ("the converses hold locally on suitable domains"), so this is only a loose restatement. The companion claim, that $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ "is solved identically by $\mathbf B = \nabla\times\mathbf A$", is the sufficiency direction and is fine as written. Fix: add "locally" or "on a simply connected region".
