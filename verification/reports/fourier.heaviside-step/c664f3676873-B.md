## Findings
1. [minor] definition[Step, sign, ramp and rectangle] ("The step as a limit"): "Hassani's piecewise-linear smoothing gives the rectangular family the same way." The claim is true — it is Hassani's Example 7.3.3, pp. 231–232, where $T_\varepsilon(x-x')$ is the piecewise-linear ramp and $dT_\varepsilon/dx = 1/2\varepsilon$ on $(x'-\varepsilon, x'+\varepsilon)$ — but `hassani-2013` is not among this unit's frontmatter `sources` and the sentence carries no `src` locator, so a reader has no way to check it and the copyright ledger does not see the attribution. Fix: either add `hassani-2013` with `loc: "§7.3, Example 7.3.3, pp. 231–232"` and extend the block's `src`, or drop the sentence (it is already carried, correctly cited, by the table in `fourier.delta-identities`).
2. [minor] definition[Step, sign, ramp and rectangle], the line after the five relations: "each valid for every $x \ne 0$ and hence as an identity of distributions." The exceptional points of the last relation, $\Pi_{a,b}(x) = H(x-a) - H(x-b)$, are $x=a$ and $x=b$, not $x=0$. The conclusion is unaffected (a finite set still has measure zero), but as written the exclusion is the wrong one. Fix: "each valid off the finitely many jump points, and hence as an identity of distributions".
3. [minor] method[Transforming a switched-on signal], step 5: "For a signal of compact support these hold automatically." The moment conditions $\sum_j c_j = 0$, $\sum_j c_j a_j = 0$, ... do follow for a compactly supported piecewise-linear signal, but the reason (that $\widehat{f^{(n)}}$ must vanish to order $n$ because $\hat f$ is finite at $0$) is left implicit in a step whose whole point is the legitimacy of the division. Fix: add the half-sentence "because $f$ then has a finite transform at $\omega=0$, and $\widehat{f^{(n)}} = (i\omega)^n\hat f$".

None of these is wrong mathematics or a wrong number; the unit passes.
