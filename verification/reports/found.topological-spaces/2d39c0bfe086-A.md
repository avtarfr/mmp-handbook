## Findings
1. [minor] application (line 284): "Only the left bound is ever an equality" and "the lower bound is attained exactly at the antipodal pair" both ignore the degenerate pair $p = q$, where $d_a = d_c = 0$ and *both* inequalities are equalities. The next clause gets it right ("strict at every pair of distinct points"), so this is a wording slip rather than a wrong claim. Fix: restrict both sentences to distinct points, e.g. "Among distinct points only the left bound is ever an equality".
2. [minor] definition[Subspace topology] (line 141) and definition[Basis], "Building a topology from a candidate basis" (lines 147-152): four assertions are made without proof - that $K \subseteq S$ is closed in $S$ iff $K = L \cap S$ with $L$ closed in $X$, that bases restrict, that Hausdorffness is inherited, and that conditions 1-2 make the unions of $\mathcal{B}$ a topology with basis $\mathcal{B}$. Each is correct and each is covered by a cited source (Lee's Prop A.17(c), (f), (g), p. 602, which he leaves to his Exercise A.18; Folland's Prop 4.3, p. 115, which he proves), but the unit neither proves them nor says where the proof is, while it does prove the companion claims about the metric topology in Step 7 and Steps 1-5. Fix: either add the two- or three-line arguments, or add a `note` saying these are Lee's Prop A.17(c), (f), (g) and Folland's Prop 4.3 and giving the locators, as `docs/contributing/phase-full.md` requires for a statement left unproved.
3. [minor] definition[Metric space and the metric topology] (line 131): "Folland, p. 15, instead lets $\operatorname{diam}$ take values in $[0,\infty]$" is a gloss - Folland writes $\operatorname{diam} E = \sup\{\rho(x,y) : x,y \in E\}$ and calls $E$ bounded when that is finite, without saying what he intends at $E = \emptyset$. The substantive part of the parenthetical (bounded means finite diameter, and the two definitions agree on nonempty sets) is faithful. Fix: "Folland, p. 15, instead defines $\operatorname{diam}$ by the same supremum for every $E$ and calls $S$ bounded when it is finite, which agrees ...".
