[blocking] remark "Conventions that differ between books", first bullet (line 154), last sentence: "Statements of the form "$U$ is a neighbourhood of each of its points, therefore $U$ is open" do depend on the convention, and in the wide sense they need "$p \in \operatorname{Int} U$" instead." This is false, it is contradicted by the pages the block itself cites, and it contradicts the sentence immediately before it.
   - Under the wide convention, "$U$ is a neighbourhood of $p$" means precisely that $U$ contains an open set containing $p$, i.e. $p \in \operatorname{Int} U$; so the proposed replacement is the same statement, not a repair, and the implication is valid. It is in fact Ahlfors's *definition* of an open set (Ch. 3 §1.2, Definition 2, p. 52 - a page cited by this very block): "A set is open if it is a neighborhood of each of its elements." Folland states the same equivalence in the wide sense on p. 114 ("a set $A$ is open iff it is a neighborhood of itself") and uses it as the key step of his proof of Prop 4.8 on p. 119.
   - Under the unit's narrow convention the implication is also valid, trivially, since "$U$ is a neighbourhood of $p$" then already says $U$ is open.
   - So no statement of that form changes truth value when the convention changes. Worse, the unit's own statement block, item 1 (line 75), is exactly of that form ("openness of $S$ amounts to ... $S$ being a neighbourhood of each point it contains"), so the bullet simultaneously claims that every statement in the unit is true under either reading and that statements of item 1's form are convention-dependent.
   This is the unit's own claim, attributable to no source, so source-authority.md puts it in the "our own material ... must be right" class. Fix: replace the sentence with what is actually true, e.g. "the same words carry different weight under the two conventions: in the wide sense 'a set is a neighbourhood of each of its points' says that every one of its points is interior, which is Ahlfors's definition of *open* (p. 52), while under our convention it is a restatement of 'open'. The implication is valid either way."
