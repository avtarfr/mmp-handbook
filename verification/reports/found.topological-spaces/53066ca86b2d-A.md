## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Conventions that differ between books", first bullet (line 154): the claim 'Statements of the form "$U$ is a neighbourhood of each of its points, therefore $U$ is open" do depend on the convention, and in the wide sense they need "$p \in \operatorname{Int} U$" instead' is false on both counts. In the wide sense (Folland p. 114, Kreyszig p. 19, Ahlfors p. 52, Reed-Simon p. 91) "$U$ is a neighbourhood of $p$" means precisely $p \in \operatorname{Int} U$, so the suggested replacement is the same condition, not a correction; and the implication is true under *both* conventions, since $\forall p \in U : p \in \operatorname{Int} U$ gives $U \subseteq \operatorname{Int} U$, i.e. $U$ open. Folland relies on exactly this inference in his wide convention ("a set $A$ is open iff it is a neighborhood of itself", p. 114, and again in the proof of Prop 4.8, p. 119), and it is Ahlfors's *definition* of an open set (Def 2, p. 52). The genuine convention-dependence lies elsewhere: in the wide sense "$U$ is a neighbourhood of $p$" does **not** imply that $U$ is open, so it is statements that pass from a single neighbourhood to openness that break. Fix: replace the sentence with that correct statement, and correct the preceding sentence's reason as well - the unit's conditions are convention-robust not only because they are of the form "every neighbourhood of $p$ meets $S$" (the isolated-point definition at line 56 and item 1 at line 75 are not of that form) but because each is monotone in the neighbourhood family in the direction that makes enlarging it harmless.
2. [minor] statement "How the derived sets are computed", item 1 (line 75): under this unit's convention (a neighbourhood *is* an open set containing the point, line 49) the second characterisation, "$S$ being a neighbourhood of each point it contains", unfolds to "$S$ is open and contains each of its points", so it is circular and carries no information; it is informative only in the wide convention that the unit does not adopt. Fix: state it as "for each $p \in S$ there is an open $U$ with $p \in U \subseteq S$", which is the content intended and is convention-free.
3. [minor] definition "Metric space and the metric topology" (line 99): $\operatorname{diam} S = \sup\{d(x,y) : x, y \in S\}$ is given for every bounded $S$, including $S = \emptyset$, where the supremum is over the empty set. Lee (p. 598), the cited source, defines the diameter only for a nonempty bounded set. Fix: add "nonempty".
4. [minor] statement "Hausdorff spaces" (line 138): "Items 2 and 3 both use the hypothesis and both fail without it; the remark below gives the counterexample" - the remark (line 145) exhibits the failure of item 3 only. The same trivial topology does break item 2 (for $|X| \ge 2$, $X \setminus \{p\}$ is not open, so $\{p\}$ is not closed), but the unit does not say so. Fix: add that clause to the remark, or point item 2 at the non-Hausdorff example used for the closure/boundary discussion.
