## Findings
1. [blocking] method, step 5 (line 261): "use ... the recurrences to move in $\nu$ (downwards, for the same
   stability reason as for $J_\nu$)". The direction is backwards for $Y_\nu$, and the reason cited is exactly
   why. In the three-term recurrence $f_{\nu+1}=\frac{2\nu}{x}f_\nu-f_{\nu-1}$, $J_\nu$ is the minimal solution
   as $\nu$ grows and $Y_\nu$ the dominant one, so downward recursion is stable for $J$ and *upward* recursion
   is stable for $Y$. Recurring $Y$ downwards contaminates it with a growing multiple of $J$. Demonstrated in
   double precision at $x=1$: starting from exact $Y_{19},Y_{20}$ and recurring downwards gives
   $Y_{10}=-1.81\times10^{18}$ against the true $-1.216\times10^{8}$ (relative error $1.5\times10^{10}$), and
   $Y_{0}=-5.27\times10^{27}$ against the true $0.08826$ — the advice destroys every digit. Recurring upwards
   from exact $Y_{0},Y_{1}$ reproduces $Y_{20}=-4.1140\times10^{22}$ to $2\times10^{-16}$ relative error.
   The unit's own prerequisite says so: spec.bessel-first-kind (line 245) explains that upward recursion for
   $J$ "amplifies rounding error into a spurious multiple of the second solution $Y_\nu$, which grows as $\nu$
   increases instead of decaying". That growth is precisely what makes upward recursion safe for $Y$. Neither
   source cited by the method block (arfken-2013 §14.3 p. 671, §14.6 Example 14.6.1; rhb-2006 §18.5.3 p. 608)
   says anything about recurrence stability, so this sentence is our own and it is wrong. Fix: "upwards in
   $\nu$ — the opposite direction to $J_\nu$, and for the same reason: $Y_\nu$ is the dominant solution of the
   recurrence, so error in it is not amplified going up."
2. [minor] statement "Behaviour at the origin", first bullet (line 83): the premise is stated for $\nu\ge0$
   ("$J_\nu$, which is bounded there for $\nu\ge0$"), but the conclusion drawn from it is "a basis of solutions
   ... for **every** $\nu$, integer or not". For $\nu<0$ both $J_\nu$ and $Y_\nu$ are unbounded at the origin
   and the singularity argument separates nothing. The conclusion is true and is supported both by
   rhb-2006 p. 607 ("the Wronskian of $J_\nu$ and $Y_\nu$ is non-zero for all values of $\nu$") and by the
   unit's own Wronskian statement, which says so two blocks later; only this bullet's "so" overreaches.
   Fix: restrict the bullet to $\nu\ge0$ and point forward to the Wronskian for the general case.
3. [minor] proof, Step 4 (line 187): "(This is an alternative to reading the independence off the logarithm
   in the series, and it does not require the series at all.)" Two sentences earlier the same step justifies
   the limit by the series: "the series above converges locally uniformly in $x$ and is continuous in $\nu$,
   and $\partial_\nu$ of the defining series of $J_\nu$ is again a locally uniformly convergent series."
   As written the step does require a series, just not the logarithm in it. Fix: "does not require reading
   the logarithmic term", or justify the continuity in $\nu$ without the series.
4. [minor] example, part (c) (line 288), `provenance=original`: the setup is Arfken, Weber and Harris's
   Example 14.3.1 (coaxial guide, $E_z = c J_m + d Y_m$, the two perfect-conductor conditions (14.74)-(14.75),
   the vanishing determinant and the cutoff $\omega \ge c\gamma_{mn}$), and the determinant function
   $J_0(z)Y_0(\lambda z)-J_0(\lambda z)Y_0(z)$ is the $g(x)$ of their Exercise 14.3.10. Every number in our
   part (c) is ours (they use $b/a=2$ and give only roots; we use $2.5$ and compute the mode shape and the
   cutoff), and the method block does cite Example 14.3.1, so nothing is passed off as ours that is not; but
   the block would be more honest as `adapted` with that `src`, or with one sentence saying whose setup it is.
