## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Behaviour at the origin, and independence from $J_\nu$" (line 83): the
   unit asserts "For $\nu < 0$ this particular argument separates nothing, since $J_\nu$ is then
   unbounded at the origin as well". This is false at negative integer order, and it contradicts
   the unit's own opening sentence (line 33), which states $J_{-n}=(-1)^nJ_n$ — so $J_{-n}$ is
   bounded at the origin, being $\pm J_n$. Counterexample: $\nu=-1$. Numerically
   $J_{-1}(10^{-3})=-4.9999994\times10^{-4}$ (bounded, and $\to0$), while
   $Y_{-1}(10^{-3})=+636.62217$ (unbounded). At $\nu=-1$ the boundedness argument therefore
   separates $J_\nu$ from $Y_\nu$ perfectly well, contrary to what the bullet says; it is only
   at *non-integer* $\nu<0$ that it fails, where indeed $J_\nu\sim(x/2)^\nu/\Gamma(1+\nu)$
   diverges (e.g. $J_{-3/2}(10^{-3})=-2.5231\times10^4$). The conclusion the sentence is
   supporting — that $\{J_\nu,Y_\nu\}$ is a basis for every $\nu$ by the Wronskian — is correct
   and unaffected. Fix: write "For non-integer $\nu<0$ this particular argument separates
   nothing, since $J_\nu$ is then unbounded at the origin as well (negative integer orders
   reduce to positive ones by $J_{-n}=(-1)^nJ_n$ and $Y_{-n}=(-1)^nY_n$)".

2. [minor] application (line 326): "Example (c) is the design calculation for a coaxial cable".
   There is no block labelled (c). The unit has one example block containing parts (a) and (b)
   and a second example block titled "The cutoff of a coaxial line" with no letter. The
   statement block's forward reference to "Example (a) below" (line 241) is correct, so only
   this one dangles. Fix: letter the coaxial block (c), or refer to it by title.

3. [minor] example "The cutoff of a coaxial line" (line 291): "differs from our determinant at
   $b/a = 2$ only by the nonvanishing factor $-Y_0(2x)Y_0(x)$". The relation itself is right —
   $J_0(2x)/Y_0(2x)-J_0(x)/Y_0(x) = -[J_0(x)Y_0(2x)-J_0(2x)Y_0(x)]/[Y_0(2x)Y_0(x)]$ — but the
   factor does vanish, at every zero of $Y_0$, which is exactly where Arfken's quotient form
   blows up (his Fig. 14.9 shows the poles, and his own Note recommends our product form for
   that reason). Fix: "by the factor $-Y_0(2x)Y_0(x)$, which is non-zero away from the zeros of
   $Y_0$ — the poles visible in Arfken's Fig. 14.9".
