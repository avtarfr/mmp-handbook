## Findings
1. [minor] frontmatter `summary` (line 6): "whose normal frequencies and normal modes are the eigenvalues and $B$-orthonormal eigenvectors of the pencil" identifies the normal *frequencies* with the eigenvalues, but the unit's own statement (line 76, 87) has $\lambda_k$ as the eigenvalue and $\omega_k = \sqrt{\lambda_k}$ as the frequency. The summary understates nothing and overstates nothing, but it does not agree with the statement on this point. Second, "$B$-orthonormal" is risky shorthand here: in the pencil the definite matrix is $B$, which in the mechanical application is the *kinetic* matrix $M$ — while Riley, Hobson and Bence, this unit's main source, use $B$ for the *potential* matrix (their Eq. (9.2)). A reader coming from RHB will read "$B$-orthonormal" as "orthonormal with respect to the stiffness matrix". Fix: "whose squared normal frequencies and normal modes are the eigenvalues and $M$-orthonormal eigenvectors of the pencil $(K, M)$".
2. [minor] statement "Normal frequencies and normal modes", part 4 (line 91): the displayed bound is written $\omega_1^2 \le x^{\mathsf T}Kx / x^{\mathsf T}Mx \le \omega_n^2$, but $\omega_k$ is defined three lines earlier only for the $k$ with $\lambda_k > 0$ ("The positive $\omega_k = \sqrt{\lambda_k}$"), and part 3 of the same statement explicitly allows $\lambda_k \le 0$. So the symbols $\omega_1^2$ and $\omega_n^2$ are undefined exactly in the unstable and marginal cases the statement has just set up. Fix: write $\lambda_1 \le x^{\mathsf T}Kx / x^{\mathsf T}Mx \le \lambda_n$, which is what part 4 of the pencil statement gives, and keep the $\omega$ reading for the stable case.
3. [source] rhb-2006, Ch. 9 introduction, Eq. (9.2), p. 317: RHB assert that "the requirement that the potential is a minimum means that the potential matrix $B$, like the kinetic energy matrix $A$, is real and positive definite". A minimum gives only positive *semi*definiteness, and their own linear triatomic molecule in §9.1 (p. 321) has $\lambda = \omega^2 = 0$ for the bodily-translation mode, so their $B$ is singular there. The unit does not follow the over-claim: its second remark bullet states the semidefinite version, says definiteness is strictly stronger, and cites RHB's own p. 321 example as the standard case where it fails. Correct handling; recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add linalg.normal-modes rhb-2006 "Ch. 9 introduction, Eq. (9.2), p. 317" ...`. Passes.

- Calculations:
  - Degree and leading coefficient (part 1): $\det(A - \lambda B)$ has leading term $\det(-\lambda B) = (-1)^n\lambda^n\det B$, so the leading coefficient is $(-1)^n\det B \neq 0$ — exactly as printed.
  - Part 3 equivalence: substituting $x = B^{-1/2}y$ into $Ax = \lambda Bx$ and multiplying by $B^{-1/2}$ gives $B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2}y = \lambda y$, and $(x^j)^\dagger Bx^k = (B^{1/2}x^j)^\dagger(B^{1/2}x^k)$, so the map does carry a $B$-orthonormal eigenbasis to an orthonormal one — both halves confirmed symbolically.
  - **Normal-mode method run end to end** on a three-mass chain ($m = 1$, $M = 2$, $m = 1$; $K = 3\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}$, $M = \operatorname{diag}(1,2,1)$), which is the CO$_2$-type system of both sources. `scipy.linalg.eigh(K, M)` gives $\lambda = (0, 3, 6)$.
