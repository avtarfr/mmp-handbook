## Findings
1. [blocking] proof "Normal frequencies and normal modes", Part 4 (line 177): "the example below exhibits two modes with $(x^1)^{\mathsf T}Mx^2 = 0$ and $(x^1)^{\mathsf T}x^2 = -2 \neq 0$". Throughout this unit $x^1, \dots, x^n$ are the $M$-*orthonormal* modes (statement part 2; definition line 42; the example fixes them at lines 273-274). For those vectors the plain dot product is
   $$ (x^1)^{\mathsf T}x^2 = \frac{(1,\sqrt3)\cdot(1,-\sqrt3)}{ml^2\sqrt{(6+2\sqrt3)(6-2\sqrt3)}} = \frac{-2}{ml^2\sqrt{24}} = \frac{-0.408248}{ml^2}, $$
   not $-2$ — which is not even dimensionless, so the quoted number cannot be the one the notation names. $-2$ is the dot product of the unnormalised representatives $(1,\sqrt3)$ and $(1,-\sqrt3)$, which is what the example itself correctly says at line 276. Fix: "two modes with $(x^1)^{\mathsf T}Mx^2 = 0$ while their representatives $(1,\sqrt3)$ and $(1,-\sqrt3)$ have dot product $-2 \neq 0$", or quote $-2/(ml^2\sqrt{24})$.
2. [blocking] application, "Vibrational spectroscopy" (line 296): "Diagonalizing the pencil $(K,M)$ gives $3N$ eigenvalues, of which exactly six vanish — three rigid translations and three rigid rotations ... leaving $3N-6$ vibrational frequencies". False for a linear molecule, where only five vanish and there are $3N-5$ vibrations: rotation about the molecular axis moves no atom, so it is not an independent null direction. The unit's own remark at line 199 invokes "the linear triatomic molecule of Riley, Hobson and Bence §9.1 (p. 321)" — i.e. CO$_2$, which has $3\cdot3-5 = 4$ vibrations, not $3$ — and for any diatomic the formula gives $3N-6 = 0$ vibrational modes although a diatomic has one. Fix: "six for a nonlinear molecule and five for a linear one (so $3N-6$ or $3N-5$ vibrational frequencies)".

[minor] statement "Normal frequencies and normal modes", part 4 (lines 135-139): the bound is written $\omega_1^2 \le x^{\mathsf T}Kx/x^{\mathsf T}Mx \le \omega_n^2$, but part 2 defines $\omega_k = \sqrt{\lambda_k}$ only for the $k$ with $\lambda_k > 0$, and parts 2-3 explicitly allow $\lambda_k \le 0$. The proof (line 177) states the bound correctly as "part 4 of the pencil theorem with $A = K$, $B = M$ and $\lambda_k = \omega_k^2$". Fix: write $\lambda_1 \le \cdots \le \lambda_n$ in part 4 and add "$= \omega_1^2$, $= \omega_n^2$ when these are positive".

5. [minor] definition "The generalized eigenvalue problem" (line 27): "Since $B$ is invertible, $Ax = \lambda Bx$ has a non-zero solution exactly when $\det(A - \lambda B) = 0$." Fix: drop "Since $B$ is invertible", or attach it to the degree claim where it belongs.
6. [minor] definition "Small oscillations" (line 106): Lagrange's equations are written $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_i} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial q_i} = 0$, i.e. with $\partial T/\partial q_i$ dropped.
