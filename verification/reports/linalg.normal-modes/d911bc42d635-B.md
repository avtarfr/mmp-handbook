## Findings
1. [blocking] application, "Vibrational spectroscopy" (line 296, `provenance=original`): "Diagonalizing the pencil $(K, M)$ gives $3N$ eigenvalues, of which exactly six vanish — three rigid translations and three rigid rotations, which change no bond and so leave $V$ flat ... leaving $3N-6$ vibrational frequencies". This is false for a linear molecule, and the counterexample is the molecule the unit's own sources use. For a linear molecule every atom lies on the axis, so the displacement field of a rotation about that axis is zero at every atom: it is not a direction in configuration space at all, and the null space of $K$ has dimension **five**, not six, giving $3N-5$ vibrations. CO$_2$ ($N=3$, linear — Arfken's Example 6.5.2, §6.5 pp. 322-324, cited by this unit, and RHB's linear molecule, §9.1 p. 321, cited in the remark above) therefore has $4$ vibrational frequencies, not $3N-6 = 3$. The claim also fails for a diatomic, where $3N-6 = 0$ but there is one vibration. The word "exactly" makes this a definite assertion, and it is our own material. Fix: "of which six vanish for a nonlinear molecule — three rigid translations and three rigid rotations — leaving $3N-6$ vibrational frequencies; for a linear molecule, where rotation about the axis moves no atom, five vanish and there are $3N-5$."
2. [minor] proof, "Part 4" (line 177): "the example below exhibits two modes with $(x^1)^{\mathsf T}Mx^2 = 0$ and $(x^1)^{\mathsf T}x^2 = -2 \neq 0$". In the statement and in part 2 of the pencil theorem $x^1,\dots,x^n$ are the $M$-*orthonormal* eigenvectors, and for those (example, line 273) I compute $(x^1)^{\mathsf T}x^2 = -0.408248/(ml^2)$, not $-2$. The number $-2$ belongs to the unnormalised representatives $(1,\sqrt3)^{\mathsf T}$ and $(1,-\sqrt3)^{\mathsf T}$, which is how the example itself puts it at line 276. The conclusion ($\neq 0$) is right; only the number as attributed is not. Fix: "exhibits two modes whose unnormalised shapes $(1,\sqrt3)^{\mathsf T}$ and $(1,-\sqrt3)^{\mathsf T}$ have $x^{\mathsf T}Mx' = 0$ but $x^{\mathsf T}x' = -2 \neq 0$".
