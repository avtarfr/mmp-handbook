## Findings
1. [minor] application (line 302): "The three-mass chain of the second remark above" points at the wrong remark. The three-mass chain is in the *third* remark ("Do not throw away the symmetry", Arfken §6.5, pp. 322-324, where it is called "their three-mass chain"); the second remark ("Positive definite $K$ is about *strict* minima", line 205) calls its example "the linear triatomic molecule of Riley, Hobson and Bence". The unit contradicts itself here, because the example's step 4 (line 256) refers to the same object as "the third remark above". The physics asserted is correct for either example — both are three masses in a line with one $\omega^2 = 0$ translation and two axial vibrations — so only the pointer is wrong. Fix: write "third remark", or name the object with the wording the remark uses.
2. [minor] definition "Small oscillations" (line 103): the displayed $T = \tfrac12 \dot q^{\mathsf T} M \dot q$ is an exact equality while the companion $V$ display carries $+ O(\|q\|^3)$, yet the same block (line 118) says the neglected part of $T$ is $\tfrac12 \dot q^{\mathsf T}(M(q) - M(0))\dot q = O(\|q\|\,\|\dot q\|^2)$, and the unit's own example has a genuinely $q$-dependent $T$ (the $\cos(q_1 - q_2)$ factor, line 217). The asymmetry between the two displays is the only problem; the prose that follows is correct and explicit. Fix: write $T = \tfrac12 \dot q^{\mathsf T} M \dot q + O(\|q\|\,\|\dot q\|^2)$, or qualify both displays as the second-order truncation rather than only $V$.
3. [minor] proof of "Normal frequencies and normal modes", Part 2 (line 175): the step "its solution space is two-dimensional — a solution is determined by the two numbers $\eta(0), \dot\eta(0)$" is a uniqueness theorem, used to conclude that the exhibited two-parameter family is *every* solution. Part 3's forward direction ("every solution is bounded") rests on that exhaustiveness, but the unit deliberately does not list `ode.linear-ode-theory` as a prerequisite. The fact is standard undergraduate material and the deferral is stated openly, so this is not a wrong step, but the one load-bearing ingredient of the argument is the one not proved. Fix: add a two-line inline lemma — with $r$ a root of $r^2 + \lambda = 0$ and $u = \dot\eta - r\eta$, the equation gives $\dot u = -ru$, so zero initial data forces $u \equiv 0$ and then $\dot\eta = r\eta$ forces $\eta \equiv 0$ — or list the prerequisite.
4. [minor] proof of "Spectral theorem for a definite pencil", step 1 (line 62): the uniqueness of the Hermitian positive definite square root is compressed past a step. The argument given shows only $E_\gamma(X) \subseteq E_{\gamma^2}(B)$ for each eigenvalue $\gamma$ of $X$; concluding that $X$ acts as $\sqrt\beta$ on the *whole* of $E_\beta(B)$ needs the extra remark that the $E_\gamma(X)$ already decompose $\mathbb{C}^n$, so the inclusions between two direct-sum decompositions are equalities. Uniqueness is used only to license the definite article in part 3, so nothing downstream breaks. Fix: add that sentence.
5. [source] remark "Conventions and where the hypotheses are used", second paragraph (line 205): the unit reports accurately that Riley, Hobson and Bence state on p. 317 that a potential minimum makes the potential matrix positive definite, and then gives the correct semidefinite version as ours. The book's claim is wrong as printed, and is contradicted by its own linear triatomic on p. 321, which has $\omega^2 = 0$. This is already on file in `verification/discrepancies.md` against this unit for rhb-2006, "Ch. 9 introduction, Eq. (9.2), p. 317", so no new entry was added. No action on the unit: the difference is already noted in the remark, as the two-source rule requires.
