## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Small oscillations" (line 112): the order count attached to the dropped
   Lagrange term is off by one as written. The text says "$\partial T/\partial q_i$ is a first
   derivative of $M$ times two factors of $\dot q$, hence of third order in the small quantities
   $q, \dot q$". With $q \sim \dot q \sim \varepsilon$ the quantity $\partial T/\partial q_i =
   \tfrac12 \dot q^{\mathsf T}(\partial_i M)(q)\dot q$ is generically $O(\varepsilon^2)$, not
   $O(\varepsilon^3)$, because $(\partial_i M)(0)$ need not vanish: for $T = \tfrac12\dot q_1^2 +
   \tfrac12(1+q_1)\dot q_2^2$, sympy gives $\partial T/\partial q_1 = \tfrac12\dot q_2^2$, exactly
   second order. The unit's own double pendulum is the special case $(\partial_i M)(0) = 0$ (the
   coupling is $\cos(q_1-q_2)$, even), where it really is third order, which is presumably where the
   count came from. The conclusion is unaffected — second order is still one order beyond the
   first-order terms $M\ddot q$ and $Kq$ that are kept, so the term is correctly dropped — and the
   same sentence's "one order beyond everything else kept" is right under the paragraph's other
   reading, in which orders are counted in the *energies* (the neglected piece of $T$ is indeed
   $O(\varepsilon^3)$ against the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ quadratic forms kept). That is why this is
   minor rather than blocking: ambiguous reference, not a false result.
   Fix: attach the order to the right object — either "the neglected term in $T$ is of third order
   against the second-order forms kept", or "$\partial T/\partial q_i$ is of second order, one order
   beyond the first-order terms $M\ddot q$ and $Kq$".
2. [minor] statement "Normal frequencies and normal modes", part 4 (line 135): the prose
   ("modes of distinct frequencies are orthogonal with respect to both energies") is weaker than the
   display immediately above it, which asserts $(x^j)^{\mathsf T}Mx^k = \delta_{jk}$ and
   $(x^j)^{\mathsf T}Kx^k = \lambda_k\delta_{jk}$ for *all* $j \neq k$, degenerate frequencies
   included. That stronger form is what part 2 of the pencil theorem delivers and what the proof
   cites, and it is what the method's step 5 relies on when it Gram-Schmidts a degenerate
   eigenspace. The narrower prose matches RHB (9.16), which is stated for $\omega_i^2 \neq
   \omega_j^2$, so the risk is that a reader takes the weaker sentence as the unit's claim.
   Fix: say "modes belonging to distinct frequencies are automatically orthogonal in both energies,
   and within a degenerate eigenspace the basis can be chosen so that the relations above hold for
   every $j \neq k$".
3. [minor] frontmatter/remark (lines 11, 199): the unit silently drops a standing hypothesis of its
   main source. RHB state at p. 317 that "the requirement that the potential is a minimum means that
   the potential matrix $B$, like the kinetic energy matrix $A$, is real and positive definite", and
   §9.1 works throughout with stable oscillations (p. 320, "a general system undergoing stable
   oscillations"). Parts 2 and 3 of our statement deliberately admit $\lambda_k = 0$ and
   $\lambda_k < 0$, and the `adapted` proof block (line 146, src §9.1 pp. 318-321) proves that
   extension, which is not in the cited pages. This is not infidelity — nothing stronger is
   attributed to RHB, the general result is proved here from the unit's own `original` pencil
   theorem, and the second bullet of the remark does flag that a minimum gives only semidefiniteness
   and points at RHB's own zero-frequency example (p. 321) — but the remark never says that RHB
   assume definiteness, so a reader comparing the two texts sees an unexplained divergence. Note
   also that RHB p. 317's claim and RHB p. 321's $\omega^2 = 0$ translation mode are in tension with
   each other; under source-authority.md that is the book's business, not ours, and I have not
   filed it as a discrepancy because the unit does not report it either way.
   Fix: one clause in the "strict minima" bullet of the remark, e.g. that RHB assume the potential
   matrix positive definite throughout Ch. 9 (p. 317), whereas part 3 here treats the general sign
   pattern.
4. [minor] not a defect in this unit, recorded because the check surfaced it: the warn line
   "content/found/found.taylor-several-variables.md: prerequisite linalg.normal-modes comes after
   found.taylor-several-variables in the map" belongs to found.taylor-several-variables, which

- **Counterexample search.** Pencil theorem, 6 random Hermitian/positive-definite pairs
  (`numpy`/`scipy`, $n$ from 2 to 5, complex Hermitian $A$, $B = RR^\dagger + nI$): eigenvalues real
  in every case; the fitted leading coefficient of $\det(A-\lambda B)$ matched $(-1)^n\det B$ to 6
  decimals in all 6 (e.g. $n=5$: $-115813.931243$ both ways); $V^\dagger BV = I$ and
  $V^\dagger AV = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda)$ to $10^{-8}$; the quotient $x^\dagger Ax/x^\dagger
  Bx$ stayed inside $[\lambda_1,\lambda_n]$ for 300 random complex $x$ per pair; and
  $\operatorname{eig}(B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2})$ matched the pencil spectrum to $10^{-8}$, confirming part 3.
  Degenerate $B$-eigenvalues: the $B^{1/2}$ construction and the uniqueness argument of step 1 go
  through, and the method's step 5 handles a degenerate $\lambda_k$ by $M$-Gram-Schmidt. Edge cases
  tried against the statements: $B=I$ (recovers the ordinary problem, as the definition says);
  $A=0$ (all $\lambda_k=0$, part 3 of the normal-mode statement gives pure drift, consistent);
  $n=1$; $M$ a multiple of $I$ (then $M$-orthogonality does coincide with the dot product, which is
  why part 4's prose correctly hedges "unless $M$ is a multiple of the identity"). Indefinite $B$:
  the remark's own counterexample $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$,
  $B=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ gives $\det(A-\lambda B) = -\lambda^2-1$, roots $\pm i$ (confirmed by
  `np.roots`), so "definite, not merely invertible" is genuinely needed and part 1 is not
  over-stated. Part 3 of the normal-mode statement: with $\lambda_m=0$ the solution $a_m + b_mt$ is
  bounded when $b_m=0$, so the "if and only if" is only correct under the quantifier "for every
  choice of initial data" — which the statement does carry, and the proof's chosen data
  $\eta_m(0)=0$, $\dot\eta_m(0)=1$ realises the unbounded solution. No counterexample found to any
  statement as written.

  not just plausible.

    matching $K$; the $q$-expansion of $V$ has no cubic term, so the text's $O(\|q\|^4)$ is right.
  - Step 3: $\det(K-\lambda M)/(ml^2)^2 = 2\lambda^2 - 6\mu\lambda + 3\mu^2$ (difference from the
