## Findings

[blocking] proof "Of the forward and backward equations", Step B5 (line 184): Step B4 delivers $\alpha_r = 0$ almost surely, that is $u_s + au_x + \tfrac12 bu_{xx} = 0$ at the random point $(r, X_r)$, so the identity is known only on the support of the law of $X_r$. Step B5 then extends it to all $x$ with the reason "both sides being continuous". Continuity extends an identity from a set to its *closure*, not beyond it, so this does not cover an $x$ outside the support, and the standing assumptions do not exclude that case: they require only that $\sigma$ be bounded and smooth and that $X_t$ have a density, and a density need not be positive everywhere. The remark at line 202 states explicitly that non-degeneracy of $b$ is not needed for the derivation, which closes off the one route (full support) under which the stated reason would work. Fix: do not argue from a single trajectory. Note instead that $u(s,x)$ is defined for every starting point through $f(t,y\mid s,x)$, run Steps B2-B4 for the diffusion started at $X_s = x$ for each fixed $x$, and conclude the identity at every $(s,x)$; continuity is then needed only to upgrade "for almost every $s$" to "for every $s$".
2. [minor] proof, Step A2 (line 117): the admissibility integral is written $\int_0^t\mathbb{E}\bigl[\sigma g'\bigr]^2 dr$, which reads as the square of the expectation. The quantity meant, and the one the class $\mathcal{A}$ of prob.ito-calculus requires, is $\int_0^t\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\sigma g')^2\bigr]dr$. Fix: move the square inside. (The step is otherwise right: the integrand is bounded, adapted and has continuous sample paths, which is what Theorem (13.8.11) of the source needs.)

Fix: add that link.
