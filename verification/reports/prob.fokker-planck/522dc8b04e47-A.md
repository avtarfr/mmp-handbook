## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first two paragraphs (lines 200 and 202): the remark contradicts itself. Paragraph 1 asserts "Compact support of the test function is used in Step A5 **and nowhere else**"; paragraph 2 then asserts "Boundedness of $\sigma$ and **the compact support of $g'$** are used in Step A2". Both cannot be true, and the second is the correct one: compact support of $g$ (hence of $g'$, $g''$) is what makes $\sigma g'$ bounded in Step A2, and it is also what makes $(\mathcal{L}_rg)(X_r)$ bounded in Step A3, which is the differentiation argument behind (8). The remark is the block whose job is to say which step needs which hypothesis, so this is not a slip of wording but a wrong answer to the block's own question. Fix: drop "and nowhere else" and list A2, A3 and A5 as the three places compact support is spent, distinguishing the role in A5 (killing the boundary terms) from the role in A2-A3 (boundedness).

2. [blocking] proof, Standing assumptions (line 105) and Step B3 (line 172): Itô's formula is applied to $u(v,X_v)$ although the standing assumptions give $u$ only "continuously differentiable once in $s$ and twice in $x$". The general form of Itô's formula as stated in the listed prerequisite prob.ito-calculus — and in the source, Theorem (13.9.2), p. 610 — requires $f$ to be *twice continuously differentiable on $[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}$*, i.e. $C^2$ jointly, which includes $u_{ss}$. The handbook nowhere states the $C^{1,2}$ version, so as written Step B3 applies a quoted theorem outside its stated hypotheses, and the hypothesis the unit does state is strictly weaker than the one it needs. Fix: since the standing assumptions are assumed and not proved anyway, simply assume $u$ twice continuously differentiable in $(s,x)$ with bounded derivatives; or add the $C^{1,2}$ form of Itô's formula to prob.ito-calculus and cite it here.

3. [minor] definition, line 44: the verification of condition (1) for an SDE solution invokes "the fourth-moment inequality for martingales", which is not in `prereqs`, is not stated anywhere in the handbook, and is not a standard named result the reader can look up unambiguously (Doob's $L^4$ inequality and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality are different statements). The claim itself is right: for $|\sigma| \le K$, Itô's formula on $M_h^4$ gives $\mathbb{E}M_h^4 = 6\int_0^h\mathbb{E}(M_r^2\sigma^2)\,dr \le 3K^4h^2$, which is the $O(h^2)$ the argument needs. Fix: give that two-line computation instead of naming an inequality, or list the inequality as a prerequisite.

4. [minor] statement, line 62: the Markov property of $D$ is not among the stated hypotheses — the statement asks only for (1), (2), (3) and continuous paths — yet it is indispensable: the proof's Step B2 uses $\mathbb{E}(g(X_t)\mid\mathcal{F}_v) = u(v,X_v)$, and the source reaches both equations only "the Markov property provides a method" (p. 583). It does appear in the proof's standing assumptions. Fix: say in the statement that $D$ is a Markov process, as the source's surrounding text does, rather than leaving it to the umbrella "further technical conditions".

5. [minor] proof, Step A3 (line 122): the passage from Step A1 to the displayed identity interchanges $\mathbb{E}$ with $\int_0^t \cdot\,dr$. This is legitimate (the integrand is bounded, so Fubini applies) but is not mentioned, while the neighbouring and more innocuous interchange in Step A4 is explicitly licensed by assumption.

6. [minor] method, step 2 (line 235): the argument that $J \equiv c$ forces $c = 0$ assumes without saying so that $bp$ has limits at $\pm\infty$ — the stated premise is that $p$ and $p'$ are integrable, which gives limits for $p$, not directly for $bp$ with $b$ merely bounded (e.g. $b$ oscillating). Fix: assume $b$ bounded with bounded derivative, or argue directly from $\int_{y_0}^{y}(ap - c) = \tfrac12[bp]_{y_0}^{y}$.
