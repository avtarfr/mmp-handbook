## Findings
1. [minor] method, step 5 (line 127): "Draw cuts that block exactly the harmful loops." Exact blocking is not always possible, so the step asks for more than can be delivered. Take $a_1,a_2,a_3$ with $p_1=p_2=p_3=\tfrac12$: single loops and the triple loop are harmful, all three pair loops are harmless, and no system of cuts permits every pair loop while blocking every single loop (a cut from $a_1$ to $a_2$ plus one from $a_3$ to $\infty$ blocks the harmless $\{a_1,a_3\}$ loop as well). Fix: "Draw cuts that block every harmful loop, forbidding as few harmless ones as possible", keeping the parenthesis in the example that already makes this point.
2. [minor] statement, part 1 (line 53): "Both ranges are in use in this unit, for those two different purposes." There is really one convention, $\operatorname{Arg}:\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\to(-\pi,\pi]$, whose restriction to the cut plane takes values in the open interval; saying that instead of "both ranges" would remove the appearance of two conflicting conventions inside one unit. The mathematics as written is consistent, which is why this is minor.
3. [minor] example "A square root with two branch points and a finite cut", item 4 (line 152): "about $48\,000$ points" — the stated grid over $-8\le\operatorname{Re}z\le12$, $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert\le6$ is reproducible to within the choice of mesh (I get 48 410 points on a $201\times241$ grid with the segment excluded, and agreement to $5.4\times10^{-16}$ relative), but the mesh itself is not stated.
