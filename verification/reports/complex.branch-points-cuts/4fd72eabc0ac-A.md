2. [blocking] `example` "A square root with two branch points and a finite cut", step 4 (line 158): "their product does not jump: $F$ is continuous, and hence analytic, across $(-\infty,1)$." Continuity across a line plus analyticity off it does not give analyticity across it without a further theorem (Morera, which this unit does not have and which lives in `complex.cauchy-integral-formula`). The conclusion is true, and there is a one-line elementary argument available: for $x_0 < 1$ take a disc $D$ about $x_0$ disjoint from $[1,4]$; on $D$ the product $(z-1)(z-4)$ is analytic, nonzero, and takes values in a neighbourhood of the positive number $(x_0-1)(x_0-4)$, so $G = \exp\bigl(\tfrac12\ln((z-1)(z-4))\bigr)$ is analytic on $D$ with $G^2 = F^2$; then $F/G$ is continuous on $D$ with values in $\{\pm1\}$, hence constant on the connected $D$, so $F = \pm G$ is analytic. Fix: replace "and hence analytic" with that argument (or with a reference to Morera if this unit is moved after `complex.cauchy-integral-formula`).
