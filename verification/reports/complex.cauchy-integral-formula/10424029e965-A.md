## Findings
1. [minor] `prereqs` (line 7) lists only `complex.cauchy-theorem`, but the unit leans directly on `complex.contour-integrals` three times: the ML estimate carries every bound in the lemma ("All estimates below are the ML estimate of :ref[contour integrals]"), the modulus inequality carries Step 3 of the first proof, and part 4 of that unit — the fundamental theorem for contour integrals — is the whole first sentence of Morera's proof. It is available transitively (`complex.cauchy-theorem` lists it), so nothing is unavailable, but the unit relies on it directly and should name it. The lemma's set-up also uses the compactness of $\gamma$ ("pick $\delta>0$ with $\lvert\zeta - z_1\rvert \ge \delta$ for all $\zeta \in \gamma$, which is possible because $\gamma$ is compact"), and `found.compactness` is in neither this unit's nor `complex.cauchy-theorem`'s prerequisites. Fix: `prereqs: [complex.cauchy-theorem, complex.contour-integrals]`, and either add `found.compactness` or replace the appeal with the elementary reason (a continuous $\lvert z(t) - z_1\rvert$ on the closed parameter interval attains a positive minimum).

2. [minor] `idea` (line 48): the paragraph leads with a route that is *not* the proof that follows — Cauchy's theorem applied to $(f(z)-f(z_0))/(z-z_0)$ — and only then offers the shrinking-circle argument, which is what the `proof` block actually does, introduced by "Equivalently". The two are not equivalent; they are two different proofs. In addition, the lead route's one nontrivial step, "analytic except at $z_0$, where it stays bounded, so its integral around $C$ vanishes", is exactly the point that needs an argument (it does follow from the tools to hand: deform to $\sigma_\rho$ and bound by $2\pi\rho M \to 0$), and leaving it as an assertion in the `idea` invites a reader to think Cauchy's theorem applies directly to a function with a non-analytic point. Fix: lead with the shrinking-circle idea, and present the quotient route as an alternative with its boundedness step named ("bounded near $z_0$, so the small-circle integral is $O(\rho)$").

3. [minor] `remark`, the Morera bullet (line 147), last sentence: "On a simply connected region the three statements coincide." Ahlfors (p. 122) and Arfken (p. 489) do coincide there — both require $\oint f\,dz = 0$ for *every* closed contour — but Hassani's Theorem 10.5.8 (p. 319) requires it only "for each simple closed contour $C$", a weaker hypothesis, so his version is not the same statement even on a simply connected region. The rest of the bullet is accurate, including the claim that Ahlfors's version is the stronger one and that the proof given never uses simple connectivity. Fix: "Ahlfors's and Arfken's versions then coincide; Hassani's asks only for the simple closed contours, which is weaker still."

4. [minor] `remark`, second bullet (line 143): "On the contour the integral is not even absolutely convergent as written" needs the qualifier $f(z_0) \ne 0$ — the divergence of $\int \lvert dz\rvert/\lvert z - z_0\rvert$ is what makes it fail, and a numerator vanishing at $z_0$ can rescue it. Fix: add "when $f(z_0) \ne 0$".
