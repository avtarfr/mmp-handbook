## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Analytic solutions at an ordinary point" (lines 55-81) and the whole `proof`: the letter $n$ is used for two different things inside one sentence -- "For $n = 2$, with $p = \sum_m a_mx^{m}$ ... substituting $y = \sum_k c_kx^{k}$ gives, for every $n \ge 0$, the recurrence $(n+2)(n+1)c_{n+2} = \dots$". In the first half $n$ is the order of the equation (fixed at $2$); in the second it is the free recurrence index. The same collision runs through the proof ("Take $n = 2$" in the preamble, then $n$ as the index in Steps 1-7) and into the remark's second bullet. The mathematics is unambiguous once read, so I have not made this blocking, but the statement as printed is momentarily self-contradictory. Fix: use $k$ or $j$ for the recurrence index, e.g. $(k+2)(k+1)c_{k+2} = -\sum_{m=0}^{k}[\dots]$.
2. [minor] example, step 7 (line 244): "the partial sum through $x^{8}$ is $1+0.25-0.020833+0.003125-0.000558 = 1.231\,734$, already right to four decimals". The two values are $y(\tfrac12) = 1.231\,823\,8$ and the partial sum $1.231\,733\,7$; they agree to three decimal places ($1.232$), not four ($1.2318$ against $1.2317$). The printed numbers themselves are right. Fix: "right to three decimals", or quote the error ($9\times10^{-5}$).
3. [minor] application, first paragraph (line 248): "the recurrence is $c_{n+2} = 2(n-\nu)c_n/\bigl((n+2)(n+1)\bigr)$, which terminates exactly when $\nu$ is a non-negative integer". The recurrence is right, but for a *given* solution termination needs $\nu$ to be a non-negative integer of the same parity as the starting index: with $\nu = 2$ the even series terminates while the odd one ($n = 1,3,5,\dots$) never does. Fix: "which makes one of the two series terminate exactly when $\nu$ is a non-negative integer".
4. [minor] method, step 5 (line 168): the Wronskian of the pair with $(c_0,c_1) = (1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ is given as $\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} = 1$, but the unit's own example takes them in the other order ($y_1 = x$ from $(0,1)$, $y_2$ from $(1,0)$) and correctly gets $-1$. No error, but the sign flip between method and example is a small trap. Fix: fix an order, or say "$\pm1$ according to the order chosen".

- Calculations (`.venv/bin/python` and by hand), example $(1+x^2)y''+2xy'-2y=0$:
  - Index shifting: $y'' \to (n+2)(n+1)c_{n+2}$, $x^2y'' \to n(n-1)c_n$, $2xy' \to 2nc_n$, $-2y \to -2c_n$, giving $(n+2)(n+1)c_{n+2}+[n(n-1)+2n-2]c_n = 0$; and $n^2+n-2 = (n+2)(n-1)$, so $c_{n+2} = -\frac{n-1}{n+1}c_n$ -- as printed.
  - Odd branch: $n=1$ gives $c_3 = 0$, hence all higher odd coefficients vanish; $y_1 = x$ solves the equation ($0+2x-2x = 0$).
  - Even branch with $c_0=1$: $c_2 = 1$, $c_4 = -\tfrac13$, $c_6 = \tfrac15$, $c_8 = -\tfrac17$; the closed form $c_{2k} = (-1)^{k+1}/(2k-1)$ reproduces all four and satisfies the recurrence, as the printed induction shows.
  - Closed form: $x\arctan x = \sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k-1}x^{2k}/(2k-1)$, so $y_2 = 1+x\arctan x$. I verified the substitution independently: $(1+x^2)y_2'' = 1+\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}$, $2xy_2' = 2x\arctan x+\frac{2x^2}{1+x^2}$, $-2y_2 = -2-2x\arctan x$, and the sum is $1+\frac{1-x^2+2x^2}{1+x^2}-2 = 1+1-2 = 0$.
  - Radius: $|c_{2k+2}/c_{2k}| = (2k-1)/(2k+1) \to 1$, radius $1$ for $y_2$; infinite for $y_1$ -- as claimed, and $1$ is the distance from $0$ to $\pm i$.
  - Numbers (finding 2): $y(\tfrac12) = 1+\tfrac12\arctan\tfrac12 = 1.231\,823\,765\ldots$ (printed $1.231\,823\,8$, correct); partial sum through $x^8$ $= 1+0.25-0.020\,833\,3+0.003\,125-0.000\,558\,0 = 1.231\,733\,7$ (printed $1.231\,734$, correct).
  - Wronskian at $0$: $y_1(0)y_2'(0)-y_1'(0)y_2(0) = 0-1 = -1$ -- as printed.
  - Application, Hermite: substituting a plain series into $y''-2xy'+2\nu y=0$ gives $(n+2)(n+1)c_{n+2} = 2(n-\nu)c_n$, matching the printed recurrence (see finding 3).
