## Findings
1. [minor] proof "Hölder", step 1 (line 99): "If $b = 0$ both sides are what they are and the claim is immediate" is not a justification, and the style guide bans this kind of phrase. It also silently covers the equality clause, which at $b=0$ reads: the left side is $0$, the right side is $\lambda a$, and equality holds iff $a = 0$, i.e. iff $a = b$. Fix: say that in one clause.
2. [minor] statement (line 75): "the closure of $C_c(\mathbb{R})$ in $L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ contains no discontinuous function." Elements of $L^\infty$ are a.e.-equivalence classes and $L^\infty$ convergence is uniform off a null set, so a class in that closure can perfectly well have discontinuous representatives; what is true is that the closure is $C_0(\mathbb{R})$, i.e. every class in it has a continuous representative, and classes without one (such as $\chi_{[0,1]}$) are excluded. Fix: phrase it as "contains no class without a continuous representative", or name $C_0(\mathbb{R})$ as the closure.
3. [minor] proof "Minkowski" (line 117): "For $p = 1$ the claim is ...; and if $f+g = 0$ a.e. there is nothing to prove." The second clause is not about the $p=1$ case — it is the degenerate case $A = 0$ of the $p>1$ argument, and step 4 separately says "we may assume $A>0$". As placed it reads as part of the $p=1$ sentence. Fix: move it to step 4, or make clear it is a separate trivial case.
