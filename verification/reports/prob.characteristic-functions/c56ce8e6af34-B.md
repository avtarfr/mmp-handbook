1. [blocking] statement "The standard characteristic functions", line 159: "The Cauchy law has no mean, so by statement (a) above its characteristic function cannot be differentiable at the origin." This does not follow from statement (a), and the unit's own remark says so. Statement (a) for odd $k$ gives only $\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{k-1}\rvert<\infty$, so at $k=1$ it yields $\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{0}\rvert<\infty$, which is vacuous; the remark at line 215 states this explicitly ("the existence of $\varphi'(0)$ gives only $\mathbb{E}\lvert X^{0}\rvert<\infty$, which is no information at all; it does *not* give $\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert<\infty$"). The implication is also false in general: a symmetric law with $\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert=\infty$ can still have $\varphi'(0)$ existing (and equal to $0$), so absence of a mean does not forbid differentiability at the origin. The two blocks therefore contradict each other, and the one the reader meets first is the wrong one. (The *conclusion* about the Cauchy is true - $e^{-\lvert t\rvert}$ does have a corner - it is the stated reason that fails.) Fix: drop the appeal to statement (a) and say instead that $e^{-\lvert t\rvert}$ is directly seen to be non-differentiable at $0$, which is consistent with the Cauchy law having no mean, while noting that the converse direction is not available.
2. [blocking] remark, fifth bullet (line 217): "the class of characteristic functions is a convex cone closed under products, but not under square roots." The class of characteristic functions is not a cone: every member satisfies $\varphi(0)=1$, so $\lambda\varphi$ for $\lambda\ne1$ is not a characteristic function, and closure under positive scalar multiples fails. It is a convex set (mixtures) closed under products and under conjugation. The word "cone" belongs to Reed and Simon's *functions of positive type*, of which the characteristic functions are exactly the members normalised at the origin - they do say on p. 330 that those "form a cone", which is presumably where the word came from. Fix: "a convex set closed under products and conjugation but not under square roots" (or attach "cone" to the functions of positive type, where it is correct).
