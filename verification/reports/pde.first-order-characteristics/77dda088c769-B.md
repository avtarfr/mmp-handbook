## Findings
1. [minor] :::definition (line 55): "These two linear equations for $(u_x,u_y)$ are solvable
   precisely when [$b\,dx-a\,dy\ne0$]" — as a biconditional this is false: a singular system can be
   consistent (take $(dx,dy)=(1,0)$, $(a,b)=(2,0)$, $du=1$, $c=2$; then $u_x=1$ solves both and
   $u_y$ is free, yet the determinant is $0$). Both cited sources say "unique": Hassani p. 639 "the
   system has a unique solution if and only if the matrix of coefficients is invertible", and
   Stakgold and Holst (8.1.9) "will have a unique solution if and only if". The conclusion drawn in
   the next clause is right, and the block's own definition of characteristic is "$u_x$ and $u_y$
   cannot be computed", i.e. not determined, so nothing downstream is affected. Fix: "uniquely
   solvable precisely when".
2. [minor] :::definition (line 36): "Equation (1) is **semilinear** if $a$ and $b$ do not depend on
   $u$ but $c$ does, and **linear** if in addition $c = -q(x,y)u + F(x,y)$", attributed by the
   preceding clause ("the two narrower cases named next are theirs") to Stakgold and Holst. On
   p. 463 they reserve "semilinear" for the case where $c(x,y,u)$ depends *nonlinearly* on $u$,
   treating linear and semilinear as alternatives, whereas the unit nests linear inside semilinear.
   The unit's convention is the mainstream one and is consistent with their parenthetical "(note
   that in a semilinear equation the highest derivatives appear linearly)"; the definition of
   *linear* matches theirs exactly, including the sign flip forced by their writing $+c$ on the
   left. Fix: say the nesting is ours, or restrict the attribution to the word "linear".
3. [minor] :::proof Step 1 (line 120): "Because the initial data $f,h,g$ are $C^1$ in $\tau$ and the
   right-hand sides are $C^1$, the solution depends $C^1$ on $\tau$ as well" uses $C^1$ dependence
   of an ODE solution on its initial values, which is a separate theorem from the existence and
   uniqueness result the step cites; `ode.existence-uniqueness` (checked) proves Picard convergence
   and uniqueness only and says nothing about differentiability in the data. The claim is true and
   standard, but the step is the only place in the proof that is not justified by a listed
   prerequisite, and Step 2 needs it (the Jacobian involves $x_\tau$, $y_\tau$). Fix: add the
   smooth-dependence theorem to `prereqs`, or say explicitly that it is being quoted.
