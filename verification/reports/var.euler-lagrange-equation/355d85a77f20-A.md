## Findings
1. [blocking] frontmatter summary (line 6): "it is automatically twice differentiable wherever the integrand is strictly convex in the slope" is false, and it does not match part 2 of the statement, whose hypothesis is $F_{y'y'}\ne 0$. Strict convexity in $p$ allows $F_{pp}=0$. Counterexample: $F(x,y,p)=p^4/4-xp$ is $C^\infty$ and strictly convex in $p$; on $[-1,1]$ with $y(\pm1)=3/4$ the curve $y=\tfrac34|x|^{4/3}$ has $y'=x^{1/3}$, so $F_{y'}=y'^3-x\equiv0$ and $F_y=0$: it is a $C^1$ extremal, and it is the global minimiser because it minimises the integrand pointwise. Yet $y'=x^{1/3}$ is not differentiable at $0$, where $F_{y'y'}=3y'^2=0$. Fix: write "wherever $F_{y'y'}\ne 0$" (or "where the integrand is strictly convex in the slope with $F_{y'y'}\ne 0$").
2. [minor] statement part 2 (lines 36-40): $x_0$ is introduced only inside the hypothesis and is never quantified, and "near $x_0$" is one-sided if $x_0$ is an endpoint. Fix: "if, for some $x_0\in[a,b]$, ... then $y$ is $C^2$ on a (one-sided, at an endpoint) neighbourhood of $x_0$".
3. [minor] proof Step 5 (line 77): $G(x,p)=F_{y'}(x,y(x),p)-Q(x)$ is defined only for $x\in[a,b]$, so the implicit function theorem as stated (open $U\ni x_0$) does not apply when $x_0=a$ or $b$. Fix: one sentence, either restricting to interior $x_0$ or extending $y$ and $Q$ a little past the end.
