[minor] note "Three other conventions in the library" (line 67): "...delivers the conjugate, which is the form we use and the form :cite[Reed and Simon]{work=reed-simon-1980 loc="§VI.3, p. 194"} state", and, at the end of the same bullet, "Reed and Simon record the same duality: if $\lambda$ lies in the residual spectrum of $T$ then $\bar\lambda$ lies in the point spectrum of $T^*$." The proposition actually printed on p. 194 is for the *Banach* adjoint and carries no conjugate: "If lambda is in the residual spectrum of T, then lambda is in the point spectrum of T'." The conjugated Hilbert-space form is theirs, but it is Thm VI.7 (Phillips) on p. 192, sigma(T*) = {conj(lambda) : lambda in sigma(T)}. The statement in our text is mathematically right and the two are the same fact once the Banach adjoint is converted to the Hilbert adjoint, so this is a locator imprecision rather than a wrong attribution. Fix: cite "§VI.3, Thm VI.7, p. 192, and the proposition on p. 194", or say that p. 194 states it for the Banach adjoint and p. 192 supplies the conjugate in the Hilbert case.
2. [minor] example "A self-adjoint operator that is all continuous spectrum" (line 203) is labelled `provenance=original` and closes with "it is the example :cite[Stakgold and Holst] use for the same purpose" (Example 5, p. 329). I note it only because the chapter should be consistent about where the line falls.
