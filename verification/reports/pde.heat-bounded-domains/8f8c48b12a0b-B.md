- **Counterexample search.** Against the statement as written, with $\varphi$ only square-integrable:
  - $\varphi$ a step datum (sine coefficients $O(1/n)$, so $\sum A_n$ diverges absolutely at $t=0$):
    part 1's restriction to $t \ge \delta$ is exactly what saves it, and part 3 is the only thing
    claimed at $t=0$ — consistent.
  - $\varphi \equiv 1$ (not compatible with the Dirichlet condition at either end): part 1 still
    holds for $t>0$, and the series does not converge to 1 at $x=0$ — the unit never claims it does.
  - $\varphi$ with $A_1 = 0$ (e.g. $\varphi = \sin 2\pi x$ on $(0,1)$): part 4's bound is then far
    from tight but still true, as it must be, since it is stated as an inequality.
  - $\varphi \in L^2 \setminus L^\infty$ (e.g. $x^{-1/4}$ near 0): part 4 is fine while
    $\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi$ are useless — which is precisely what the fourth bullet of the
    hypotheses remark says, so no contradiction.
  - $k < 0$: every factor becomes $e^{+\lambda_n|k|t}$ and the series diverges for bounded
    coefficients — the third bullet is right.
  - Tried to break part 1's "any number of times": the dominating series
    $n^{2j+m}e^{-ck n^2\pi^2/\ell^2}$ converges for every fixed $j,m$ because
    $n^pe^{-an^2} \le n^pe^{-an}$ for $n \ge 1$ — correct as written, and the hypotheses named in
    Step 2 are exactly the three in `found.termwise-operations` (uniform convergence of the series,
    continuously differentiable terms, uniform convergence of the differentiated series), which I
    opened to confirm.
  - Direct PDE residual test on the series: with $\ell=k=1$, $\varphi = x(1-x)$, 60 terms, at
    $(x,t)=(0.3,0.07)$ I got $u_t - u_{xx} = 0$ to 25 digits and $u(0,t)=0$,
    $u(1,t) = -2.4\times10^{-27}$ — the series does solve the problem and does meet the boundary
    conditions.
  - Eigenvalue table spot-checked against the ODE: $-X''=\lambda X$ with $X(0)=X'(\ell)=0$ forces
    $\cos\beta\ell = 0$, i.e. $\beta\ell = (n+\tfrac12)\pi$, so
    $\lambda = ((n+\tfrac12)\pi/\ell)^2$ with $X = \sin((n+\tfrac12)\pi x/\ell)$ — matches row 3.
