## Findings
1. [blocking] method "Inhomogeneous boundary data" (line 198): "Route A is quicker whenever the data are steady or **share a single time dependence**, for instance $h = H\cos\omega t$, $j = J\cos\omega t$, when one looks for $\bar u = u_0(x)\cos\omega t$". The whole method block is set in the diffusion equation $u_t = k u_{xx}$ (line 165), and no such $\bar u$ exists there: substituting gives $-\omega u_0(x)\sin\omega t = k\,u_0''(x)\cos\omega t$ for all $t$, and since $\sin$ and $\cos$ are independent this forces $\omega u_0 \equiv 0$ and $u_0'' \equiv 0$, i.e. $u_0 \equiv 0$ for $\omega \neq 0$. The cited page is also about a different equation: :cite{work=strauss-2008 loc="p. 150"} makes this guess for the *wave* problem (11), $u_{tt} - c^2u_{xx} = f$, where the ansatz works precisely because $t$-derivatives come in pairs ($-\omega^2 u_0 - c^2u_0'' = F$, $u_0(0)=H$, $u_0(\ell)=K$). As written the sentence is both false in this unit's setting and an infidelity: it transfers a wave-equation technique to diffusion under a citation that does not support it. Fix: either give the correct diffusion ansatz, $\bar u = \operatorname{Re}\bigl[u_0(x)e^{i\omega t}\bigr]$ with complex $u_0$ solving $i\omega u_0 = k u_0''$, $u_0(0)=H$, $u_0(\ell)=J$ (the thermal-wave / skin-depth solution, $u_0$ a combination of $e^{\pm\sqrt{i\omega/k}\,x}$), or attribute the $u_0(x)\cos\omega t$ guess explicitly to Strauss's wave problem and say that the diffusion equation needs the complex form.
2. [blocking] method "Inhomogeneous boundary data" (line 176): Route A is stated for general time-dependent $h, j$ and correctly leaves $v = u - \bar u$ with the source $-\bar u_t$, but the unit then supplies no way to solve a forced problem — the previous method solves only the source-free equation, and the only case the unit closes is $h, j$ constant. So "one of two routes is needed" (line 168) over-promises: for time-dependent data Route A as given does not reach a solution. Fix: say that Route A reduces the problem to the homogeneous one only for steady data, and that for time-dependent data the residual source $-\bar u_t$ must be expanded in the eigenfunctions and each coefficient solved as in Route B (or by Duhamel, :ref{to=green.wave-heat-green-functions}).
3. [minor] method "Inhomogeneous boundary data" (line 178): Route B's two key steps, differentiating under the integral sign and integrating by parts twice, need regularity of $u$ that is never stated; :cite{work=strauss-2008 loc="p. 147–148"} does state it ("provided that $u(x,t)$ is a continuous function", "If the derivatives of $u(x,t)$ are also continuous"). Fix: add that the route presumes a classical solution, $u(\cdot,t) \in C^2[0,\ell]$ with $u_t$ continuous, and that it derives the coefficients of a solution assumed to exist rather than constructing one.
4. [minor] application (line 247): the eigenfunction series is quoted as $\frac{2}{\ell}\sum \sin\frac{n\pi x}{\ell}\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}{\ell}e^{-k\lambda_n t}$, but :cite{work=stakgold-holst-2011 loc="Eq. (8.2.36a), p. 482"} is written for $u_t - u_{xx} = \cdots$, i.e. $k = 1$, and has $e^{-n^2\pi^2 t/\ell^2}$. Restoring the diffusivity is harmless and matches this unit's convention, but it is a silent change to a quoted formula, unlike the heat-kernel unit which flags the same adjustment for Evans. Fix: note that Stakgold and Holst take $k=1$ and that the factor is $e^{-k\lambda_n t}$ with diffusivity restored.
5. [minor] proof (line 124): the passage from "may be differentiated term by term, once in each variable" to "$u$ is $C^\infty$" uses, without saying so, that a uniformly convergent series of continuous terms has a continuous sum, which is what makes the mixed partials continuous and hence the function jointly smooth. Fix: add the half-sentence (it is already available from :ref{to=found.uniform-convergence}).
6. [minor] definition (line 39): "$T(t) = e^{-\lambda k t}$ up to a factor" is correct but the exclusion $T \not\equiv 0$ is implicit; also the division by $kXT$ silently assumes $XT \neq 0$. Strauss handles this the same way, so this is only a presentation point. Fix: say "on any region where $XT \neq 0$".
