## Findings
1. [minor] statement "The series is a solution, and how fast it decays" (line 100): "The same statements hold for the cosine series under Neumann conditions" leaves part 2 ambiguous, since part 2 as written asserts $u(0,t)=u(\ell,t)=0$, which is not what the cosine series satisfies. Fix: say explicitly that under Neumann conditions part 2 reads $u_x(0,t)=u_x(\ell,t)=0$.

2. [minor] proof, Step 2 (line 124): the conclusion is written "$u$ is $C^\infty$ on $[0,\ell]\times(\delta,\infty)$", but the uniform bounds just established hold on $[0,\ell]\times[\delta,\infty)$; the open interval is a slip (harmless, since $\delta>0$ is then let go to $0$). Fix: write $[\delta,\infty)$.

3. [minor] method "Inhomogeneous boundary data", Route B (line 178): "differentiate *under the integral sign* rather than term by term" is the right move, but the regularity that licenses it is not stated. Strauss is explicit that this needs the new integrand to be continuous (his §5.6 p. 148, citing Section A.3). Fix: add "assuming $u_t$ and $u_{xx}$ are continuous on $[0,\ell]\times(0,\infty)$, which is what justifies both the differentiation under the integral and the two integrations by parts".

4. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", fourth bullet (line 157): "it applies when $\varphi$ is bounded with $\varphi(0)=\varphi(\ell)=0$ and $u$ extends continuously to $t=0$, as in the previous bullet" - the previous bullet's hypothesis is $\varphi$ continuous and piecewise $C^1$ with $\varphi(0)=\varphi(\ell)=0$, not merely bounded; boundedness alone does not give the continuous extension. Fix: repeat the previous bullet's hypothesis, or say "bounded, with $u$ extending continuously to $t=0$ (for instance under the hypotheses of the previous bullet)".

5. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 156): "the series diverges for every $t>0$ unless the coefficients decay faster than every exponential" is correct as a necessary condition but may be read as sufficient; what is actually needed for convergence at every $t>0$ is decay faster than every Gaussian $e^{-cn^2}$, since $\lambda_n\sim n^2$. Fix: say "faster than $e^{-cn^2}$ for every $c>0$".
