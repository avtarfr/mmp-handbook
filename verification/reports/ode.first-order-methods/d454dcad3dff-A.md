## Findings
1. [source] method "Solving an exact equation" (line 220): "They also note that separability and exactness are independent properties: every separable equation is exact, but an exact equation need not be separable (p. 333)". This is a faithful report of arfken-2013 p. 333 ("We note that separability and exactness are independent attributes. All separable ODEs are automatically exact, but not all exact ODEs are separable"), so under docs/contributing/source-authority.md it is not a defect in the unit. Two things about the source are worth recording, and I have recorded them with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`: the word "independent" fights the one-way implication that follows it, and "all separable ODEs are automatically exact" needs the qualification that the variables have *already* been separated -- $h(y)\,\mathrm{d}y-g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0$ has $A_{y}=B_{x}=0$, but the same equation as $f(x)\sigma(y)\,\mathrm{d}x-\mathrm{d}y=0$ has $A_{y}=f\sigma'$ and $B_{x}=0$, which differ unless $\sigma$ is constant. If the project wants, one clause ("once the variables have been separated") would carry the qualification without departing from the source.
2. [minor] note "What the six methods have in common" (line 34): "All of them but one involve a division -- by a factor $\sigma(y)$, by $B$ or $A$, by $F(v)-v$, by $y^{n}$" lists four divisions for the five dividing methods. The linear method also divides twice: by the coefficient of $y'$ in step 1 of its recipe, which is where the singular points of the unnormalised equation $p_{1}y'+p_{0}y=q$ are excluded (the note on Hassani's form, line 362, makes exactly this point), and by $\mu$, which costs nothing because $\mu>0$. Fix: add "by the coefficient of $y'$" to the list, noting that the division by $\mu$ is the one harmless one.
3. [minor] statement "Separable equations" (line 66): "where $h$ vanishes or is undefined the displayed form can hold and the quotient form cannot" -- under the theorem's own hypothesis $h$ is continuous on the closed interval $c\le y\le d$, so within the scope of the block it is nowhere undefined; only its zeros matter. Fix: drop "or is undefined", or say that $h$ may also fail to be defined outside the interval of the hypothesis.
