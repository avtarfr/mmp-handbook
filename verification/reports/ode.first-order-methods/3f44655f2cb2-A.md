[blocking] statement[Integrating factors] (line 222, display at line 225; the same conflation recurs in proof[Integrating factors], line 249): the block states no hypotheses at all on $A$, $B$ or the region, and it writes "$\mu A\,dx + \mu B\,dy = 0$ is exact, that is $\partial(\mu A)/\partial y = \partial(\mu B)/\partial x$". Two defects follow. (i) The unit's own remark[Which form of the criterion we follow] (lines 149-162) establishes that the criterion is sufficient for exactness only on a suitable region and gives the punctured-plane counterexample where the criterion holds and no single-valued potential exists; "is exact, that is [criterion]" therefore contradicts the unit two blocks earlier. (ii) Cases 1 and 2 assert that $\mu = \exp(\int\alpha\,dx)$ is an integrating factor with nothing assumed that makes $A_y$ and $B_x$ exist or $\alpha$ have an antiderivative, so $\mu$ need not be differentiable and the product-rule computation of the proof need not apply; the proof quietly repairs this at line 255 ("Assuming it is, and continuous"), and Coddington states the hypothesis explicitly as "$p$ continuous and independent of $y$" (Ex. 4(b), p. 199) under the standing assumption that $M$, $N$ have continuous first partial derivatives on a rectangle (Ex. 3, p. 198). Fix: open the block with Coddington's hypotheses — $A$ and $B$ with continuous first partial derivatives on a rectangle $R$ on which the equation is inexact — whereupon the criterion *is* equivalent to exactness there (by the criterion theorem of this unit), $\alpha$ is automatically continuous on $\{B \neq 0\}$, and the proof's extra assumption is covered rather than smuggled in.

2. [minor] statement[Separable equations] (line 66): "equivalently $dy/dx = g(x)/h(y)$". The two equations are not equivalent, and the unit knows it: remark[Solutions lost by dividing] (line 113) notes that $y \equiv 0$ solves $y' = y^2$, whereas for that equation $h = 1/y^2$ is undefined at $y = 0$ so $y \equiv 0$ does not satisfy $h(y)y' = g(x)$; and the block's own last paragraph (line 79) adds Coddington's proviso $h(\varphi(x)) \neq 0$ for exactly this reason. Fix: say that Coddington defines the *class* by $f = g/h$ (p. 186) and that the two forms have the same solutions only where $h(\varphi) \neq 0$, reserving "equivalently" for the class and not the solution sets.
