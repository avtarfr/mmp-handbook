## Findings

1. [minor] note[What the six methods have in common] (line 34): "Every one of the six involves a
   division — by a factor $\sigma(y)$, by $B$, by $y^n$ — and each division can discard solutions"
   overstates the case. The exact-equation method as the unit gives it (method[Solving an exact
   equation], steps 1–5) performs no division and discards nothing: for $x\,dx + y\,dy = 0$ it
   returns $x^2+y^2=c$ with no lost solutions. Division by $B$ enters only later, when the implicit
   relation $U=k$ is solved for $y$ (definition block, line 56). Fix: write "five of the six", or
   attribute the division by $B$ to the step that converts $U=k$ into $y=\varphi(x)$.

2. [minor] idea[The criterion for exactness] (line 168): the sketch integrates $A$ "along the
   horizontal segment from the left edge of the rectangle to $(x,y)$" and then integrates $B$ "up the
   left edge", but the proof that follows (line 177) builds $U$ from the centre $(x_0,y_0)$, namely
   $U = \int_{x_0}^{x}A(s,y)\,ds + \int_{y_0}^{y}B(x_0,t)\,dt$ (Coddington's Eq. (3.8), p. 196).
   Both base points give a valid construction, so nothing is false, but the idea does not describe
   the proof it introduces. Fix: "from the vertical centre line $x = x_0$" and "up that line".

3. [minor] statement[Separable equations] (line 74): "for an interval $I$ inside $(a,b)$".
   Coddington's Theorem 1 (p. 188) says $I$ "contained in $a \le x \le b$". Our restriction to the
   open interval is the safe direction (it makes the conclusion apply to fewer $I$) and is in fact
   what the hypothesis "$g$ continuous on $a<x<b$" needs, but the change is made silently. Fix:
   either use Coddington's closed interval, or say in one clause that the open interval is our
   restriction and why. Recorded as a source issue (see [source] 8).

4. [minor] statement[Integrating factors] (line 222) against statement[The linear equation] (line
   281): the unit defines an integrating factor only for an equation that is **inexact**, yet part 1
   of the linear statement calls $\mu = \exp\!\int P$ an integrating factor without excluding
   $P \equiv 0$. In the differential form $(Py-Q)\,dx + dy = 0$ one has $A_y = P$, $B_x = 0$, so the
   equation is already exact exactly when $P \equiv 0$, and the term is then used outside the scope
   its own definition permits. (Verified symbolically: for $A = ye^x + \sin x$, $B = e^x$ the test
   quantity $\alpha$ is $0$ and $\mu = 1$.) Fix: add "with $P \not\equiv 0$" in part 1, or drop
   "inexact" from the definition and keep it as the case of interest.

5. [minor] application (lines 627, 647): $S$ is given as $C_V\ln T + nR\ln V + \text{const}$, but the
   displayed identity $\Phi = C_V e^{S/C_V}$ holds only when that constant is zero; in general
   $\Phi = C_V e^{(S-\text{const})/C_V}$. The conclusion drawn from it — that $\Phi$ is a function of
   $S$ alone — is unaffected. Fix: drop the "+ const" from the potential, or write "up to the
   additive constant in $S$".

6. [minor] statement[Exact equations and the criterion for exactness] (lines 128–134): the
   conclusion sentence of Theorem 2 tracks Coddington p. 193 closely — ours "every differentiable
   function defined implicitly by a relation … is a solution, and every solution whose graph lies in
   $R$ arises in this way" against his "Every differentiable function $\varphi$ defined implicitly by
   a relation … is a solution of (3.1), and every solution of (3.1) whose graph lies in $R$ arises
   this way". I do **not** call the block a close paraphrase: it is one sentence of standard theorem
   conclusion inside a block that also carries our own definition, the $M,N,F \to A,B,U$ translation
   and Theorem 3, and the mechanical check is clean (closest block alignment 18%, below the warn
   threshold, no verbatim-run warning). Fix, if wanted: recast as "the solutions whose graphs lie in
   $R$ are exactly the functions cut out by $U = k$", which is also the phrasing used for the
   separable theorem.

7. [minor] definition block (line 48): says Riley, Hobson and Bence take the general solution to
   contain "$n$ essential arbitrary constants". They write "$n$ arbitrary parameters (constants)"
   (p. 470); "essential" is ours and is not in the source. Fix: drop the word or mark it as our
   gloss.

8. [source] rhb-2006 §5.3, Eq. (5.9), p. 156: the exactness criterion is declared "both a necessary
   and a sufficient condition" with no hypothesis on the region, and sufficiency fails on a region
   that is not simply connected. The unit follows Coddington instead and gives the punctured-plane
   counterexample in its remark, so our text is right. Recorded with
   `tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.

9. [source] rhb-2006 §14.2.3, p. 473: "the differential $A\,dx+B\,dy$ can always be made exact" is
   asserted with no differentiability hypothesis and no locality qualifier. The unit attributes the
   claim to RHB by name in the statement and in the application rather than asserting it itself.
   Recorded.

10. [source] rhb-2006 §14.2.4, p. 474: "Linear first-order ODEs are a special case of inexact ODEs"
    is false for $P \equiv 0$ — the source of finding 4. Recorded.

11. [source] coddington-1989 §5.2, Theorem 1, p. 188: $g$ is assumed continuous on the open interval
    $a<x<b$ while $I$ is allowed to be "contained in $a \le x \le b$" — the source of finding 3.
    Recorded.

12. [source] hassani-2013 §14.2, Theorem 14.2.1, p. 420: continuity of $p_0,p_1,q$ is assumed but
    both formulas divide by $p_1$ with no non-vanishing hypothesis. The unit's note says so
    explicitly. Recorded.
