## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", second bullet (line 188): the bullet frames the issue as "Without the second [i.e. without $f(\pm\infty)=0$], the rule acquires a boundary term", but the counterexample supplied, $f=H(x)e^{-x}$, does satisfy $f(\pm\infty)=0$; what fails for it is absolute continuity at the interior point $0$, as the bullet itself then says. The counterexample is genuine (I checked: $ik\hat f(k)-\widehat{(-f)}(k)=(ik+1)/(1+ik)=1$, exactly the jump), so nothing is wrong mathematically; only the lead-in sentence points at the wrong hypothesis. Fix: split the bullet, or change the lead-in to "Without absolute continuity the rule acquires a term from each jump; without $f(\pm\infty)=0$ it acquires a boundary term", and keep the present example under the first clause.
2. [minor] proof, rule 7 (line 177): "$\lvert e^{-ihx}-1\rvert \le \lvert hx\rvert$ by the mean value theorem applied to $t\mapsto e^{-itx}$". The mean value theorem in the form used does not hold for complex-valued functions of a real variable. The bound itself is correct. Fix: justify it by $e^{-ihx}-1=-ix\int_0^h e^{-itx}dt$, or by $\lvert e^{i\theta}-1\rvert=2\lvert\sin(\theta/2)\rvert\le\lvert\theta\rvert$.
3. [minor] statement, rule 6, and remark (lines 110, 188): "absolutely continuous" is the operative hypothesis of rule 6 but is neither defined in the unit nor supplied by a listed prerequisite (`measure.dominated-convergence` and `fourier.fourier-series` do not give it, and `measure.radon-nikodym` concerns absolute continuity of measures). The proof in fact uses only the consequence $f(x)=f(0)+\int_0^x f'$. Fix: add a parenthetical gloss at the first use, "(that is, $f(x)=f(0)+\int_0^x f'$ with $f'\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$)".
4. [minor] conventions table, last row (line 87): the entry reads "$(2\pi)^{-n/2}\hat f(y)$ at $n=1$", which still carries the symbol $n$ while specialising to $n=1$. Fix: write $(2\pi)^{-1/2}\hat f(y)$.
