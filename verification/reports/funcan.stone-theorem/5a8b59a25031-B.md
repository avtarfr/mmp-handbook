[blocking] remark, bullet "Strong, not norm, continuity" (line 197): the claim "$\lVert U(t) - I\rVert = 2$ for every $t \notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$", and the reason given for it ("the factors $e^{int}$ come arbitrarily close to $-1$ as $n$ varies"), are both false. On $L^2(0,2\pi)$ with $U(t)e_n = e^{int}e_n$ the operator $U(t)-I$ is diagonal, so $\lVert U(t)-I\rVert = \sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert e^{int}-1\rvert$. For $t = 2\pi/3$ the set $\{e^{int}\}$ is the three cube roots of unity, so the supremum is $\lvert e^{2\pi i/3}-1\rvert = \sqrt3 = 1.7320508\ldots$, not $2$, and no $e^{int}$ comes near $-1$. Verified numerically (sup over $n$ from $-2000$ to $2000$): $t=\pi \to 2.0000000000$, $t=2\pi/3 \to 1.7320508076$, $t=2\pi/5 \to 1.9021130326$, $t=0.7 \to 1.9999997892$. The equality holds only when $t/2\pi$ is irrational or has even denominator in lowest terms. Fix: the sharp true statement is $\lVert U(t)-I\rVert \ge \sqrt3$ for every $t \notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ (with equality at $t = \pm 2\pi/3$, and value $2$ when $t/2\pi$ is irrational), which still gives the bullet its point - that $\lVert U(t)-I\rVert \not\to 0$, so $U$ is not norm continuous. Replace "$= 2$ for every $t\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$" by "$\ge\sqrt3$ for every $t\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, and $=2$ whenever $t/2\pi$ is irrational", and drop or qualify the "arbitrarily close to $-1$" justification accordingly.
