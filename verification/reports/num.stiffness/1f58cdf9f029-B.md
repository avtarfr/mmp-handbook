## Findings
1. [blocking] :::proof[Parts 1 and 2], "Part 2, the real intervals": "$c(-3) = -21 < 0 < c(-2) = 8$ places it in $(-3,-2)$." With $c(z) = z^3+4z^2+12z+24$, $c(-3) = -27 + 36 - 36 + 24 = -3$, not $-21$. ($c(-2) = -8+16-24+24 = 8$ is right.) The bracket conclusion survives, since $-3 < 0$, but the quoted value is simply wrong arithmetic in an `original` block. Fix: $c(-3) = -3$.
2. [blocking] :::example, fourth bullet: "Halving the step to $0.02$ ... with an error forty times smaller than backward Euler's at twice the step." No reading of "backward Euler at twice the step" gives $40$. Using the unit's own table: trapezoidal at $h=0.02$ has relative error $8.601\times10^{-5}$; backward Euler at $h = 0.05$ (the only larger backward-Euler row) has $2.449\times10^{-2}$, a ratio of $285$; at the same step $h=0.02$ it has $9.917\times10^{-3}$, a ratio of $115$; and I computed backward Euler at exactly twice the step, $h=0.04$, which gives relative error $1.967\times10^{-2}$, a ratio of $229$. Fix: either "roughly $300$ times smaller than backward Euler's at $h = 0.05$" or "over a hundred times smaller than backward Euler's at the same step" -- both are true and both make the point more forcefully than $40$.
3. [minor] :::example, "Cross-check against the semi-discrete heat equation": "the stiffness ratio of the semi-discrete system is $\sigma \approx 4/(\pi\,\Delta x)^{2}\cdot\pi^2 = O((\Delta x)^{-2})$". As written the expression equals $4/(\Delta x)^2$, which is the largest $|\lambda|$, not the ratio. From the eigenvalues given, $|\lambda|_{\max} \approx 4/(\Delta x)^2$ and $|\lambda|_{\min} \approx \frac{4}{(\Delta x)^2}\sin^2\frac{\pi\Delta x}{2} \approx \pi^2$, so $\sigma \approx 4/(\pi^2(\Delta x)^2)$ -- the stray $\cdot\pi^2$ should be a division. Also, the eigenvalue formula is printed as $-\frac{4}{(\Delta x)^2}\sin^2\bigl(\frac{k\,\Delta x}{2}\bigr)$, which needs $k$ to carry the $\pi$; writing $\sin^2(k\pi\Delta x/2)$ would make the subsequent $\pi^2$ visible.
4. [minor] :::proof[Parts 1 and 2], the RK4 positivity argument: "grouping as $(1 - t + t^{2}/2) + t^{3}(t - 4)/24$ shows positivity for $t \le 4$ (the first bracket is $\ge 1/2$ and the second is $\ge -32/24\cdot\ldots$)". The grouping is algebraically right, but the parenthetical trails off unfinished and as stated it does not close: the second group's minimum over $[0,4]$ is $-27/24 = -1.125$ at $t=3$, which is larger in magnitude than the $1/2$ floor on the first group, so the two bounds together do not prove positivity (at $t=3$ one needs the first bracket's actual value $2.5$). The unit then gives a correct argument ("more simply ... its minimum ... is $0.270405 > 0$"), which I verified: $g'$ has its unique real root at $z = -1.5960716$ and $g$ there is $0.27039477$. Fix: delete the abandoned grouping and keep the minimum argument.
5. [minor] :::statement part 2's table and :::definition: the stability function of the *implicit* midpoint rule (part 5) and of the *explicit* midpoint rule (part 2) are different ($\frac{2+z}{2-z}$ against $1+z+z^2/2$), and both are called "the midpoint method" in different blocks. The text is careful each time, but a reader scanning the table and then part 5 will trip. Worth distinguishing the names explicitly.
