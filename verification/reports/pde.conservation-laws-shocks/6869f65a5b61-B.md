2. [minor] remark after the breaking proof (line 133): "If it is approached but not attained the
   solution still ceases to be $C^1$ at $t_*$" is not right as written. When the infimum is not
   attained, $\alpha'(y) > m$ for every $y$, so $\partial_y\Phi(y,t_*) = 1 + t_*\alpha'(y) > 0$
   everywhere; $\Phi(\cdot,t_*)$ is still a strictly increasing bijection and Steps 2-4 still give
   a $C^1$ solution *at* $t = t_*$, on the closed strip $0 \le t \le t_*$. What actually fails is
   (i) boundedness of the derivative — $\sup_x|u_x(\cdot,t)| \to \infty$ as $t \uparrow t_*$ still
   holds, since $F \in C^2$ and $g$ bounded make $a'\circ g$ bounded, so $|g'(y_n)|$ stays bounded
   below along a minimising sequence — and (ii) continuation past $t_*$, because for $t > t_*$
   there is a $y$ with $1 + t\alpha'(y) < 0$, $\Phi(\cdot,t)$ is then non-monotone, and two
   characteristics carrying different values meet. The second half of the sentence (the minimising
   sequence must escape to infinity, since a finite limit point would attain the infimum by
   continuity of $\alpha'$) is correct. Fix: "the solution still cannot be continued as a bounded
   $C^1$ solution beyond $t_*$, and $\sup_x|u_x|$ still blows up as $t \uparrow t_*$, but along a
   sequence escaping to spatial infinity rather than at a point".

3. [minor] statement "The entropy condition and the definition of a shock" (line 254): "at a jump
   it forces $u_l \ge u_r$, which is (12)" equates a non-strict inequality with (12), which is the
   strict $u_l > u_r$. The two agree only because at a genuine discontinuity $u_l \ne u_r$; Evans
   makes that step explicitly on p. 143 ("$u_l(x,t) \ge u_r(x,t)$ ... in particular the original
   form of the entropy condition (19) holds at any point of discontinuity"). Fix: "forces
   $u_l \ge u_r$, hence (12) wherever $u$ actually jumps".

4. [minor] application (line 395): "When the light turns green ... (14b) supplies a rarefaction fan
   instead". (14b) is stated two blocks earlier only "for $F$ uniformly convex and $C^2$", and the
   LWR flux used here is strictly concave — the same convexity distinction the unit is careful to
   draw for (11) versus (12) in the preceding remark, and again in the first sentence of this same
   paragraph for the entropy inequalities. The conclusion is right: for strictly concave $F$,
   $F'$ is strictly decreasing, $G = (F')^{-1}$ still exists, and $\rho_l > \rho_r$ gives
   $F'(\rho_l) < F'(\rho_r)$ (here $-100 < 100$ for $\rho_r = 0$), so (14b) applies verbatim with
   the two states exchanged. Fix: say so in half a sentence, rather than invoking (14b) outside
   its stated hypothesis.
