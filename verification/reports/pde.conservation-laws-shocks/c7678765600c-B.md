1. [blocking] definition "Scalar conservation laws, flux, and Burgers' equation" (line 55): the
   third of the "three facts" is stated for the general flux but is only true for Burgers'
   equation, and it contradicts the rest of the same sentence and the display that follows it.
   The text reads: the characteristic equations are "$\dot x = a(z)$, $\dot t = 1$, $\dot z = 0$,
   and Strauss draws from them the three facts ... and the slope $dx/dt$ of that line is the value
   of $u$ carried on it." From $\dot x = a(z)$ the slope is $a(u)$, not $u$; Eq. (5) on the next
   line correctly gives $x = y + t\,a(g(y))$, slope $a(g(y))$. The two disagree whenever
   $a \neq \mathrm{id}$ (e.g. the concave traffic flux used in this unit's own application, where
   the jump speeds are $-20$ and the characteristic speeds $60$ and $-100$, none of them equal to
   the densities $36$ and $180$).

   p. 382 are derived inside Example 3 for $u_t + uu_x = 0$, where $a(u) = u$, so (γ) "the slope of
   each such line is equal to the value of $u(x,t)$ on it" is a statement about Burgers' equation
   only. Strauss's general-$a$ version is on p. 385: the line through $(x,t)$ and $(z,0)$ has slope
   $(x-z)/t = a(u(x,t)) = a(\varphi(z))$. The unit attributes the Burgers-only form of (γ) to the
   general equation and sources it to p. 382.
   Fix: write "the slope $dx/dt$ of that line is the characteristic speed $a(u)$ carried on it",
   and attribute the three lettered principles to Strauss's Burgers example on p. 382 with the
   general-flux form taken from p. 385 (adjusting the frontmatter `loc`, which currently cites only
   "the three lettered principles on p. 382" for this material).
