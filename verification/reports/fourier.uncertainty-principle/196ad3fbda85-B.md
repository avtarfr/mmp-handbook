## Findings
1. [source] proof, step 7 (line 137): "For $\psi$ to be a Schwartz function $\beta$ must be real and negative" reports Stein and Shakarchi (Ch. 5, p. 159, "we must take $\beta = -2B < 0$") faithfully, but the source's reasoning is incomplete: a complex $\beta$ with negative real part also gives a Schwartz function, namely the chirped Gaussian $Ae^{-Bx^2}e^{i\gamma x^2/2}$, which satisfies $\psi' = \beta x\psi$ and is rapidly decreasing with all its derivatives. What actually excludes the chirp is equality in step 2 ($\lvert\mathrm{Re}\,z\rvert \le \lvert z\rvert$), which the unit's own remark supplies. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add fourier.uncertainty-principle stein-shakarchi-2003 "Ch. 5, Thm 4.1 proof, p. 159"`; not a failure, since the unit follows its source and repairs the gap in the remark.
2. [minor] remark, fourth bullet (line 146): the step-2 argument is called "a second way of seeing why the minimiser cannot carry a chirp". By finding 1 it is the only valid way here, the appeal to the Schwartz class in step 7 being the source's gap. Fix: say that step 2 is what rules out a complex $\beta$, rather than presenting it as an alternative to the Schwartz argument.
3. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 143): "for a normalised $\psi$ with $\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^2 \sim c/\lvert x\rvert^{3}$ the boundary term still vanishes, but for slower decay the first integral $\int x^2\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ is infinite". At exactly $c/\lvert x\rvert^3$ the integrand of $\int x^2\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ behaves like $c/\lvert x\rvert$, so that integral already diverges (logarithmically). Fix: "for that decay rate or slower", or move the exponent to $\lvert x\rvert^{3+\varepsilon}$ for the case where the boundary term vanishes and the variance is finite.
