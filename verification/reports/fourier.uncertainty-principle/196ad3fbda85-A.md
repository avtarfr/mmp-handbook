## Findings
1. [source] proof, step 7 (line 137): "For $\psi$ to be a Schwartz function $\beta$ must be real and negative" is not true on its own. With $\beta=-2B+2i\gamma$, $B>0$, $\gamma\neq0$, the chirped Gaussian $\psi=Ae^{-Bx^2}e^{i\gamma x^2}$ is Schwartz and satisfies $\psi'=\beta x\psi$; it fails to attain the bound only because step 2 is then strict (I checked numerically: $B=1$, $\gamma=0.7$ gives $16\pi^2V_xV_\xi=1.49$). Nor does the Cauchy-Schwarz equality case of step 3, applied as it is to the non-negative functions $\lvert x\psi\rvert$ and $\lvert\psi'\rvert$, give more than $\lvert\psi'\rvert=\lambda\lvert x\rvert\lvert\psi\rvert$ pointwise. This is exactly the step Stein and Shakarchi take on p. 159 ("we must take $\beta=-2B<0$"), the unit follows them, and the missing argument is supplied in the remark, so by `docs/contributing/source-authority.md` this is not a failure. It is already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (fourier.uncertainty-principle / stein-shakarchi-2003 / Ch. 5, Thm 4.1 proof, p. 159), so I have not duplicated the entry.
2. [minor] proof, step 7 (line 137) and remark, fourth bullet (line 146): read together, the two blocks give different reasons for $\beta$ being real — the proof attributes it to the Schwartz condition, the remark to the equality in step 2 — and a reader who stops at the proof gets the wrong one. Fix: add half a sentence at the end of step 7, "the reality of $\beta$ in fact comes from the equality in step 2; see the remark", so the deference to the source is visible where the gap is.
3. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 143): "for a normalised $\psi$ with $\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^2\sim c/\lvert x\rvert^{3}$ the boundary term still vanishes, but for slower decay the first integral $\int x^2\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ is infinite" puts the threshold in the wrong place: at exactly $\lvert x\rvert^{-3}$ the variance already diverges (logarithmically), so the contrast the sentence draws does not exist at that exponent. Fix: use $c/\lvert x\rvert^{3+\varepsilon}$, or say "already at $\lvert x\rvert^{-3}$ the first integral is infinite".
4. [minor] proof, step 1 (line 90): "This is the only place the normalisation is used, and the only place rapid decay is needed" is contradicted three lines later by "Both integrals are finite because $\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$" in step 3 (and by step 4, which needs $\psi'\in\mathcal{S}$). Fix: "the only place decay is needed for the identity itself; elsewhere it only keeps the two integrals finite".
5. [minor] proof, step 7 (line 137): "Separating variables, $(\log\psi)' = \beta x$ wherever $\psi\neq0$, so $\psi(x)=Ae^{\beta x^2/2}$" needs one clause to cross the zeros of $\psi$. Fix: "by uniqueness for the linear equation $\psi'=\beta x\psi$, $\psi(x)=\psi(0)e^{\beta x^2/2}$ for every $x$"; the following remark about $A=0$ then reads naturally.
