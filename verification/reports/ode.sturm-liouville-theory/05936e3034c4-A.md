## Findings
1. [minor] proof (lines 90 and 109): "the master identity" names two different things. Step 1 is headed "*The master identity*" for Green's formula $\int(gLf-fLg)=[p(fg'-gf')]_a^b$, and step 3 ends "This is the master identity" of the quite different relation $(\lambda-\overline\mu)(u,v)_w=0$. Fix: head step 1 "*Green's formula for $L$*" (or similar) and keep "the master identity" for step 3, which is the one parts 1, 2 and 6 refer back to.

2. [minor] definition "Regular Sturm-Liouville problem" (line 61): "*every* second-order linear equation with a non-vanishing leading coefficient can be brought to this shape by an integrating factor". For the differential equation that is right, and $p=h a_2=\exp\int a_1/a_2$ is automatically positive; but "this shape" has just been defined to include $w>0$, and $w=h\rho$ has the sign of $\rho/a_2$. An equation such as $u''-\lambda u=0$ comes out with $w=-1$. Fix: add the proviso that the coefficient of the $\lambda$-term must have the same sign as the leading coefficient (otherwise relabel $\lambda\mapsto-\lambda$), so that the weight produced is positive.

3. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", second bullet (line 142): "Where $p$ vanishes the problem is singular and simplicity can fail" is asserted with nothing to back it, unlike the periodic bullet just below, which carries Hassani's Example 19.2.2(b). The classical $p$-vanishing problems a reader will have in mind (Legendre with regularity at $\pm1$, Bessel with boundedness at $0$) all have simple eigenvalues, so the claim reads as unsupported. Fix: either name the mechanism and a case — e.g. Legendre on $(-1,1)$ with no endpoint condition beyond square-integrability, where both $P_n$ and $Q_n$ lie in $L^2(-1,1)$ for $\lambda=n(n+1)$, because $Q_n$'s logarithmic singularities are square-integrable, so the eigenvalue is doubly degenerate — or weaken to "the Wronskian argument no longer applies, because the condition at a singular endpoint is not a linear condition on $(u(a),u'(a))$".

4. [minor] statement part 4 (line 72) versus the second example (lines 218–236): part 4 numbers the eigenvalues $\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\cdots$, while the Robin example numbers from $\lambda_0$ (its negative eigenvalue), so that its $\lambda_1=k_1^2$ is the *second* eigenvalue, not the first. The labels are only labels, but within one unit the clash is avoidable. Fix: say in the example that the eigenvalues have been indexed from $0$ so that $\varphi_0$ is the ground state, or shift the example to $\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\cdots$.

5. [source] note "Part 4 is not proved here" (line 127) and the example's *Zeros* paragraph (line 202), citing hassani-2013 Thm 19.1.9, p. 569: the theorem's last clause, "the eigenfunction $u_n$ corresponding to the eigenvalue $\lambda_n$ has exactly $n$ zeros in its interval of definition", is off by one against the book's own Example 19.2.2(a), p. 571, which numbers the Dirichlet eigenfunctions $\sin(n\pi t/T)$ from $n=1$. The unit reports the theorem as printed and flags the shift explicitly, which is the right handling; recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs`, not held against the unit.
