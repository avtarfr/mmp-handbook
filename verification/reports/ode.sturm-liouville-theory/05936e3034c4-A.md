## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, "Where the hypotheses are used", third bullet (line 143): "*Separated* boundary conditions are needed only for part 3. Parts 1 and 2 hold for any symmetric boundary conditions, periodic ones included." Part 1 of this unit's statement has two clauses: the eigenvalues are real, **and** "every eigenfunction is a constant multiple of a real-valued eigenfunction". The second clause fails for periodic conditions. For $-u''=\lambda u$ on $[-T,T]$ with $u(-T)=u(T)$, $u'(-T)=u'(T)$ — the very example the same bullet goes on to quote — $u = e^{in\pi t/T} = \cos(n\pi t/T) + i\sin(n\pi t/T)$ is an eigenfunction, and $u/c$ is real-valued for no constant $c$, since $e^{i\theta}$ takes values off every line through the origin. The unit's own proof shows why: step 5 (line 113) gets that clause only "By part 3, proved below", and part 3 is what the bullet concedes needs separated conditions. So the remark contradicts the proof. What does survive for merely symmetric conditions is Strauss's Thm 5.3.2 as he states it, "all the eigenfunctions can be chosen to be real valued" (p. 121), which the periodic example satisfies via $\cos$ and $\sin$. Fix: say that reality of the eigenvalues and part 2 hold for any symmetric conditions, and that under symmetric conditions the real-eigenfunction conclusion weakens to Strauss's form — each eigenspace has a real basis — the "constant multiple" form needing simplicity and hence separated conditions.
2. [source] note (line 127) and example (line 202): Hassani's Thm 19.1.9 ends "the eigenfunction $u_n(x)$ corresponding to the eigenvalue $\lambda_n$ has exactly $n$ zeros in its interval of definition" (p. 569), which is consistent only if the eigenvalues are numbered from $n=0$, whereas his own Example 19.2.2(a) numbers $\sin(n\pi t/T)$ on $[0,T]$ from $n=1$. The unit reports the theorem as printed and flags the index shift in its example, which is the right treatment; recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add ode.sturm-liouville-theory hassani-2013 "§19.1, Thm 19.1.9, pp. 568-569"`. Not a defect of our text.
3. [minor] proof, step 5 (line 113): "since the eight boundary constants are real". This unit's definition imposes four constants, $\alpha_1,\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2$; the number eight belongs to Strauss's general (possibly non-separated) form (5.3.4). Fix: "the four boundary constants".
4. [minor] proof, step 1 (line 98): "This is Green's formula of :ref[the previous unit]". That unit states Lagrange's identity and Green's formula for coefficients in $C^2$, i.e. $a_2 = -p \in C^2$ and $a_1 = -p' \in C^1$, while a regular Sturm-Liouville problem assumes only $p \in C^1$. The step's own product-rule derivation needs only $p\in C^1$ and $f,g\in C^2$ and is what actually carries the argument, so there is no gap in the proof, but the cross-reference as phrased appeals to a statement whose hypotheses the data here do not meet. Fix: say that the identity is re-derived under the weaker hypothesis $p \in C^1$, and that it is the $p$-form of the previous unit's Green's formula.
5. [minor] remark, second bullet (line 142): "Where $p$ vanishes the problem is singular and simplicity can fail." No example is given, and the $p$-vanishing cases this handbook actually treats (Legendre, Bessel on the natural interval) all have simple eigenvalues. Fix: either name a problem where it fails or weaken to "the argument of step 7 is no longer available".
