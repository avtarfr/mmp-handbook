[minor] definition (line 61): "A problem failing any of the hypotheses — an infinite interval, a $p$ or $w$ vanishing at an endpoint, a singular coefficient, or boundary conditions that are not separated — is **singular**". The block's `src` names strauss-2008 §11.4 pp. 314-315 and hassani-2013 §19.1 pp. 566-567, neither of which defines a singular system. The definition is Hassani's, §19.2, p. 572, and his four conditions are: interval infinite; $p$ or $w$ vanishes at an endpoint; $q$ not continuous; any of $p,q,w$ singular at an endpoint. Non-separated boundary conditions are **not** on that list — p. 569 says only that changing them so they are no longer separated "may destroy the regularity" — so a periodic problem with smooth positive $p,w$ on a finite interval is neither regular nor singular in Hassani's sense, while this sentence calls it singular. (The frontmatter `loc` does declare pp. 571-572, so the work and pages are covered; it is the block locator and the added clause that are off.) Fix: point the `src` at hassani-2013 §19.2, p. 572 for the list, and either say "not regular" for the boundary-condition clause or cite p. 569 for it.

2. [minor] definition (line 59): "Riley-Hobson-Bence require only that the weight be real and of one sign, so that it may be taken non-negative". RHB state the requirement directly at (17.6), p. 557: $\rho(x)$ is "required to be real and non-negative in the interval $a\le x\le b$" (and p. 567 notes the weight is "finite and non-negative over the natural interval"). They do not phrase it as "of one sign". The effect is the same, but as written the sentence reports a weaker hypothesis than the source gives. Fix: report their condition as stated.

3. [minor] proof, step 1 (line 96): "This is Green's formula of :ref[the previous unit] with $J(f,g) = p(gf' - fg')$". The identity displayed just above has boundary term $[p(fg'-gf')]_a^b$, which is $-J(f,g)$ for that $J$; the sign flip is exactly because $Lu=-(pu')'+qu$ is the negative of the previous unit's divergence-form operator. Everything used later is the displayed identity, which is correct, but the sentence as written equates two expressions of opposite sign. Fix: say that $L$ here is minus the previous unit's $(pu')'+a_0u$, so the boundary term is $-J(f,g)$.
