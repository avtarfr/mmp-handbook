   satisfy the boundary conditions too". This unit's regular Sturm-Liouville problem has **four**
   boundary constants $\alpha_1,\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2$ (definition, line 47). Eight constants
   belong to Strauss's general condition (5.3.4), which is the setting of the previous unit, not
   this one; the slip is the more awkward because the unit's whole point in part 3 is that
   separated conditions (four constants) are stronger than symmetric ones.
   Fix: "since the four boundary constants are real".
2. [minor] statement (line 78): "Parts 1-3 use only Green's formula and the separated boundary
   conditions." Part 3 is not proved from Green's formula: step 7 uses the vanishing of the
   Wronskian at $a$ plus Abel's identity, and leans on $p>0$ and $p\in C^1$ rather than on the
   boundary term. The unit's own idea block (line 84) says so explicitly — "Simplicity is a
   different mechanism" — so the two sentences pull against each other.
   Fix: "Parts 1 and 2 use only Green's formula and the vanishing of the boundary term; part 3
   uses the Wronskian of two solutions; part 4 is where compactness enters."
