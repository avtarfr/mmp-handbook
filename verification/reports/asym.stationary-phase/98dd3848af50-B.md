## Findings
1. [blocking] application (line 343): the oscillatory Airy asymptotic is given with the wrong
   constant. The unit writes $\pi^{-1}(-x)^{-1/4}\sin(\tfrac23(-x)^{3/2}+\tfrac\pi4)$ and attributes
   it to Riley, Hobson and Bence. Their worked example (statement p. 919, conclusion p. 920) ends at
   $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\,(-x)^{1/4}}\sin(\tfrac23(-x)^{3/2}+\tfrac\pi4)$, i.e. $\pi^{-1/2}$, not
   $\pi^{-1}$; so does the standard asymptotic for $\mathrm{Ai}$. The unit is therefore both
   misreporting the cited source and stating a false formula: the claim is off by a factor
   $\sqrt{\pi}=1.7725$. Numerically, at $-x=25$, $\mathrm{Ai}(-25)=0.16352658$, the $\pi^{-1/2}$
   form gives $0.16336723$ and the $\pi^{-1}$ form gives $0.09217009$; at $-x=100$,
   $\mathrm{Ai}(-100)=0.17675339$ against $0.17675593$ and $0.09972386$.
   Fix: write $\pi^{-1/2}(-x)^{-1/4}\sin(\tfrac23(-x)^{3/2}+\tfrac\pi4)$.

2. [blocking] frontmatter, `rhb-2006` loc (line 23): the same wrong constant appears in the
   source locator, which records the book's example as "adding the two contributions to get
   $\pi^{-1}(-x)^{-1/4}\sin(\dots)$". The book gets $\pi^{-1/2}$ (pp. 919-920). The locator
   therefore misdescribes the cited pages.
   Fix: correct $\pi^{-1}$ to $\pi^{-1/2}$ in the locator, and extend the page range of that
   clause to 918-920, since the example concludes on p. 920.

3. [blocking] method, step 6 (line 267): "the relative error should fall like $\lambda^{-1/2}$
   once the endpoint terms are included" contradicts the unit's own part 5 and its own example.
   Part 5 asserts an absolute error $O(\lambda^{-3/2})$ against a leading term of size
   $\lambda^{-1/2}$, so the relative error of the two-term answer falls like $\lambda^{-1}$;
   $\lambda^{-1/2}$ is the relative error of the stationary term *without* the endpoint terms.
   Measured on the unit's own example (relative error of $S+E$ against quadrature):
   $\lambda=50:1.942\times10^{-3}$, $100:9.709\times10^{-4}$, $200:4.787\times10^{-4}$,
   $400:2.663\times10^{-4}$ — the product (relative error)$\times\lambda$ is flat at $0.096$-$0.107$,
   while (relative error)$\times\sqrt{\lambda}$ falls by a factor of $2.6$ over the same range.
   Fix: say the relative error falls like $\lambda^{-1}$ once the endpoint terms are included, and
   like $\lambda^{-1/2}$ without them.

4. [minor] proof "Part 3", step 4 (line ~205): "it is $O(1)$ at once, which already gives the
   error $O(\lambda^{-1})$ relative to the leading term" is ambiguous and, on the natural reading,
   wrong in the same way as finding 3: $O(\lambda^{-1})$ is the *absolute* size of that remainder,
   and relative to the $\lambda^{-1/2}$ leading term it is $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$ at this stage. The
   next sentence, which bootstraps to $O(\lambda^{-3/2})$, is right.
   Fix: "gives an absolute error $O(\lambda^{-1})$, i.e. a relative error $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$ at
   this stage".

5. [minor] application (line 343): "the two symmetric contributions" for $J_n(x)$ is loose. In
   $\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\cos(n\theta-x\sin\theta)\,d\theta$ the phase has exactly one stationary
   point in $(0,\pi)$ for $x>n$; the pair appears only after writing the cosine as
   $\tfrac12(e^{i\cdot}+e^{-i\cdot})$, which is what produces the real combination.
   Fix: say so in half a clause.

6. [minor] application (line ~337): Evans's (56), p. 216, is
   $\det(D^2_{y,z}\phi_+)=(-1)^n\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1-t\kappa_i)$, a product over the $n-1$ principal
   curvatures of the level surface. The unit writes $\prod_i(1-t\kappa_i)^{-1/2}$ with the range of
   $i$ unstated and without the absolute value that $|\det|^{-1/2}$ requires once some
   $1-t\kappa_i<0$.
   Fix: write $\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}|1-t\kappa_i|^{-1/2}$.
