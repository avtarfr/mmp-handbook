- Part 5 (two stationary points **and** two live endpoints, non-constant amplitude): $f=\sin t$, $g=2+t^2$ on $[0,5]$, stationary points $\pi/2$ (max, $\sigma=-1$) and $3\pi/2$ (min, $\sigma=+1$). $\lvert I-S-E\rvert\lambda^{3/2}=105.8,\ 57.2,\ 27.8,\ 29.5$ at $\lambda=50,200,800,3200$ — bounded, so the $O(\lambda^{-3/2})$ of part 5 holds with both $\sigma$'s present. - Edge cases probed without finding a counterexample: $g(c)=0$ (statement still holds, both sides then $O(\lambda^{-3/2})$); $f$ constant on a subinterval (excluded by the finitely-many-non-degenerate hypothesis, and correctly flagged in the remark); $\lambda\to\infty$ with $f''(c)\to0$ (correctly flagged as the failure mode).
- Algebraic steps re-derived by hand and found correct: the Jordan-type bound $\cos2\theta\ge1-4\theta/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/4]$ (both sides agree at the ends and $(\cos2\theta)''=-4\cos2\theta\le0$ there, so concavity gives it), the arc estimate $R\int_0^{\pi/4}e^{-R^2(1-4\theta/\pi)}d\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{4R}(1-e^{-R^2})$, the Taylor-with-integral-remainder formula $h(t)=2\int_0^1(1-s)f''(c+s(t-c))ds$ with $h(c)=f''(c)$, $u'(c)=\sqrt\mu$ and $G(0)=g(c)/\sqrt\mu$, the identity $u\,e^{i\sigma\lambda u^2/2}=\tfrac{\sigma}{i\lambda}\tfrac{d}{du}e^{i\sigma\lambda u^2/2}$ (since $\sigma^2=1$), and the two-step bootstrap in step 4 that upgrades $O(\lambda^{-1})$ to $O(\lambda^{-3/2})$.
