[blocking] :::definition[Standardised sums, ...] (lines 33-38): the block opens "Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be random variables with partial sums $S_n$" -- no independence, no uncorrelatedness -- and then asserts that $U_n = (S_n - n\mu)/\sqrt{n\sigma^2}$ "has mean $0$ and variance $1$ for every $n$". The variance claim is false as written. Counterexample: let $X_i = X$ for every $i$, with $\mathbb{E}X = \mu$ and $\operatorname{var}(X) = \sigma^2 > 0$. These have the stated common mean and common finite variance, but $S_n = nX$, so $U_n = \sqrt{n}\,(X-\mu)/\sigma$ and $\operatorname{var}(U_n) = n$, not $1$. $\operatorname{var}(S_n) = n\sigma^2$ needs the summands to be uncorrelated, and Grimmett & Stirzaker's set-up on pp. 217-218, which this block cites, has them independent. The statement block that follows does assume independence, so this is an omission in the definition only -- but it is a false displayed claim about our own material. Fix: begin the block "Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be independent random variables" (or "uncorrelated", which is all the variance computation needs), and say at the variance claim that this is where it is used.
2. [source] :::remark (line 161) and :::application (line 227), rhb-2006 §30.10, p. 1195: Riley, Hobson and Bence state the theorem for independent $X_i$ with individually different densities, requiring only that each $f_i$ "possess formal means and variances", and conclude in their property (iii) that $Z = n^{-1}\sum_i X_i$ tends to a Gaussian. Finite means and variances alone do not suffice in the non-identically-distributed case; a Lindeberg or Lyapunov condition is needed, which is exactly Grimmett & Stirzaker's (5.10.5b). Their moment-generating-function route additionally presumes every $M_{X_i}$ exists, which is stronger than two moments. The unit handles this correctly under the two-sources rule: it states the theorem in Grimmett & Stirzaker's i.i.d. form, quotes (5.10.5) for the differently-distributed case, and says in the remark and again in the application that the Lyapunov condition is what RHB leave out.
