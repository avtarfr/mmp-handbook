## Findings
1. [minor] :::definition[The six kinds of condition], the Periodic row of the table (line 46):
   "$u$ and $\partial u/\partial n$ agree at identified boundary points". Line 37 defines
   $\partial u/\partial n$ as the *outward* normal derivative, and at two identified points the
   outward normals are opposite, so read literally the row excludes genuinely periodic functions
   (on $-l\le x\le l$, $u=\sin(\pi x/l)$ has $u_x(-l)=u_x(l)=-\pi/l$ but outward normal derivatives
   $+\pi/l$ and $-\pi/l$). I treat this as shorthand rather than an error, because the same row's
   data column says "the domain is closed up" — once the two parts are identified there is no
   outward normal — and because the unit states the condition correctly and explicitly in all three
   places where it is used (line 50 "$u(-l,t)=u(l,t)$ and $u_x(-l,t)=u_x(l,t)$", line 69 "the
   boundary has been removed altogether", and example (c)). Fix: "$u$ and its derivative in a
   consistent direction across the identification agree".
2. [minor] :::remark (line 129), the bullet "*Uniqueness is not existence*": this is the observation
   Riley, Hobson and Bence make immediately after the proof the unit adapts (p. 706: "if a solution
   ... can be found by any means whatever, then that solution is the correct one ... This result is
   the mathematical justification for the method of images"), including the method-of-images
   application, and the unit's "found by any means at all" is close to the book's "by any means
   whatever". The block is labelled `original`. The observation is a commonplace of the subject and
   the overlap is three words in a differently built sentence, so I do not call it a mislabelled
   provenance, but the bullet should carry a `:cite` to rhb p. 706 as the other borrowings in this
   unit do.
3. [minor] :::note (line 54): the table is introduced as being "for the three classes of
   second-order equation in two variables", and the paragraph at line 67 then reads off the
   well-posed rows as Hassani's Box 21.1.9, which is stated for $m$ variables. The unit says "in
   $m$ variables, Hassani's Box 21.1.9", so nothing is wrong; but a reader may miss that the
   open/closed verdicts of the main table are only asserted for two variables.

## Checked
- Counterexample search: tested the verdicts and the stated conditions at their edges.
  - Robin interpolation: $\partial u/\partial n + au = g$ divided by $a$ gives
    $a^{-1}\partial u/\partial n + u = g/a$, so $a\to\infty$ with $g/a$ fixed does approach
    Dirichlet, and $a=0$ is Neumann — the definition's claim is right.
  - The sign claim for Robin: constructed the unit's own counterexample independently and confirmed
    it, see Calculations. Checked that no $a\ge0$ case can behave that way, from
    $\int_V|\nabla w|^2 = -\int_S aw^2$.
  - Checked that the elliptic uniqueness proof really needs each hypothesis the remark names
    (smoothness for Green's identity, realness for $|\nabla w|^2\ge0$, connectedness for "$\nabla w=0
    \Rightarrow w$ constant"), and that the unit is right that rhb state the theorem without
    "connected".
  - Neumann compatibility: re-derived $\int_V\rho = \int_S g$ from the divergence theorem; it is
    necessary, not sufficient, and the unit says exactly that.
  - Periodic eigenvalues on a ring of circumference $2l$: $\cos(n\pi x/l)$ and $\sin(n\pi x/l)$ both
    satisfy $u(-l)=u(l)$, $u_x(-l)=u_x(l)$ with eigenvalue $(n\pi/l)^2$; $n=0$ gives the constant,
    every $n\ge1$ is doubled — as example (c) says, and matching Strauss's Exercise 4(a).
- Calculations:
  - Example (a): $u=A+Bx$, $B-2A=-2$ and $B+(A+B)=A+2B=4$ give $A=8/5$, $B=6/5$; recomputed
    $u'(0)-2u(0)=1.2-3.2=-2$ and $u'(1)+u(1)=1.2+2.8=4$, and $u(0)=1.6$, $u(1)=2.8$. All correct.
  - Normal-derivative rewriting: at $x=0$ the outward normal is $-1$, so $u'(0)-2u(0)=-2$ is
    $\partial u/\partial n + 2u = 2$ (multiply by $-1$), and at $x=1$, $u'(1)+u(1)=4$ is
    $\partial u/\partial n + u = 4$; both have $a>0$, as claimed.
  - The $a=-2$ failure: $-u'(0)-2u(0)=0$ and $u'(1)-2u(1)=0$ are satisfied by $u=1-2x$
    ($-(-2)-2(1)=0$ and $-2-2(-1)=0$) and by $u\equiv0$, so uniqueness fails; and in Strauss's
    normalisation $X'-a_0X=0$ at $0$, $X'+a_lX=0$ at $l$ these conditions are exactly
    $a_0=a_l=-2$, his absorbing case, with $-X''=0\cdot X$ — so "an eigenfunction at $\lambda=0$" is
    right, and so is the identification of Strauss's constants.
  - Example (b): $\int_0^1 f = u'(1)-u'(0) = 0$; for $f=\cos2\pi x$, $u'=\sin(2\pi x)/2\pi$ vanishes
    at both ends, $u=-\cos(2\pi x)/4\pi^2 + D$, and $1/(4\pi^2)=0.0253303$, so $u(0)=D-0.025330$ and
    $u(1/2)=D+0.025330$ are both right.
  - Example (c): $\sin[(n+\frac12)\pi x/L]$ vanishes at $0$ and has derivative
    $\propto\cos[(n+\frac12)\pi]=0$ at $L$; $\cos[(n+\frac12)\pi x/L]$ has zero derivative at $0$ and
    vanishes at $L$; both have eigenvalue $[(n+\frac12)\pi/L]^2$. Verified.
