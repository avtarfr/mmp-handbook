## Findings
1. [blocking] example (c), closing paragraph (line 167, `provenance=original`): "the mixed choice $u(0,t) = u_x(L,t) = 0$ ... produces the eigenfunctions $\cos\bigl[(n+\tfrac12)\pi x/L\bigr]$" is wrong, and wrong in both conditions. $\cos\bigl[(n+\tfrac12)\pi x/L\bigr]$ equals $1$ at $x=0$, so it violates $u(0)=0$; and its derivative at $x=L$ is $-\tfrac{(2n+1)\pi}{2L}\cos(n\pi) \ne 0$, so it violates $u_x(L)=0$ as well. The eigenfunctions of $-X''=\lambda X$, $X(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0$ are $\sin\bigl[(n+\tfrac12)\pi x/L\bigr]$ (checked symbolically: value $0$ at $x=0$, derivative $\propto\sin(n\pi)=0$ at $x=L$). The cosines belong to the *opposite* pairing, which is Strauss §4.1 Exercise 2, p. 92 ("$u_x(0,t)=0$, $u(l,t)=0$ (Neumann at the left, Dirichlet at the right) ... show that the eigenfunctions are $\cos[(n+\tfrac12)\pi x/l]$"), while the pairing the unit names is his Exercise 1; the two exercises appear to have been crossed. Fix: either write $\sin\bigl[(n+\tfrac12)\pi x/L\bigr]$, or swap the ends to $u_x(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$ and keep the cosines.
2. [blocking] note "Which conditions suit which equation" (line 69): "Robin and mixed conditions behave like Dirichlet and Neumann throughout: one scalar condition is imposed at each boundary point, and the table's verdicts are unchanged." The verdicts are *not* unchanged for Robin with a negative coefficient. Counterexample, in the unit's own convention $\partial u/\partial n + a u = g$ with $a=-2$ on $V=(0,1)$ (whose boundary is the closed two-point set the unit itself calls a closed boundary in example (a)): $u(x) = 1-2x$ satisfies $u''=0$, and at $x=0$, $-u'(0)+au(0) = 2-2 = 0$, at $x=1$, $u'(1)+au(1) = -2+2 = 0$ (verified symbolically). So the homogeneous elliptic Robin problem on a closed boundary has a non-trivial solution: uniqueness fails, and the corresponding inhomogeneous problem is not solvable for all data. The unit's own cited source has this: Strauss §4.3, pp. 92–93 writes the conditions as $X'-a_0X=0$, $X'+a_lX=0$ and says they correspond to radiation for $a_0,a_l>0$ and absorption for $a_0,a_l<0$, precisely because the sign changes the spectrum (the above is $a_0=a_l=-2$, giving $\lambda=0$ as an eigenvalue). The claim is also what example (a) leans on ("as the elliptic row of the table predicts"), where it is safe because $a=2$ and $a=1$ are positive. Fix: restrict the sentence to $a \ge 0$ (Strauss's radiating case), and note that a sufficiently negative coefficient puts an eigenvalue at zero and destroys uniqueness.
3. [blocking] note, last paragraph (line 71): "Arfken, Weber and Harris note that a solution may be unique only to within a constant". That is not on arfken-2013 pp. 411–413, the pages cited for this block; I grepped those three pages for "constant" and the single occurrence is about the wave speed $c$ in Eq. (9.23). What Arfken, Weber and Harris do say there is that "An exact analysis of the role of boundary conditions is complicated and beyond the scope of the present text" (p. 412) and that "Table 9.1 oversimplifies the situation in various ways", with the Helmholtz example (p. 413) — the other two clauses of the sentence, both correctly reported. The "unique, sometimes to within a constant" qualification is Riley, Hobson and Bence's, p. 705. Fix: move that clause to the Riley-Hobson-Bence half of the sentence.
4. [minor] statement "Uniqueness for the elliptic Dirichlet and Neumann problems" (line 88): "Let $V$ be a connected region". Riley, Hobson and Bence's uniqueness theorem (p. 706) says only "in a region $V$ and on its boundary $S$"; connectedness is added here. The addition is needed for the conclusion as stated (their proof's step "$\nabla w = 0$, i.e. $w$ ... is a constant in $V$" fails on a disconnected $V$), and the unit's remark does say where it is used — but the source is being quietly corrected rather than reported, which is what `docs/contributing/source-authority.md` asks drafters not to do. Fix: a half-sentence saying that Riley, Hobson and Bence do not state connectedness and that it is added because their Step 5 needs it.
5. [minor] remark, last bullet (line 136): "Strauss sets precisely this calculation, for $\nabla^2u=f$ with $\partial u/\partial n = 0$, as an exercise and reads it physically: for steady heat flow it says the total heat generated inside must equal the total flux out". Strauss §1.5 Exercise 4(c), p. 28 *asks* the reader for a physical interpretation ("Can you give a physical interpretation of part (a) and/or (b) for either heat flow or diffusion?"); he does not give one, so the reading is ours. Also, in Strauss's own case the flux out is zero by hypothesis, so for that case the reading is "the total heat generated must vanish"; the balance-of-flux reading belongs to the general identity $\int_V\rho = \int_S g$ displayed just above. Fix: mark the physical reading as ours, and attach it to the general identity.
