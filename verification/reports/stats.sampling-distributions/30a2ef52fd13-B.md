## Findings
1. [minor] :::method (line 253), step 5: "Since $F^{-1} \sim F(s,r)$, it is conventional to put the larger sample variance on top and make a one-tailed statement." The automated check flags the 10-word run against rhb-2006 p. 1294, and comparing by hand the page reads "it is conventional to put the larger sample variance on the top, so". The block is labelled `provenance=original`. My judgement is that this falls just inside standard wording -- it states a universal laboratory convention in the only natural phrasing, the reason given for it ($F^{-1}\sim F(s,r)$) is ours and is not RHB's argument, and the rest of the six-step method is entirely ours. But it is at the long end of what "standard wording" covers, and since the frontmatter already cites rhb-2006 §31.7.6, pp. 1292-1294 for exactly this material, the cheapest fix is to attach `src="rhb-2006:§31.7.6, p. 1294"` to that sentence or to reword it ("by convention the ratio is taken with the larger variance in the numerator"). A reviewer could reasonably call this blocking; I do not, for the reasons above.
2. [minor] the other two `resembles` warns are, on inspection, unavoidable terminology and need no action: ":::definition (line 78) ... 'the chi squared distribution with degrees of freedom'" against rhb-2006 p. 1297, and "'the distribution with and degrees of freedom written'" against grimmett-stirzaker-2020 p. 137 ("is said to have the F distribution with $r$ and $s$ degrees of freedom, written $F(r,s)$"). Both are the names of the objects; there is no other way to introduce them, and the block is `adapted` and cites both pages. Recorded here only so the judgement is on the record.
3. [minor] :::definition[The chi-squared, $t$ and $F$ distributions]: "the gamma moment generating function is multiplicative in the shape parameter, so a sum of independent $\chi^2(d_1)$ and $\chi^2(d_2)$ variates is $\chi^2(d_1+d_2)$". The mgf of $\Gamma(\lambda,t)$ is $(\lambda/(\lambda-\theta))^t$, which is multiplicative *in $t$ at fixed $\lambda$* -- so the sentence is right only because both variates share $\lambda = \tfrac12$. Adding "at a common rate $\lambda = \tfrac12$" removes the ambiguity.
4. [minor] :::statement: the second sample is introduced as drawn from $N(\nu,\sigma^2)$, but $\nu$ never appears again and plays no role (the $F$ ratio is free of both means). Harmless, but a reader looks for where $\nu$ is used.
