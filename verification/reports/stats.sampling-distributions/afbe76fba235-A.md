## Findings
1. [minor] chi-squared/t/F definition (line 104): the limit $f_T(t) \to (2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/2}$ is justified only by the $t$-dependent factor, and that factor is written with exponent $-r/2$, while the density has $-\tfrac12(r+1)$. The limit of the normalising constant $\Gamma(\tfrac12(r+1))/(\sqrt{\pi r}\,\Gamma(\tfrac12 r)) \to (2\pi)^{-1/2}$ is not mentioned. The claim is true. Fix: add "and the constant tends to $(2\pi)^{-1/2}$ (Stirling, or $\Gamma(x+\tfrac12)/\Gamma(x)\sim\sqrt x$)", and note that the extra factor $(1+t^2/r)^{-1/2} \to 1$.
2. [minor] chi-squared definition (line 81) against the $t$ definition (line 90 on): the symbol $t$ means the gamma shape parameter ($t=\tfrac12 d$) and, a few lines later, the $t$ variable and statistic. Grimmett and Stirzaker do the same, so the text is faithful, but within one block it reads as a change of meaning. Fix: write the shape parameter as $\tfrac12 d$ directly, or rename it (for example $\kappa$).
3. [minor] proof, Step 2 (line 180): "no other law on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with independent identically distributed coordinates has a rotation-invariant density" is slightly too strong. Any centred normal $N(0,\tau^2)$ also has one, and the remark at line 240 says "only a centred normal". Fix: say "no law other than a centred normal".
4. [minor] Step 5 and the chi-squared definition (line 88): additivity is justified by "the gamma moment generating function is multiplicative in the shape parameter", which also needs uniqueness of the mgf, and no listed prerequisite covers that (prob.generating-functions is not listed). The fact Step 5 actually needs, that a sum of $k$ independent squared standard normals is $\chi^2(k)$, is available from the listed prereqs prob.joint-distributions and prob.multivariate-normal, so the proof is not left unsupported. Fix: cite one of those for the sum-of-squares form, or add the generating-functions unit to prereqs.
5. [minor] Step 7 (line 228): the $F$ density is reached "after collecting constants", with the constants not shown. I re-derived it: the integrand is $\propto w^{\frac12(r+s)-1}e^{-\frac w2(1+rx/s)}$, and the constants give the stated formula exactly. Fix (optional): show the one line $\frac{r}{s}\,\frac{(rx/s)^{r/2-1}}{2^{(r+s)/2}\Gamma(\frac r2)\Gamma(\frac s2)}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{r+s}2)\,[\tfrac12(1+rx/s)]^{-(r+s)/2}$.
6. [minor] frontmatter, arfken-2013 loc (line 19), against the body (line 60): the loc calls Arfken's argument "second-order", but the body calls it "first-order". Arfken (p. 1168) calls the approximation $\alpha^2 \approx s^2/n$ "good to first order". Fix: make the loc say "first-order".
7. [minor] remark, "$n \ge 2$" (line 246): "there is no $S^2$" and "the theorem degenerates correctly rather than failing" sit uneasily together. Fix: say that for $n=1$ conclusions 1 and 3 are vacuous (the complement is $\{0\}$ and $\chi^2(0)$ is the point mass at $0$), and only conclusion 2 survives.
