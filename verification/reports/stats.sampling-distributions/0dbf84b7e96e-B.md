## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", Normality (line 240): the sentence "Rotation invariance of the joint density is equivalent to the coordinates being independent and normal with a common variance" is false as written. Rotation invariance does not imply independence: the uniform law on the unit ball of R^2 (or a multivariate t) has a rotation-invariant density, but its coordinates are dependent (simulated correlation of X1^2 and X2^2 on the disc: -0.333). The correct statement is the one Step 2 of our own proof already gives (line 180): among laws with *independent identically distributed* coordinates, only the centred normal has a rotation-invariant density. So the remark is wrong in an `original` block and contradicts the proof's own, correct, version. Fix: "For a vector with independent, identically distributed coordinates, rotation invariance of the joint density holds only when the common law is a centred normal" (Maxwell's characterisation), or simply refer back to Step 2.
2. [minor] method, item 5 (line 260): "we always put the larger sample variance in the numerator, so that F >= 1 and only the upper tail is ever consulted" is a usable convention, but once the numerator is chosen from the data the upper-tail probability is the one-sided figure; for the two-sided question "do the variances differ?" it must be doubled (equivalently, compared with alpha/2). The example (line 317 ff.) reports Pr(F(5,7) > 3.1241) = 0.0848 under this rule without saying it is one-sided (the two-sided value is 0.170). The conclusion drawn there is unaffected. Fix: add one clause saying so. Also, "F^{-1} ~ F(s,r)" here uses r, s, which are not defined in the method; write F(n_1-1, n_2-1).
3. [minor] definition of sample variance (line 60): "Grimmett and Stirzaker choose it because it makes E(S^2) = sigma^2 exactly" slightly overstates p. 135, which says statisticians use it "for reasons explained in statistics textbooks" and then notes unbiasedness as a property it at least has. Fix: "Grimmett and Stirzaker note that with this divisor E(S^2) = sigma^2".
4. [minor] remark, Independence (line 242): "multiply by V^{-1/2}" reuses V, which in the statement is the N(0,1) variable sqrt(n)(Xbar - mu)/sigma. Fix: use another letter for the covariance matrix (e.g. Sigma).
5. [minor] example (line 265): "a stirred ice-water-free reference bath" is an odd phrase for a bath at 20 C; "a stirred water reference bath" says what is meant.
