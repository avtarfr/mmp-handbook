## Findings
1. [blocking] method, step 5 (line 260), and example, variance ratio (lines 319-325): the method says $F = S_2^2/S_1^2 \sim F(n_2-1, n_1-1)$ and then says "we always put the larger sample variance in the numerator, so that $F \ge 1$ and only the upper tail is ever consulted". Once the numerator is chosen after looking at the data, the ratio is no longer $F(n_2-1,n_1-1)$ under equal variances: it is $\max$ of the two orientations, and its upper tail is $\Pr(F(n_2-1,n_1-1) > f) + \Pr(F(n_1-1,n_2-1) > f)$. Reading only one upper tail roughly halves the tail probability, so a nominal level $\alpha$ test really has size about $2\alpha$. The example repeats the error: "Putting the larger variance on top, $F = 3.1241$ ... a draw from $F(5,7)$ if the two days share one $\sigma$". The two-sided tail for the ordered ratio is $\Pr(F(5,7) > 3.1241) + \Pr(F(7,5) > 3.1241) = 0.1989$, not $0.0848$. The value 0.0848 is the correct number for $\Pr(F(5,7) > 3.1241)$, but it is the right tail only for a one-sided question fixed before seeing the data. This is our own (original) material. RHB pp. 1294-1295 use the same one-tailed convention, which is recorded as a discrepancy; the method block does not cite it. Fix: either (a) fix the orientation before seeing the data, for a one-sided alternative such as "re-seating made the probe noisier", say so, and keep $F(5,7)$ and $0.0848$; or (b) for a two-sided comparison, keep "larger on top" but double the upper tail (or sum the two tails as above), and give $0.199$ (or $2 \times 0.0848 = 0.170$ with the doubling rule) in the example and in the closing summary of tail probabilities at line 325.

[minor] remark, Normality (line 240): "Rotation invariance of the joint density is equivalent to the coordinates being independent and normal with a common variance" is false as written: the uniform law on a ball, or a multivariate $t$, is rotation invariant without independent coordinates. The correct statement, which Step 2 gives (line 180), needs i.i.d. coordinates as a premise. Fix: "For i.i.d. coordinates, rotation invariance of the joint density holds only for a centred normal law (Maxwell's characterisation)".

The final density is correct. Fix: "the powers of $\tfrac12$ leave a factor $\sqrt2$, which turns $\sqrt{2\pi r}$ into $\sqrt{\pi r}$".
