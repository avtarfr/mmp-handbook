## Findings

1. [minor] remark, bullet "Convergence of the Born series is not covered by our Neumann theorem"
   (line ~197): two sentences are stated without the hypothesis they need. "The wavenumbers at which
   the homogeneous equation $\chi=\mathcal{T}\chi$ has a nonzero solution are exactly the bound
   states and resonances of $U$" omits virtual (antibound) states, and "the series diverges
   precisely at energies below the binding energy of the potential's single bound state" holds for
   the *attractive* delta only. The second example takes $u_{0}=+1$ for its numbers; that potential
   is repulsive and has no bound state, yet the geometric series still diverges for $k<u_{0}/2$,
   because the pole of $T(k)=2k/(2k+iu_{0})$ then sits at $k=-iu_{0}/2$ on the *negative* imaginary
   axis — a virtual state, not a bound state. The example's own "Where the divergence comes from"
   paragraph is correctly qualified ("For an attractive delta, $u_{0}<0$"), so this is a loose
   forward reference rather than a wrong result. Fix: in the remark write "the zeros of the Jost
   function — bound states, virtual states and resonances" and add "for an attractive delta" to the
   binding-energy sentence.

2. [minor] note[Conventions] (line ~30): "Strauss suppresses $e^{+i\omega t}$ instead ... and
   Strauss's results are conjugated before being used" is drawn from §13.3 (his Eqs. (7)-(10) do fix
   $Ae^{i(\omega t-kz)}$, and p. 368 does say $e^{i\omega t}e^{\pm ikr}/r$ is outgoing for the minus
   sign — both correctly reported). But the only Strauss material actually used downstream is §13.4,
   a time-independent Schrodinger problem in which no time factor is fixed at all, and the second
   example uses his definitions of $R$ and $T$ *directly* ("exactly as in Strauss's §13.4"), not
   conjugated. Nothing computed is wrong — $\lvert R\rvert^{2}+\lvert T\rvert^{2}=1$ is invariant
   under conjugation and under swapping the two ends, and the parenthetical about his beam arriving
   from the other side is right — but the blanket sentence is wider than the sources warrant. Fix:
   restrict the claim to §13.3 and note that §13.4 fixes no time factor.

3. [minor] proof, step 2 (line ~160): "$\partial_{r}(\mathcal{T}\psi)-ik\,\mathcal{T}\psi
   = O(r^{-2})$" is asserted immediately after the asymptotic expansion of $\mathcal{T}\psi$. An
   asymptotic expansion may not in general be differentiated; here the estimate is obtained by
   differentiating the explicit kernel under the integral sign, which is legitimate (the integrand
   and its $r$-derivative are dominated on $r>2a$), but the step does not say so. Fix: one clause
   saying the derivative is taken under the integral.

4. [minor] frontmatter, prereqs (line 7): `green.neumann-series` comes *after* this unit in
