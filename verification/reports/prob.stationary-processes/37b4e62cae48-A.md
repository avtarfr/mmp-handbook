## Findings
1. [source] statement "The spectral theorem for autocorrelation functions", Inversion paragraph
   (lines ~106-111), reporting Grimmett and Stirzaker's Theorem (9.3.15), p. 442: the book's
   hypothesis is that $\widetilde F$ is differentiable with derivative $f$, and the formula is then
   asserted only "at every point $\lambda$ at which $f$ is differentiable". Differentiability of $f$
   is far stronger than anything the conclusion needs, and reads like a misprint for "at which
   $\widetilde F$ is differentiable". The unit reports the hypothesis exactly as printed, says so
   explicitly in the remark, and does not lean on it - its own Proposition reaches the same formula
   from absolute summability alone. No fault of the unit; recorded with
   `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add`.
2. [minor] proof "The spectral theorem", Step 4 (line ~190): "any function carrying those three
   properties" follows a sentence that lists four ($\rho(0)=1$, $|\rho|\le1$, non-negative
   definiteness, uniform continuity), so the reader has to work out that $|\rho|\le1$ is not one of
   Bochner's three. Fix: name them - non-negative definite, continuous, $\rho(0)=1$ - and give
   $|\rho|\le1$ as a consequence rather than in the same list.
3. [minor] definition "Autocovariance, autocorrelation, spectral distribution and density" (lines
   ~84-90): the spectral distribution function is defined only through the continuous-time
   representation $\rho(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}dF(\lambda)$, yet the Proposition's
   proof (Step 5) and the remark both speak of *the* spectral distribution and density of a
   discrete-time sequence, which live on $(-\pi,\pi]$ and are only introduced in passing inside the
   following statement. Fix: add the one-line discrete-time case to the definition block, so that
   "spectral density" has a single stated meaning before it is used.
4. [minor] statement "A clean sufficient condition in discrete time" (line ~121): the series
   $\sum_n\rho(n)e^{-in\lambda}$ converges absolutely and uniformly on the whole of $\mathbb{R}$,
   not merely on $(-\pi,\pi]$, and its sum is $2\pi$-periodic; restricting the claim to one period
   slightly understates what Step 1 proves and hides the periodicity that the aliasing discussion in
   the remark relies on. Fix: state uniform convergence on $\mathbb{R}$ and note the periodicity.
