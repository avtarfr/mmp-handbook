## Findings
1. [source] grimmett-stirzaker-2020, §9.3, Theorem (9.3.15) with (9.3.16), p. 442. The book states the inversion formula "at every point $\lambda$ at which $f$ is differentiable", asking for differentiability of the *density* rather than of $\widetilde F$ (already assumed) or continuity of $f$. The book never checks it: Example (9.3.23) applies (16) to $\rho(n)=\alpha^{|n|}$ and Example (9.3.19) to $\rho(n)=\delta_{n0}$ without comment. Our unit reports the hypothesis exactly as printed, flags it in the remark under "The hypothesis of the inversion theorem, as printed", and does not rely on it (the Proposition assumes $\sum_n|\rho(n)|<\infty$ instead). This is deference to the source, not a defect: recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add prob.stationary-processes grimmett-stirzaker-2020 "Sec. 9.3, Theorem (9.3.15) with Eq. (9.3.16), p. 442" ...`, and the unit passes.
2. [minor] statement "A clean sufficient condition in discrete time" (line 114) carries `provenance=original`. Its *proof* is genuinely ours (Fejér-style Cesàro means, no Bochner), and the extra conclusions — absolute and uniform convergence, continuity, non-negativity — are not in the book. But the formula and the summability hypothesis are Grimmett and Stirzaker's (9.3.16) and (9.3.18), which the preceding block has just attributed to them. Fix: keep `original` for the packaging and the proof but add a `src` noting that the formula is (9.3.16) and the hypothesis (9.3.18), so that a reader of this block alone is not misled.
3. [minor] naming is inconsistent: "Wiener and Khinchin" in the note and in the statement block (Grimmett and Stirzaker's spelling), "Wiener-Kinchin" in the remark heading and body (Riley, Hobson and Bence's). Since the whole point of that remark is that two different theorems share a name, the reader needs to be told the spelling difference is the sources' and not a distinction between the theorems. Fix: one sentence saying so, or one spelling throughout.
4. [minor] statement, discrete time (line 101): "the mass of the continuous-time $F$ may be folded into $(-\pi,\pi]$". The $F$ in question is the representing distribution on $\mathbb{R}$ of (9.3.11) for the *discrete-time* $\rho$, not a continuous-time object. Fix: "the mass of the representation on $\mathbb{R}$".
5. [minor] remark, "Conventions" table (line 236): the one-sided row gives $S_+(\omega)=2S(\omega)$ for $\omega\ge0$ with no statement of what happens at $\omega=0$, where the usual engineering convention leaves $S_+(0)=S(0)$. Fix: "for $\omega>0$", or a parenthesis.
