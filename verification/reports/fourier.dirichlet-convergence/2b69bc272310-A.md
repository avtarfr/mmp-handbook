## Findings
1. [blocking] method, step 5 (line 216): "if $f$ is monotone on a one-sided neighbourhood of $x_0$ on each side ... Folland's theorem still delivers the half-sum" attributes to Folland's Theorem 8.43 a hypothesis weaker than the one it has. That theorem (p. 266) assumes $f\in BV(\mathbb{T})$ — bounded variation over a whole period — not bounded variation or monotonicity near the one point $x_0$; local monotonicity at $x_0$ says nothing about the variation of $f$ elsewhere. The unit itself states the hypothesis globally two blocks earlier ("if $f$ is of bounded variation then $S_Nf(x)\to\tfrac12[f(x+)+f(x-)]$ at *every* $x$", line 201), so the method and the remark give the same theorem two different hypotheses. Fix: either state the test as Folland states it ("if $f$ has bounded variation over a period — which holds in particular when $f$ is piecewise monotone, as for $\sqrt x$ and $x^{1/3}$ — then ..."), or keep the local form and supply the missing step, which the unit already has to hand: replace $f$ near $x_0$ by a function $g$ that agrees with $f$ on $(x_0-\delta,x_0+\delta)$ and is constant outside it, so that $g\in BV$ and Theorem 8.43 applies to $g$, and then transfer the conclusion by Folland's localization Theorem 8.44, cited in the remark at line 195. Note the conclusion itself is true either way; it is the justification and the attribution that are wrong.
