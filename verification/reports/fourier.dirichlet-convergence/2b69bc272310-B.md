[minor] method, step 5 (line 216): "if $f$ is monotone on a one-sided neighbourhood of $x_0$ on each side — which covers $\sqrt{x}$, $x^{1/3}$ and the like — Folland's theorem still delivers the half-sum." Folland's Thm 8.43 (p. 266) hypothesises $f\in BV(\mathbb T)$, i.e. bounded variation over a whole period, not monotonicity near the one point; local two-sided monotonicity does not by itself give that (a function monotone on each side of $x_0$ may have unbounded variation elsewhere in the period). The unit gets this right in the remark, where it establishes global monotonicity on $(-\pi,\pi)$ before invoking Folland. Fix: in step 5 say "if $f$ is piecewise monotone over the whole period — so that $f\in BV$ — which covers $\sqrt x$, $x^{1/3}$ and the like", or route the local version through Folland's localisation theorem 8.44.

[minor] proof, Step 4 (line ~150, "Each $\phi_N$ is odd, so the identical computation on $(-\pi,0)$ gives the same conclusion there"): oddness of each $\phi_N$ makes each *product* $\phi_N\phi_M$ even, which is the property actually used; as written the inference is stated one step short. Not wrong, just compressed. Fix: "each product $\phi_N\phi_M$ is even, so the integral over $(-\pi,0)$ equals the one over $(0,\pi)$".
