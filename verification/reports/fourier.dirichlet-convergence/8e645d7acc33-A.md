## Findings
1. [minor] remark, "The hypothesis here is sufficient, never necessary": "Riley, Hobson and Bence and Arfken, Weber and Harris both quote a list called the Dirichlet conditions - periodicity, single-valuedness with finitely many finite jumps, finitely many maxima and minima per period, and convergence of $\int\lvert f\rvert$ over a period" attributes one four-item list to both books. The four items are Riley, Hobson and Bence's (p. 415) exactly; Arfken names only two as the Dirichlet conditions on p. 935 - finitely many finite discontinuities and finitely many extreme values on $[0,2\pi]$ - and requires the existence of the coefficient integrals in a separate sentence, with no periodicity or single-valuedness clause (periodicity is implicit in his setting, which is why this is only imprecision and not a changed hypothesis). The sibling unit fourier.fourier-series separates the two lists correctly, so the two units describe the same pages differently. Fix: "Riley, Hobson and Bence quote four conditions ... ; Arfken, Weber and Harris name the middle two, require the coefficient integrals to exist separately, and add explicitly that the conditions are sufficient but not necessary." The claim actually attributed to Arfken here - sufficient but not necessary - is right (footnote 1, p. 935).
2. [minor] definition, line 60 ("Conventions"): "every statement below transfers with $N+\tfrac12$ rescaled accordingly" is hard to act on, since under $x\mapsto 2\pi x/T$ it is the argument of the kernel, not the half-integer, that carries the scale. Fix: say that $D_N(2\pi\theta/T)$ replaces $D_N(\theta)$ and the oscillating factor becomes $\sin[(2N+1)\pi\theta/T]$.
