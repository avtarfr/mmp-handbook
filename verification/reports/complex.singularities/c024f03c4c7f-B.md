2. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", second bullet (line 137): "Riemann's criterion is weaker than boundedness. It requires only $(z-z_0)f(z)\to0$, which boundedness implies but which also allows mild unboundedness such as $\lvert f\rvert \sim \log\lvert z-z_0\rvert^{-1}$." For the functions this unit is about -- $f$ analytic on a punctured disc -- there is no such function. The criterion in the statement block above is an *equivalence* with removability, and a removable singularity makes $f$ bounded near $z_0$ (part 1 of the next statement), so on a punctured disc "$(z-z_0)f \to 0$" and "bounded near $z_0$" are the same hypothesis; logarithmic growth is impossible because $\log z$ is not single-valued on a punctured disc. As written the bullet tells the reader that an analytic function can satisfy Riemann's criterion while being unbounded, which is false. This is an `original` block, so the claim is ours. Fix: say that for analytic $f$ the limit form and boundedness are equivalent, and that the limit form is nevertheless the useful one because it is what the Cauchy-formula-with-exceptional-point argument needs (which is the point the bullet's last sentence, about Ahlfors p. 124, makes correctly).
