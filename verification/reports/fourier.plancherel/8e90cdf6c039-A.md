## Findings
1. [minor] idea (line 104): the idea runs the argument on $h=f\star g$, with $h(0)=\int\overline{f}g$ and $\hat h=\overline{\hat f}\hat g$, which is the complex conjugate of the identity the proof actually establishes — the proof sets $h=g\star f$, so that $h(0)=(f,g)$ and $\hat h=\overline{\hat g}\hat f$, and lands on part 1 as stated. The two are equivalent, but the idea as written describes $(g,f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\hat g,\hat f)$, not the displayed statement. Fix: write $h=g\star f$ and $h(0)=\int\overline{g}f$ in the idea, matching step 1 of the proof.
2. [minor] frontmatter, `prereqs` (line 7): `fourier.fourier-transform` is relied on repeatedly and is not listed — for the convention $\hat f(k)=\int fe^{-ikx}dx$ and the conventions table (statement, line 84), for the triangle entry and the pulse transform in the first example (lines 192–198), for $\widehat{e^{-a\lvert x\rvert}}=2a/(a^2+k^2)$ in the second (line 224), and for "read the table backwards" in method step 2 (line 178). It is in the transitive closure through both listed Fourier prerequisites, so nothing is unsupported, but `fourier.convolution-theorem` and `fourier.fourier-inversion` both list it explicitly and this unit leans on its table for a worked number. Fix: add `fourier.fourier-transform` to `prereqs`.
3. [minor] statement, part 3 (line 82) and proof "Part 3", step 3 (line 154): the words "unitary" and "$U^{\dagger}$", and the result "a surjective linear isometry of a Hilbert space is unitary, with $U^{-1}=U^{\dagger}$", are referred to :ref[]{to=funcan.hilbert-spaces}, whose scope in `content/map.yaml` is inner-product spaces, completeness, $\ell^2$ and $L^2$ and the parallelogram law. Adjoints and unitary operators are the scope of `funcan.adjoint-operators`. So the symbol $U^{\dagger}$ is used without a definition in the unit or in any listed prerequisite. (The substance is safe: step 5 proves inner-product preservation by polarisation from scratch.) Fix: point the reference at `funcan.adjoint-operators` and add it to `prereqs`, keeping `funcan.hilbert-spaces` for the Hilbert-space structure.
4. [minor] definition (line 49): "linear in the first slot, as in :ref[]{to=measure.lp-spaces}". `measure.lp-spaces` does not define the $L^2$ inner product and nowhere states a linearity convention — its only mention of an inner product is the covariance remark in its application block. The convention is the handbook's house convention (docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md, "Notation"), and the unit defines it here anyway. Fix: drop the attribution to `measure.lp-spaces` for this point, or attribute it to `funcan.hilbert-spaces`; keep the `measure.lp-spaces` reference for density and completeness, which it does contain (its parts 3 and 4).
5. [minor] note "Two different theorems called Parseval" (line 96): two small inaccuracies about Riley, Hobson and Bence. (i) Their (13.45), $\int f^{*}g\,dx=\int[\tilde f]^{*}\tilde g\,dk$, is *sesquilinear*, not "bilinear" — it is conjugate-linear in $f$, as the unit itself says in the same clause ("conjugated like (a)"). (ii) "reserve the name *multiplication theorem* for" overstates: on p. 450 they introduce (13.45) as "the most general form" of Parseval's theorem, "also called the multiplication theorem", so they use both names for it. Fix: "sesquilinear in two functions, like neither (a) nor (b) exactly", and "also call (13.45) the multiplication theorem".
6. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 170): the illustration does not exhibit a failure. For $f=\lvert x\rvert^{-1/2}\chi_{[-1,1]}\in L^1\setminus L^2$ the transform is not in $L^2$ either (if $f\in L^1$ and $\hat f\in L^2$ then $f$ agrees a.e. with the $L^2$-inverse transform of $\hat f$ and so lies in $L^2$), so part 2 would assert $\infty=\infty$ — true but empty. The phrase "what is true instead is the Hausdorff-Young inequality" implies the identity is false here, which it is not. Fix: say that the identity degenerates to $\infty=\infty$ and carries no information, so the usable substitute is Hausdorff-Young.
7. [minor] proof "Part 3", step 4 (line 156): "so $\widehat{f*g_t}\to\hat f$ uniformly and in $L^2$; the two limits must coincide" is the one assertion of the proof that is not argued. Identifying a uniform (hence everywhere-pointwise) limit with an $L^2$ limit needs the a.e.-convergent subsequence of the $L^2$-convergent sequence. This matches Folland's own phrasing on p. 253, so it is faithful, but the step is otherwise spelled out in unusual detail and this link is not. Fix: add "and an $L^2$-convergent sequence has an a.e.-convergent subsequence, so the uniform limit and the $L^2$ limit agree almost everywhere".
8. [minor] example (lines 219, 233, 236): digit presentation. $2\pi/3=2.09439510239\ldots$, so writing "$=2.0943951024\ldots$" puts an ellipsis after a rounded final digit (the quadrature line two sentences later, "returns $2.0943951024$ ... to ten digits", is correct). Similarly $\pi/70$ is printed as $0.0448798950\ldots$ (a truncation) and then the quadrature as $0.0448798951$ (a rounding) of the same number $0.04487989505\ldots$, which reads as a disagreement in the last digit. Fix: use one convention, e.g. $2.0943951024$ and $0.0448798951$ with no ellipsis, or truncate both.
