## Findings
1. [blocking] note "The three rules the method uses" (line 25), statement "The subsidiary equation"
   (line 59) and method step 5 (line 112): the letters $F$ and $f$ change meaning inside the unit.
   The note *declares the unit's convention* — "Write $\mathcal{L}\{F\}(s) = f(s) = \int_0^\infty
   e^{-st}F(t)\,dt$", i.e. $F$ is the time function and $f$ its transform (Arfken's convention), and
   rules 1-3 and the remark (lines 155-156, "$F(+0)$, not $F(0)$", and the Bessel discussion with
   $tF''+F'+tF = 0$ transforming to $(s^2+1)f'(s)+sf(s)=0$) all use it. But the statement block writes
   "write $Y = \mathcal{L}\{y\}$, $F = \mathcal{L}\{f\}$", exactly swapping the two roles, and the
   statement, its proof and both examples then use $F(s)$ for a transform and $f(t)$ for the forcing
   throughout. Worst, **method step 5 uses both conventions in one bullet**: it gives
   "$\mathcal{L}\{(-t)^nF(t)\} = f^{(n)}(s)$" (Arfken: $F$ a function of $t$) and two lines later the
   Bromwich integral "$f(t) = \int_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}e^{st}F(s)\,ds/2\pi i$" (Strauss:
   $F$ a transform, $f$ a function). Read under the unit's own declared convention the second formula
   says that a function equals a contour integral of itself. This is the two-sources case: Arfken
   (p. 1016) writes $\mathcal{L}\{F(t)\} = f(s)$, while Strauss (p. 353, Eq. (1)) and Riley, Hobson and
   Bence (p. 453, Eq. (13.53)) write $F(s)$ and $\bar f(s)$ for the transform of $f(t)$; the unit
   declares one and then silently imports the other. Fix: pick one convention, say in the note which
   book uses which (STYLE_GUIDE.md "Notation" asks for exactly this), and restate every imported
   formula in it — in particular rewrite the Bromwich integral and the statement block's
   "$F = \mathcal{L}\{f\}$".
