## Findings
1. [minor] remark bullet 1 (line ~127) against method step 4: the Bartlett/Fejer taper is written $1-\lvert k\rvert/K$ in the remark and $1-\lvert k\rvert/(K+1)$ in step 4, with no word about the difference. Both are correct — over $\lvert k\rvert \le K$ the first is the Fejer mean of order $K-1$ (its end weights vanish) and the second the Fejer mean of order $K$ — but a reader comparing the two displays will think one is a typo. Fix: use one form in both places, or note in step 4 that the end weight is the only difference.
2. [minor] remark "Real weights are used in part 3 and part 4" (line ~131): "For complex weights the correct general statement is Grimmett and Stirzaker's" is convention-dependent in a way the bullet only half-discloses. I checked both placements of the conjugate: with $c(n) = \mathbb{E}[X_0\overline{X_n}]$ the filtered density carries $\lvert\sum_k a_k\mathrm{e}^{ik\lambda}\rvert^2 = \lvert A(-\lambda)\rvert^2$, which is their factor; with $c(n) = \mathbb{E}[\overline{X_0}X_n]$ it carries $\lvert A(\lambda)\rvert^2$. So neither is "the correct" one absolutely; the bullet's own closing clause gives the right diagnosis, but the opening sentence overstates it. Also, their Problem 2 is written for real constants, so attributing a complex-weight statement to them is a small stretch. Fix: "under the covariance convention $c(n) = \mathbb{E}[X_0\overline{X_n}]$ the right factor is theirs, $\lvert A(-\lambda)\rvert^2$; under the other placement of the conjugate it is $\lvert A(\lambda)\rvert^2$."
3. [minor] definition block: $A(\lambda)$ is declared $2\pi$-periodic and then restricted to $\lambda\in(-\pi,\pi]$ in the same display, which reads as a definition only on that interval; later uses (part 3's $A(-\lambda)$, the forward-backward argument, the cascade) all stay inside it, so nothing is wrong, but the restriction is redundant and slightly confusing next to "periodic".
