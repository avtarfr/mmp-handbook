## Findings
1. [minor] application (line 283): "whose eigenfunctions with eigenvalues $i^{n}\sqrt{2\pi}$ are the Hermite functions". Read as an unordered list of the four possible eigenvalues this is right, but read with $n$ as the Hermite index it is wrong for odd $n$: in this handbook's convention $\mathcal{F}[H_n(x)e^{-x^2/2}] = \sqrt{2\pi}\,(-i)^{n}H_n(k)e^{-k^2/2}$, not $(+i)^n$. Verified numerically at $k=0.7$ for $n=0,1,2,3$: the ratios are $2.5066282746$, $-2.5066282746\,i$, $-2.5066282746$, $+2.5066282746\,i$, i.e. $\sqrt{2\pi}(-i)^n$. Fix: write $(-i)^{n}\sqrt{2\pi}$ and say that $n$ is the Hermite index, or drop the index and say "eigenvalues among $\pm\sqrt{2\pi},\pm i\sqrt{2\pi}$".
2. [minor] example, step 1 (line 241): the quoted cross-check "$0.5197038$ from direct quadrature" is itself inaccurate. The closed form $\frac{\pi}{2}(1+2.3)e^{-2.3}$ and the integral $\int(1+x^2)^{-2}e^{-2.3ix}dx$ agree to 25 digits at $0.5197045373799961548$ (oscillatory quadrature, mpmath dps 25); the apparent disagreement in the seventh digit is an artefact of a loose quadrature and should not be presented as the result of an independent check. Fix: quote $0.5197045$ for both, or state the quadrature tolerance.
3. [minor] definition, item 1 (line 48): "Taking $m=2$, $n=0$ gives $\lvert f(x)\rvert \le C/(1+x^2)$" needs $m=0$ as well — $\sup x^2\lvert f\rvert<\infty$ alone gives only $\lvert f\rvert\le C/x^2$, which says nothing near the origin. Fix: "taking $m=0$ and $m=2$ with $n=0$".
4. [minor] proof of part 3 (line 180): "applying part 2 to the function $\mathcal{F}^{-1}[g]$ for $g\in\mathcal{S}$ gives $\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}^{-1}=I$" skips a step — what part 2 yields directly is $\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}^{-1}g] = \mathcal{F}^{-1}[g]$, and cancelling the outer $\mathcal{F}^{-1}$ needs its injectivity. One extra clause fixes it: $\mathcal{F}^{-1} = \frac{1}{2\pi}R\mathcal{F}$, so $\mathcal{F}^{-1}$ is injective because $\mathcal{F}$ is. The conclusion is correct as stated.
