## Findings
1. [blocking] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", bullet 5 (line 182): "the inequality is strict as soon as two of the sets genuinely overlap and have finite measure" is false, in two independent ways. (i) Set-theoretic overlap is not enough: with Lebesgue measure, $E_1=[0,1]$ and $E_2=[1,2]$ overlap, both are finite, and $\mu(E_1\cup E_2)=2=\mu(E_1)+\mu(E_2)$ — no strictness, because the overlap is null. (ii) Even when two sets overlap in a set of positive measure, strictness can fail if some *other* member of the family is infinite: $E_1=E_2=[0,1]$, $E_3=\mathbb{R}$ gives $\mu(\bigcup_j E_j)=\infty=\sum_j\mu(E_j)$. The surrounding point (that disjointness is what turns (b) into (2)) is right; only this clause is wrong. Fix: "...and the inequality is strict whenever two of the sets overlap in a set of positive measure and $\sum_j \mu(E_j) < \infty$."

2. [minor] method, step 4 (line 191): "For a finite $\mu$ you may instead verify continuity from above at $\varnothing$: $E_n\downarrow\varnothing$ implies $\mu(E_n)\to 0$" is not licensed by the lemma it leans on. The lemma (line 103) asks for continuity from above "in the sense of (d)", i.e. for *every* decreasing sequence; continuity at $\varnothing$ alone is formally weaker, so the method is claiming more than the unit has proved. It is in fact true, and the unit's own proof of the lemma (line 152) already proves it — the only decreasing sequence used there is $R_n\downarrow\varnothing$. Fix: state the second half of the lemma in the at-$\varnothing$ form ("$\mu$ is a measure iff $E_n\downarrow\varnothing$ implies $\mu(E_n)\to 0$"), which is what the proof gives and what the method needs; a sentence can record that this is formally weaker than (d) and still enough.

3. [minor] proof "Generators of the Borel sigma-algebra", step 3 (line 174): the two ray families are produced jointly ("$\mathcal{E}_5$ together with $\mathcal{E}_6$") and then separated by $\mathcal{M}_5=\mathcal{M}_8$ and $\mathcal{M}_6=\mathcal{M}_7$, but only the $\mathcal{M}_5$ half is actually carried out: $(-\infty,a]=((a,\infty))^{c}$ and $(-\infty,a)=\bigcup_n(-\infty,a-\frac1n]$ put $\mathcal{E}_6$ inside $\mathcal{M}_5$, hence all open intervals. The mirror step needed for $\mathcal{M}_6$ — getting $(a,\infty)$, e.g. as $(\bigcap_n(-\infty,a+\frac1n))^{c}$ — is not written, and the word "symmetrically" as placed attaches only to the identity $\mathcal{M}_6=\mathcal{M}_7$. Fix: spell the mirror inclusion out in half a line.

4. [minor] proof "Generators", step 3 (line 174): the clauses "$(a,\infty)=\bigcup_n(a,a+n)$ shows $\mathcal{M}_5\subseteq\mathcal{M}_1$" and "$(-\infty,b)=\bigcup_n(b-n,b)$ shows $\mathcal{M}_6\subseteq\mathcal{M}_1$" prove an inclusion that step 1 has already given (the rays are open, hence Borel) and that points the opposite way from what step 3 needs. Fix: drop them, or mark them as a cross-check, so the logical direction of the step stays visible.
