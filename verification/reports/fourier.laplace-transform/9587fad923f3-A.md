## Findings
1. [minor] statement "The Bromwich inversion integral": the placement condition on the contour is given as "$\lambda$ is chosen so that every singularity of $\bar f$ lies strictly to its left", but what the proof establishes is the formula on a line $\mathrm{Re}\,s=\beta$ with $\beta>s_0$, i.e. inside the half-plane where the defining integral converges (step 3 needs $e^{-\beta t}f(t)\in L^1$, which is a statement about convergence, not about singularities). The two conditions coincide in every case the unit treats, and both cited books phrase it the way the unit does (Riley, Hobson and Bence p. 884; Arfken p. 1039 argues from "nonsingular for $s\ge\beta$"), but $\bar f$ can in principle continue analytically to the left of its abscissa of convergence, and there the statement would promise more than the proof delivers. Fix: write the hypothesis as "$\lambda>s_0$, which by step 1 puts every singularity of $\bar f$ strictly to the left of the line", and keep the singularity formulation as the practical reading.
2. [minor] application "Transfer functions and stability": "the ratio $\bar y(s)/\bar g(s)=1/Q(s)$ is the transfer function" holds only for zero initial data; with the $P(s)$ of step 2 of the method the ratio is $[P(s)+\bar g(s)]/[Q(s)\bar g(s)]$. Fix: add "for a system started from rest" (which is also what the next sentence of that paragraph and the $RLC$ paragraph assume).
3. [source] statement "Existence, and standard pairs", the "Decay of the transform" paragraph. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs` against arfken-2013, "§20.7, p. 1010 vs (20.140), p. 1011": Arfken's unrestricted claim that every Laplace transform tends to $0$ as $s\to\infty$ is contradicted one page later by his own $\mathcal{L}[\delta(t)]=1$, and is correct only for the functions of exponential order that (20.127) sets up. The unit reports both and says in terms that the book leaves them unreconciled, which is the right handling; no change wanted.
