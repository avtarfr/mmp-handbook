## Findings
1. [source] statement "Existence, and standard pairs" (lines 49 and 66–68): "Whenever $\bar f$ is a Laplace transform, $\bar f(s)\to0$ as $s\to\infty$" is Arfken's sentence on p. 1010, reported faithfully, but the same table then carries $\delta(t-t_0)\mapsto e^{-st_0}$ together with Arfken's convention $\mathcal{L}[\delta(t)] = 1$ (their (20.140), p. 1011), and the constant $1$ does not tend to $0$. Arfken states both without reconciling them; the decay statement holds for transforms of functions of exponential order, not for distributions concentrated at the endpoint. Recorded with `tools/discrepancy.mjs add fourier.laplace-transform arfken-2013 "Sec. 20.7, p. 1010 against (20.140), p. 1011"`; the unit follows its source, so this is not a failure. A half-line clause in our text ("for $f$ of exponential order; the endpoint impulse of the table is the exception") would close it without departing from the source.
2. [minor] statement "Operational rules" (line 86): "Strauss lists the same eight as properties (i)–(ix)". Strauss's table on p. 354 has nine entries, and they are not the same eight: it includes linearity (i), which is not one of the eight (the unit gives linearity in the definition block), it gives rule 6 only in the case $n = 1$ (vi, $tf(t)\mapsto -dF/ds$), and it has **no** integral rule, so rule 2 ($\mathcal{L}[\int_0^t f] = \bar f/s$) cannot be found there. I treat this as minor rather than blocking because the locator and page are right, the page does carry the property table, and every rule's content is attributed to, and checks out against, Riley, Hobson and Bence (13.57)–(13.63) and their convolution theorem, so nothing in the unit rests on the Strauss sentence. Fix: "Strauss lists all of these except the integral rule, as properties (ii)–(ix), with rule 6 only for $n = 1$".
3. [minor] statement "Operational rules" (line 72): the preamble fixes $a > 0$, but rule 3 ($\mathcal{L}[e^{at}f] = \bar f(s-a)$) and the table entries for $e^{at}$, $t^ne^{at}$ and $e^{at}\sin bt$ hold for every real (indeed complex) $a$, and the table's $s_0 = a$ column already presumes $a$ of either sign. Rule 6's $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and rule 4's $b>0$ are genuinely needed; $a>0$ is not, and the worked example in fact uses rule 1 and poles at negative $a$. Fix: restrict $a>0$ to rule 5 (scaling), where it is required.
