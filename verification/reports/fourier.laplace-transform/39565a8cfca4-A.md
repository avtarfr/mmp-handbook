## Findings
1. [blocking] statement, "The Bromwich inversion integral" (line 90): "Let $\bar f(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,dt$ converge for $\mathrm{Re}\,s>s_0$. Then $f$ is recovered from $\bar f$ by the Bromwich integral" is stated with no regularity hypothesis on $f$, and in that generality it is false — and the unit says so itself in the sixth remark bullet (line 172): "the value of $f$ at a jump returned by the Bromwich integral is the average of the one-sided limits", which is not $f$. Both sources hedge exactly here and the unit drops the hedge: Arfken requires $G$ "suitably restricted so that it may be represented by a Fourier integral" (p. 1039), and Riley-Hobson-Bence call the general statement "difficult to show for general functions and transforms" and give only a plausibility calculation (p. 884). Fix: state the hypothesis the proof needs — $f$ piecewise smooth (or of bounded variation on compacts), with the conclusion reading $\tfrac12[f(t^+)+f(t^-)]$, which is the piecewise-smooth version in `fourier.fourier-inversion`; or assume $g$ and $\hat g$ both integrable. The remark bullet then confirms the statement instead of contradicting it.
2. [blocking] proof, steps 3-4 (lines 128-143), and frontmatter `prereqs` (line 7): step 3 establishes only that $g\in L^1(\mathbb{R})$, and step 4 then applies the pointwise Fourier inversion formula to it. Integrability of $g$ alone does not license that: the handbook's own `fourier.fourier-inversion` gives it for $\mathcal{S}$, for $g,\hat g\in L^1$ (and then only off a null set), and for $g\in L^1$ satisfying Dirichlet conditions (midpoint value). The result is used unstated and uncited, and `fourier.fourier-inversion` is not among the prerequisites although the handbook has the unit. Fix: add `fourier.fourier-inversion` to `prereqs`, `:ref` the version being used at step 4, and check its hypotheses there (the `:ref` at line 136 points at `fourier.fourier-transform`, which supplies only the convention).
3. [minor] statement, "Operational rules" (line 86): "Strauss lists the same eight as properties (i)-(ix)". His table on p. 354 has no integral rule, so our rule 2 is not there; seven of the eight appear, and his (i) is linearity, which is not one of the eight. Fix: "seven of these appear among Strauss's properties (i)-(ix)".

Fix: "whenever $\bar f$ is the Laplace transform of a function" and a clause excluding the distributional entries.
