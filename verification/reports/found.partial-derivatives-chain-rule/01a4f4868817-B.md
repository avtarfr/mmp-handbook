   `src` names only Lee's Prop. C.8, p. 646, but the proof covers all three parts of the statement.
   Part 1's uniqueness is Lee's Exercise C.1 (p. 642), its continuity is Exercise C.2(a) (p. 643), the
   matrix identification is Prop. C.8 (p. 646), part 2 is Prop. C.10 (p. 647) and part 3 is Exercise

   telescoping-plus-mean-value argument for part 2 is the handbook's own and nothing on p. 646 supports
   it. Fix: list the locators the proof actually uses (as the statement block already does) and say
   that Lee gives Prop. C.10 unproved, so the part 2 argument is ours - the unit is scrupulous about
   this elsewhere (the chain rule's intermediate $C^k$, the finite-$C^r$ mixed partials).

2. [minor] proof of equality of mixed partials, final paragraph (line 204): "Those two act on a partial
   derivative of $F$ of order $m-2 \le r-2$" is the worst case only. If the exchanged pair sits at
   positions $p, p+1$ counting from the outside, the function they act on has order $m-p-1$, which runs
   from $m-2$ (outermost pair) down to $0$ (innermost pair). The conclusion is unaffected - a lower
   order leaves more derivatives, so the function is still $C^2$ - but the sentence as written covers
   one case. Fix: "of order at most $m-2$".

3. [source] rhb-2006, §5.1, p. 153: equality of mixed second partials is asserted there under
   continuity of the second partials *at the point in question*, weaker than the $C^2$ hypothesis of
   Lee's Prop. C.6 that the unit follows. Both are true statements; the unit follows Lee, says so in
   the fourth remark bullet, and the difference is already on file in `verification/discrepancies.md`.
   No new entry added. Not blocking.

4. [source] docarmo-2016, Appendix, p. 127: do Carmo defines "$f$ is differentiable at $(x_0,y_0)$" to
   mean that $f$ has continuous partial derivatives of *all* orders there, and then states equality of
   mixed partials "when $f$ is differentiable" - far stronger than the standard definition the unit
   uses. The unit cites p. 127 only for the subscript convention, and takes differentiability and $C^k$
   from Lee, so there is no conflict in our text. Already on file. Not blocking.
