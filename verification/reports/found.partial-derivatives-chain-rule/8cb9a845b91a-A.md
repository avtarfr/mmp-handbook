## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, bullet "Notation that differs between fields" (line 230): the description of RHB p. 205 is wrong and contradicts the claim it is meant to support. RHB's displayed Jacobian determinant has $\partial x/\partial u,\ \partial y/\partial u,\ \partial z/\partial u$ as its **first row**, not its first column; its first column is $\partial x/\partial u,\ \partial x/\partial v,\ \partial x/\partial w$. The overall claim ("transposed relative to the Jacobian matrix defined above") is correct precisely because of that, but as written the em-dash clause describes the first column of the unit's own $J_F$, so a reader who trusts it would conclude the two arrays agree. Fix: "their first row is $\partial x/\partial u$, $\partial y/\partial u$, $\partial z/\partial u$".

2. [minor] proof of the chain rule, part 2, higher-order induction (line 163): the letter $k$ carries two meanings in the same paragraph. In the displayed formula it is the summation index over intermediate variables ($1 \le k \le m$), and in the induction it is the order of differentiability ($C^k$, $C^{k-1}$); the phrase "the composite $\partial G_i/\partial y_k \circ F$ by the induction hypothesis, applied to the $C^{k-1}$ maps $\partial G_i/\partial y_k$ and $F$" uses both at once. Fix: rename the induction order, e.g. argue by induction on $r$ with $C^r$, $C^{r-1}$, leaving $k$ as the summation index (the mixed-partials proof already uses $r$ this way).

3. [minor] statement "Chain rule", part 2, parenthesis (line 129): the attribution of the intermediate-$k$ case is weak. The paragraph after Lee's Cor. C.11 (p. 647) says only that "from the chain rule and induction one can derive formulas for the higher partial derivatives of a composite function as needed, provided the functions in question are sufficiently differentiable"; it does not assert that a composite of $C^k$ maps is $C^k$. The unit proves that case itself, so no mathematics is at risk, but the parenthesis claims more for the source than it says. Fix: "Lee's Cor. C.11 states the cases $k=1$ and smooth; the intermediate $k$, which the paragraph after it leaves to the reader, is proved below."
