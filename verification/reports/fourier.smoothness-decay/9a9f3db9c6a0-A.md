## Findings

1. [blocking] statement "Term-by-term integration and term-by-term differentiation", part 2: the sentence "differentiating term by term gives $\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}\cos nx$, whose terms do not tend to $0$, so it diverges at every point" is false at $x = \pi/2$ (and at every odd multiple of $\pi/2$). There $\cos(n\pi/2) = 0$ for *every* odd $n$, so every term of the differentiated series vanishes and the series converges — to $0$, which is in fact the correct value of $f'$. I checked this numerically: the partial sums at $x=\pi/2$ out to $n = 10^4$ are zero to machine precision ($<10^{-11}$), the individual terms being zero to $10^{-12}$. More generally $\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}\cos nx$ has partial sums $\sin\bigl(2(K+1)x\bigr)/(2\sin x)$, which converge exactly when $x \in \frac{\pi}{2}\mathbb{Z}$; at $x = 0$ and $x = \pi$ the terms are $\pm 1$ and it does diverge. This is also a strengthening of the source: Strauss (p. 130) says only that the series "is clearly divergent because the terms don't even tend to zero as $n \to \infty$ (the nth term test for divergence)" and makes no claim about every point, so the unit asserts more than its citation supports. Fix: "whose terms do not tend to $0$ for any $x$ outside $\frac{\pi}{2}\mathbb{Z}$, so it diverges there — in particular at every point of $(0,\pi)$ except $x = \pi/2$, where every term happens to vanish".

2. [minor] statement "Term-by-term integration and term-by-term differentiation", part 3 ("If $f \in C^2(S)$ then the differentiated series ... converges absolutely and uniformly, and its sum is $f'$") is never proved. It is a one-line consequence of the material already in the unit — $f \in C^2(S)$ gives $f' \in C^1(S)$, so part 2 of the decay statement applies to $f'$, and $\widehat{f'}(m) = imc_m$ by the first statement identifies the differentiated series as the Fourier series of $f'$ — but the three proof blocks cover only the decay statements, the integration statement and the converse. Fix: add that sentence to the end of the "Term-by-term integration" proof, or retitle that proof block.

3. [minor] proof "Term-by-term integration", step 2: the integration by parts is performed with $F' = f - c_0$ justified only by "at every point where $f$ is continuous (a set whose complement does not affect the integral)". The conclusion is correct, but the standard integration-by-parts theorem requires a continuous derivative and $F$ here is only Lipschitz; what makes the step legitimate is that $F$ is an indefinite Riemann integral, for which $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} F\,G' = [FG]_{-\pi}^{\pi} - \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(f-c_0)G$ holds for $C^1$ $G$ (by Fubini on the double integral, or by the product rule for $FG$). The handbook's style guide asks for the justification at the step. Fix: name the fact being used — "integration by parts is valid for an indefinite Riemann integral against a $C^1$ factor" — rather than appealing to a null set.

4. [minor] method, step 5: "if $\lvert c_m\rvert \le A\lvert m\rvert^{-q}$ then then for $q > 1/2$ ..." — "then then" is a typo.
