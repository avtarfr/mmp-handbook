## Findings
1. [minor] statement "Divergence theorem (Gauss's theorem)" (line 61): "The theorem holds whether or not $V$ is simply connected: the region between two concentric spheres is admissible". The colon offers the spherical shell as the witness for the "or not", but the shell *is* simply connected — Riley, Hobson and Bence say so explicitly on p. 384 ("The region between two concentric spheres of different radii is simply connected"), two sections before the page cited here. What the shell illustrates is a *disconnected boundary*, not failure of simple connectivity; the solid torus is the right witness (RHB p. 384 gives it as the non-simply-connected example). Fix: "whether or not $V$ is simply connected (a solid torus is admissible) and whether or not $S$ is connected: for the region between two concentric spheres the normal points out of $V$ on both components of $S$, hence into the inner cavity."
2. [minor] remark, third bullet (line 147): same conflation, in a milder form. The bullet is headed "Simple connectivity is not required" and then offers the shell alongside the solid torus; since the shell is simply connected it supports the sign point but not the heading. The linked ledger entry in `verification/discrepancies.md` (the `vcalc.divergence-theorem` / arfken-2013 row) goes further and states that Arfken's simply-connected hypothesis "excludes" the spherical shell, which is not so — Arfken's hypothesis excludes the doughnut, not the shell. Fix: keep the torus as the example that Arfken's hypothesis excludes, present the shell only for the inner-normal sign, and correct the wording of the discrepancy row.
3. [minor] example "A tetrahedron, both sides computed", step 4 (line 170): "As a dimensional check" — what follows is a bound check on the flux (max and min of the integrand times the face area), not a check of dimensions. The arithmetic is right. Fix: "As a sanity check".
