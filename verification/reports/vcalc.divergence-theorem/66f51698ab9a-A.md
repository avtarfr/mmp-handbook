## Findings
1. [minor] definition[Simple region] (lines 33–41) versus statement (line 45): $V$ is a *compact set* in the definition and in the proof, and a *bounded spatial domain* (hence open, following Strauss) in the statement, where $\overline V = V \cup S$ is then written. Both readings make every integral in the unit mean the same number, since $\partial V$ is Lebesgue-null, but the same letter carries two meanings and $\overline V = V\cup S$ is false on the compact reading. Fix: one clause fixing the convention — e.g. in the definition, "Call a compact set $V$ ... (we write $V$ for the closed region throughout; whether the boundary is included changes no integral below)".
2. [minor] definition[Simple region], final paragraph (line 41): the description of $\partial V$ as top, bottom and a lateral part with $\hat{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}} = 0$ is right when $\psi_1 < \psi_2$ on the interior of $D$, and the parenthesis covers the degenerate case $\psi_1 \equiv \psi_2$; it does not cover the mixed case in which $\{\psi_1 = \psi_2\}$ has interior, where $V$ has a two-dimensional sheet attached and the lateral part is a cylinder over the boundary of $\{\psi_1 < \psi_2\}$ rather than over $\partial D$. Such a $V$ is excluded by the statement's hypotheses anyway (its boundary is not that of a spatial domain), so nothing downstream is wrong. Fix: require $\psi_1 < \psi_2$ on the interior of $D$ in the definition, which is what Steps 1–2 actually use, and drop the parenthesis.
3. [minor] proof, Step 1 (line 86) and Step 2 (line 96): the hypothesis under which these steps run is "$V$ is $z$-simple", but the unit's own definition of a simple region requires the *same* $D$-and-graphs description in all three directions, and the proof at line 84 says only "We prove the theorem for a simple region $V$". Step 3 then says the argument applies "verbatim with the coordinates permuted", which is right, but the reader has to notice that each direction comes with its own $D$, $\psi_1$, $\psi_2$. Fix: in Step 3, say explicitly "with $D$, $\psi_1$, $\psi_2$ replaced by the data of the $x$- (resp. $y$-) simple description, which need have nothing to do with the $z$-simple one".
