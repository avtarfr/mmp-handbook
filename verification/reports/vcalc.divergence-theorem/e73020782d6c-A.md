## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Simple region" (line 43): the parenthesis illustrates the degenerate lateral part with "the whole rim of the disc ... for the two hemispheres of a ball", but a ball is *not* a $z$-simple region under the definition just given — $\psi_{1,2} = \mp\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}$ are not $C^1$ on the closed disc, exactly as the note at line 139 says. As written the definition block offers as an instance something it later excludes. Fix: keep the illustration but mark it, e.g. "as the rim of the disc does for the two halves of a ball — a region which, as the note below explains, is not itself $z$-simple in this sense".
2. [minor] statement "Companion identities" (lines 64-70): the two vector identities are stated but never proved, and there is no `note` saying they are taken from the sources, which `docs/contributing/phase-full.md` requires for a statement given without proof. The unit's own `sources` entries advertise "the constant-vector trick for the companion identities" (rhb-2006 §11.8.2; arfken-2013 Eqs. (3.87)-(3.90)), but the device is never used, whereas the third consequence (integration by parts) *is* derived inline. Fix: add the two-line derivation — put $\mathbf{F} = \varphi\mathbf{c}$ and $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}$ with $\mathbf{c}$ constant in the theorem and use that $\mathbf{c}$ is arbitrary — or a note citing RHB Eqs. (11.21)-(11.22).
3. [minor] proof, Step 5 (line 135): "every face interior to $V$ occurs in exactly two of the surface integrals" is asserted rather than argued; "meeting only along pieces of their boundaries" does not by itself exclude an interior face shared only in part, or a face lying partly in $\partial V$. This is RHB's subdivision argument (p. 401) and is standard, but it is the one step of the proof carrying no justification. Fix: state the decomposition hypothesis as "the interiors are disjoint and each interior face is a common face of exactly two pieces, with matching outward normals", which is what the cancellation uses.
4. [minor] definition (line 45) versus proof (line 88): $V$ denotes the open region in the statement and the closed region in the proof. Both switches are declared and the volume integral is unaffected, so nothing is wrong; a single convention would read better.
5. [source] remark (line 147) and sources (arfken-2013, §3.8, p. 164): Arfken states Gauss' theorem only for "a simply connected region of $\mathbb{R}^3$", a hypothesis neither his subdivision argument nor ours uses, and which would exclude the spherical shell. The unit follows RHB/Strauss/Evans and names the difference explicitly, which is the right handling. The discrepancy is already recorded in `verification/discrepancies.md` (entry: vcalc.divergence-theorem | arfken-2013 | §3.8, Eq. (3.81), p. 164), so the remark's claim that it is logged is true and no new entry was added.
