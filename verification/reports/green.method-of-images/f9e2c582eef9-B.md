## Findings
1. [blocking] example "A charged patch on a grounded plane", item 5 (line 234): the framing of the check is mathematically false. The text says "the flux of $-\nabla u$ out of the half-space through the plane is $\int_{z=0}\partial u/\partial n\, dS$, and by (H) with $f\equiv1$ this is $1$ at every interior point". Two things are wrong. (a) With $\partial/\partial n$ the outward normal derivative, the outward flux of $-\nabla u$ is $-\int_{z=0}\partial u/\partial n\,dS$, so the stated identity has the wrong sign. (b) The quantity that equals $1$ is not that flux but the value of (H) itself with $f\equiv1$, i.e. the normalisation $\int_{\mathbb R^2}K\,dx\,dy = 1$ of the Poisson kernel. For $f\equiv1$ one has $u\equiv1$, hence $\int_{z=0}\partial u/\partial n\,dS = 0$, not $1$; a reader who takes the sentence at face value comes away believing that the normal-derivative integral of *any* half-space Dirichlet solution is $1$. What the identity $\int_{z=0}\partial G_D/\partial n\,dS = 1$ does hold for is the Green's function, not $u$ (that is the flux identity of `green.laplace-green-functions`). Everything after the clause is right: the kernel integral is the step-2 integral with upper limit $\infty$, it equals $z_0\cdot(1/z_0)=1$, and the consequence $0\le u\le 1$ follows. Fix: delete the flux clause and state the check as it is actually performed — "by (H) with $f\equiv1$, $u\equiv1$, so the Poisson kernel integrates to $1$ over the plane; it is positive, hence $0\le u\le1$ whenever $0\le f\le1$" — or, if the flux reading is wanted, write it for $G_D$ and quote the identity from the previous unit.
2. [minor] remark "Scope and variants", last bullet (line 210): "A right-angled corner in two dimensions needs three images, at the reflections of $\mathbf{r}_0$ in the two lines and in the corner, with signs $-,+,-$." Read against the order in which the three images are just named (line 1, line 2, corner) the signs are wrong: both line reflections carry $-$ and the corner reflection carries $+$ (rhb-2006 figure 21.13, p. 762: $-\lambda$ at $\mathbf r_1$ and $\mathbf r_3$, the two line reflections, and $+\lambda$ at $\mathbf r_2$, the corner). The list $-,+,-$ is the correct sequence if one walks round the four quadrants instead, which is presumably what was meant. Fix: write "with $-$ at each of the two line reflections and $+$ at the corner".

[minor] statement of the ball Green's function (line 143) and remark, last bullet (line 211): "Evans proves (his Theorem 15) that for continuous $f$ it does define a harmonic function in the ball with boundary values $f$", and "Evans's Theorems 14 and 15 supply the missing half, by verifying directly that (H) and (P) define harmonic functions".
