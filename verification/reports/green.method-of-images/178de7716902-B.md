## Findings
1. [minor] :::method, last paragraph (line 48): "Riley, Hobson and Bence stress that for a general
   curved boundary the positions and strengths cannot be found at all". They say something weaker:
   on p. 759, "In general it is very difficult to find the correct positions and strengths for the
   images ... Nevertheless, it is possible to do so for certain problems that have simple geometry";
   and on p. 762, "For more complicated (curved) boundaries this is no longer possible", where
   "this" is fixing the positions by simple reflection, not the existence of an image system. The
   second half of our sentence ("confined to a short list of geometries") is faithful. Fix: "very
   difficult", or make the stronger claim in our own voice.
2. [minor] :::statement[Green's function of a ball ...] (line ~150): "Strauss notes that for
   $\mathbf r_0=\mathbf 0$ formula (P) reduces to $u(\mathbf 0)=\langle f\rangle$". On p. 194
   Strauss invokes the mean value property from his Chapter 6 in order to *exclude* $x_0=0$ from
   the derivation ("We already know from Chapter 6 that $u(0)$ is the average ... so let's consider
   $x_0\ne0$"); he does not present it as a reduction of (16). The reduction is true — I checked
   that (P) at $r_0=0$ gives $(4\pi a^2)^{-1}\oint f\,dS$ — but it is our observation, not his.
   Fix: "formula (P) at $\mathbf r_0=\mathbf 0$ reduces to ..., which is the mean value property
   Strauss invokes at this point".
