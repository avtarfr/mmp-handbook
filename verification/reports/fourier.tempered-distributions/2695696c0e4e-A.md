## Findings
1. [blocking] note "Convention" (line 87, last sentence before "A complex conjugation..."): "$2\pi\delta(k)$ becomes $\delta(\xi)$ and loses its $2\pi$, while $-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}k$ becomes $-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}\xi$ and loses none, **so the two terms of $\hat H$ do not scale alike**." The conclusion is false, and this is an `original` block, so it is our own material. The homogeneity observation is right ($\delta$ has degree $-1$, $\operatorname{sgn}$ degree $0$), but the two examples quoted are the transforms of $1$ and of $\operatorname{pv}(1/x)$, not the two terms of $\hat H$. The terms of $\hat H$ are $\pi\delta(k)$ and $-i\operatorname{pv}(1/k)$, and $\operatorname{pv}(1/\cdot)$ is homogeneous of degree $-1$ exactly like $\delta$, so under $k=2\pi\xi$ both terms are divided by the same $2\pi$: $\pi\delta(2\pi\xi)=\tfrac12\delta(\xi)$ and $-i\operatorname{pv}\frac{1}{2\pi\xi}=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\operatorname{pv}\frac1\xi$, giving exactly the $\hat H(\xi)=\tfrac12\delta(\xi)-\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\operatorname{pv}(1/\xi)$ quoted two lines earlier (and independently re-derived from $2\pi i\xi\hat H=1$ with $\hat H(\xi)+\hat H(-\xi)=\hat1=\delta(\xi)$). The appearance of a $2\pi$ in one Folland coefficient and not the other is an artefact of the numbers $\pi$ and $1$, not of different scaling. Fix: drop the clause, or replace it with a pair that really does scale differently, e.g. "the $\delta$ term of $\hat1$ loses a $2\pi$ while the $\operatorname{sgn}$ of $\widehat{\operatorname{pv}(1/x)}$ loses none; both terms of $\hat H$, being homogeneous of degree $-1$, lose the same $2\pi$."

2. [blocking] proof "The transform pairs", $\widehat{\operatorname{pv}(1/x)}$ (line 249): "Exchanging the order of integration, which is permitted because $\lvert\sin kx\rvert/x$ is bounded by $\lvert k\rvert$ near $x=0$ and $\varphi$ decays rapidly". The stated reason only disposes of the singularity at $x=0$; the obstruction is at $x=\infty$, and there the double integral is *not* absolutely convergent, so Fubini does not apply. Explicitly, $\int_{\mathbb R}\lvert\varphi(k)\rvert\,2\lvert\sin kx\rvert\,dk\to\frac{4}{\pi}\lVert\varphi\rVert_1\neq0$ as $x\to\infty$, so $\int_0^\infty\frac{dx}{x}\int\lvert\varphi(k)\rvert\lvert e^{-ikx}-e^{ikx}\rvert dk=\infty$. (The outer integral before the exchange is fine, because $\hat\varphi\in\mathcal S$; it is only the exchanged form, whose inner Dirichlet integral is merely conditionally convergent, that needs an argument.) Fix: truncate, $\int_0^R$, exchange there by Fubini (the double integral over $(0,R)\times\mathbb R$ is absolutely convergent), then let $R\to\infty$ using that $\int_0^{R}\frac{\sin kx}{x}dx$ is bounded uniformly in $R$ and $k$ and converges pointwise to $\tfrac\pi2\operatorname{sgn}k$, so dominated convergence applies against $\varphi\in L^1$.
