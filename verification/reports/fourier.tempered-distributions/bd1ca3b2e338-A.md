## Findings
1. [minor] frontmatter (line 7): `prereqs` omits measure.dominated-convergence, although the convergence theorem is invoked by name twice and is load-bearing both times — at the end of the $\operatorname{pv}(1/x)$ computation ("Dominated convergence therefore gives", line 267), which is the only delicate limit in the unit, and in route 4 for $\hat H$ ("the convergence being dominated", line 311). The four sibling units in this chapter that use it (fourier.fourier-transform, fourier.fourier-inversion, fourier.delta-identities, fourier.fourier-transform-rn) all list it. Fix: add measure.dominated-convergence to `prereqs`.
2. [minor] proof of the transform pairs, route 4 for $\hat H$ (line 311): the dominating function is supplied only "near $k=0$", via $\lvert\varphi(k)-\varphi(-k)\rvert\le2k\sup\lvert\varphi'\rvert$. That bound gives $2\sup\lvert\varphi'\rvert$, which is not integrable on $(0,\infty)$, so the tail is left uncovered. The missing half is one line — for $k\ge1$, $k\lvert\varphi(k)-\varphi(-k)\rvert/(\varepsilon^2+k^2)\le(\lvert\varphi(k)\rvert+\lvert\varphi(-k)\rvert)/k$, integrable because $\varphi\in\mathcal{S}$ — and the proof of $\widehat{\operatorname{pv}(1/x)}$ above is careful to give exactly this kind of split, so the omission is a presentational inconsistency rather than a gap in the mathematics. Fix: add the $k\ge1$ bound.
3. [minor] statement, part 2 (line 114): the $L^2$ half ("It also agrees with the Plancherel transform on $L^2$ … on $L^1+L^2$") is correctly attributed — it is Folland's sentence on p. 295 — but the proof block's item (2) establishes only the $L^1$ half, and the statement has just said the $L^2$ case does not follow from it. Fix: add the one-line argument (take $f_n\in L^1\cap L^2$ with $f_n\to f$ in $L^2$; both $f_n\to f$ and $\hat f_n\to\hat f_{\text{Pl}}$ hold in $\mathcal{S}'$, and part 1's weak continuity identifies the limits), or say explicitly that this half is quoted from Folland and not proved here.
4. [minor] note[Convention] (line 89): "A complex conjugation exchanges the sign conventions" is too compressed to be checked as written — conjugating $\hat f$ gives the opposite-sign transform of $\bar f$, not of $f$, so the statement needs the function conjugated too; and for the one place it matters, Stakgold and Holst, the paragraph has already given the cleaner and exactly correct rule, their $u$ is our $-k$. Fix: drop the sentence, or write it as "for real $f$, conjugating the transform exchanges the two sign conventions".
5. [minor] method (line 211): "Four routes, in increasing order of effort" does not match the unit's own practice — route 2 is the cheapest route wherever a known pair exists, and the proof block uses it rather than route 1 for $\hat1$, $\widehat{x^n}$, $\widehat{e^{iax}}$ and $\widehat{\operatorname{sgn}}$. Fix: drop "in increasing order of effort", or reorder.
