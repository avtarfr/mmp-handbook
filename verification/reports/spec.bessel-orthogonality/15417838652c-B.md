## Findings
1. [blocking] example, "Check 1: the value at the centre" (line 294): "converging to $1$, slowly, as $c_j = O(j^{-3})$ predicts" states a decay rate that the unit's own coefficients do not have. From the unit's formula $c_j = 8/[\alpha_j^{3}J_1(\alpha_j)]$ and $J_1(\alpha_j) \approx \pm\sqrt{2/\pi\alpha_j}$, the true rate is $c_j \sim \pm 10.027\,\alpha_j^{-5/2} = \Theta(j^{-5/2})$, so $c_jj^{3} \to \infty$ like $j^{1/2}$ and the claim is false. Checked numerically: $c_j\alpha_j^{5/2} = -10.02206$ ($j=4$), $10.02596$ ($j=11$), $10.02649$ ($j=51$), $10.02651$ ($j=101$) — flat, confirming the $-5/2$ exponent and excluding $-3$. Fix: write $O(j^{-5/2})$ (the $\alpha_j^{-3}$ in the formula is partly undone by $J_1(\alpha_j) \sim \alpha_j^{-1/2}$). The observed partial sums are unaffected and correct.
2. [blocking] remark, first bullet (line 214): "At $\nu = -1$ the integral diverges logarithmically and everything fails at once" is false at $\nu = -1$ itself, because $J_{-1} = -J_1$ (established in spec.bessel-first-kind), so $rJ_{-1}(\alpha r)^{2} \sim r^{3}$, not $r^{-1}$. Verified: $\int_0^1 rJ_{-1}(\alpha_{11}r)^{2}dr = 0.081107565413$, finite, and exactly $\tfrac12J_0(\alpha_{11})^{2} = 0.081107565413$, i.e. the unit's own normalisation formula holds at $\nu = -1$ with $\alpha_{-1,j} = \alpha_{1j}$ and $J_{\nu+1} = J_0$. It also contradicts a cited page: Arfken p. 661, footnote 3, says the lower-limit boundary term vanishes "for all $\nu \ge -1$". What is true is the statement about non-integer order: as $\nu \downarrow -1$ through non-integers, $\int_0^a r^{2\nu+1}dr = a^{2\nu+2}/(2\nu+2)$ blows up. Fix: "As $\nu \downarrow -1$ through non-integer values the integral diverges; at $\nu = -1$ itself the Gamma poles rescue it, since $J_{-1} = -J_1$." The hypothesis $\nu > -1$ in the statement is fine and need not change (it is Arfken Eq. (14.47)'s own range).
3. [minor] statement "Fourier-Bessel series" (line 223) and Parseval (line 234): the space is $L^{2}([0,a], r\,dr)$, which is normally complex, but Parseval is written with $f(r)^{2}$ and $c_j^{2}$ rather than $|f|^{2}$, $|c_j|^{2}$, and the coefficient formula has no conjugate. Harmless for the real functions the unit expands, but the space should be said to be real (or the moduli added).
4. [minor] definition (line 37): the interlacing bullet is stated for general $\nu$ ("between two consecutive zeros of $J_\nu$ there lies exactly one zero of $J_{\nu+1}$") but the attached citation, Arfken Exercise 14.1.11, p. 656, states it for integer order only ("between any two consecutive zeros of $J_n(x)$ there is one and only one zero of $J_{n+1}(x)$"). The general-$\nu$ version is Strauss p. 283 ("Between any two zeros of $J_s$ is a zero of $J_{s+1}$, and vice versa"), which the bullet list's preamble does credit by default. Fix: cite Strauss on this bullet, or restrict the Arfken cite to integer order.
5. [minor] definition (line 37): the bullet refs :ref[Sturm separation]{to=ode.sturm-comparison}, which is not among the unit's `prereqs`. It is used only for a passing "more generally", so nothing in the proofs depends on it, but either the prereq list or the mention should give way.
6. [minor] application, second paragraph (line 318): "The identical series with $\alpha_{0j}^{2}$ replaced in the exponent by $-\mathrm{i}$ times itself solves the corresponding Schrodinger problem." With the usual $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$ and $E = \hbar^{2}\alpha_{0j}^{2}/(2ma^{2}) > 0$, the heat exponent $-\kappa\alpha_{0j}^{2}t/a^{2}$ must become $-i\hbar\alpha_{0j}^{2}t/(2ma^{2})$, i.e. the real diffusivity is replaced by an imaginary one — in the unit's phrasing, $+i$ times itself, not $-i$ (with $-i$ one gets $e^{+iEt/\hbar}$, backwards time evolution). Fix: say that $\kappa$ is replaced by $i\hbar/2m$, which avoids the convention trap altogether.
