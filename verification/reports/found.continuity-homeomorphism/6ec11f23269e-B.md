## Findings
1. [minor] remark, last bullet (line 123): "where it would assert that $f^{-1}(V)$ is open for every open $V$ containing $f(x)$ - that is, global continuity". The gloss is wrong if $x$ is read as a fixed point, which is how the surrounding sentence reads (it is explaining Folland's definition of continuity *at a point*). At one point the condition is strictly between continuity at that point and global continuity. Counterexample: $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $f = 0$ on $(-1,1)$, $f = 2$ on $[1, \infty)$, $f = 3$ on $(-\infty, -1]$, and $p = 0$. For every open $V \ni f(p) = 0$ the preimage is $(-1,1)$, $(-1,\infty)$, $(-\infty,1)$ or $\mathbb{R}$ according as $V$ contains $2$, $3$, both or neither - open in every case - yet $f$ is discontinuous at $x = 1$ and $x = -1$. The claim becomes true once the form is imposed at *every* point (then any open $V$ meeting $f(X)$ has open preimage and any other has preimage $\emptyset$), which is presumably what was meant. Fix: "...would assert that $f^{-1}(V)$ is open for every open $V$ containing $f(x)$, which, imposed at every $x$, is global continuity".
2. [minor] statement "Metric spaces: the epsilon-delta form" (line 73): the sentences introducing uniform continuity and the Lipschitz condition, and the claims that each is strictly stronger than the previous one, are not supported by either page cited on the block (kreyszig-1978 pp. 20-21 give only Def 1.3-3 and Thm 1.3-4; docarmo-2016 p. 124 gives Props 2 and 3). lee-2013 p. 609 defines both notions and Exercises A.47 and A.49 there give the implications and their non-reversibility ($\sqrt{x}$ on $[0,\infty)$ is uniformly continuous but not Lipschitz), and lee-2013 is already a frontmatter source for this unit, but p. 609 is not in its `pages: "597, 602, 606-607"`. The claims are true as written - I checked both strictness directions - so this is a citation gap, not an error. Fix: add lee-2013 p. 609 (Exercises A.47, A.49) to the frontmatter and to the block `src`, or label those two sentences as the unit's own.

"Building continuous maps" (lines 79-84): items 1-4 checked against Lee Prop A.17(a), (e) and Exercise A.2; item 4's "precisely when" is the characteristic property and I verified both directions; item 5 (locality) and item 6 (gluing) are Lee Lemmas A.19 and A.20 with the same two alternatives. The gluing counterexample in the remark works: $\mathbb{R}$ covered by the closed singletons $\{0\}$ and $\{x\}$, $x \neq 0$, with $f_0 = 1$ and $f_x = 0$, overlaps empty, glued map $= \chi_{\{0\}}$, which is discontinuous - so finiteness is needed in the closed case, and the open case needs none because openness is local. "When a continuous bijection is a homeomorphism": I checked item 1 in the closed case too, which is the easy one to get wrong - if $f$ is closed as a map into $Y$ then $f(K)$ is closed in $Y$, hence $f(K) \cap f(X) = f(K)$ is closed in $f(X)$, so $f : X \to f(X)$ is a homeomorphism; the open case is immediate. $f(x) = x^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$: $f((-1,1)) = [0,1)$, which is not open, so a continuous map need not be open - verified numerically ($\min = 0$ attained, $\sup = 1$ not attained: on a grid of $10^5$ points in $(-1,1)$ the image ranges over $[0, 0.999998]$). $t \mapsto (\cos t, \sin t)$ on $[0, 2\pi)$: bijective onto $S^1$ and continuous; $[0,1) = (-1,1) \cap [0,2\pi)$ is open in the subspace, its image contains $(1,0)$ but no arc around it, so the map is not open and the inverse is discontinuous at $(1,0)$ - all three assertions correct. The identity from $\mathbb{R}_{\text{discrete}}$ to $\mathbb{R}_{\text{Euclidean}}$ is a continuous bijection that is not a homeomorphism - correct. Topological versus metric properties: $\tanh : \mathbb{R} \to (-1,1)$ is a strictly increasing continuous bijection with continuous inverse $\operatorname{artanh}$ (checked $\tanh(10) = 0.9999999959$, $\operatorname{artanh}(0.9) = 1.47222$), $\mathbb{R}$ is complete and unbounded while $(-1,1)$ is bounded and incomplete, and $x_n = 1 - 1/n$ is Cauchy in $(-1,1)$ with no limit there - so neither completeness nor boundedness is topological, as claimed. Degenerate cases checked: $X = \emptyset$ (every map out of it is continuous, and items 1-6 survive), constant maps, and $X$ discrete or trivial.
