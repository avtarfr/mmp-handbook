## Findings
1. [minor] proof, Chapman-Kolmogorov (line 134): the sentence "homogeneity then identifies the two factors as $p_{kj}(r)$ and $p_{ik}(n)$" writes the two conditional probabilities in the epoch-free notation $p_{\cdot\cdot}(n)$ before independence of the starting epoch $m$ has been established — that independence is only obtained two sentences later, from the induction. Read literally the paragraph assumes what it concludes; read as intended (induct on $n$ with $r=1$, where homogeneity alone gives $\mathbb{P}(X_{m+n+1}=j\mid X_{m+n}=k)=p_{kj}$) it is correct. Fix: derive the identity first in the epoch-dependent form $P(m,m+n+r)=P(m,m+n)\,P(m+n,m+n+r)$, as the source does on p. 241, then say that the induction with $r=1$ both gives $P(m,m+n)=P^{\,n}$ and removes the dependence on $m$.
2. [minor] proof, the summability criterion (line 144): the convolution identity is obtained "by the Markov property applied at the first-passage time". The conditioning event here is $B_r=\{X_1\ne j,\dots,X_{r-1}\ne j,\,X_r=j\}$, which is not a single-state event, so the extended form (2)/(3) quoted in the definition block has to be applied to each path $(x_1,\dots,x_{r-1})$ in $B_r$ and the results summed; no stopping time and no strong Markov property is needed. Fix: say so in half a sentence ("partition $B_r$ into cylinder events and apply the Markov property in the form that conditions on $X_1,\dots,X_r$"), which also avoids suggesting a stronger tool than is used.
3. [minor] method, step 3 (line 182): "For a finite irreducible chain, stop ... **Otherwise** identify the closed recurrent classes and the transient states; from a transient state the chain eventually enters one of the closed classes and stays there". On a countably infinite state space that is false: an asymmetric simple random walk on $\mathbb{Z}$ is all transient and has no closed recurrent class at all, and G&S p. 252 explicitly keeps both alternatives open ("either the chain always takes values in the set of transient states or it lies eventually in some irreducible closed set"). The rest of the method (steps 1, 5, 6, 7) plainly assumes a finite $P$, so the intended scope is right; only "Otherwise" over-reaches. Fix: open the method with "for a finite chain", or write step 3 as "Otherwise (still with $S$ finite) ...".
4. [minor] definition (line 30): the quantifier list "all states $s, x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}$" omits $x_0$, which does appear in the displayed conditioning event. This copies the source's own quantifier list (G&S Definition (6.1.1), p. 240), so it is not an infidelity, but the handbook can simply be right. Fix: "all states $s, x_0, x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}$".
5. [minor] method, step 7 (line 186), and statement (d): with $B$ the matrix whose rows are the left eigenvectors, $P = B^{-1}\Lambda B$ is correct and is the source's convention (p. 269), but a reader who has just been told to find the *left* eigenvector $\pi$ may read $B$ as a matrix of right eigenvectors and get the inverse wrong. Fix: add "where the rows of $B$ are the left eigenvectors of $P$, so that $BP = \Lambda B$".
