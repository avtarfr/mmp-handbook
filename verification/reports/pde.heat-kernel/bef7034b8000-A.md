3. [minor] statement "Smoothing" (line 162): "No smoothness whatever is assumed of $g$" sits under a hypothesis that $g$ *is* continuous. 4. [minor] example "Gaussian initial data" (line 212): "the datum has variance $a^2$" treats $g = \theta_0 e^{-x^2/2a^2}$ as a distribution, although $\int g = \theta_0 a\sqrt{2\pi} \neq 1$. The conclusion is right because $g$ is a constant multiple of a density. Fix: "$g$ is $\theta_0 a\sqrt{2\pi}$ times the density of variance $a^2$, and convolving densities adds variances".
5. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used" (line 203): "The sign of $k$ matters" is then argued by replacing $t$ by $-t$, which is an argument about the sign of $kt$. It is correct (negative $k$ with $t>0$ is the same exponent as positive $k$ with $t<0$) but the step is left implicit. Fix: say that only the product $kt$ enters the exponent, so $k<0$ gives the same non-integrable $e^{+x^2/4|k|t}$.

[minor] application (line 248): "half the diffusion coefficient in that normalisation" is only correct if "diffusion coefficient" means $\sigma^2$ itself; under the commoner convention (variance $2Dt$) one gets $k = D$, not $D/2$. Fix: name which quantity is meant, e.g. "$k = \sigma^2/2$, so $k$ equals the diffusion coefficient $D$ when Brownian variance is written $2Dt$".
