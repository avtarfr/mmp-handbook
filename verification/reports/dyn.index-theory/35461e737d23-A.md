## Findings
1. [blocking] statement[Closed orbits enclose index one] (line 73): "Every closed orbit of a $C^1$ planar system encloses finitely many fixed points, all isolated or not, and if they are isolated their indices sum to $+1$." The finiteness claim is false without isolation. Counterexample: the $C^1$ field $f(x,y) = \max(r-1,0)^2\,(-y,x)$, $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, has $\dot r = 0$ and $\dot\theta = \max(r-1,0)^2$, so the circle $r=2$ is a closed orbit, while the whole disc $r \le 1$ inside it consists of fixed points — uncountably many. The claim also contradicts the rest of the unit: the proof of this very statement (line 136) says "the fixed points inside are finitely many **when isolated**, by step 2 of the additivity proof", and the remark (line 147) says "the finiteness came from isolation plus compactness". Strogatz's Thm 6.8.2 (p. 180) asserts no such finiteness; this is our own addition. Fix: "Every closed orbit encloses at least one fixed point; if every fixed point it encloses is isolated, there are finitely many of them and their indices sum to $+1$."
2. [minor] application (line 207): "**every** continuous tangent field on a sphere vanishes somewhere" is presented as following from the theorem just quoted, but do Carmo's Poincare theorem (p. 286) is stated for a *differentiable* vector field with isolated singular points, and his corollary is that no sphere-like surface carries a nowhere-zero *differentiable* field. The continuous statement is true (it is the hairy-ball theorem) but needs a smoothing argument that is not the quoted theorem. Fix: write "differentiable" in the sentence derived from do Carmo, and, if the continuous case is wanted, add half a sentence that it follows by approximating a continuous field uniformly by a smooth one.
3. [minor] proof[(I4)], step 2 (line 120): "it equals that of $-\gamma'$, which by **(I3)** is again the winding number of $\gamma'$". (I3) is the statement that the *index of the system's field* is unchanged when $f$ is replaced by $-f$; the loop $-\gamma'$ here is a tangent field of a curve, not the field of a system, so (I3) does not literally apply. The reason is the same one line that proves (I3) — a continuous angle determination for $-w$ is one for $w$ plus $\pi$, and a constant shift does not change the net change. Fix: give that one line directly instead of citing (I3).
4. [minor] definition (line 23) against (I1) (line 52): the index is defined only for piecewise $C^1$ curves, but (I1) deforms $C$ through arbitrary continuous intermediate curves and its proof uses homotopy of continuous loops. Fix: say in the definition that the winding number, hence the index, is defined for any continuous loop avoiding the fixed points, and that the piecewise $C^1$ assumption is only for the integral formula.
5. [minor] proof[(I5)], step 2 (line 101): "Such arcs exist; this is the topological input that the balloon picture in Strogatz makes plausible." This is the one unjustified step of the proof and it is honestly flagged, but it is the same Jordan-Schoenflies input already invoked in (I2): under a homeomorphism $D \to \overline{B}$ the circles and bridges can be drawn in the disc and pulled back. Fix: say that, so the reader sees that no new topology is being assumed.
6. [minor] example (line 187): "returns $0$ to twenty digits". Double-precision quadrature of that integral returns about $6\times10^{-16}$; twenty digits requires a high-precision routine. Fix: name the precision used (as the other numbers in the handbook do), or state the figure that the quadrature actually produced.
