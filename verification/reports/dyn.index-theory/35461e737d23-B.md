## Findings
1. [blocking] statement "Closed orbits enclose index one" (line 73): "Every closed orbit of a $C^1$ planar system encloses finitely many fixed points, all isolated or not, and if they are isolated their indices sum to $+1$." The finiteness claim is asserted unconditionally and is false; it is also contradicted by the unit's own proof of the same statement at line 136, which says "The fixed points inside are finitely many **when isolated**, by step 2 of the additivity proof", and by step 2 of the additivity proof at line 101, which derives finiteness from isolation plus compactness. Counterexample: let $g(s)=0$ for $s\le\tfrac14$ and $g(s)=e^{-1/(s-1/4)}$ for $s>\tfrac14$ (smooth), and take $f(x,y)=g(x^2+y^2)\,(-y,x)$, which is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Its fixed-point set is the whole closed disc $x^2+y^2\le\tfrac14$, an uncountable set, while the circle $x^2+y^2=1$ is a closed orbit of period $2\pi/g(1)=23.836318\ldots$ (verified by integrating to $t=T$ from $(1,0)$ with rtol $10^{-12}$: the endpoint returns to $(1,-1.2\times10^{-12})$). That closed orbit encloses infinitely many fixed points. Nothing outside the statement block relies on the false clause. Fix: make the finiteness conditional, e.g. "Every closed orbit of a $C^1$ planar system encloses at least one fixed point. If all the fixed points it encloses are isolated then there are finitely many of them and their indices sum to $+1$." Consequence 1 then needs its own one-line argument (an empty sum is $0\neq1$, which already appears in the proof) and consequences 2 and 3 are unaffected.
2. [minor] statement "Properties of the index", (I6) (line 65): the block is `provenance=adapted` with `src="strogatz-1994:§6.8, pp. 177-178; docarmo-2016:§4-5, pp. 270, 284"`. Those pages carry (I1)-(I5) and, via Example 6.8.4 and Exercise 6.8.1 on pp. 178-179, the index values $+1$ and $-1$ of the elementary linear types, but neither source states the determinant criterion $I(p)=\operatorname{sign}\det Df(p)$. The proof of (I6) is correctly labelled `original`; the statement of it is not attributable to the cited pages. Fix: split (I6) into its own `original` statement, or name it in the block as ours.
3. [minor] example "An analytic field", third bullet (line 187): "The same quadrature ... returns $0$ to twenty digits ... on circles of radius $\tfrac12$ about $(1,0)$ and about $(-1,0)$ it returns $+1$ each". The quadrature returns the contour integral, which is $2\pi$ on those circles (the *index* is $+1$); the preceding bullet is consistent on this point ("returns $12.566\ldots=4\pi$, so $I_C=2$"), so the units change within three lines. Fix: write "returns $2\pi$, that is $I=+1$, on each".
4. [minor] definition (line 23): $C$ is required to be piecewise $C^1$, but the index as a winding number needs only continuity, and (I1)/(I2) are stated for curves that are merely deformable. The computational line-integral form at line 34 is what needs piecewise $C^1$, and the text already says "When $f$ is $C^1$" there. Fix: define $I_C$ for a continuous simple closed curve and impose piecewise $C^1$ only for the integral formula.
