2. [minor] statement, property 1 (lines 56-66): "if $\hat a$ is unbiased for $a$ and $\alpha$ is non-linear, $\alpha(\hat a)$ is not unbiased for $\alpha(a)$, by Jensen's inequality". Jensen needs convexity or concavity, not mere non-linearity, and the unit's own proof block (line ~115) correctly restricts to "$\alpha$ strictly convex and non-affine"; as written the statement claims more than it proves and more than is true (a non-convex, non-concave $\alpha$ can leave the estimator unbiased by cancellation, e.g. $\alpha$ odd about $a$ with $\hat a$ symmetric). Fix: "if $\alpha$ is strictly convex or strictly concave and non-affine".
3. [source] statement, property 1 (line 66) and rhb-2006 §31.5.2, p. 1261: the unit faithfully reports "They note that the bias of an ML estimator always vanishes as $n\to\infty$", which is the book's unqualified sentence on p. 1261. The claim needs the regularity conditions that make the ML estimator consistent; it fails in the Neyman-Scott problem (the ML $\hat\sigma^2$ converges to $\sigma^2/2$, bias $\to -\sigma^2/2$) and where ML is inconsistent. Not a defect of our text - the unit attributes the sentence to the authors and its own remark "The regularity behind statement 3" supplies the missing conditions.
