  - The cardinality definitions leave $=$, $<$, $>$ undefined when either set is empty and extend only $\le$ and $\ge$, exactly as Folland does; every later statement that uses the strict relations says "nonempty", including Cantor's theorem (whose first assertion is correctly flagged as needing no hypothesis - and it does hold for $X=\emptyset$, where $g$ has empty range). I looked specifically for a place where $<$ or $=$ is used on a possibly-empty set and found none.
  - Item 1 of the countability list handles $X$ or $Y$ empty explicitly; item 2 handles all-$X_\alpha$-empty and index-deletion explicitly. Item 3's hypothesis really needs "infinite" (a finite set is countable with no bijection onto $\mathbb{N}$), and the remark says so.
  - Image/inverse-image statement: tried to break "images commute with unions" and the three inverse-image identities with non-injective, non-surjective and constant $f$, and with an infinite family; all hold. The three one-sided relations in the remark, and their equality criteria, hold in both directions ($f$ not injective $\Rightarrow$ $D\subsetneq f^{-1}(f(D))$ for $D$ a singleton; $f$ not surjective $\Rightarrow$ $f(f^{-1}(E))\subsetneq E$ for $E$ containing a missed point).
  - Schröder-Bernstein proof: checked that the three-way classification is exhaustive and exclusive, that Step 3's "prefix one term" argument gives type preservation for both $f$ and $g$, and that Steps 4-5 use the right piece in each case ($f$ onto $Y_\infty$ and $Y_X$, $g$ onto $X_Y$); the glueing in Step 6 is a bijection because the two partitions correspond blockwise. No gap found. Item 3's recursion is well founded for the stated reason (the removed set is finite, $S$ infinite).
  - Item 5, Step 2: the equality case is genuinely excluded. $\sum_{n>m}2^{-n} = 2^{-m}$, so equality forces $B \supseteq \{n : n>m\}$ and $A\cap\{n>m\} = \emptyset$, whence $A \subseteq \{1,\dots,m\}$ has infinite complement while $\mathbb{N}\setminus B \subseteq \{1,\dots,m\}$ is finite - different cases, contradiction. The two value ranges $[0,1]$ and $(1,2]$ are disjoint, and $s(A)>0$ when $\mathbb{N}\setminus A$ is finite, as claimed.
