## Findings
1. [minor] :::statement[The Wronskian criterion] (line 108): "their Wronskian vanishes at a single
   point of $I$" can be read as "at exactly one point". Stakgold and Holst use the same phrase
   (p. 187), so this is inherited, but "at some point of $I$" would be unambiguous.
2. [minor] :::statement[Abel's formula], second bullet (line ~116): "where $m$ is any particular
   solution of $m' = a_1/a_0$ and the constant $C$ depends on the solutions used to form $W$" sits
   close to Stakgold and Holst p. 187 ("$m(x)$ is a particular solution of $m' = a_{p-1}/a_p$" and
   "the constant $C$ is determined by the solutions $\{u_k\}$ used in forming the Wronskian"). I do
   not judge it a close paraphrase (see Provenance below), but one clause could be reworded, e.g.
   "$m$ is any antiderivative of $a_1/a_0$, and $C$ changes if the solutions do".
3. [minor] :::definition[Linear equations of order $n$] (line 36): the block presents the same seven
   items in the same order as Coddington p. 103 (equation form; complex-valued coefficients on a
   real interval; $a_0=0$ points are singular and need special treatment; assume $a_0\ne0$ and
   divide; name the left side $L(y)$; homogeneous vs inhomogeneous; what a solution is). The wording
   is independently written and no run of words is shared, and the block adds the continuity
   assumption, the interval types and the superposition paragraph, so it passes; but the ordering is
   worth varying if this is ever revisited.

## Checked
- Counterexample search: tried to break the three statements at their hypotheses.
  - Dropping $a_0\ne0$: $x^2y''-2xy'+2y=0$ on $\mathbb R$ — verified $x$ and $x^2$ are both
    solutions, independent on $\mathbb R$, and $W=x^2$ vanishes at $0$; so the criterion really does
    need the hypothesis, exactly as the remark says. Also verified the two failure modes stated
    there (no solution with $\varphi(0)=1$, because $x=0$ in the equation forces $2y(0)=0$; and
    non-uniqueness from $c_2x^2$).
  - Dropping "are solutions": $f_1=x^2$, $f_2=x|x|$ on $(-1,1)$ — $f_2'=2|x|$ including at $0$, so
    $W\equiv0$; independence verified by evaluating at $\pm\tfrac12$ (giving $c_1+c_2=0$ and
    $c_1-c_2=0$). Checked the unit's extra claim that the pair satisfies $x^2y''-2xy'+2y=0$ only off
    the origin: for $x<0$, $f_2=-x^2$ gives $-2x^2+4x^2-2x^2=0$, and $f_2''$ does not exist at $0$.
  - Checked the unit's claim that the $x^2,x|x|$ pair violates Arfken's footnote hypothesis: the
    minors of the bottom row of (7.55) here are $f_2=x|x|$ and $f_1=x^2$, and both vanish at $x=0$,
    so no minor is nonvanishing on $[-1,1]$ — the claim is correct. Likewise it violates Hassani's
    "nonzero in the interval" hypothesis.
  - Abel without independence: for dependent solutions $W(x_0)=0$ and the formula gives $W\equiv0$,
    consistent, as the last remark bullet says.
  - Equivalence of items 2 and 3 of the criterion: consistent with Abel (an exponential never
    vanishes), as the proof's closing paragraph notes.
