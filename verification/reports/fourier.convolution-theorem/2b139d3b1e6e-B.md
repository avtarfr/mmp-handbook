## Findings
1. [minor] remark, first bullet (line 181): "For $f=g=\lvert x\rvert^{-1/2}\chi_{[-1,1]}$, which is in $L^1$, the convolution exists" — it exists for almost every $z$, but not at $z=0$, where $(f*g)(0)=\int_{-1}^{1}\lvert x\rvert^{-1}dx=\infty$. The intended contrast with $\lvert x\rvert^{-1}\chi_{[-1,1]}$ (whose convolution diverges at *every* $z\in[-2,2]$, not only at $0$) therefore lands weakly as written, and the sentence sits oddly beside part 1 of the statement, which is careful to say "almost every $z$". Fix: "the convolution exists for almost every $z$ and lies in $L^1$ (it is still infinite at $z=0$), whereas for $\lvert x\rvert^{-1}\chi_{[-1,1]}$ the integral diverges at every $z$ in $[-2,2]$".
2. [minor] application, "Power spectra of noise" (line 246): the sentence says a power spectrum is estimated "by transforming the autocorrelation rather than by squaring a single transform, and it is the basis of the periodogram" — but the periodogram *is* the squared single transform, so the two halves of the sentence pull against each other. What is true is that Wiener-Kinchin underlies both estimators and that the correlogram route is preferred because the raw periodogram is not a consistent estimator. Fix: separate the two claims, or drop "rather than by squaring a single transform".
3. [minor] definition (line 55) and application (line 248): the unit is stated throughout for $\mathbb{R}$, but the electrostatics paragraph uses the three-dimensional convolution theorem and the three-dimensional pair $1/r \leftrightarrow 4\pi/k^2$ without pointing at the $n$-dimensional unit. The algebra is right (with $\psi=\frac{1}{4\pi}\rho*r^{-1}$ one gets $\hat\psi=\hat\rho/k^2$, which is Poisson's equation), but a `:ref` to `fourier.fourier-transform-rn` would close the gap, as Arfken's own (20.72) is the 3-D statement.
