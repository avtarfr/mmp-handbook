## Findings

1. [minor] proof "The averaged equations", Step 2 (line ~288): the letter $r$ changes meaning inside three lines. First "its general solution is $x_0 = r\cos\tau + s\sin\tau$ with $r$ and $s$ constant in $\tau$", where $r$ is the coefficient of $\cos\tau$; then "Writing the same thing in amplitude-phase form, $x_0(\tau,T) = r(T)\cos\theta$", where $r$ is the amplitude $\sqrt{r^2+s^2}$ of the previous line. The style guide asks that notation not change meaning inside a unit. Fix: call the first pair $(A,B)$ or $(c_1,c_2)$ and introduce $r = \sqrt{A^2+B^2}$, $\phi = -\arctan(B/A)$ when passing to amplitude-phase form.

2. [minor] statement "The period of a relaxation oscillation" (line ~85): "Then $F$ is odd, has its only positive minimum at $x_B$ with $F(x_B) < 0$". "Only positive minimum" reads as "only minimum whose value is positive", which is the opposite of what is meant and of what the same sentence then says ($F(x_B)<0$). Fix: "has its only minimum on the positive axis at $x_B$, where $F(x_B) < 0$".

3. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first sentence (line ~337): "Evenness of $f$ and oddness of $g$ enter only in Step 4". The statement being proved has already fixed $g(x) = x$, so oddness of $g$ is not one of its hypotheses; it is a hypothesis of Lienard's theorem in the block above. As written the remark names a hypothesis the proof's statement does not carry. Fix: say "evenness of $f$, together with the oddness of the restoring force $g(x)=x$ that the statement assumes, enters only in Step 4".

4. [minor] method, step 2 (line ~383): "If the damping coefficient is large compared with the frequency, you are in the relaxation limit". A damping coefficient and a frequency are only comparable after the nondimensionalisation that step 2 goes on to recommend, so the test is stated before the thing that makes it meaningful. Fix: reverse the order - rescale $x$ and $t$ so that the restoring force is $x$ and the frequency is $1$, and *then* read off whether the damping coefficient is large or small.
