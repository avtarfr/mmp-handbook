## Findings

1. [minor] statement "The period of a relaxation oscillation": the block speaks of "the period of *the* limit cycle" without saying why one exists and is unique under the hypotheses listed there. It does follow: $f$ even and $C^1$ with $f<0$ on $(0,x_B)$, $f>0$ on $(x_B,\infty)$ and $F\to\infty$ makes $F$ strictly decreasing then strictly increasing, hence with exactly one positive zero $a>x_B$, negative on $(0,a)$, positive and increasing on $(a,\infty)$ - which is Lienard condition 5 - and $g(x)=x$, $\mu f$ supply conditions 1-4. Fix: add one sentence saying that these hypotheses imply the five of the preceding statement, so that Lienard's theorem supplies the cycle.

2. [minor] proof "The period of a relaxation oscillation", Steps 2-3: the boxed conclusion carries an explicit remainder $+o(\mu)$, which is sharper than Strogatz's "$T\approx$", but the argument never bounds that remainder. Step 3's "The trajectory cannot stay off the curve, and it cannot cross it transversally at speed $\mu$, so it must settle into the region where $y-F(x)=O(\mu^{-2})$", and the ensuing "To leading order the trajectory then follows $y=F(x)$", are asserted rather than proved, and the deviation from the slow branch is never integrated over the $O(\mu)$ duration of a crawl. The conclusion is true (I checked it numerically, below), and the argument is exactly Strogatz's at p. 213 carried over to a general even $f$, so this is a matter of rigour inherited from the source rather than an error. Fix: either weaken the remainder to "$T = 2\mu\int_{x_B}^{x_A}f/x\,dx\,(1+o(1))$ at the level of rigour of the nullcline argument", or say in the proof's opening that the slow-manifold step is the one the argument assumes.

3. [minor] remark, first paragraph: "Evenness of $f$ and oddness of $g$ enter only in Step 4". Two inaccuracies. (i) In the statement being proved $g(x)=x$ is fixed, so oddness of $g$ is not one of its hypotheses. (ii) Evenness of $f$ is used before Step 4: the hypotheses give the sign of $f$ only on $(0,\infty)$, so Step 2's claim that "the middle branch $|x|<x_B$ repels horizontally" needs $f<0$ on $(-x_B,0)$ too, which is evenness. Fix: "Evenness of $f$ enters in Step 2, to give the sign of $f$ on the left half-plane, and in Step 4, to make the two crawls mirror images".

4. [minor] definition, last paragraph: "which is our van der Pol equation with $\mu=1$ after the rescaling $x\mapsto x/\sqrt3$" is ambiguous and is wrong on the reading that substitutes $x/\sqrt3$ for $x$ (that gives $\ddot u + (\tfrac13u^2-1)\dot u + u=0$, not $\ddot u + (3u^2-1)\dot u + u = 0$). What is true is that the *new variables* are $u = x/\sqrt3$ and $v = y/\sqrt3$, both coordinates rescaled by the same factor, under which $\dot x = y - (\tfrac13x^3-x)$, $\dot y=-x$ becomes $\dot u = v - (u^3-u)$, $\dot v = -u$. Fix: "after setting $u = x/\sqrt3$, $v = y/\sqrt3$".

5. [minor] application, last sentence: the pointer ":ref[]{to=dyn.global-bifurcations}" is attached to "saddle-node bifurcations of those fixed points are the jumps of the familiar bent resonance curve". That unit treats autonomous planar bifurcations of cycles (saddle-node of cycles, infinite-period, homoclinic) and nowhere discusses a driven weakly nonlinear oscillator, the averaged fixed points of one, or the bent resonance curve, so the reference does not support the sentence. Fix: drop the cross-reference, or point it at the saddle-node of fixed points in dyn.one-dimensional-bifurcations and say that the resonance curve itself is beyond this unit.

6. [minor] frontmatter, `role` line of the hirsch-smale-devaney-2013 source: "the sign of $f'(0)$ as the stability test at the origin". In their general Lienard system the unique equilibrium is $(0,f(0))$ (p. 262), which is the origin only when $f(0)=0$; it is the origin for their van der Pol case and for the $F$ of this unit, but not in the generality the line claims. Fix: "at the equilibrium $(0,f(0))$".

Proofs, re-derived line by line. Relaxation: $\ddot x+\mu f\dot x = \tfrac{d}{dt}(\dot x+\mu F)$ and hence ($\ast$); $\tfrac{d}{dt}(y-F)=-x/\mu-\mu f\,(y-F)$, so the outer branches ($f>0$) attract horizontally and the middle one repels, which is the generalisation of Strogatz's one-sided argument and is correct; $\dot x = -x/(\mu f(x))$ on the slow branch, $dt=-\mu f\,dx/x$, and the orientation of the limits in Step 5 gives a positive time. The existence and uniqueness of $x_A$ from strict monotonicity of $F$ on $(x_B,\infty)$ is correct. Averaging: the two-time chain rule, the $O(1)$ and $O(\epsilon)$ equations, $\partial_{\tau T}x_0=-r'\sin\theta-r\phi'\cos\theta$, the Fourier normalisation (the unit's $a_k,b_k$ carry $1/\pi$, so $r'=b_1/2=\langle h\sin\theta\rangle$ and $r\phi'=a_1/2$ are consistent), and the resonance argument all check out; the unit is internally consistent in using $\langle\cdot\rangle=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}$ throughout.

Numbers, all recomputed with scipy/mpmath rather than taken from the text.
