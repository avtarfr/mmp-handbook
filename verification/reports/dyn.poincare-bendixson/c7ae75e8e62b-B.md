## Findings
1. [blocking] remark, "Planarity" bullet (line 156): "In $\mathbb{R}^3$ a trajectory may be confined to a compact fixed-point-free region forever without approaching any closed orbit; the Lorenz system does exactly that." The Lorenz system does not do that. Every trapping region for the Lorenz flow contains its equilibria -- the origin lies *on* the Lorenz attractor, and $C_\pm$ lie inside any bounded absorbing set -- so no compact forward-invariant region of the Lorenz flow is free of fixed points. The sentence is in an `original` block and is our own claim, and it also misstates the unit's own cross-referenced material. The general assertion before the semicolon is true and does need a fixed-point-free witness: an irrational linear flow on an invariant $2$-torus embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is one (the torus is compact, invariant, nowhere zero, every orbit dense, no closed orbit at all); Kuperberg's plug is the sharper one. Fix: either drop "fixed-point-free" and keep Lorenz, matching Strogatz p. 210 ("trajectories may wander around forever in a bounded region without settling down to a fixed point or a closed orbit"), or keep "fixed-point-free" and replace the witness by the irrational flow on an invariant torus, mentioning Lorenz separately as the physically important bounded-but-aperiodic example.
2. [minor] method step 7 (line 185) and example step 3 (line 228): "there is a small disc $B$ every trajectory of which leaves $B$ in forward time" is not what makes $R\setminus B$ positively invariant -- a trajectory could enter $B$ and leave later and the complement would still not be invariant. What the repeller hypothesis actually supplies, via a quadratic Liapunov function for the time-reversed system, is that $B$ is *negatively* invariant for the forward flow, equivalently that $f\cdot n>0$ on $\partial B$, which is the property used. Fix: state it as "a small disc $B$ on whose boundary the field points strictly outward, so that no forward trajectory enters $B$".
