## Findings
1. [minor] Lemma 2, statement and step 7 (lines 115, 131): the hypothesis "suppose they are distinct" is both stronger than the proof needs and, as stated, makes the following sentence ("Consequently the whole sequence of successive crossings is monotone along $S$") a non-sequitur - to chain the triple result along the sequence one would have to know in advance that no crossing point repeats, which is not given. In fact step 7 uses only $Y_0\neq Y_1$: $\Sigma$ is a Jordan curve as soon as the two are distinct, and the conclusion $Y_2\in I_1$ then *gives* $Y_2\notin\{Y_0,Y_1\}$, so distinctness propagates by itself. This matters for Lemma 3, whose contradiction needs the crossing sequence of the orbit of $X$ to be monotone: as the lemmas stand, nothing in them excludes an $X$ on a closed orbit that crosses $S$ at two points, whose crossing sequence alternates and is not monotone - and that is exactly the configuration Lemma 3 must rule out. Step 7 does rule it out (after $Y_1$ the orbit never meets $\overline D$ again, and $Y_0\in T\subset\overline D$), so nothing is actually false. Fix: hypothesise $Y_0\neq Y_1$ only; state $Y_2\in I_1$, hence distinct, as part of the conclusion; and add the degenerate case $Y_0=Y_1$ (the orbit is periodic and crosses $S$ at one point, so the sequence is constant and Lemma 3's two disjoint subsegments cannot both be met). Hirsch, Smale and Devaney leave the same step implicit on pp. 220-222, so this is our exposition, not infidelity.
2. [minor] Dulac statement (line 44) and proof steps 1-2 (lines 94-95): $R$ is not required to be open. Strogatz says "a simply connected subset $R$ of the plane", so following him is faithful, but our proof needs more than his does: step 1 concludes that the Jordan interior $A$ of $C$ lies in $R$, which is a statement about open simply connected plane sets, and step 2 needs $gf$ to be $C^1$ on a *neighbourhood* of $\overline A$. Both examples and the counterexample in the remark use open regions. Fix: say "open and simply connected" in the statement.
