- Counterexample search.

No counterexample.
  - Weierstrass M-test: tried $M_n$ depending on $x$ (excluded by "constants ... for all $x \in E$"), $\sum M_n$ divergent with $\sum u_n$ still uniformly convergent (the remark's own $\sum (-1)^n/(n+x^2)$: the test is silent, as the unit says, not wrong), and $M_n \ge 0$ with some $M_n = 0$. The conclusion "and it converges absolutely at every point" is correct and is the limitation the third remark bullet exploits.
  - Independence claims: verified both directions. $\sum_{n\ge0}(1-x)x^n$ on $[0,1]$ converges absolutely at every point (non-negative terms) but not uniformly; $\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^n/(n+x^2)$ converges uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$ (tail $\le 1/(m+1+x^2) \le 1/(m+1)$ by the alternating bound, independent of $x$) but not absolutely (at $x = 0$ it is the harmonic series). Both match the unit.
- Calculations (`.venv/bin/python`).
  - $f_n(x) = x^n$ on $[0,1]$: $\sup_{x}|f_n(x) - f(x)| = 1$ for every $n$ (approached as $x \to 1^-$, where $f = 0$); confirmed numerically at $n = 1, 5, 50$ by sampling $x \to 1^-$ (values $\to 1$). The unit's claim of $\sup = 1$ is correct as a supremum (it is not attained), and it correctly uses $\sup$, not $\max$.
  - Arfken's Example 1.2.1 sum: $\sum_{n\ge0}(1-x)x^n = (1-x)/(1-x) = 1$ for $0 \le x < 1$ and $0$ at $x = 1$; partial sums $s_N(x) = 1 - x^N$, so $\sup_{[0,1)}|1 - s_N| = \sup x^N = 1$. Matches the unit and the book.
  - Uniform tail bound for $\sum (-1)^n/(n+x^2)$: computed $\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|S(x) - s_m(x)|$ for $m = 10, 100$ by sampling; it is attained at $x = 0$ and equals $\approx 1/(2(m+1))$, so $\to 0$ uniformly, while $\sum_x$-wise the absolute series diverges. Confirms the remark.
  - Restriction to $[0,\rho]$, $\rho < 1$: $\sup_{[0,\rho]}|1 - s_N| = \rho^N \to 0$, so uniform convergence is restored, as the unit says.
