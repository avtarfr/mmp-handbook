  - *Mixed integer and half-integer $m$.* Searched for an irreducible $V$ with both: impossible, since all $m$ differ by integers from $j$. The claim "never mixed" is tight.
  - *The remark's non-Hermitian counterexample.* Verified the whole construction symbolically. With $J_ze_n=(\mu+n)e_n$, $J_+e_n=e_{n+1}$, $J_-e_n=(\lambda-(\mu+n)(\mu+n-1))e_{n-1}$: $[J_z,J_+]=J_+$ and $[J_z,J_-]=-J_-$ by direct evaluation; $[J_+,J_-]e_n=\bigl[(\mu+n+1)(\mu+n)-(\mu+n)(\mu+n-1)\bigr]e_n=2(\mu+n)e_n=2J_ze_n$. I then checked that these three relations really do give the full angular-momentum algebra for $J_x=\tfrac12(J_++J_-)$, $J_y=\tfrac1{2i}(J_+-J_-)$: $[J_x,J_y]=\tfrac{1}{4i}(-2[J_+,J_-])=iJ_z$, $[J_z,J_x]=\tfrac12(J_+-J_-)=iJ_y$, $[J_z,J_y]=\tfrac{1}{2i}(J_++J_-)=-iJ_x$. All three hold. And $J_x^2+J_y^2=\tfrac12(J_+J_-+J_-J_+)$, so $J^2=J_-J_++J_z^2+J_z$, which the construction makes $\lambda\,\mathrm{Id}$ — confirmed. So the example does what it claims: the full algebra, an arbitrary complex "$J^2$", and a two-way infinite ladder. The closing impossibility argument ($\|J_+e_n\|^2=(\lambda-(\mu+n)(\mu+n+1))\|e_n\|^2<0$ for large $n$ once $\lambda,\mu$ are real) is correct.
  - *Could the ladder fail to terminate with Hermiticity but infinite dimension?* No: with $J^2=\lambda\,\mathrm{Id}$ and $\|J_+v\|^2=\lambda-m^2-m\ge0$, $m$ is bounded above, and since each rung raises $m$ by exactly $1$ the chain must hit $0$ in finitely many steps. So the unit's parenthetical in step 4 is a genuine substitute for finite-dimensionality, and the counterexample is correctly aimed at Hermiticity (finding 3 is only about saying so).
