## Findings
1. [minor] definition "The equation, and how nonlinear it is" (line 52): "the two agree because $B = 2b$, and all statements below are unchanged if $\delta$ is replaced by $B^2 - 4AC$". Only the *sign* survives the substitution: $B^2-4AC = 4b^2-4ac = 4\delta$, so any statement that uses the magnitude of $\delta$ does change. Two such statements are below: Step 3/4 of the canonical-form proof sets $\beta = \sqrt{-\delta}$ and $\beta=\sqrt\delta$ (replacing $\delta$ by $B^2-4AC$ doubles $\beta$ and so changes the change of variables), and example (b) computes $\delta = -y$ for the Tricomi equation. Fix: say that the three cases are decided by the sign alone, and that $B^2-4AC$ has the same sign as $\delta$, rather than claiming every statement is unchanged.
2. [minor] proof of "The type counts the real characteristic directions", Step 4 (line 116): the enumeration of the $a=0$ case omits $b \ne 0$, $c = 0$ — the pure mixed equation $u_{xy} = F$, which is the standard non-generic hyperbolic example. There (2) reads $-2b\,dx\,dy = 0$, so the two characteristic directions are $dy = 0$ *and* $dx = 0$; the latter has no slope $dy/dx$, so the sentence "a second direction $dy/dx = 2b/c$ appears when $b \ne 0$ and $c \ne 0$" does not reach it, yet "The count is as stated in both cases" claims the whole $a=0$ case is covered. Nothing in the statement is affected, because the statement's count is asserted only for $a(P) \ne 0$. Fix: add the vertical direction $dx = 0$ to the $a = 0$ discussion, or say the count claim is only for $a \ne 0$.
3. [minor] remark after the many-variables definition (line 212): "Diagonalising $A$ by $A \mapsto BAB^{\mathsf T}$ does not preserve the eigenvalues". The diagonalisation performed in the definition block immediately above is by a *rotation*, for which $B^{\mathsf T} = B^{-1}$ and the eigenvalues are preserved — which is exactly why that block (and Strauss, p. 30) may speak of "the eigenvalues $d_1,\dots,d_n$ of $A$". What the remark means is a general invertible $B$, in particular the subsequent change of scale. Fix: write "a general invertible $B$" (the rest of the bullet, and Sylvester's law, are right).
