## Findings
1. [minor] remark (line 93): the Hilbert matrix is given as "$A_{ij} = 1/(i+j+1)$" with no index range, and the range is what makes the formula right. Arfken's Exercise 5.1.3 (p. 267) writes $A_{ij} = \int_0^1 x^{i+j}\,dx = 1/(i+j+1)$ **with "$i, j = 0, 1, 2, \dots, n-1$"** printed on the same line, and the unit drops that qualification while quoting the determinant that depends on it. Everywhere else in this unit and this chapter indices start at $1$ (e.g. $\|x\|_q = (\sum_i |x_i|^q)^{1/q}$, $s_1 \ge \cdots \ge s_n$), so a reader will read $i,j \ge 1$ and build the wrong matrix. Exact determinants from sympy at $n=5$: with $i,j$ from $0$, $\det = 3.7493\times10^{-12}$, which is the $3.7\times10^{-12}$ the unit quotes; with $i,j$ from $1$, $\det = 1.9322\times10^{-16}$. Fix: add "for $i, j = 0, \dots, n-1$", or write the 1-based form $A_{ij} = 1/(i+j-1)$.
2. [minor] statement "Sensitivity of a linear system", part 4 (line 83): "Solving $Ax = b$ in arithmetic with unit roundoff $\varepsilon$ gives a relative error of order $\kappa(A)\varepsilon$ ... whatever algorithm is used" is the right rule of thumb pointing the wrong way. $\kappa(A)\varepsilon$ is the accuracy a *backward-stable* algorithm achieves, and it is a floor that no algorithm can beat; an unstable algorithm does strictly worse, so "whatever algorithm is used" cannot be attached to "gives". Fix: "a backward-stable algorithm gives a relative error of order $\kappa(A)\varepsilon$, and no algorithm does better".
3. [minor] remark, bullet "**Conditioning is a property of the problem**" (line 93), together with [source] strauss-2008 §1.5 p. 27: the sentence attributed to Strauss reads "a nonsingular matrix with a very small *singular value* ...", but Strauss writes "consider a nonsingular matrix $A$ with one very small *eigenvalue*. The solution is unique ... Such a matrix, in the context of scientific computation, is called ill-conditioned." Our version is the correct criterion and Strauss's is not — $\begin{pmatrix}1&10^6\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has both eigenvalues equal to $1$ (computed: eigenvalues $1, 1$) yet singular values $10^6$ and $10^{-6}$ and $\kappa_2 = 1.0\times10^{12}$ — so the unit is right on the mathematics. But it silently upgrades the book's statement inside a sentence that names the book, which is the pattern `docs/contributing/source-authority.md` tells drafters not to use. Recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add linalg.matrix-norms-conditioning strauss-2008 "Sec. 1.5, p. 27" ...`. Fix: attribute to Strauss only the framing (ill-conditioning as the stability leg of well-posedness, which is exactly what pp. 26-27 give) and add a half-sentence saying he characterises it by a small eigenvalue while the right quantity is the smallest singular value.
4. [minor] definition "Induced matrix norm" (line 31): "Taking the vector norms $\|x\|_q = (\sum_i|x_i|^q)^{1/q}$ gives" the $1$- and $\infty$-norm formulas, but $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is not of that form — it is the $q\to\infty$ limit, $\max_i|x_i|$ — and the sentence as written defines it by a formula that does not produce it. Also, "the Frobenius norm ... is *not* induced by any pair of vector norms, since $\|I_n\|_F = \sqrt n$" needs $n \ge 2$: at $n = 1$ the Frobenius norm is the absolute value and is induced ($\|I_1\|_F = 1$, computed). Fix: say "and, with the max-norm $\|x\|_\infty = \max_i|x_i|$", and add "for $n \ge 2$" to the Frobenius remark.

## Checked
- Counterexample search and numerical verification:
  - The two displayed $p$-norm formulas and $\|A\|_2 = s_1$ checked on random $4\times5$ matrices: largest column sum for $\|\cdot\|_1$, largest row sum for $\|\cdot\|_\infty$, largest singular value for $\|\cdot\|_2$ — all True, and the row/column assignment is the right way round (it is easy to get backwards). $\|I_n\|_F = 1, \sqrt2, \sqrt3$ for $n=1,2,3$. Frobenius submultiplicativity held on 200 random pairs.
  - Statement part 2 ($\rho(A) \le \|A\|$) and part 4 (strictness): $A = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ gives $\rho(A) = 0$ and $\|A\|_2 = 1$ — the printed example is correct, and $\rho$ is not a norm for exactly that reason, as the remark says.
  - **Part 1 of the sensitivity statement, both inequalities, in three induced norms** ($2$, $1$, $\infty$): 2000 random invertible $4\times4$ systems with random perturbations, checking $\kappa^{-1}\|\delta b\|/\|b\| \le \|\delta x\|/\|x\| \le \kappa\|\delta b\|/\|b\|$ — **0 violations**. Derived independently: $\delta x = A^{-1}\delta b$ and $\|b\| \le \|A\|\|x\|$ give the upper bound; $\|\delta b\| \le \|A\|\|\delta x\|$ and $\|x\| \le \|A^{-1}\|\|b\|$ give the lower one.
  - **The attainment claim**, which is the sharp part: taking $b = s_1u_1$ and $\delta b \propto u_n$ on a random $4\times4$ gave an amplification of $9.53546657032044$ against $\kappa_2 = 9.535466570320436$ — equal to 14 digits, so $\kappa$ really is the worst case and "no sharper constant exists" is right. (Derivation: $x = A^{-1}s_1u_1 = v_1$, $\delta x = cv_n/s_n$, so the ratio is exactly $s_1/s_n$.)
  - **Part 2**: the identity $\hat x - x = -A^{-1}\,\delta A\,\hat x$ follows from $(A+\delta A)\hat x = Ax$, and the bound was checked on 2000 random $4\times4$ systems with $\|\delta A\| \sim 10^{-3}$ — **0 violations**.
  - **Part 3**: with $\delta A = -s_nu_nv_n^\dagger$ the smallest singular value of $A + \delta A$ came out $3.87\times10^{-16}$ (singular, as claimed) while $\|\delta A\|_2 = 0.31827215024038275$ equals $s_n = 0.3182721502403828$ — both the minimising perturbation and the value $s_n = \|A\|_2/\kappa_2$ are correct.
  - Condition-number definition: $\kappa(A) \ge \|AA^{-1}\| = \|I\| = 1$ is immediate from submultiplicativity, and $\kappa_2 = s_1/s_n$ and $\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ for Hermitian positive definite were checked numerically; a random unitary gave $\kappa_2 = 1.0000000000000007$.
  - Remark's determinant traps: $\det(10^{-3}I_4) = 10^{-12}$ with $\kappa_2 = 1.0$, and $\operatorname{diag}(10^6,10^{-6})$ has $\det = 1.0$ and $\kappa_2 = 1.0\times10^{12}$ — both printed claims confirmed, including the scale invariance of $\kappa_2$.
  - **The norm-equivalence bound** $n^{-1}\kappa_\infty \le \kappa_2 \le n\kappa_\infty$: 2000 random invertible matrices of sizes $2$ through $6$ — **0 violations**.
  - Degenerate cases: $n=1$ (where finding 4's Frobenius caveat bites, and nothing else), $A$ unitary ($\kappa_2 = 1$, bounds tight at equality), $A$ nearly singular ($\kappa_2 \sim 10^{12}$, bounds still hold), $b$ an eigenvector/singular vector, and $\delta b = 0$ (both inequalities trivial).
