1. [blocking] definition[Induced matrix norm] (lines 25-38): the block fixes "a norm $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$ and one on $\mathbb{C}^m$" — two norms, allowed to differ — and then asserts two properties that are false in that generality.
   - "Induced norms satisfy $\|I\| = 1$" (line 31). Counterexample with $m=n=2$, inside the block's own set-up: take $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on the domain and $\|\cdot\|_1$ on the codomain. Then $\|I\| = \max_{\|x\|_\infty = 1}\|x\|_1 = 2$, attained at $x=(1,1)$; in general $n$, not $1$.
   - "are **submultiplicative**: $\|AB\| \le \|A\|\|B\|$ whenever the product is defined" (line 31). Counterexample: take $\|\cdot\|_1$ on the domain and $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on the codomain, so that $\|M\| = \max_{i,j}|M_{ij}|$. For $A$ the $2\times2$ all-ones matrix, $\|A\| = 1$ but $A^2$ has every entry $2$, so $\|A^2\| = 2 > 1 = \|A\|\,\|A\|$.
   - Consequently the inference at line 38, "the Frobenius norm ... is *not* induced by any pair of vector norms, since $\|I_n\|_F = \sqrt n$", does not go through: $\sqrt n$ is itself the value of a mixed induced norm of $I_n$ (with $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on the domain and $\|\cdot\|_2$ on the codomain), so $\|I_n\|_F \neq 1$ rules nothing out about pairs. The conclusion happens to be true — testing a pair $(a,b)$ on rank-one $A = uv^\dagger$ forces $\|u\|_2\|v\|_2 = \|u\|_b\,\|v\|_{a^\ast}$ for all $u,v$, hence $b$ and $a$ proportional to $\|\cdot\|_2$, hence the induced norm proportional to $\|A\|_2$, which $\|\cdot\|_F$ is not — but the argument given does not establish it.
   This is also internally inconsistent with the proof: step 0 (line 55) proves $\|I\|=1$ from "$\|Ix\| = \|x\|$ for every $x$", and chains $\|ABx\| \le \|A\|\|Bx\| \le \|A\|\|B\|\|x\|$ through an intermediate $\|Bx\|$, both of which need one and the same norm on the two spaces. The cited source (stakgold-holst-2011 p. 772) defines the operator norm for $M$ acting on a single space with a single norm, which is the setting in which the two properties hold. Downstream nothing breaks, because every use — $\kappa(A) = \|A\|\|A^{-1}\| \ge \|I\| = 1$ (line 80), and parts 1-2 of the sensitivity statement — is for square $A$ with one norm. Fix: fix one norm family, used on both spaces (and say that this is what makes $\|I\|=1$ and submultiplicativity available), keeping the rectangular case for the $\|A\|_1$, $\|A\|_\infty$, $\|A\|_2$ formulas; and either restrict the Frobenius remark to that setting or replace its justification with the rank-one argument.
