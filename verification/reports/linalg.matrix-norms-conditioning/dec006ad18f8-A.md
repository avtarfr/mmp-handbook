## Findings
1. [minor] idea (line 53, before the proof of "The spectral norm and the spectrum"): "Everything here comes from one change of coordinates" describes parts 1-4 only. The proof that follows opens with step 0 (the $1$- and $\infty$-norm formulas, $\|I\|=1$, submultiplicativity, and the rank-one argument that no pair of norms induces $\|\cdot\|_F$) and closes with step 5 (the inverse, $\kappa_2 = s_1/s_n$, $\|Ax\|_2 \ge s_n\|x\|_2$), neither of which is a change of coordinates and neither of which the idea mentions. Fix: add a sentence covering the two framing steps, or move step 0's material behind its own short idea.

2. [minor] application, second bullet (line 246): the interval changes silently. The Hilbert matrix is introduced as the Gram matrix of $1, x, \dots, x^{n-1}$ on $[0,1]$, but the "cure" is then given as the Legendre polynomials, which are orthogonal on $[-1,1]$; on $[0,1]$ one needs the shifted Legendre polynomials. The number $\kappa_2 = 21$ is correct for the weights $\int_{-1}^{1}P_k^2 = 2/(2k+1)$ with $k \le 10$ (I checked $\max/\min = 2/(2/21) = 21$), but as written the reader is invited to use a basis orthogonal on the wrong interval. Fix: say "shifted Legendre polynomials on $[0,1]$", or state that the comparison is made on $[-1,1]$.

3. [minor] application, third bullet (line 248): the eigenvalues are given as $(4/h^2)\sin^2(k\pi h/2)$ without saying that $k$ runs over $1, \dots, N$. That range is what makes $\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} = \sin^2(N\pi h/2)/\sin^2(\pi h/2) = \cot^2(\pi h/2)$, since $Nh = 1-h$. Fix: give the index range.
