## Findings
1. [minor] remark "Traps and conventions" (line 175): "The sharp condition is a small smallest *singular* value" is sharp for the absolute amplification $\|A^{-1}\|=1/s_n$ that Strauss is describing, but not for conditioning, and the block's own first trap (line 173) gives the counterexample — $\varepsilon I_n$ has $s_n=\varepsilon$ as small as you like and $\kappa_2=1$. Because the paragraph is headed "Conditioning is a property of the problem", a reader will take "the sharp condition" to be the condition for ill-conditioning, which this is not. Fix: say "a small $s_n$ *relative to* $s_1$" (equivalently: a large $\|A^{-1}\|$ relative to $1/\|A\|$), which keeps the intended eigenvalue-versus-singular-value correction intact.
2. [minor] statement "Sensitivity of a linear system", part 1 (line 114): "In the spectral norm the upper bound is attained" is weaker than the truth. The bound $\kappa$ is attained in *every* induced norm: pick $x$ with $\|Ax\|=\|A\|\,\|x\|$ and set $b=Ax$, then pick $\delta b$ with $\|A^{-1}\delta b\|=\|A^{-1}\|\,\|\delta b\|$ — both maxima exist by the compactness argument of the first definition — and the amplification is exactly $\|A\|\,\|A^{-1}\|$. Since part 1 is stated for an arbitrary induced norm and the next sentence ("no sharper constant exists") is unrestricted, the restriction to the spectral norm reads as a limitation that is not there. Fix: state the attainment for a general induced norm, and say that proof step 3 exhibits it in the spectral norm because the singular bases make the extremal $b$ and $\delta b$ explicit.
3. [minor] statement "The spectral norm and the spectrum" (line 43): the block-level `src` is `stakgold-holst-2011:§10.6, pp. 771-772 (including Lemma 10.6.1)`, but only part 3 is on those pages — $\|A\|_2=\rho(A)$ for Hermitian $A$ (p. 772's "if $M$ is self-adjoint, then $\rho(M)=\|M\|$", and Lemma 10.6.1 at $A=I$) together with the two-sided Rayleigh estimate of p. 771. Part 1 ($\|A\|_2=s_1$) comes from `linalg.svd`, part 2 ($\rho(A)\le\|A\|$ for every induced norm) is nowhere on those pages — the book treats only the self-adjoint equality — and part 4 is ours. The proof block does localise this correctly at step 3 ("the matrix case of Lemma 10.6.1"). Fix: narrow the statement's `src` to the parts it covers, as the proof does.
4. [minor] frontmatter `sources`, `stakgold-holst-2011` `loc` (line 11): the loc reads as though p. 772 displays "$\kappa(M)=\|M\|\,\|M^{-1}\|$" as "the standard notion". The page writes only "we define the $A$-condition number of an invertible operator $M$ by extending the standard notion to the $A$-inner product: $\kappa_A(M)=\|M\|_A\|M^{-1}\|_A$"; the plain formula is never displayed. The definition block at line 104 is careful about this (it quotes "the standard notion" and says the plain $\kappa$ *is* that notion), and the `role` field is honest. Fix: mark the formula in the loc as our gloss.
5. [minor] application, second bullet (line 246): the Hilbert-matrix discussion is set on $[0,1]$ and the orthogonal-polynomial comparison then uses the Legendre polynomials, whose $\int_{-1}^{1}P_k^2=2/(2k+1)$ is on $[-1,1]$; the interval changes without a word. The conclusion is unaffected — the shifted Legendre polynomials on $[0,1]$ have $\int_0^1 \tilde P_k^2 = 1/(2k+1)$ and the same ratio $21$ — but the switch should be said. Fix: name the interval, or use the shifted family.
6. [source] `strauss-2008` §1.5, p. 27: Strauss characterises an ill-conditioned matrix as "a nonsingular matrix with one very small eigenvalue". That criterion is not sharp in either direction — $\begin{pmatrix}1&M\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has both eigenvalues equal to $1$ and $\kappa_2\approx M^2$ (checked: $M=10,100,1000$ give $\kappa_2=1.02\times10^2, 1.00\times10^4, 1.00\times10^6$), while $\varepsilon I$ has an arbitrarily small eigenvalue and $\kappa_2=1$. The unit reports Strauss faithfully and supplies the correction in its own remark, so this does not count against it; recorded with `node tools/discrepancy.mjs add linalg.matrix-norms-conditioning strauss-2008 "§1.5, p. 27" ...`.

## Findings that are not findings
Checked and found correct, in case another reviewer flags them: the Frobenius sub-argument's dual norm $\|v\|_{a^\ast}=\max_{\|x\|_a=1}|v^\dagger x|$ really is a norm and the step $\|x\|_{a^\ast}\ge|x^\dagger x|=\|x\|_2^2$ is legitimate because $x$ itself is admissible in the maximum; the $\infty$-norm equality vector $x_j=\overline{A_{i^\ast j}}/|A_{i^\ast j}|$ does give $(Ax)_{i^\ast}=\sum_j|A_{i^\ast j}|$; and part 2's bound is correctly stated in terms of $\|\hat x\|$, not $\|x\|$.
