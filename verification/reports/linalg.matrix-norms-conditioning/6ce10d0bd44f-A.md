## Findings
1. [minor] definition "Induced matrix norm" (lines 25-38) and proof step 0 (line 55): the block fixes *two* norms, one on $\mathbb{C}^n$ and one on $\mathbb{C}^m$, and then asserts "$\|I\| = 1$" and submultiplicativity. Both need the two spaces to carry the *same* norm. With $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on the domain and $\|\cdot\|_1$ on the codomain, the induced norm of $I_n$ is $\max_{\|x\|_\infty=1}\|x\|_1 = n$, not $1$; step 0's one-line justification ("$\|Ix\| = \|x\|$ for every $x$") quietly assumes they coincide. The Frobenius conclusion inherits the gap, since it is drawn from "every induced norm has $\|I\|=1$". The conclusion itself is still true for any pair of norms — a pair-induced norm makes $\|uv^\dagger\|$ a product $\|u\|_{(2)}\|v\|_{(1)}^{*}$, matching $\|u\|_2\|v\|_2$ forces both norms to be multiples of $\|\cdot\|_2$, and the induced norm is then $\|\cdot\|_2 \neq \|\cdot\|_F$ — and nothing downstream is affected, because the rest of the unit fixes one induced norm throughout. Fix: say that $\|I\|=1$, submultiplicativity and the Frobenius remark are for one norm used on both spaces.
2. [minor] remark "Traps and conventions", second paragraph (line 160): "Strauss (§1.5, pp. 26-27) places it as the stability requirement in well-posedness: a nonsingular matrix with a very small *singular value* ...". Strauss p. 26 writes "a nonsingular matrix $A$ with one very small *eigenvalue*". The colon reads as a paraphrase of him, and the word has been changed. Our version is the right criterion and is in fact more general (a small eigenvalue forces $s_n \le |\lambda|$, so it implies a small smallest singular value, but not conversely), so this is a stronger claim attributed to the source. Fix: keep Strauss's "eigenvalue" in the sentence attributed to him and add our own clause, e.g. "— the sharp condition being a small *singular value*, since $s_n \le |\lambda|$ for every eigenvalue".
3. [minor] remark (line 160) and application, second bullet (line 231): the Hilbert matrix is given as $A_{ij} = 1/(i+j+1)$ with no index range. The quoted numbers — $\det A = 3.7\times10^{-12}$ at $n=5$, $\kappa_2 \approx 4.8\times10^{5}$ at $n=5$ and $1.6\times10^{13}$ at $n=10$ — are those of the standard Hilbert matrix, i.e. of $1/(i+j+1)$ read with $i,j = 0,\dots,n-1$, which is how Arfken writes it (Exercise 5.1.3, p. 267, explicitly "$i, j = 0, 1, 2, \dots, n-1$"). Read with $i,j = 1,\dots,n$, as $A_{ij}$ is indexed everywhere else in this unit, the $5\times5$ matrix has $\det \approx 1.9\times10^{-16}$ and $\kappa_2 \approx 4.6\times10^{6}$, so none of the three numbers would be right. Fix: add "$i, j = 0, \dots, n-1$".
4. [minor] application, second bullet (line 231): "orthogonal polynomials, for which the corresponding Gram matrix is the identity and $\kappa_2 = 1$" needs them *orthonormal*. Merely orthogonal polynomials (Legendre, say, with $\int_{-1}^{1}P_k^2 = 2/(2k+1)$) give a diagonal Gram matrix whose condition number is the ratio of the extreme squared norms — $21$ for degrees up to $10$, so the point survives easily, but the claim as written does not. Fix: "orthonormal polynomials", or "for which the Gram matrix is diagonal, and the identity after normalisation".
5. [minor] application, first bullet (line 229): "squares the condition number and halves the number of trustworthy digits" is not what the squaring does. It *doubles the digits lost*: with $\varepsilon \approx 10^{-16}$ and $\kappa_2(A) = 10^4$, one goes from about 12 good digits to about 8, not to 6. Fix: "and doubles the number of digits lost".
6. [minor] statement "Sensitivity of a linear system", parts 1-2 (lines 99-100), and proof step 5 (line 69): statements carry material that belongs in the proof — part 1's attainment recipe ("take $b = s_1u_1$ and $\delta b$ any multiple of $u_n$", proved in step 3) and part 2's derivation ("then $\hat x - x = -A^{-1}\delta A\,\hat x$, hence", proved in step 4) — while step 5 of the first proof proves the $\kappa_2 = s_1/s_n$ formula of the *next* definition block and ends the proof with $\square$ after it. All of it is correct and the two copies agree; it is the block order of `docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md` that is bent. Fix: leave the derivations in the `proof` blocks, and either move step 5 after the condition-number definition or mark it as a lemma for what follows.
7. [minor] proof step 5 (line 73) and step 3 (line 65): "If $A$ is Hermitian positive definite its singular values are its eigenvalues ... by part 3" — part 3 of the statement asserts $\|A\|_2 = \rho(A)$ and the Rayleigh bounds, not the identification of the whole singular-value list with the eigenvalues. That identification is part 5 of the SVD existence statement, which step 3 does cite for the Hermitian case. Fix: cite the SVD statement there too.

Compared against the sources. Stakgold & Holst pp. 771-772 do carry the Rayleigh-quotient eigenvalue bounds and the spectral radius on p. 771, the induced operator norms on p. 772, the property "if $M$ is self-adjoint then $\rho(M) = \|M\|$" on p. 772, Lemma 10.6.1 (the $A$-self-adjoint version, whose $A = I$ case is exactly our part 3), and the condition number as $\|M\|\,\|M^{-1}\|$ extended to the $A$-inner product — so the two `adapted` blocks and the `src` locators are faithful. Strauss pp. 26-27 do place conditioning as the stability leg of well-posedness and do call such a matrix ill-conditioned, with the wording difference recorded in finding 2. Arfken Exercise 5.1.3 p. 267 gives the Hilbert matrix from mean-square power-series fitting and states $\det A = 3.7\times10^{-12}$ at $n = 5$, which the remark quotes correctly.
