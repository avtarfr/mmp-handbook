## Findings
1. [blocking] definition "The chain, its semigroup and its generator" (line 47) and proof "The forward and backward equations", step 2 (line 146): the whole Kolmogorov derivation rests on the short-time expansion of $P_h$, and the unit never establishes it. The definition block asserts $\mathbb{P}(X(t+h)=j\mid X(t)=i)=g_{ij}h+o(h)$ for $j\ne i$ with the words "a statement that is easy to prove when $S$ is finite" — the phrase the style guide forbids — and step 2 then uses it together with the diagonal case $p_{jj}(h)=1+g_{jj}h+o(h)$, whose stated reason ("the chance of leaving $j$ in time $h$ is $g_jh+o(h)$") is not a proof of it: the chance of not leaving is $e^{-g_jh}=1-g_jh+o(h)$, so that argument gives only $p_{jj}(h)\ge 1-g_jh+o(h)$, and closing the gap needs the separate fact that leaving and returning within $[0,h]$ has probability $O(h^2)$ when $S$ is finite. Without that fact the forward and backward equations are not proved. (G&S are explicit that this is an extra step: they cite (6.9.9) and set it as Exercise (6.9.11), p. 296.) Fix: insert a short derivation, for finite $S$, from the architecture statement — $\mathbb{P}_i(\text{no jump by }h)=e^{-g_ih}$, $\mathbb{P}_i(\text{exactly one jump, to }j)=y_{ij}g_ih+o(h)$, $\mathbb{P}_i(\ge 2\text{ jumps by }h)\le (\max_k g_k)^2h^2$ — and then quote the resulting expansion in step 2.
2. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", second paragraph (line 202): "Finiteness of $S$ is used once, in step 2 of the proof, to collect infinitely many $o(h)$ terms into one" contradicts step 2 itself, which says "The $o(h)$ terms may be collected into one because $S$ is finite: there are finitely many of them." Finiteness is used precisely because the terms are finite in number. Fix: "...to collect the $o(h)$ terms into one, there being only finitely many of them".
3. [minor] remark "Where the hypotheses are used", first paragraph (line 200): "It enters at exactly one place, the proof that $g_i<\infty$" points at a proof the unit does not contain (part 2 of the architecture statement is quoted from G&S Theorem (6.9.6) without proof); and right-continuity is used in the unit at a second place, in the definition block, to make the jump times $T_m$ well defined. Fix: refer to the assertion $g_i<\infty$ in part 2 of the statement rather than to "the proof", and mention the well-definedness of the $T_m$.
4. [minor] statement "The architecture of a continuous-time chain", part 4 (line 62): "given a generator — a transition matrix $Y$ with $y_{ii}=0$ and non-negative constants $g_i$" conflicts with the term fixed two blocks earlier, where a generator is a matrix with non-negative off-diagonal entries and zero row sums. The pair $(Y,(g_i))$ carries the same information, but the word has been given a different meaning inside one unit. Fix: "given the data $(Y,(g_i))$ — equivalently, a generator $G$ — where $Y$ is a transition matrix with $y_{ii}=0$ and the $g_i$ are non-negative constants".
5. [minor] idea, second paragraph (line 129): the memorylessness step conditions on $\{U_0>s\}$, which is an event about the whole path on $[0,s]$ and is not covered by the Markov property as the unit states it (finitely many times $t_1<\cdots<t_n$). The source uses the extended Markov property (6.9.5a), which this unit never states although part 3 of the architecture statement also depends on its strong form (6.9.5b). Fix: state the extended and strong Markov properties in the definition block, or say in the idea that the step uses the extended Markov property of the source.
6. [minor] method, step 6 (line 221): "Diagonalise $G=B\Lambda B^{-1}$" is offered as the recipe, but a generator need not be diagonalisable (a defective $G$ gives $t^ke^{\theta t}$ terms), and the method gives the reader no fallback. Fix: add a clause, e.g. "if $G$ is defective, use the Jordan form (:ref to linalg.jordan-form) or solve the system directly; the relaxation estimate below is unaffected".
