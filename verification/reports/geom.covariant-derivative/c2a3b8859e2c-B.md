Page 728 carries the Levi-Civita connection ($\nabla_ah_{bc}=0$), the failure of covariant derivatives to commute, the Riemann tensor formula, and the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature — everything else the block uses, so the citation is one page short rather than wrong. Fix: cite pp. 727-728 in both the block `src` and the frontmatter `pages`/`loc`.
2. [minor] application, same block: "the Einstein tensor $G_{ab}=R_{ab}-\tfrac12Rg_{ab}$, which encodes the deformation of spacetime ... the stress-energy tensor that encodes matter and energy" tracks p. 727's phrasing ("the Einstein tensor encoding the deformation of spacetime", "the stress-energy tensor that encodes the matter and energy distribution of spacetime") closely for two short clauses, and "is what is left over" repeats p. 728's "What is left over is the Riemann tensor" (used twice, here and in the remark). Each is short and definitional, and the automated overlap check is clean, so this is not a close paraphrase of a passage; still worth rewording one of the two "left over"s.
3. [minor] definition "Christoffel symbols": "$[ij,k]$ ... a notation due to Arfken, Weber and Harris". Arfken, Weber and Harris *define* the symbol of the first kind at Eq. (4.59), p. 223, and the unit's identity $[ij,k]=g_{mk}\Gamma^m{}_{ij}=e_k\cdot\partial e_i/\partial u^j$ reproduces their (4.59)-(4.60) exactly, but the bracket is classical Christoffel/Ricci notation and they make no claim to it. Fix: "the notation used by Arfken, Weber and Harris".
4. [minor] example "Parallel transport around a circle of latitude on the sphere", labelled `provenance=original`: do Carmo's Example 1, p. 246 — inside the unit's cited range 241-246 — is the parallel transport of a unit vector round a parallel of colatitude on the unit sphere, with the same answer ($2\pi\sin\psi=2\pi\cos\varphi$, his Fig. 4-13). The route here is genuinely different and genuinely ours (metric formula, the two-component linear ODE, the complex substitution $z=a+ib$), and the numbers ($\theta_0=\pi/3$, $\Delta=\pi$, the Foucault figures) are ours, so I do not read this as a mislabelled block. But the block should say in one clause that do Carmo computes the same transport by unrolling the tangent cone, and that the two agree.
5. [minor] proof of "Parallel transport exists, is unique, and preserves the metric" (src `docarmo-2016:section 4-4, Proposition 1, p. 244`): Proposition 1, p. 244, is the inner-product preservation used in part 2; existence and uniqueness, part 1, is do Carmo's **Proposition 2, p. 245**, and the reparametrisation argument of part 3 is his remark on p. 245. Fix: widen the block `src` to "Propositions 1 and 2 and the remark, pp. 244-245" (the frontmatter already lists all three).
6. [minor] statement "The metric is covariantly constant" (src `rhb-2006:section 26.18, Eq. (26.79), p. 966`): (26.79) is $\partial g_{ij}/\partial u^k=\Gamma^l{}_{ik}g_{lj}+\Gamma^l{}_{jk}g_{il}$, the input to the result, not the result; Riley, Hobson and Bence do not state $g_{ij;k}=0$ anywhere in pp. 965-971. The derivation is ours and is correct, and Stakgold and Holst p. 728 does state the Levi-Civita property $\nabla_ah_{bc}=0$ that the block names. Fix: mark the three vanishing statements as ours, derived from RHB (26.79).
7. [minor] remark, "The coordinate basis, in the symmetry": "Connection coefficients defined the same way for a frame that is *not* the coordinate frame ... **are not** symmetric in their lower indices". They *need not* be; for the Cartesian orthonormal frame, which is also a coordinate frame, they are symmetric (indeed zero). The polar example given is correct — for $\hat e_\rho,\hat e_\phi$ one gets $\omega^\phi{}_{\rho\phi}=1$, $\omega^\phi{}_{\phi\rho}=0$.
8. [minor] block order: the unit has three `proof` blocks but one `idea` block. The style guide asks for an idea before the proof; the two short proofs (metric compatibility, parallel transport) have none. A sentence each would do.

## Checked
- Counterexample search: tried to break each of the four items of the main statement. Symmetry: holds for any coordinate frame, fails for a non-coordinate frame (the remark's own point, verified on the plane-polar orthonormal frame, finding 7). Metric formula: verified that it is forced, i.e. that the three cyclic equations have the stated unique solution, by redoing the cancellation index by index — the two cancelling pairs are $\Gamma^l{}_{ki}g_{jl}$ against $\Gamma^l{}_{ik}g_{lj}$ and $\Gamma^l{}_{kj}g_{li}$ against $\Gamma^l{}_{jk}g_{il}$, each using both symmetries, leaving $2\Gamma^l{}_{ij}g_{kl}$ as claimed. Transformation law: the "difference of two connections is a tensor" corollary is right because the inhomogeneous term is independent of $\Gamma$. Vanishing-in-one-system argument in the remark: correct, since every term of a tensor law is linear in the components. Degenerate points: $\Gamma^\phi{}_{\rho\phi}=1/\rho$ blows up at $\rho=0$, matching RHB (26.83). Transport statement: item 1 needs only continuity of $\Gamma(u(t))\dot u(t)$ and linearity — no completeness or boundedness hypothesis is hidden; item 2 needs metric compatibility, as the remark says; item 3 needs regularity of the reparametrisation, which is stated.
