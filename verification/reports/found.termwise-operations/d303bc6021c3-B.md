## Findings
1. [blocking] example "A geometric cosine series...", step 6 "What made it work" (line 158): the stated reason why hypothesis 3 fails for the comparison series is false. With $u_n = \cos(nx)/n^2$ the differentiated series is $-\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$, whose **terms** satisfy $\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \lvert \sin(nx)/n\rvert = 1/n \to 0$, so they *do* tend to $0$ uniformly, on every neighbourhood of $0$ and indeed on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (checked numerically: $\sup_{|x| \le 0.01}|\sin(nx)/n| = 0.0099998, 0.0099833, 0.0084147, 0.00098936$ for $n = 1, 10, 100, 1000$). The conclusion is right but the mechanism is different: it is the *series* $\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$ that fails to converge uniformly near $0$, because its sum is $(\pi-x)/2$ on $(0,2\pi)$ and $0$ at $x=0$, hence discontinuous there. Numerically the remainder at $x = \pi/N$ after $N$ terms is $-0.278521$, $-0.281115$, $-0.281140$ for $N = 10, 100, 1000$, bounded away from $0$. Fix: say that the tails of the differentiated series do not go to $0$ uniformly on any neighbourhood of $0$ (equivalently, its sum is discontinuous at $0$), instead of attributing the failure to the terms.
