## Findings
1. [blocking] example, step 6 "What made it work" (line 158): the stated reason hypothesis 3 fails for the comparison series is false. For $u_n(x) = \cos(nx)/n^2$ the differentiated series is $-\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$, and its *terms* do tend to $0$ uniformly on $\mathbb{R}$: $\sup_x \lvert \sin(nx)/n\rvert = 1/n \to 0$ (attained at $x = \pi/(2n)$). So "has terms that do not tend to $0$ uniformly in any neighbourhood of $0$" is wrong, and as written it is also not a reason for anything, since uniformly vanishing terms are compatible with hypothesis 3. What actually fails is uniform convergence of the *series*: $\sum_{n\ge1}\sin(nx)/n = (\pi-x)/2$ for $0 < x < 2\pi$ and $0$ at $x = 0$, so the sum is discontinuous at $0$ and cannot be a uniform limit of continuous partial sums on any neighbourhood of $0$. The conclusion drawn ("hypothesis 3 is lost") is correct; only the justification is wrong. Fix: replace the clause with "the differentiated series $-\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$ does not converge uniformly on any neighbourhood of $0$ — it converges pointwise to a function with a jump at $0$, which no uniformly convergent series of continuous terms can have — so hypothesis 3 is lost". (The terms-tend-to-zero phrasing can be dropped entirely.)

2. [minor] example, step 1 "The hypotheses" (line 127): the hypotheses of the term-by-term integration theorem are not numbered — that statement has *parts* 1 (sequence form) and 2 (series form), and only the differentiation theorem has hypotheses 1, 2, 3. So "hypothesis 2 of term-by-term integration holds on every finite interval" names something that does not exist, and "All three hypotheses of both theorems are met on any $[a,b]$" is wrong for the integration theorem. Fix: say "the hypotheses of part 2 of term-by-term integration hold on every finite interval" and close the item with "all the hypotheses of both theorems are met on any $[a,b]$".
