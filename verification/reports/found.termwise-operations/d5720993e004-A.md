## Findings
1. [minor] example, step 6 "What made it work" (line 158): the three quoted tail values are given as the tail of the differentiated series, which the preceding sentence writes as $-\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$ (with the minus sign); the numbers $-0.278521$, $-0.281115$, $-0.281140$ are the tails of $\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$, and the tails of the differentiated series as written are $+0.278521$, $+0.281115$, $+0.281140$. The quoted limit $\pi/2 - \operatorname{Si}(\pi) = -0.2811407$ has the same sign slip. The conclusion drawn (the tail does not shrink) is unaffected. Fix: attribute the numbers to the tail of $\sum_n \sin(nx)/n$, or flip all four signs.
2. [minor] example, step 6 (line 158): the closed form $\sum_{n\ge1}\sin(nx)/n = (\pi-x)/2$ on $(0,2\pi)$ is quoted without proof, source or prerequisite; neither listed prereq supplies it and it is not elementary calculus. The non-uniformity conclusion does not depend on it (the tail estimate in the same sentence establishes it directly). Fix: cite it, or drop the closed form and let the non-shrinking tail carry the argument.
3. [minor] remark, fifth bullet (line 120): the contrast drawn between the two Riley-Hobson-Bence warnings is not supported by the pages. Item (v) on p. 131 is itself stated for series in general ("it is not true in general that term-by-term differentiation or integration..."), and the §4.5.2(iii) note on p. 135 is likewise about the interval of convergence being unaltered, not about termwise operations in general. Calling the first "the narrower one" and the second "the general warning" reverses nothing but distinguishes two statements of the same scope. Fix: say that both pages make the same point, p. 135 adding that power series are the exception.
4. [minor] application, second paragraph (line 164): the M-test bound $n^2\lvert b_n\rvert e^{-n^2\kappa t_0}$ is summed as if $\lvert b_n\rvert$ were bounded, which is true for Fourier sine coefficients of an integrable profile but is not said. Fix: add "with the $b_n$ bounded, as Fourier coefficients of an integrable profile are".
