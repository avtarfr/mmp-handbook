## Findings
1. [blocking] proof (line 37): step 1 says to "approximate $f$ first by a continuous periodic function" and gives no justification. This is the hard part of the proof for a merely Riemann-integrable $f$. A continuous $g$ that is close in $L^1$ is not automatically close in mean square. The argument needs the bound $\sup|g| \le \sup|f| = B$, which holds because a Riemann-integrable function is bounded. With that bound, $\|f-g\|^2 \le \frac{2B}{2\pi}\int|f-g| \le C\varepsilon^2$ (compare the book, p. 79). Without that bound, the step is unproved. This is also the only place where the hypothesis (Riemann integrability, and so boundedness) is used, and the proof never says so. Fix: state the approximation lemma that is used (a continuous periodic $g$ with $\sup|g|\le\sup|f|$ and $\int|f-g|<\varepsilon^2$), derive the mean-square estimate from the uniform bound, and name the boundedness of $f$ at this step.
2. [blocking] prereqs (line 7): the proof uses two results that are not listed as prerequisites and are not standard undergraduate calculus or linear algebra. One is Fejér's theorem: the Fejér means of a continuous periodic function converge to it uniformly (line 37). The other is the approximation of an integrable function by continuous periodic functions (line 37). The only listed prerequisite is `fixtures.bessel-inequality`. Fix: add units for Fejér's theorem (or, equivalently, uniform density of trigonometric polynomials in the continuous periodic functions) and for the continuous-approximation lemma, and add them to `prereqs`.
3. [minor] proof (line 38): the logic of step 2 runs backwards. It asserts $\|f - S_N f\| \le \|f - P\|$ "because" of orthogonality, then says "Hence" the Pythagorean identity holds. In fact orthogonality gives the identity, and the identity gives the inequality. The step also leaves out why orthogonality applies here: $S_N f - P$ has degree at most $N$ when $N \ge M$. Fix: "For $N \ge M$, $S_N f - P$ is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most $N$, so it is orthogonal to $f - S_N f$. Pythagoras gives $\|f-P\|^2 = \|f-S_Nf\|^2 + \|S_Nf-P\|^2$, hence $\|f-S_Nf\| \le \|f-P\|$."
4. [minor] proof (lines 29–38): this unit writes $S_N f$, while the prerequisite writes $S_N$, and the unit never defines "trigonometric polynomial of degree at most $M$". Fix: define $S_N f = \sum_{|n|\le N}\hat f(n)e_n$ and "degree at most $M$" (a combination of the $e_n$ with $|n|\le M$) in a short definition block or at first use.
5. [minor] structure (after line 41): there is no "where the hypotheses are used" remark, which the style guide expects for a theorem. Fix: add a remark that says boundedness of the Riemann-integrable $f$ is what turns $L^1$ approximation into mean-square approximation in step 1.
6. [minor] application (line 54): "a signal's variance equals the sum of its spectral power over frequencies" holds only once the mean is removed. $\|f\|^2$ is the mean square, and the variance is $\sum_{n\ne 0}|\hat f(n)|^2$, which leaves out the zero-frequency term $|\hat f(0)|^2$. Fix: say "the variance equals the sum of spectral power over the non-zero frequencies (equivalently, for a zero-mean signal, the mean square equals the total spectral power)".
