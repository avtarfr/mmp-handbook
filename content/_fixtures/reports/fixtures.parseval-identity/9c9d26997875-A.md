## Findings
1. [minor] proof (line 49): The triangle inequality is justified by the Cauchy–Schwarz prerequisite, but that unit is stated for inner-product spaces with $(f,f)>0$ for $f\neq 0$. The Riemann-integrable functions with this form are only positive semidefinite (a function nonzero at one point has norm 0). The step is still valid, because the Cauchy–Schwarz proof divides only by the squared norm of its second argument, and here that argument is $v = g - P$, which is continuous, so $\|v\| = 0$ forces $v = 0$. Fix: add a clause saying so, e.g. "apply Cauchy–Schwarz with $g - P$, which is continuous, in the second slot", or note that Cauchy–Schwarz holds for positive semidefinite forms.
2. [minor] remark (line 59): The example $|x|^{-1/2}$ is correct as stated ($\|f\|^2 = \infty$), but it is not a counterexample to the identity. Its coefficients satisfy $|\hat f(n)|^2 \sim 1/(2\pi |n|)$, so the left side is also infinite, and the identity holds formally as $\infty = \infty$. Fix: say that both sides diverge, so the identity leaves the setting of finite real numbers rather than becoming false. Readers should not take it as a failure of the identity.
3. [minor] proof (line 32): $S_N f$ is not defined in this unit. The Bessel prerequisite calls it $S_N$, and the Fejér prerequisite defines $S_n f$. Fix: define $S_N f = \sum_{|n|\le N} \hat f(n) e_n$ once at the start of the proof.
