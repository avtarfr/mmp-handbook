## Findings
1. [minor] proof (line 50): the order of reasoning is reversed. The inequality $\|f - S_N f\| \le \|f - P\|$ is a consequence of the Pythagorean identity, but the text asserts it first and then says "Hence" the identity. "Polynomial of degree at most $N$" should also read "trigonometric polynomial", and the step silently uses that $S_N f - P$ has degree at most $N$ when $N \ge M$. Fix: say that for $N \ge M$, $S_N f - P$ is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most $N$, so it is orthogonal to $f - S_N f$ (Bessel's proof gives orthogonality to each $e_m$, $|m| \le N$). Pythagoras then gives $\|f - P\|^2 = \|f - S_N f\|^2 + \|S_N f - P\|^2 \ge \|f - S_N f\|^2$.
2. [minor] proof (line 37): "the only place in the proof where the hypothesis on $f$ is used" is too strong taken literally. Riemann integrability is also what makes the Bessel splitting (line 32) and the orthogonality in step 2 available, because both need $\hat f(n)$, $\|f\|$ and the inner products to exist. Fix: say this is the only place where the hypothesis is used beyond making the integrals defined, namely through the boundedness of $f$.
3. [minor] unit (no remark block): there is no "where the hypotheses are used" remark (STYLE_GUIDE item 5). The relevant point is made only inside the proof (line 47), and no counterexample is given. Fix: add a remark that boundedness is used only in the passage from $f$ to $g$. Note there that for an unbounded, improperly integrable $f$ such as $|x|^{-1/2}$ the right-hand side $\|f\|^2$ is infinite, so the statement does not even make sense.
4. [minor] proof (line 49): the triangle inequality for $\|\cdot\|$ is used without justification. On Riemann-integrable functions $\|\cdot\|$ is only a seminorm, and the triangle inequality comes from Cauchy–Schwarz, which exists as fixtures.cauchy-schwarz but is not listed in prereqs. This is standard, so it is not blocking. Fix: add fixtures.cauchy-schwarz to prereqs and cite it here, noting that its proof does not need definiteness. Alternatively, state that the triangle inequality for the mean-square seminorm is standard.
